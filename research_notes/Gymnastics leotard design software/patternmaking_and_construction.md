# Patternmaking, cutting, sewing, decoration and finishing of competitive WAG leotards — factory and atelier practice (as of 13 October 2026)

Method note (read first). Evidence was collected on 13 Oct 2026. The shared web-search budget for this research run was exhausted after 22 searches, so most later evidence came from directly fetching first-party pages and PDFs (brand sites, pattern-company instruction sheets, supplier manuals, patents, vendor documentation, one peer-reviewed paper) and, for four JavaScript-rendered or bot-blocked sites (gkelite.com, support.clo3d.com, minervapatterns.com, chalkbucket.com), reading them in the in-app browser. Leads that could NOT be verified are listed in each Gaps block. "Factory" = volume manufacturer; "atelier" = small custom maker; "home" = home-sewing pattern companies and hobbyists. Swimwear, skating and dancewear sources are flagged where used; they concern the same fabric class (nylon- or polyester-elastane stretch knits). Prose from sources is paraphrased; numbers are as published. Where a figure came only from a search-result snippet and the page was not opened, it is marked "(snippet only)".

## 1. Pattern drafting for stretch fabric: block, negative ease, stretch measurement, body measurements, backs, colour-block panels, sleeves, grading, size charts

### Takeaway
There is no agreed industry standard for leotard negative ease: published reductions range from 0–10% of girth (home-sewing textbook rules) through roughly 15–22% of width and 0–11% of length (German trade press and the one quantitative CAD method found), and every source says the figure must be set per fabric by stretch-testing and fitting. The girth (torso loop) is the controlling measurement, sizes are picked by bust/chest for width and girth for length, brands grade the torso about 3 in (7.5 cm) per size, and design lines are realised either as 6 mm seams (straight or gently curved lines only) or as stitched overlays/appliqué (anything intricate).

### Cited Findings

**How stretch blocks are derived**
- Stretch garments are cut smaller than the body (negative ease) and rely on fabric extension for final size. The conventional route is: take a woven block, remove ease and darts, true the outline, then shrink it proportionally in width and length by a subjectively chosen stretch percentage; alternatives are draping the stretch fabric on a stand or simply using a smaller-size block. — [Watkins 2011, Indian J. Fibre & Textile Research 36:366–379](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- Knit blocks are dartless and carry zero ease at the "stable knit" level; each stretch category needs its own block, and the sleeve has to be re-matched every time the body width is reduced. — [Dresspatternmaking.com, Stretch Basics 2](https://dresspatternmaking.com/blog/stretch-basics-for-patternmaking-2-blocks)
- For mixed-material designs (e.g. lace plus jersey) the German method drafts to body measurements with no deduction and then reduces each individual piece by that material's own stretch factor, scaling width and length separately (10% stretch = scale 1:0.9). — [Müller & Sohn, "Pattern Cutting for Body Fashion", extract from Rundschau 2/2015, page dated 12 Jun 2022, modified 29 Oct 2025](https://www.muellerundsohn.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/204)
- Because pressure equals fabric tension divided by body radius (Laplace), small-radius parts (wrists, ankles, small children) need less reduction for the same pressure; Watkins applies a "tension release factor" of 1.0 for adults falling to 0.5 for young children. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)

- Sources for the table rows, in order: [Müller & Sohn](https://www.muellerundsohn.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/204); [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf); [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf); [Dresspatternmaking, Stretch Basics 2](https://dresspatternmaking.com/blog/stretch-basics-for-patternmaking-2-blocks); [Dresspatternmaking, Reduction in Width](https://dresspatternmaking.com/blog/reduction-in-width-for-stretch-blocks); [Mood Fabrics blog](https://www.moodfabrics.com/blog/?p=9524)
- Watkins' reduction algorithm in full: (1) measure fabric stretch % in course (c), wale (w) and bias (b); (2) course-bias vector cb = (c+b)/2, wale-bias vector wb = (w+b)/2; (3) allocate the available stretch with an axis ratio of 60% to girth and 40% to length for adults; (4) multiply by the tension release factor (1.0 adult … 0.5 small child); (5) stretch reduction SR% = vector × axis ratio / 100; (6) stretch reduction factor SRF = 100 / (100 + SR). Example: c 56, w 20, b 40 → cb 48, wb 30 → SR 28.8 and 12.0 → SRF 0.78 (X) and 0.89 (Y). — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- Watkins classes fit by proximity: form fit (no pressure), cling fit, action fit (most stretch sportswear), power fit (compressive); more of the available stretch is reserved for length than for girth because body length changes more in movement. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- A leotard-specialist author (Marie Porter, "Spandex Simplified") gives no percentage at all: start from the pattern's size chart, make a practice garment in a fabric of the same weight and stretch, size down in width only if needed, and do not size down in length unless measurements say so. — [Spandex Simplified, Basic Pattern Alterations, 16 Jan 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-pattern-alterations/)

**How stretch percentage is measured**
- Home patterns print a stretch gauge: Jalie 3901 (KATE gymnastics leotard, © 2019) requires a minimum 50% stretch in both width and length and prints a "fabric should stretch to here" ruler; Jalie 2792 (2007 envelope) requires 70% four-way stretch, while its current web listing says 60% in both directions. — [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698); [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf); [Jalie 2792 product page](https://jalie.com/products/gymnastics-leotards-sewing-pattern)
- Other home-sewing requirements: at least 75% stretch (Treasurie); 50–75% stretch with about 80% nylon / 20% spandex and four-way stretch (Peek-a-Boo). — [Treasurie leotard sew-along](https://blog.treasurie.com/how-to-make-a-leotard/); [Peek-a-Boo Pattern Shop](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/leotard-patterns)
- The research literature is inconsistent on test specimen and load: 20 × 20 cm with a 500 g load (Ziegert & Keil) versus 7.5 × 25 cm at about 1 kg/cm (Murden); the formal standards BS 4952:1992, BS EN 14704-1:2005 and ASTM D 4964-96 are QA/QC tests that Watkins judges unsuited to deriving pattern geometry. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- Watkins' "quad load" test, designed for pattern reduction: cut 5 × 20 cm strips in course, wale, 45° bias and 135° bias; mark benchmarks 10 cm apart; fold 2.5 cm at each end for hanger bars; hang a 250 g weight; wait one minute; stretch % = extended benchmark length in mm minus 100. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- Measured values with that test on five polyester/elastane sport knits (180–260 g, 12–20% elastane): course 18–56%, wale 10–32%, bias 14–57%; some fabrics stretched differently on the two biases (e.g. 39% vs 35%), which produced visible left/right fit differences in test garments. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- In wear, garments use only the lower part of the stretch curve (roughly up to 30–40%), well below the fabric's maximum. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- Factory practice at GK Elite: every incoming fabric roll gets a manual four-way stretch test and failing rolls go back to the vendor; full-time fit specialists check each style and live models (mostly gymnasts, all ages) try on new styles, fabrics, custom designs and special orders. — [GK Elite, The GK Fit](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-the-gk-fit)

**Key body measurements**
- GK measures chest (fullest part, arms down), waist (narrowest part), hip (fullest part of seat, feet together) and torso (from the centre of one shoulder down the front, through the legs and back up to the same shoulder); torso is called the most important measurement; partial inches are rounded up and between-size athletes take the larger size. — [GK size chart, inches, rev. March 2015](https://gkelite.azureedge.net/images/static/sizecharts/size-charts-inches-womens-leos.pdf)
- Jalie's rule for one-piece bodysuits: choose the size by bust for width and by girth for length. — [Jalie 2677 instructions](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/2677NEW.pdf)
- Porter's atelier measurement set: bust, underbust, waist, hips, girth loop, back-waist length, arm, inseam; measured over a leotard and taken twice. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Measure, 18 May 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-measure-for-spandex-costuming/)
- Stretch absorbs small width errors but not torso-length errors, so making a leotard oversized for growth is discouraged (Peek-a-Boo); Sylvia P likewise tells clubs not to add centimetres for growing room. — [Peek-a-Boo Pattern Shop](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/leotard-patterns); [Sylvia P Teamwear sizing guide](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/sizing-measuring-guide-team-wear)
- GK states that fit and feel vary significantly with fabric type and embellishment, and that it keeps one body-measurement size chart regardless. — [GK women's sizing instructions](https://gkelite.azureedge.net/images/static/gkforms/gk-gymnastics-womens-sizing-measuring-instruction.pdf)

**Grading evidence**
- In the GK chart the torso grades 3 in per child size (CXS 40–42 → CL 49–51) and 2–3 in per adult size; each "long torso" equals the regular torso of the next size up and each "short torso" the next size down, i.e. the length variants are a one-size length grade applied without a width change. — [GK size chart, inches](https://gkelite.azureedge.net/images/static/sizecharts/size-charts-inches-womens-leos.pdf)
- Jalie nests 22–28 sizes in one pattern; in 3901 the girls' torso grades 5 cm per size (F–L) then about 4–5 cm, bust 1 in per size, while women's torso grades only about ½ in per size through the middle sizes (S–Y) and ½–1¼ in per size above that, as bust and hip keep growing 1–1½ in. — [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698)
- Jalie also grades trim: leg-elastic cut length on its bodysuit 2677 runs 35.5 cm (size F) to 63.3 cm (size AA), stepping 0.8 cm per size for the small girls' sizes and about 1.5–1.6 cm per size above that. — [Jalie 2677 instructions](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/2677NEW.pdf)
- Atelier grading between sizes: pick the largest size any measurement falls in, then blend freehand between size lines from chest to waist to hip; change length by slash-and-spread (or fold) above the waist for upper-torso differences and below it for lower-torso differences, 1 in above the waist being a reasonable default. — [Spandex Simplified, Basic Pattern Alterations](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-pattern-alterations/)
- Other atelier adjustments with numbers: add about ½ cm of length per cup size above C at the fullest chest; remove about 1 in at the back waist for a curved back; redraw the back leg opening outward by 1–2+ in to stop it riding up. — [Spandex Simplified, Basic Pattern Alterations](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-pattern-alterations/)
- Commercial CAD advertises grading features aimed at this product class: TUKAcad lists custom grade rules, automatic half-size grading and angle grading for lingerie and swim, plus made-to-measure alteration from imported customer measurements. — [Tukatech TUKAcad](https://tukatech.com/tukacad/)
- Home sewers report brand-to-brand block differences: opinions on Jalie range from slightly small to slightly large against its chart, Kwik Sew is consistently reported as running large (one or more sizes), and several makers alter a master pattern (longer body, narrower width) for competitive gymnasts. — [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard, Jan–Feb 2014](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)

**Altering the block for different necklines and backs (atelier, Porter)**
- Do size and length alterations first, then neck/back changes on a traced copy. Scoop neck: mark the low point and redraw; allow about ¾ in of fabric lost to the elastic finish and cut conservatively. Boat neck: needs a shelf-bra lining. — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations, 5 Nov 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Keyhole / hole back: keep more than 1.5 in between the neckline and the top of the opening for the two elastic finishes; neck plus hole must be big enough to step into; extend the opening ½–1 in for closure adjustment; close with a butterfly clasp. — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Strappy backs: shoulder width = 2 × elastic width + (number of straps × strap width), e.g. three ⅜ in straps with ⅜ in elastic = 1⅞ in; keep at least 2 in between the side seam and the nearest strap and at least 1.5 in between centre back and the nearest strap; a single-strap shoulder tapers to strap width plus twice the elastic width. — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Banded (T-shirt style) collar: strip width = 2 × (finished height + ¼ in); strip length = measured neckline minus 1–2 in; choker collar length = neck measurement + 2 in. — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Illusion ("cutaway") necklines: cut the bodice to the false neckline and back it with sheer mesh that overlaps 1 in inside the bodice edge; glue, let dry, then zigzag as an appliqué. — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Commercial home patterns offer the same back vocabulary: Jalie 2792 has a plain scoop back, a keyhole back with back-neck clasp and banded jewel neckline, and a banded square neck with clasp. — [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf)
- GK's own blog summary of competition dress codes says leg openings may not be cut above the hip bone, bare midriffs and "backless" openings are not allowed, and racerback and spaghetti-strap leotards are prohibited; it does not attribute each rule to a specific governing body. — [GK Elite blog, Gymnastics Dress Code, 5 Sep 2026](https://blog.gkelite.com/gymnastics-dress-code-what-to-wear-to-gymnastics-competitions/)

**Splitting a block into colour-block panels; pieced seams versus overlays**
- Atelier method: trace the fitted pattern, draw design lines, mark a grain guide parallel to centre front on every piece, cut apart, and add ¼ in (6 mm) seam allowance only to the new edges (existing edges already include theirs); for asymmetric designs make a full (unfolded) pattern. — [Spandex Simplified, Colour Blocking, 23 Mar 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)
- Keep pieced lines straight or gently curved; plan the sewing order so no seam has to turn a corner or set a square edge into an L-shaped opening; join small pieces into blocks first; stagger pieces at points so allowances cross on the seam line; press allowances toward the darker fabric. Intricate or "wild" shapes should be appliquéd instead. — [Spandex Simplified, Colour Blocking](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)
- An experienced home maker of gymnastics leotards uses the same decision rule: simple lines and curves are seamed and then coverstitched flat; swirly designs are overlaid and zigzagged like appliqué. — [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)
- A V-shaped inset seam needs stay-stitching ¼ in from the edge for about 3 in up from the point and clipping into the V before sewing. — [Baby Lock, Gymnast Leotard project](https://babylock.com/gymnast-leotard)
- Jalie's 3901 gymnastics pattern combines both: inverted-V seaming front and back plus optional appliqués at waist and neck, with about 30 cm of contrast fabric allowed for the appliqués. — [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698)

**Sleeves**
- A conventional high-crowned set-in sleeve drafted in stretch fabric restricts arm lift and leaves a fold at the crown when the arm drops; a stretch sleeve should have a shallower, shirt-like crown (wider underarm), and Watkins' analysis bodysuit is drafted as if the arms were held out at about 45°. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- Mesh is the usual fabric for competition-leotard sleeves at GK. — [GK Elite blog, Leotard Fabrics & Embellishments, 29 Jan 2026](https://blog.gkelite.com/leotard-fabrics-embellishments/)
- A home maker found pattern long sleeves too tight for a muscular child gymnast and cut them looser. — [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)

### Inferences
- A design tool should not hard-code one ease percentage. It should hold a fabric record (stretch % in course, wale and both biases at a stated load) and compute separate X and Y scale factors per fabric, using Watkins' formula as the documented default and letting the manufacturer override; the published examples bracket the outputs at roughly 0.78–0.95 in width and 0.89–1.00 in length.
- Because Müller & Sohn reduce each piece by its own fabric's stretch and brands mix mesh, foil and velvet in one garment, reduction factors need to be per panel (per fabric assignment), not per garment, with seam lengths re-trued between neighbouring panels after scaling.
- The driving parameters for the parametric block are girth (length), chest/bust (width), then waist and hip; long/short torso variants can be generated as a pure one-size length grade (about ±3 in / 7.5 cm of girth), which matches GK's and k-Bee's charts.
- Grade rules for a default size run can be derived from the charts above (torso about 3 in per size for girls; about 2–3 in for women at GK but only ½ in at Jalie), so the app must let each manufacturer load its own grade table rather than ship one.
- A design line can be realised three ways with different cost and constraints — pieced seam (straight/gentle curves, 6 mm allowances each side), stitched overlay/appliqué (any shape, extra layer), or print (sublimation, no seam) — so the tool should store the intended realisation per line and validate geometry accordingly (flag inside corners, L/V insets and tight curves on lines marked "pieced").
- Strap and keyhole formulas from Porter are directly encodable as minimum-width and minimum-spacing checks.

### Gaps
- No factory block or factory ease percentage from any leotard manufacturer (GK, Sylvia P, Quatro, Milano, Christian Moreau) was found; they publish only body-measurement charts.
- Stuart Anderson's "Pattern School" (a lead from memory; widely cited for a free leotard block with percentage reductions) could not be verified: patternschool.com now redirects to an unrelated advertising domain, and archive services were not used.
- The standard textbooks (Armstrong; Aldrich; Haggar; Richardson; Shoben's stretch guide) are named in Watkins' reference list but their actual leotard-draft numbers were not accessible; Richardson is the probable origin of the 0/2/3/5% table but that attribution is unconfirmed.

## 2. How fabric stretch changes the pattern (mesh, velvet, foil), direction of greatest stretch, grainline and nap

### Takeaway
The greatest stretch always runs around the body (the knit's course direction), even "four-way" fabrics stretch unequally in the two directions, and foil-printed, velvet, illusion and mesh fabrics each behave differently enough that makers say sizing goes off if a less stretchy fabric is substituted; brands warn about durability of mesh, foil ("Mystique") and sublimated fabrics but none publishes a numeric pattern correction.

### Cited Findings
- Cutting rule from the leotard-specialist literature: find the direction of greatest stretch and lay every body piece so that direction runs around the body, never swapping it to suit a print, or the garment will not fit; grain lines are parallel and perpendicular to the factory edge. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Cut Spandex, 17 Jan 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-cut-spandex/)
- Instrumented testing agrees: within the working range of wear the course (crosswise) direction offers the least resistance and the largest extension in every fabric tested, even though the wale stretches more at very high loads. — [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)
- Two-way stretch means stretch in one direction only (length or width); four-way means both; a good four-way spandex still differed by about 1 in of extension on a roughly 5 in sample between its two directions. — [Spandex Simplified, Types of Stretch Fabric, 17 May 2022](https://spandexsimplified.com/types-of-stretch-fabric/)
- Exceptions to "stretch around the body": straps are cut with the greatest stretch along their length, and long narrow appliqués may need stretch running lengthwise. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Make Elastic Straps, 24 Mar 2022](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-make-elastic-straps/); [Spandex Simplified, Padded Applique, 27 Apr 2022](https://spandexsimplified.com/padded-applique/)
- Fabric families as described by Porter: nylon/spandex tricot (preferred 80–85% nylon, 15–20% spandex; shiny, matte or wet-look); polyester/spandex (thinner, usually printed on one side so the white base shows when stretched); spandex-backed stretch velvet (four-way, about $20–40 per metre, has nap); panne velvet (two-way, not on a spandex base, only for low-stretch accents); power mesh (firm, used as internal support); stretch mesh (sheer four-way, delicate, comes in skin tones and can be tea- or coffee-dyed); "illusion"/glistenette (limited stretch in one direction); hologram spandex (foil fused onto nylon spandex in patterns); Mystique (hologram with very small close microdots; a brand name used generically); solid metallic foil (good four-way versions are printed on a coordinating base colour, cheap ones on thin white spandex rub off); foil-print accent fabrics (usually only two-way). — [Spandex Simplified, Types of Stretch Fabric](https://spandexsimplified.com/types-of-stretch-fabric/)
- Supplier data for the same classes: Pine Crest's Kaleidoscope foiled stretch tricot is 200 gsm, four-way stretch, nylon/spandex in solid colours or polyester/spandex as a white base for digital printing, made in Korea, minimum 15 yards per colour; its Mesh 65 is a 65 gsm four-way-stretch mesh sold for inserts and panels with a 220-yard minimum from overseas stock in 128 colours. — [Pine Crest Fabrics, Kaleidoscope](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/blog/back-in-stock-kaleidoscope-foiled-stretch-tricot/); [Pine Crest Fabrics, Mesh 65](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/blog/mesh-65-soft-weightless-and-stylish-inserts-for-your-activewear-collection/)
- Brand fabric menus in 2026: GK names SoftLux, Mystique, Primatex, SubFuse (its sublimation base), Velvet, Mesh, Rib and DryTech, and sublimates onto white SubFuse, Rib, Holotek or Opalescence; Sylvia P offers Matt Lycra, Mystique, Mesh, Fog Mesh (mesh with metallic foil), Wet Look, Diamond Mesh, and sublimated colours on Sublimated Matt and Sublimated Mesh; Quatro offers Lycra (matt), Mystique, Mesh and Sublimated Lycra plus three nude tones for lining and mesh. — [GK blog, Leotard Fabrics & Embellishments](https://blog.gkelite.com/leotard-fabrics-embellishments/); [GK, About Sublimation](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/about-sublimation); [Sylvia P Teamwear fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options); [Quatro fabrics](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/tw-fabrics)
- Home pattern companies treat Mystique as an acceptable main fabric provided it meets the stretch gauge (Jalie 3901 lists nylon+spandex, polyester+spandex and mystique at 50% minimum stretch both ways). — [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698)
- Practitioner reports that plain lycra is stretchier than the shiny foil fabrics and that a fabric slightly less stretchy than the pattern assumes throws the sizing off. — [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)
- Müller & Sohn warn that some fabrics lose length when stretched in width, so stretch must be verified for each material before fixing reductions. — [Müller & Sohn](https://www.muellerundsohn.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/204)
- Nap: all velvet pieces (including straps) must be laid in the same direction because pile direction changes the apparent colour; Porter cuts with the nap smoothing downward for a sleeker look. One-way prints likewise need all pieces facing the same way. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Cut Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-cut-spandex/)
- Durability ranking from a manufacturer: matt lycra is the most resilient and fades least; Mystique is sensitive and may fade with wear and washing, and its crystal-decorated areas may dull slightly from the heat of application; mesh and foil mesh are fine and can pull or tear on rough surfaces; sublimated fabrics are prone to pilling on rough surfaces. — [Sylvia P Teamwear fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options)
- GK classes hologram, mesh and foil ("Mystique") fabrics as fragile: fabric softener attacks the adhesives, deodorant and perspiration can cause foil loss or colour bleed, mesh can be damaged by contact during routines and carries no lifespan guarantee (mesh designs require a signed waiver), and dark Mystique colours and one red velvet are listed as bleed risks next to light fabrics. — [GK Elite care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions)
- Foil and PVC surfaces drag under the presser foot; a Teflon foot is the usual fix. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Applique Spandex, 29 Dec 2020](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-applique-spandex/)

### Inferences
- The fabric library in the app needs, per fabric: fibre content, weight, stretch % per direction, which direction is greatest, nap/one-way flag, printable-side flag (white base showing on stretch), sublimation compatibility, and a fragility class that drives warnings (mesh in contact zones, foil under crystals, sublimated fabric in abrasion zones).
- The grainline exported on each pattern piece should be defined as "greatest stretch across", with nested pieces locked to one orientation (no 180° rotation) whenever the fabric is flagged as napped, foil-printed with a directional pattern, or one-way printed; that reduces marker efficiency and should feed the yield estimate.
- Since no manufacturer publishes a correction, the practical encoding of "foil/velvet/mesh fits tighter" is the per-panel stretch-derived scale factor from section 1 rather than a fixed size bump.

### Gaps
- No numeric stretch percentages were found for stretch velvet, Mystique/hologram, or stretch mesh specifically, and no quantified loss of stretch from foil printing.
- No source addressed a nap or directional-cutting rule for foil fabrics beyond the general one-way-print rule.
- GK's fabric compositions (SubFuse, Holotek, Opalescence, Primatex) are not published on the pages read; the swatch catalogue is behind a dealer login.

## 3. Cutting: single-ply versus stacked, hand/rotary, die, automated knife and laser; marker making and fabric yield

### Takeaway
GK spreads four-way-stretch fabric in layers and laser-cuts it, bundling pieces by athlete size and alteration, and (for sublimated styles) cuts before printing; other sublimation makers print the roll first and laser-cut afterwards. Ateliers cut single garments by hand with shears or a rotary cutter and no pins. A one-colour leotard needs roughly 0.5–1.35 m of 150 cm fabric depending on size and sleeves.

### Cited Findings
- GK (2021): four-way-stretch fabric is spread and laser cut, then pieces are bundled by athlete size and alteration; staff roles include fabric spreaders and cutters. — [NBC Sports, Inside the Creation of Simone Biles' GOAT Leotard, 24 Jul 2021](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/)
- GK (2024, as reported by the Washington Post and summarised by NAM): several layers of fabric are cut to shape from a pattern laid out to minimise waste, and sublimation comes after cutting. — [NAM, Small Manufacturer Shines at Olympics, 30 Jul 2024](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)
- The opposite order is also used in cut-and-sew sublimated sportswear: print transfer paper, heat-transfer onto fabric rolls, laser-cut the printed rolls into panels, then sew while aligning the artwork. — [Gabe Clothing sublimation program](https://gabeclothing.ca/pages/sublimation-program)
- Calender makers support both orders: "single-piece" machines take blank cut parts laid on the printed paper at an infeed table, while roll-to-roll machines transfer onto continuous fabric with low-tension unwinding for stretch-sensitive materials. — [Klieverik single-piece sublimation](https://www.klieverik.com/dye-sublimation-fixation-calender/singlepiecesublimation/); [Klieverik roll-to-roll](https://www.klieverik.com/dye-sublimation-fixation-calender/sublimationroll2roll/)
- Automated single-ply cutting: Zünd markets digital cutters for fashion with pattern-matching and feature-recognition software, automatic feeding/sorting, and camera registration modules (ICC camera, Over Cutter Camera, UNDERCAM) that capture printed material before cutting; jersey is among the listed materials. — [Zünd fashion](https://www.zund.com/en/applications/fashion); [Zünd registration methods](https://www.zund.com/en/cutting-systems/registration-methods)
- Multi-ply CNC cutting: Lectra's Vector fabric-cutting line dates from 1993 and Gerber Technology (owned by Lectra since 2021) supplies computer-controlled cutting systems. — [Wikipedia, Lectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lectra); [Wikipedia, Gerber Scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerber_Scientific)
- Appliqué shapes at GK are laser-cut. — [NBC Sports, 24 Jul 2021](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/)
- Atelier/home cutting: sharp (spring-loaded) shears are the main tool, a rotary cutter with ruler is used for strips and freehand appliqué; no pins — hold by hand or use weights; cut symmetric pieces on the fold and mirrored pairs through two layers, but cut strips single-layer; a paper pattern can be stuck to the fabric's wrong side with repositionable spray adhesive, which is also used to cut two mirror-image appliqués at once. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Cut Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-cut-spandex/)
- Off-grain cutting is tolerable for appliqué but twists structural pieces. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Cut Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-cut-spandex/)
- Fabric yield from pattern-company charts at 150 cm (59–60 in) width: Jalie 2792 one-colour sleeveless leotard 0.50–0.70 m for girls' sizes and 0.90–1.10 m for women's; with long sleeves 0.60–1.15 m (girls) and 1.20–1.35 m (women); crotch lining 20 cm; contrast bands 0.10 m. Jalie 3901: main fabric 0.45–0.75 m (girls) and 0.75–0.85 m (women), contrast yoke 0.25–0.40 m, yoke plus three-quarter sleeves 0.35–0.75 m, lining 15 cm. — [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf); [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698)
- Marker-making software sold for this sector advertises tighter nesting, buffers, blocking, stripe/plaid matching and yield/consumption reports. — [Tukatech TUKAcad](https://tukatech.com/tukacad/)
- Fabric purchase minimums that constrain small custom colour runs: 15 yards per colour for a US-stocked foil tricot and 220 yards for an overseas-stock mesh. — [Pine Crest Fabrics, Kaleidoscope](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/blog/back-in-stock-kaleidoscope-foiled-stretch-tricot/); [Pine Crest Fabrics, Mesh 65](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/blog/mesh-65-soft-weightless-and-stylish-inserts-for-your-activewear-collection/)

### Inferences
- A team order of mixed sizes in several fabrics produces many short, multi-size markers; exported cut data therefore needs piece labels carrying style, size, fabric and athlete/alteration identifiers, mirroring GK's bundling by athlete size and alteration.

- Colour-blocked designs increase piece count and reduce yield compared with a one-colour block; the Jalie contrast-yoke figures (an extra 0.25–0.40 m for a small yoke) show how quickly secondary fabrics add consumption.

- No marker-efficiency percentage for leotards was found (a general reference page returned 404).
- Whether laser cutting at GK is single-ply or low-ply, and how laser heat-sealing interacts with elastane edges, was not documented.

## 4. Sublimation workflow: artwork per piece, transfer, temperatures and times, alignment, shrinkage, colour

### Takeaway
Sublimation is done flat, before assembly, onto white polyester-elastane: artwork is engineered per pattern piece and per size, printed mirrored on transfer paper, and transferred at about 190–230 °C for 30–60 s (GK quotes 450 °F), either onto cut parts or onto the roll before laser cutting. Published settings differ by source and machine, no fabric-shrinkage percentage was found, and colour is controlled in practice by press tests.

### Cited Findings
- Process order at GK for the 2024 Olympic leotards: cut fabric layers → print designs on paper and heat-transfer them at 450 °F (≈232 °C), the ink turning to gas and fusing into the fabric → fasten pieces with temporary glue and sew, embroider logos → apply crystals → final seams. — [NAM summary of Washington Post reporting, 30 Jul 2024](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)
- GK in 2021 described custom artwork printed and transferred by heat press using sublimation, after spreading and cutting. — [NBC Sports, 24 Jul 2021](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/)
- A sportswear maker's step list for full (all-over) sublimation: press test to match colours; set up artwork for all panels so it covers every edge; scale the design for each size; add individual names/numbers; print panels on transfer paper; sublimate with a heat press; sew. — [VC Ultimate, Sublimation](https://ca.vcultimate.com/pages/sublimation)
- Small colour discrepancies, dust spots and ghosting (colour shadowing) are described as inherent to the process. — [VC Ultimate, Sublimation](https://ca.vcultimate.com/pages/sublimation)

- Registration and colour vary between printers and calenders; an in-line print-and-calender design claims registration within 0.1 mm. — [US10189245](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10189245)
- Aligning artwork across seams is done by the sewing operators on cut-and-sew sublimated garments. — [Gabe Clothing sublimation program](https://gabeclothing.ca/pages/sublimation-program)
- Plastic trims must be kept out of the press, and all-over prints have to stop short of them. — [LAT Apparel tip sheet](https://www.latapparel.com/img/pdfs/LAT-Apparel-SublimationPrintingTips-TMv2.pdf)
- GK's 2026 sublimation programme has three tiers: Engineered (four leotard silhouettes, 35 prints recoloured to team colours, logo and embellishment added), SoftLux (10 prints on 15 base colours, for training wear) and Custom (any print or pattern across competition leotards, tanks and warm-ups). — [GK, About Sublimation](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/about-sublimation)

### Inferences
- "Engineered" sublimation means the artwork is positioned on each pattern piece rather than cut from an all-over print, so the app's print export should be one image per piece per size, mirrored, with bleed beyond the cut line and with seam-matching guaranteed geometrically (the two sides of a seam sampled from continuous artwork along the stitch line, not the cut line).
- Because VC Ultimate scales designs for each size and grading changes piece shapes non-uniformly, the tool must decide per design element whether it scales with the piece, keeps absolute size (logos, lettering), or is re-flowed; this should be an explicit property.
- With no published shrinkage figure, the export should carry a per-fabric, per-axis compensation factor that a manufacturer sets from its own press test; the paper-web figures (about 0.25–0.35% in width) suggest the correction is small for paper but fabric relaxation under heat may be larger.
- Cut-then-press (GK, single-piece calenders) avoids relying on roll-to-roll registration and suits small mixed-size team orders; print-then-cut suits volume. Both need the cut contour and the print to share one coordinate system.
- White grin-through on stretched printed fabric argues for a warning when dark sublimated artwork is placed on high-strain zones (seat, underarm, across the back) and for print bleed that covers seam allowances so white never shows at needle holes.

### Gaps
- No leotard manufacturer's actual sublimation time or pressure was published; GK's 450 °F is a single journalist-reported figure with no dwell time.
- No shrinkage percentage for polyester-elastane fabric under sublimation was found, and no supplier guidance specific to elastane blends beyond one blog.
- No primary source on colour management (ICC profiles, RIP software, spot-colour libraries) for sublimated team colours was reached before the search budget ran out; the only evidence is "run a press test".
- Bleed widths, file formats and resolutions that leotard factories require from designers were not found.

## 5. Sewing: machines and stitches, threads, needles, seam allowances, assembly sequence, elastic, edge finishes, lining, mesh edges, straps, closures, labels

### Takeaway
Industrial recommendations for this fabric class are overlock seams (ISO 504, or four-thread 514), coverstitch (406) or four-needle flat seams (607) for topstitching, and overedge stitch 503 for attaching elastic, sewn with fine polyester threads (Tex 18–35) at about 12–15 stitches per inch; home and atelier makers reproduce the same constructions with a four-thread serger plus zigzag. Seam allowance is 6 mm (¼ in) almost universally in patterns, elastic is ⅜ in (1 cm) and is cut shorter than the opening by rules that vary from 0% to 25%, and competition leotards are front- or fully lined.

### Cited Findings

- Home/atelier equivalents: four-thread overlock for structural seams and elastic, three-thread only for hems; or a stretched straight stitch / narrow zigzag (about 1.0–1.5 mm wide, 2.5–3 mm long) on a domestic machine; serger differential feed neutral, medium stitch length for seams and long (setting 4) for elastic. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Sew Spandex, 30 Dec 2020](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-sew-spandex/); [Spandex Simplified, How to Apply Elastic](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-apply-elastic/); [Treasurie](https://blog.treasurie.com/how-to-make-a-leotard/); [Peek-a-Boo Pattern Shop](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/leotard-patterns)
- Jalie's no-serger seam is two passes: a wide zigzag on the raw edge without stretching, then a straight stitch 6 mm in while stretching slightly. — [Jalie 2914 instructions](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/2914.pdf)
- Needles and thread in home practice: stretch or ballpoint needles 75/11 or 80/12; polyester thread; regular cone serger thread is acceptable and woolly (textured) nylon is optional, used mainly for hems or softer seams. — [Peek-a-Boo Pattern Shop](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/leotard-patterns); [Spandex Simplified, How to Sew Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-sew-spandex/); [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)
- Thread-colour rule from an atelier: sew structural seams in one colour start to finish; match the mesh colour wherever mesh is in the seam or under the elastic; on multicolour suits use the mid or lightest colour. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Sew Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-sew-spandex/)

**Seam allowances**
- 6 mm (¼ in) seam and hem allowances are built into Jalie leotard patterns; Treasurie uses 6 mm seams and a 1 cm sleeve hem; Porter adds ¼ in to colour-block seams and turns cuffs about ½ in. — [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698); [Treasurie](https://blog.treasurie.com/how-to-make-a-leotard/); [Spandex Simplified, Colour Blocking](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)

**Assembly sequences**
- Factory (GK, competition leotard): cut → sublimate → place laser-cut appliqués by hand with glue, check placement, stitch → embroider logos → apply crystal motifs by heat transfer on the flat pieces, hand-set extras → sew front to back at sides, shoulders and crotch → finishing on the sewing floor → quality inspection. — [NBC Sports, 24 Jul 2021](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/); [NAM, 30 Jul 2024](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)
- Atelier (Porter, lined bodysuit): alter pattern, add neck/back changes and colour blocking → cut outer with greatest stretch across → spray-bond each outer piece to lining and cut lining to match, then treat the pair as one layer → centre-back seam → side, crotch and shoulder seams → elastic at legs, armholes and neck, flipped and zigzagged; with sleeves: shoulders only → set sleeve flat → one continuous seam from wrist through underarm to leg opening → hem cuffs. — [Spandex Simplified, Basic Bodysuit, 2 May 2024](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-bodysuit/)
- Home (Treasurie): shoulders → sleeves in flat → sleeve hems → side seams and crotch → neck and leg elastic. — [Treasurie](https://blog.treasurie.com/how-to-make-a-leotard/)
- Home (Baby Lock, Jalie-based, pieced front with lining): piece and topstitch the front design → centre back and shoulders → sleeves → sides → join brief to bodice → hem sleeves → elastic; lining is cut from the unaltered pattern, joined at centre back and shoulders, basted wrong sides together to the shell, then sewn as one. — [Baby Lock, Gymnast Leotard project](https://babylock.com/gymnast-leotard)
- Flat sleeve insertion (shoulders first, sleeve in, then underarm-and-side in one pass) and set-in insertion are both used; cap midpoint is matched to the shoulder seam. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Sew Sleeves, 26 Apr 2024](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-sew-sleeves/)

**Elastic at leg, neck and arm openings**
- Standard material is ⅜ in (1 cm) elastic: Jalie patterns specify 1 cm; Treasurie recommends ⅜ in chlorine-resistant swim elastic; Porter uses ⅜ in braided white (white so it does not shadow through light fabric) and 1 in for waistbands and shelf bras; Baby Lock's project used ¼ in. — [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698); [Treasurie](https://blog.treasurie.com/how-to-make-a-leotard/); [Spandex Simplified, How to Apply Elastic, 15 Apr 2022](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-apply-elastic/); [Baby Lock](https://babylock.com/gymnast-leotard)
- Disagreement on clear elastic: Peek-a-Boo recommends clear elastic for leg openings and fold-over elastic for necklines and armholes; Treasurie says avoid clear elastic; Porter rejects it for legs because it does not hold a clean edge and reserves it for gathering. — [Peek-a-Boo Pattern Shop](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/leotard-patterns); [Treasurie](https://blog.treasurie.com/how-to-make-a-leotard/); [Spandex Simplified, How to Apply Elastic](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-apply-elastic/)

**Turned-and-topstitched versus bound edges**
- Jalie 3901 binds the neckline and (sleeveless view) the armholes and puts elastic only at the legs; Jalie 2792 offers an elasticated scoop neck or banded necklines made from a separate stretch-trim strip (1.5–2.1 m per garment). — [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698); [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf)
- Home makers finish bindings with a coverstitch machine, or a twin needle or three-step zigzag if they have none; one builds binding by wrapping lycra round clear elastic and twin-needling it. — [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)

**Lining and modesty layers**
- Sylvia P lines all competitive leotards at no extra charge, with full front lining as standard. — [Sylvia P, The Teamwear Difference](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/the-sylvia-p-teamwear-difference)
- Quatro stocks three nude tones for leotard lining and mesh. — [Quatro fabrics](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/tw-fabrics)
- Jalie's gymnastics patterns include a crotch lining (15–20 cm of lining fabric). — [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf)
- Atelier full lining is made by bonding shell to lining with temporary spray adhesive and sewing them as one layer; boat necklines get a shelf-bra lining, with 1 in elastic under the bust for fuller chests. — [Spandex Simplified, Basic Bodysuit](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-bodysuit/); [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/); [Spandex Simplified, How to Apply Elastic](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-apply-elastic/)
- Home makers line some fabrics even when they are not see-through because they can still be revealing. — [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)

**Mesh edges, straps, closures**
- Mesh edges are finished with the same turned elastic, using elastic and thread matched to skin tone or mesh colour; clear elastic is a possible alternative on unlined mesh necklines. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Apply Elastic](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-apply-elastic/); [Spandex Simplified, How to Make Elastic Straps](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-make-elastic-straps/)
- Straps are fabric-covered elastic. Turned strap: cut width = 2 × (elastic width + seam allowance), e.g. 1¼ in for ⅜ in elastic with ¼ in allowances; rolled strap: about 4 × elastic width (about 2 in). Narrower elastic stretches out more; elastic sewn into the seam (turned type) is the more stable. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Make Elastic Straps](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-make-elastic-straps/)
- Closures in use: back-neck clasp on keyhole and banded necklines (one clasp per garment in Jalie 2792), butterfly clasps on hole backs and halters, invisible zips and snaps on other bodysuits. — [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf); [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)

### Inferences
- The stitch densities convert to roughly 12–13.5 stitches per inch for overlock seams and elastic, 12–15 for coverseams and 10–12 for lockstitch (14–16, 14–18 and 12–14 stitches per 3 cm respectively); these are suitable defaults for the construction page of an exported tech pack.
- Elastic should be modelled per opening segment as a ratio of elastic length to seam length (defaults in the 0.75–1.0 range, tighter at the back leg than the front) and exported as cut lengths per size in the bill of materials, since pattern companies publish it exactly that way.
- Porter's chord-length method is a geometric way of getting an elastic that is shorter than a curved edge in proportion to its curvature; a tool can reproduce it by measuring chord versus arc on the pattern.
- Allowance bookkeeping the tool needs: 6 mm on body seams; an edge allowance equal to the elastic width (about 1 cm) on turned-elastic openings; zero on bound edges (binding wraps the cut edge) with a separate binding strip in the BOM; 1 cm or about ½ in on sleeve hems.
- Because the factory decorates flat pieces before closing side, shoulder and crotch seams, those seams are the last operations; decoration that must cross them cannot be machine-applied in the same pass (see sections 7 and 10).

### Gaps
- No leotard factory's own seam allowance, stitch specification, elastic tension setting or machine list was published; the industrial numbers are Coats' generic swimwear recommendations.
- No source documented metered elastic-attaching equipment, industrial binding folders, or flatlock use specifically on gymnastics leotards.
- Nothing was found on label types, placement or content rules for leotards.
- Sylvia P's "SP Hydro-Protect" (standard on long-sleeve competition leotards) is named but not explained on the pages read.

## 6. Appliqué and overlay techniques: zigzag appliqué, reverse appliqué, laser-cut overlays, bonded layers, heat-transfer vinyl and foil

### Takeaway
Appliqué on leotards is a stretch-on-stretch overlay held temporarily with adhesive and then stitched (zigzag in ateliers; stitched after glue-positioning of laser-cut shapes at GK), done on flat pieces before assembly; reverse appliqué, padded appliqué and mesh-backed cutaways are variants. Heat-transfer vinyl and foil are low-temperature add-ons with limited stretch tolerance, and no evidence was found of permanently bonded (film-laminated) construction in WAG leotards.

### Cited Findings
- Factory: GK laser-cuts appliqués, places them by hand with glue to check position, then stitches them; one Tokyo 2021 leotard carried 76 individual appliqué stars. — [NBC Sports, 24 Jul 2021](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/)
- GK also describes holding garment pieces together with temporary glue before sewing. — [NAM, 30 Jul 2024](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)
- Atelier zigzag appliqué: cut the shape using a spray-stuck paper pattern; tack it to the garment piece with repositionable spray adhesive (Odif 505 is the preferred product; a washable glue stick is used on mesh); stitch very close to the edge with a medium-wide zigzag at medium length in thread matching the appliqué, stretching both layers together; build multi-colour designs one layer at a time, trimming away the layer underneath to reduce bulk. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Applique Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-applique-spandex/)
- Reverse appliqué: cut the shape out of the main fabric, back the hole with contrast fabric about 1 in larger all round, zigzag round the opening in thread matching the main fabric; chosen when the contrast would show through or the base is heavily textured. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Applique Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-applique-spandex/)
- Home alternatives for holding appliqué: spray glue (which gums the needle) or wash-away stabiliser, then zigzag. — [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)
- A simple overlay band is laid on the front piece and zigzagged along both long edges before the piece is assembled. — [Baby Lock, Gymnast Leotard project](https://babylock.com/gymnast-leotard)
- Padded appliqué: thin two-way-stretch foam (typically ⅛ in neoprene) is bonded under the appliqué fabric, the edge wrapped and zigzagged; it is applied before the garment is fully sewn and after all colour blocking and flat appliqué. Appliqué fabric and padding must have their greatest stretch in the same direction or the patch becomes non-stretch; larger appliqués need more stretch, small ones tolerate low-stretch materials. — [Spandex Simplified, Padded Applique](https://spandexsimplified.com/padded-applique/)
- Mesh-backed cutaways use a 1 in overlap, glue and appliqué stitching (see section 1). — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Heat-transfer vinyl: Siser's stretch-grade film (EasyWeed Stretch, 85 microns, shown as discontinued on the maker's North American site in Oct 2026) was applied at 320 °F / 160 °C with firm pressure for 20 s and is rated for Lycra/spandex. — [Siser North America, EasyWeed Stretch](https://www.siserna.com/easyweed-stretch/)
- A 2026 decorator's guide gives lower settings for stretch films on spandex (270–305 °F for 10–15 s, light to medium pressure, cold peel), limits layering to two layers in high-stretch areas, says standard, glitter and flocked films are not stretch-compatible, and recommends keeping transfers small and in low-stretch areas. — [Printable Press, How to Heat Press on Spandex, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.printablepress.com/how-to-heat-press-on-spandex/)
- Hand foil embossing in ateliers uses a flexible adhesive (Fabri-Fuse or clear water-based caulk) piped onto the stretched, finished garment, cured until tacky, then thin transfer foil rubbed on; results depend heavily on the adhesive/foil pairing and care is hand-wash only. — [Spandex Simplified, Stretch Foil Embossing, 14 Mar 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/stretch-foil-embossing-on-spandex/)
- Embroidery of team logos is applied at GK after piece assembly and before crystals; an embroidery set-up fee is charged once on the first order. — [NAM, 30 Jul 2024](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672); [GK FAQs](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-help-center-faqs)

### Inferences
- Every appliqué adds a second fabric layer and a perimeter of zigzag or coverstitch, both of which locally reduce stretch; the tool should treat appliqué area as "reduced-stretch" in any fit simulation and require the appliqué's stretch direction to match the base.
- Laser cutting removes the detail limit on appliqué outlines, but each outline still has to be stitched, so stitchability (minimum feature width of roughly two zigzag widths, no slivers) is the practical limit; no published minimum was found, so this should be a manufacturer-set parameter.
- Appliqué export needs: the appliqué cut contour (no seam allowance), a placement outline on the base piece, layer order, and stitch type along the edge.

### Gaps
- No source described laser-cut foil overlays specifically, their material, or how their edges are finished.
- No evidence was found for adhesive-film bonded seams or permanently laminated layers in WAG leotards.
- No quantitative data on how much an appliqué or vinyl layer reduces stretch, and no stated minimum appliqué feature size from any maker.

## 7. Crystals and sequins: when applied, templates and heat pressing, quality checks

### Takeaway
Factories apply hotfix crystals to flat, decorated pieces before the final seams: a machine sets stones onto transfer film as motifs (up to 400 × 400 mm, stones 2–5 mm, about 170 stones per minute per head), the motif is heat-pressed at about 160–170 °C for roughly 18–25 s on polyester/lycra, and stragglers are hand-set with tweezers; ateliers more often glue stones onto the finished garment stretched on a board. Counts on elite leotards run from several thousand to about 10,000.

### Cited Findings

**Sequence and equipment in the factory**
- GK (2024): after assembly of the decorated pieces and embroidery, a rhinestone-transfer machine lays crystals onto heat-transfer paper and heat-activated glue fixes them to the fabric; some are placed by hand with tweezers; only then are front and back sewn together at sides, top and bottom. — [NAM summary of Washington Post, 30 Jul 2024](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)
- GK (2021): crystal motifs are built by jewel machines on a flat sheet and sealed on with a heat press; some stones are applied by hand and fill-in stones by a laser machine. — [NBC Sports, 24 Jul 2021](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/)
- Counts: 7,600 crystals on one Tokyo 2021 leotard; up to about 10,000 on a Paris 2024 leotard and more than 47,000 across the eight women's designs; some Paris stones were attached by hand in Reading; pearls appeared for the first time. — [NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/); [Fortune, 11 Jul 2024](https://www.fortune.com/2024/07/11/olympic-gynmast-simone-biles-leotards-swarovski-crystals); [USA Gymnastics release, 10 Jul 2024](https://usagym.org/a-fusion-of-fashion-performance-and-patriotism-gk-elite-and-usa-gymnastics-unveil-leotards-and-apparel-for-summer-olympics/)
- Automatic rhinestone setters (CAMS, sold by ColDesi): single-head 1V-6P places up to 170 stones per minute from six hoppers (six colours or sizes per design), stones 2–5 mm (rhinestones, rhinestuds, nailheads), maximum design area 400 × 400 mm, driven by HotFix Era software, listed at $38,999; the four-head 4H-6P reaches 680 stones per minute at $115,900. Output is stone transfers pressed onto garments afterwards. — [ColDesi, CAMS rhinestone machines](https://coldesi.com/automatic-rhinestone-setting-machines/)
- Spangle transfer machines are sold alongside the stone setters (ProSpangle, Spangle Elite), and GK's embellishment stock includes Spanglez and Sequinz as well as crystals; GK describes Spanglez as very lightweight and flexible. — [ColDesi](https://coldesi.com/); [College Gym News, 12 Dec 2019](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=28471); [GK blog, Leotard Fabrics & Embellishments](https://blog.gkelite.com/leotard-fabrics-embellishments/)

**Stone ranges offered by leotard brands**
- GK (Jan 2026): Swarovski in three groups — shaped (7 shapes), premium (18 colours), vibrant (6 colours) — plus lower-priced standard crystals and Spanglez. — [GK blog, Leotard Fabrics & Embellishments](https://blog.gkelite.com/leotard-fabrics-embellishments/)
- Sylvia P: Swarovski and standard crystals in SS6, SS10, SS16, SS20 and SS34, clear and AB only, plus navette, square, kite and pear shapes; sequins 2–5 mm in iridescent, holographic, glitter and mirror finishes. — [Sylvia P Teamwear embellishments](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/embellishment-options-crystals)
- Quatro: three grades (Swarovski, DMC, standard) in SS6, SS10, SS16 and SS20, round, boat and waterdrop shapes, 21 colours. — [Quatro stones](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/tw-stones)
- Size conversion: ss5 ≈ 1.4–1.5 mm, ss12 ≈ 3.0–3.2, ss16 ≈ 3.8–4.0, ss20 ≈ 4.6–4.8 (the most used), ss30 ≈ 6.3–6.5, ss48 ≈ 10.9–11.3 mm; stones are sold by the gross (144). — [Spandex Simplified, Everything You Need to Know about Rhinestones, 2 Mar 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-spandex)
- Swarovski discontinued its DIY/general crystal line, making its stones harder for small makers to source; Preciosa is the usual substitute. — [Spandex Simplified, rhinestone guide](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-spandex)

**Hotfix parameters (Preciosa application manual, 2019 edition)**
- Usable glue range 120–200 °C; base material must withstand at least 120 °C; stones are fixed after about 5 minutes' cooling and fully cured after 24 hours; cured work can be washed at 60 °C. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Single ss10 stones on "artificial fabrics (PES, lycra)" of 150–250 g/m², heat from the stone side: 25 s at 160 °C, 18 s at 170 °C, 15 s at 180 °C, 9 s at 200 °C (52 s at 120 °C); heat from the fabric side: 9 s at 160 °C, 6 s at 170 °C, 4 s at 200 °C. Stone-side time rises with stone size (cotton at 160 °C: 15 s for ss4, 22 s for ss10, 60 s for ss48). — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Hotfix transfers (motifs on film) on PES/lycra: 25 s at 160 °C or 18 s at 170 °C, medium pressure; start at 160 °C for 20 s; cover the PVC carrier film with cotton cloth or Teflon/silicone sheet; after peeling, re-press about 15 s; if stones are loose raise by 3–4 °C and 3–4 s. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Motifs mixing sizes, or stones larger than ss16, should be pressed from the reverse (stones face down) with increased pressure. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Stretch fabric should be secured slightly stretched on a pad before application; a silicone-foam compensation pad evens out height differences; pressure must act directly on the stones and needs special care near seams, zips and other protrusions. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Unsuitable bases include Teflon, water-repellent, softener and easy-care finishes, dyes with metal pigments and smooth leather-like surfaces; the glue must partly soak into the base (water-drop absorption test); minimum surface tension about 38 mN/m. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Retail-level settings broadly agree: polyester/lycra 140–150 °C for 25–32 s (Rhinestones Unlimited); 335 °F (168 °C) for 15 s plus a 5 s second press, lower (280–300 °F) for delicate fabrics, with a pressing pillow over seams (Be Createful). — [Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/how-to-apply-hotfix-rhinestones-3-methods-compared/); [Be Createful](https://becreateful.com/blogs/tips-and-tricks-from-a-pro/the-guide-to-pressing-hotfix-rhinestones)

**Atelier practice (glue, after assembly)**
- Porter prefers glued (non-hotfix) stones on the finished garment because glue grips the stone's edge as well as its back; her adhesive is E-6000 Fabri-Fuse (about 5 minutes' working time per batch). — [Spandex Simplified, Crystalling on Spandex, 28 Mar 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-on-spandex/)
- The garment is stretched to within an inch or two of the wearer's size on a board (cardboard cut to half the torso length by half the larger of chest or hip, or a commercial stoning board) so placement is seen at worn size, the glue cures at worn size, and front and back are kept apart; cure a couple of days before removing. — [Spandex Simplified, Crystalling on Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-on-spandex/)
- Glue must be placed as separate dots, never lines or touching clusters, because touching glue reduces stretch, durability and fit. — [Spandex Simplified, Crystalling on Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-on-spandex/)
- Placement advice: smaller stones on high-stress areas such as elastic straps; avoid high-friction areas such as the underarm or use small low-profile stones there; larger stones are likelier to pop off and are sewn as well as glued; order about 50% more stones than counted. — [Spandex Simplified, rhinestone guide](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-spandex)
- A home project glued stones with gem adhesive and allowed 24 hours' drying. — [Baby Lock, Gymnast Leotard project](https://babylock.com/gymnast-leotard)

**Quality checks and durability**
- Correct hotfix bond shows a barely visible ring of glue round the stone and, on thin fabric, slight strike-through on the reverse; excess glue around the stone means too much heat or pressure and a weak bond; test adhesion by trying to pull stones with moderate force after cooling, replace by hand and re-press; do not test or wash before 24 hours. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- The manual lists stones failing to hold on seams or layered material as a standard fault, traced to insufficient temperature, time or pressure, uneven platens or an unsuitable pad. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Brands warn that stones loosen with wear as the garment stretches; Quatro offers a free six-month stone repair service. — [Sylvia P Teamwear embellishments](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/embellishment-options-crystals); [Quatro stones](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/tw-stones); [Quatro club service](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/club-service)
- GK inspects garments after the sewing floor. — [NBC Sports, 24 Jul 2021](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/)

### Inferences
- Stone templates exported by the app should be per pattern piece (front, back, each sleeve), tiled into motifs no larger than the setter's field (400 × 400 mm on the CAMS machines documented), with each stone as a centre point plus size and colour code, and at most six size/colour combinations per machine pass.
- Stones above 5 mm (ss30/ss34 and fancy shapes) fall outside that machine's range and need hand setting or separate pressing from the reverse side, so the tool should cost them differently.
- At the catalogue rate of 170 stones per minute, 10,000 stones is about an hour of single-head machine time before film handling, pressing and hand-setting; this is a theoretical lower bound, not a measured factory time.
- Required keep-out zones follow from the cited faults: seam lines and seam allowances, elastic-turned edges, multi-layer overlaps, underarm friction zones; plus a minimum centre spacing greater than the stone diameter so glue footprints do not merge.
- Hotfix on foil ("Mystique") or metallic-pigment fabrics is at the edge of the glue maker's guidance (metal-pigment finishes are listed as unsuitable, and Sylvia P reports dulling), so crystal-on-foil should trigger a test-required flag.
- Machine-applied stones cannot span the side, shoulder or crotch seams because those are sewn after stoning; any motif crossing them needs a deliberate break or hand-set stones after assembly.

### Gaps
- GK's and other factories' actual press temperature/time, transfer film type, and stone-spacing rules are unpublished.
- No source gave a minimum stone-to-stone or stone-to-seam distance in millimetres.
- How sequins/spangles ("Spanglez", "Sequinz") are constructed and applied at GK is not described beyond the product names.
- The meaning of "fill-in jewels by laser machine" in the NBC report is unclear (laser-guided placement versus another process).
- Template file formats accepted by rhinestone setters were not listed by the vendor.

## 9. CAD/CAM used for this garment type, exchange file formats, and tech-pack contents

### Takeaway
The practical interchange format in 2026 is still DXF in the AAMA flavour (often the old R10/R12-style export), optionally with grading carried in a separate rule file; DXF-ASTM (ASTM D6673) is supported by CLO and others but the standard was withdrawn in 2019 without replacement, and real-world imports break on layer mapping, notches, seam-allowance/cut-line confusion and units. A design tool should export DXF-AAMA and DXF-ASTM with selectable layer conventions, PDF and HPGL/PLT for plotting, per-piece print files, stone data and a tech pack.

### Cited Findings

**Systems**
- Lectra (Paris) makes Modaris pattern software (first released 1984) and Vector cutters, and acquired Gerber Technology in 2021 (deal announced February 2021, reported final by 1 July 2021), so AccuMark and Modaris now sit under one owner. — [Wikipedia, Lectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lectra); [Wikipedia, Gerber Scientific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerber_Scientific)
- Tukatech's TUKAcad covers pattern drafting, grading (including angle grading for lingerie and swim), marker making and made-to-measure, with data import/export and digitising in its paid tiers (from $199 per month) and companion products for automatic nesting (SMARTmark), 3D (TUKA3D) and cutting (TUKAcut). — [Tukatech TUKAcad](https://tukatech.com/tukacad/)
- Seamly2D is open-source (GPLv3+) parametric pattern software for Windows, macOS and Linux driven by measurement files; Valentina is a second open-source pattern program whose repository carries a DXF-export module at the same path, cited below. — [Seamly2D on GitHub](https://github.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D); [Valentina source, vdxfengine.cpp](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/raw/develop/src/libs/vdxf/vdxfengine.cpp)
- CLO states that DXF is how it exchanges patterns with Yuka, Gerber, Lectra, StyleCAD, Optitex and PAD System. — [CLO Help Center, 2D Pattern (DXF) Import/Export, updated Oct 2026](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export)
- Brand-side configurators already exist in this market: GK's UDesign (an online 3D studio; GK's site notes its product customizer needs a WebGL-capable browser), Sylvia P's Studio SP (3D), Quatro's Teamwear Design Studio, and Velocity Pro Sport's Kitbuilder, which prices a leotard automatically from design complexity. — [GK UDesign](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/udesign); [GK About Sublimation (site notice)](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/about-sublimation); [Design with Sylvia P Teamwear](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/design-with-sylvia-p-teamwear); [Quatro club service](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/club-service); [Velocity club FAQ](https://gymnasticsleotards.co.uk/pages/clubs)

**Pattern exchange formats**
- ASTM D6673 ("Sewn Products Pattern Data Interchange — Data Format") defines 2D pattern-piece exchange as a DXF file following the AutoCAD Release 13 DXF specification plus a separate ASCII grade-rule-table file; it explicitly excludes cutter and plotter instructions, marker information and 3D relationships; the 2010 edition is marked withdrawn in 2019 with no replacement. — [ASTM D6673-10 store page](https://store.astm.org/d6673-10.html)
- CLO imports Standard DXF, DXF-AAMA and DXF-ASTM (the user now picks the flavour; it used to be auto-detected) with options for unit scale, rotation, pattern annotations, tracing baselines as internal lines, swapping cutting and sewing lines, where notches land (seam allowance, outline or both), optimising curve points and removing non-grading points on AAMA files; since version 2026.1 DXF seam allowances are read automatically as locked custom seam allowances. — [CLO Help Center](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export)
- CLO exports DXF-AAMA (identical to its former "Gerber" option), DXF-ASTM or Standard DXF, one grading size group per file, with grading either embedded or written to a separate rule file (identical to its former "Lectra rule file" option), and options to export the base size only, omit baselines, duplicate notches onto the seam allowance, swap cutting and sewing lines, and add bounding boxes for use in Illustrator or Photoshop. — [CLO Help Center](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export)
- Layer conventions as implemented by an open-source exporter (Valentina): layer 1 piece boundary (cut line) and piece text; 2 turn points; 3 curve points; 4 notches; 6 mirror line; 7 grain line; 8 internal lines; 11 internal cut-outs; 13 drill holes; 14 sew line; annotation text on layer 15 (ASTM) or 19 (AAMA); ASTM-only layers 80–83 for T, castle, check and U notches and 84–87 for quality-validation curves; each piece is a DXF BLOCK placed by an INSERT. — [Valentina source, vdxfengine.cpp](https://gitlab.com/smart-pattern/valentina/-/raw/develop/src/libs/vdxf/vdxfengine.cpp)
- Seamly2D's AAMA exporter writes the outline on layer 1, seam line and internal paths on 8, internal cut-outs on 11, notches on 4 and grain line on 7, using POLYLINE (or LWPOLYLINE in newer DXF versions), LINE, TEXT, BLOCK and INSERT entities. — [Seamly2D source, vdxfengine.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/FashionFreedom/Seamly2D/develop/src/libs/vdxf/vdxfengine.cpp)
- A different numbering is documented for Lectra-style DXF by Browzwear: 1 contour (must contain the external cut line), 2 end points, 3 notches, 5 grading lines/mirror axis, 7 lines to plot, 8 internal lines to plot, 9–10 grain and cross lines, 11 internal lines to cut, 13 drill holes, 14 seam lines, 19 text; VStitcher imports pattern pieces as DXF (AAMA). — [Browzwear Help, DXF layers](https://help.browzwear.com/hc/en-us/articles/4921417374617)
- Field reports from 2025–2026: an R10 "AAMA" DXF was the variant that imported into Lectra Modaris and the only one a VStitcher user could open, with notches and grain lines sometimes lost; a Seamly developer called DXF-ASTM too loosely defined to be very useful; a CLO user found R10 curves arrived as many points (slow) while a 2013 AAMA export arrived with few points and notches intact, and that seam-allowance visibility had to be set per piece. — [Seamly forum, export to Lectra, Mar–Apr 2026](https://forum.seamly.io/t/exporting-dxf-pattern-to-be-imported-into-lectra/17010); [Seamly forum, DXF without seam allowance, Jan–Feb 2025](https://forum.seamly.io/t/now-we-cant-export-dxf-file-without-seam-allowance/11760)
- Typical silent failures listed by a pattern vendor: vanished notches, grain line imported as an ordinary line, seam allowance fused into the cut line, millimetre/inch mismatch, curves faceted into micro-segments; their advice is to match the receiving system's flavour from a sample file and to spot-check one graded size. The same vendor ships every pattern as DXF-AAMA plus PLT plus PDF. — [Minerva Patterns, DXF AAMA vs ASTM](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/dxf-aama-vs-dxf-astm); [Minerva Patterns, DXF layers and entities](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/dxf-layers-and-entities-what-is-inside-a-pattern-file)
- Plotter format: HP-GL (1977) uses two-letter commands (IN, SP, PU, PD, PA) and plotter units of 25 µm (40 per mm, 1,016 per inch); HP-GL/2 adds pen width and colour and a compact binary encoding. — [Wikipedia, HP-GL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP-GL)
- Cutting-room software names at one cutter maker: Zünd Cut Center, Design Center, PreCut Center, PrimeCenter and Mind. — [Zünd fashion](https://www.zund.com/en/applications/fashion)
- Rhinestone-setter data: CAMS machines are driven from HotFix Era software and accept jobs by SD card, USB or LAN. — [ColDesi, CAMS](https://coldesi.com/automatic-rhinestone-setting-machines/)

**Tech-pack contents**
- A complete tech pack comprises: cover/style page (style name and number, season, date, version, colourways); technical flat sketches front, back and side with call-outs for seams, closures, hardware and label placement; bill of materials (every fabric, trim, thread, label and tag with colour references, supplier codes and units); point-of-measure spec for the base size with tolerances plus grading rules; construction notes (seam types, stitches per inch, thread colour, finishing); cost sheet (materials, cut-make-trim labour, target price); and a revision log. It is traditionally built in Illustrator and Excel and sent to the factory as a PDF. — [Techpacker, What is a tech pack](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/what-is-a-tech-pack/)
- Leotard-specific BOM lines evidenced by pattern envelopes: main fabric, contrast fabric(s), lining (15–20 cm), ⅜ in elastic by total length, stretch binding trim by length, one back-neck clasp, plus contrast appliqué fabric. — [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf); [Jalie 3901 size/yardage sheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable export set for the app: (1) DXF-AAMA with an R12-style polyline option and a "true curves" option, selectable units, cut line on layer 1, sew line on 14 (or 8 for Lectra-style receivers), notches on 4, grain on 7, internal lines on 8, cut-outs on 11, drill points on 13, text on 15/19, one BLOCK per piece; (2) DXF-ASTM with the same content; (3) optional separate grade-rule file or one file per size (nested sizes are safer than relying on rule import); (4) tiled and full-size PDF; (5) HPGL/PLT; (6) per-piece print images for sublimation in the same coordinate frame as the cut line; (7) stone lists and motif tiles; (8) a tech-pack PDF with BOM and graded spec.
- Because receivers disagree on layer numbers (Valentina/AAMA-style versus the Lectra-style table) the layer map should be a user-editable preset per target system, and the tool should ship a round-trip test piece.
- Since elastic lengths, stone counts by size/colour, thread colours and lining lengths are all computable from the design, the BOM can be generated automatically per size; this is where a dedicated leotard tool beats generic CAD.
- CLO's handling (locked seam allowances, swap of cut and sew lines) shows that exporting both the stitch line and the cut line explicitly, on separate layers, is the least ambiguous choice.

### Gaps
- Optitex, Gerber AccuMark and Lectra Modaris native formats and their documented import/export lists were not verified (vendor pages were not reachable by guessed URL and search was unavailable); Style3D's DXF help page returned 404 when fetched, though a search snippet indicated it supports DXF import/export with a notch-endpoint option.
- The full text of ASTM D6673 and the older AAMA specification (and the exact record structure of the grade-rule file) was not retrieved; the layer list above is taken from open-source implementations and one vendor help page, not from the standard itself.
- No evidence of which CAD system GK or any other leotard maker actually uses.
- Cutter-native formats (for example ISO-style cut files, Gerber cut data, Zünd job formats) were not documented in the sources reached.
- Only one tech-pack reference was read; no leotard- or swimwear-specific template (e.g. with stone-placement or print-placement pages) was found.

## 10. Manufacturability constraints a design tool should encode

### Takeaway
The sources support a concrete rule set — straight or gently curved pieced seams with no inset corners, 6 mm allowances, minimum strap and keyhole spacings, flat-piece decoration before final seams, crystal keep-outs at seams, layers, elastic and friction zones, fragile-fabric and heat-compatibility flags, mandatory lining — but almost none of the limits are published as hard numbers by manufacturers, so most thresholds must be configurable per factory.

### Cited Findings
- Seam geometry: pieced colour-block lines should be straight or gently curved; corners within a seam and L-shaped insets are to be avoided; intricate shapes go to appliqué. — [Spandex Simplified, Colour Blocking](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)
- V-point seams are sewable but need stay-stitching and clipping, i.e. extra operations. — [Baby Lock, Gymnast Leotard project](https://babylock.com/gymnast-leotard)
- Seam allowance on pieced seams is ¼ in (6 mm) each side, so two adjacent seams cannot be closer than their combined allowances without overlap. — [Spandex Simplified, Colour Blocking](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)
- Openings: allow about ¾ in of fabric for an elastic-finished scoop edge; keep more than 1.5 in between a neckline and a keyhole; neck plus back opening must be large enough to step through; boat necks need internal support. — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Straps: minimum shoulder width = 2 × elastic width + straps × strap width; at least 2 in from side seam to nearest strap and 1.5 in from centre back; narrower elastic stretches out more; crossover backs are described as unusual for competition suits. — [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/); [Spandex Simplified, How to Make Elastic Straps](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-make-elastic-straps/)
- Dress-code limits summarised by GK: leg line not above the hip bone, no bare midriff, no "backless" opening, no racerback or spaghetti straps. — [GK blog, Gymnastics Dress Code](https://blog.gkelite.com/gymnastics-dress-code-what-to-wear-to-gymnastics-competitions/)
- Mesh: not guaranteed against contact damage and sold under waiver at GK; can pull or tear on rough surfaces; needs a 1 in overlap where it backs a cutaway; elastic and thread must be colour-matched because they show through. — [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions); [Sylvia P fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options); [Spandex Simplified, Neck & Back Style Variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Fabric direction: greatest stretch around the body for every structural piece; appliqué and padding must share the base's stretch direction; napped and one-way fabrics are cut one way. — [Spandex Simplified, How to Cut Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-cut-spandex/); [Spandex Simplified, Padded Applique](https://spandexsimplified.com/padded-applique/)
- Crystals: hotfix stones are a known failure on seams and layered material and need special care near seams and protrusions; stones over ss16 need reverse-side pressing; base must tolerate at least 120 °C and absorb glue; metal-pigment and repellent finishes are unsuitable. — [Preciosa Hotfix application manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)
- Crystals and stretch: glue footprints must stay separate or stretch and fit suffer; small stones only on elastic straps; avoid underarm friction zones; big stones detach more easily; brands state that stones loosen as the garment stretches in wear. — [Spandex Simplified, Crystalling on Spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-on-spandex/); [Spandex Simplified, rhinestone guide](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-spandex); [Quatro stones](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/tw-stones)
- Machine limits for stone motifs: 400 × 400 mm field, 2–5 mm stones, six hoppers. — [ColDesi, CAMS](https://coldesi.com/automatic-rhinestone-setting-machines/)
- Heat-applied decoration on foil fabric: crystal areas on Mystique may dull from application heat. — [Sylvia P fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options)
- Sublimation: white polyester-based fabric only; white base shows when stretched; plastic trims stay out of the press; ghosting and slight colour shifts are normal; sublimated fabric pills on rough surfaces. — [GK About Sublimation](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/about-sublimation); [Spandex Simplified, Types of Stretch Fabric](https://spandexsimplified.com/types-of-stretch-fabric/); [LAT Apparel](https://www.latapparel.com/img/pdfs/LAT-Apparel-SublimationPrintingTips-TMv2.pdf); [VC Ultimate](https://ca.vcultimate.com/pages/sublimation); [Sylvia P fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options)
- Colour adjacency: dark Mystique colours and a red velvet are flagged as bleed risks next to light fabrics. — [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions)
- Lining: competitive leotards are front-lined as standard at Sylvia P; pattern companies include a crotch lining; makers line revealing fabrics even when opaque. — [Sylvia P, The Teamwear Difference](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/the-sylvia-p-teamwear-difference); [Jalie 2792 envelope back](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_2792.pdf); [ChalkBucket, Tips for making a leotard](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)
- Fit sensitivity: fit and feel change with fabric and embellishment; length cannot be recovered by stretch; fabrics are stretch-tested on arrival. — [GK women's sizing instructions](https://gkelite.azureedge.net/images/static/gkforms/gk-gymnastics-womens-sizing-measuring-instruction.pdf); [Peek-a-Boo Pattern Shop](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/leotard-patterns); [GK, The GK Fit](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-the-gk-fit)
- Process order constraint: decoration (print, appliqué, embroidery, crystals) is done on flat pieces and the side, shoulder and crotch seams are closed last. — [NAM, 30 Jul 2024](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)

### Inferences
Suggested rule set (thresholds marked "configurable" are not published by any manufacturer and must be set per factory):
- Panels: every pieced edge is a line or a smooth curve; flag interior corners, insets and curve radii below a configurable minimum; flag panels whose narrowest width is below 2 × seam allowance plus a configurable visible minimum (with 6 mm allowances a panel narrower than about 12 mm has no visible face); cap seam count per garment (configurable) because each seam adds two allowance layers, reduces stretch across it and adds an operation.
- Seams to avoid: do not run pieced seams through the crotch point, under the arm, or across the centre of the seat; this is consistent with the sources' emphasis on friction and strain zones but no source states it explicitly, so treat as a default warning rather than a rule.
- Openings and straps: enforce Porter's spacing formulas; require a closure or a step-in-size check for high-neck/keyhole designs; require elastic on every unbound opening.
- Mesh: require a lining or self-backing choice for mesh over the torso; warn on mesh in contact or abrasion zones; forbid crystals on unsupported single-layer mesh unless a test flag is set; require colour-matched elastic.
- Decoration zoning: because final seams close last, any element crossing a side, shoulder or crotch seam must be split per piece (print, appliqué) or deferred to hand application (crystals).
- Crystals: keep-out bands along seams and turned-elastic edges (configurable width), exclusion in underarm zones, maximum stone size on straps, minimum spacing so glue does not merge, maximum six size/colour classes per machine motif, motif tiles ≤ 400 × 400 mm; estimate stretch loss from stone coverage and warn above a configurable coverage per region; flag crystals on foil or metallic finishes as test-required.
- Heat stack-up: order of heat processes should be sublimation (about 190–230 °C) first, then vinyl/foil transfers (about 130–160 °C), then hotfix (about 160–170 °C for 18–25 s on polyester/lycra); flag designs that would need a hotter process after a cooler heat-sensitive layer is in place.
- Sizing compensation: apply per-panel stretch factors (section 1) instead of a blanket size bump; offer long/short torso; warn when a low-stretch fabric (two-way foil print, illusion, panne velvet) is assigned to a structural body panel.
- Lining: default to a full front lining for competition styles and a crotch lining always; require lining under light or sublimated white-base fabrics.

### Gaps
- No manufacturer publishes minimum panel size, maximum seam count, minimum seam radius, crystal-to-seam distance, maximum crystal coverage, or any sizing compensation for heavily stoned or overlaid garments; all such thresholds above are proposed defaults, not sourced facts.
- No strap-strength test data or standard was found.
- Official rulebook wording on attire (FIG Code of Points 2025–2028, USA Gymnastics, NCAA) was not retrieved, so the modesty and cut limits rest on GK's unattributed summary.
- No quantified data on stretch loss from seams, appliqué, foil or stone coverage.

## 11. Typical cost and time breakdown per stage

### Takeaway
No source publishes a stage-by-stage cost or time breakdown for a leotard. What is documented: end-to-end lead times (2–13 weeks depending on maker, product type and season), retail price points (about $100–$355 for US custom-capable competition leotards, £48–£53 base for a UK custom style, roughly $3,000 notional retail for an Olympic leotard, with crystal count named as the main cost driver), and machine rates and material quantities from which stage times can be bounded.

### Inferences
- Elapsed lead time is dominated by queueing, design approval and sizing collection, not touch time: the same makers that quote 5–8 weeks can produce a non-stock item in days (k-Bee's 4-day make time; GK's 24-hour record).
- Embellishment is the largest variable cost and the clearest price lever: the spread between a $99.95 foil-sparkle competition leotard and a $354.95 rhinestone one at the same maker, three stone grades at Quatro, and the Olympic figure all point the same way. A cost estimator in the app should therefore be driven mainly by stone count by size and grade, then by fabric count and class, panel/seam count, appliqué perimeter and print area.
- Machine time for stones is small (10,000 stones is roughly an hour at the catalogue rate); the cost sits in the stones themselves, film handling, pressing in sections and hand-set corrections.
- A defensible stage list for an estimator, in factory order: design and approval; pattern and grading; marker and cutting; printing and transfer; appliqué and embroidery; crystal motifs and hand setting; assembly and elastic; finishing, inspection and packing.

### Gaps
- No per-stage cost or standard-minute data was found for any leotard maker, factory or atelier, and no hours-per-leotard figure for hand-made custom work.
- GK's competition-leotard price list, custom design fee and embellishment price tiers were not accessible (collection pages did not render prices).
- Crystal unit prices by brand and size, and per-stone application charges, were not found.
- No cost data for Europe or Australia beyond Quatro's base prices.
