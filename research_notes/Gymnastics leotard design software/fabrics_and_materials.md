## 1. What are ALL the fabric families used (composition, weight, stretch, finish, opacity, use)?

### Takeaway
Fifteen-plus distinct families are in live 2026 catalogues, but almost everything is a warp-knit tricot of roughly 78-87% nylon or polyester with 13-25% elastane at 180-250 g/m², differentiated by surface treatment (dyed matte or shiny, adhesive-bonded foil dots, holographic foil, glitter, coating, pile, or sublimation print), plus a second group of light meshes (65-115 g/m²) and linings (85-95 g/m²). Makers publish composition and restrictions; weight and measured stretch only exist on mill and wholesaler spec sheets (Carvico, Pine Crest, Spandex World), and thickness is published by nobody found.

### Inferences
- The app's material library can be built on roughly eight base constructions (nylon tricot, polyester tricot, velvet, fine mesh, structured mesh, lace, rib, lining) with a separate surface-treatment layer (dye finish, foil-dot, holographic foil, continuous foil, glitter, coating, sublimation print), because every catalogued fabric is one of those bases plus one treatment.
- Carvico's data imply these warp knits stretch far more along the length (135-200%) than across the width (85-120%) under the same 3.6 kg load, with Shiro and the Siviglia lining the exceptions; "4-way stretch" in maker copy therefore hides a strong anisotropy that a cloth simulation should model per article.
- Foil-dot and hologram fabrics cluster at 200-230 g/m², only 10-40 g/m² above the 190 g/m² base tricot, so the foil and adhesive layer is light; the practical differences are hand and slightly reduced stretch, not weight.
- Velvet has almost disappeared from GK's competition range (two colours in 2026-27) while the catalogue items flagged NEW are cire, stretch satin and two SoftLux colours, and the swatch store introduces a "New Novelty ribbed fabric"; shine without foil, and texture, appear to be the current direction at the largest US maker.
- Figures of 270-306 "grams" quoted by some US sellers for mystique are most likely grams per linear yard of 58-60 in cloth (Solid Stone states 280 g per yard explicitly), which converts to roughly 200 g/m² and agrees with the mill figures.
- Several Spandex World listings give ounce and gram weights that do not convert to each other (for example 6.5 oz/yd² beside 184 g/m²); the GSM figure should be treated as approximate.

### Gaps
- Thickness (mm) was not published by any maker, mill or wholesaler found; Carvico sheets give weight, stitch density and elongation but no thickness.
- No stretch percentages exist for foil fabrics specifically; sources say only "4-way" and that the foil makes the cloth feel slightly tighter.
- GK, Sylvia P, Quatro and Snowflake publish no weight for any fabric; GK SubFuse and Holotek compositions were not found on the catalogue pages read (pp.126-133).
- No spec sheet was found giving denier or hole count for a nylon/spandex stretch mesh; "power mesh" and "illusion mesh" are trade descriptions without a common standard.
- Eurojersey Sensitive and Jersey Lomellina specifications could not be retrieved (Eurojersey's technical sheets need an article code; Jersey Lomellina's site failed TLS).

### Inferences

- Because GK, Snowflake's supplier and the UK makers all converge on 80/20 compositions and Pine Crest's foil lines are 200 GSM, a default "mystique" material of 80% nylon / 20% elastane, 200 g/m², 56-60 in wide is a safe library baseline.

## 4. How are foil/hologram ("mystique") fabrics made, what patterns and scales exist, and how do they behave?

### Takeaway
Sources consistently describe mystique as a dyed stretch tricot carrying an adhesive-bonded foil applied as micro-dots, with the dot (glue) pattern and the foil type chosen independently; holographic variants swap in a diffractive foil and larger motifs such as shards, dots, scales or abstract patterns. The foil is the weak point: rubbing, heat, fabric softener, deodorant, sweat and sunscreen dull or lift it, and the adhesive slightly stiffens the stretch.

### Cited Findings
- Construction: "A tricot treated on the surface with micro dots of foil. Creates a 'wet look'."; hologram is "A tricot treated on the surface with a foil-like hologram" — [The Lineup novelty fabrics](https://thelineup.com/pages/fabrics/novelty)
- The pattern and the foil are separate choices: a UK converter's custom foiling offers "37 GLUE PATTERNS / 5 HOLOGRAM & 11 METALLIC FOIL COLOURS" and is "AVAILABLE ON ALL DIGITAL PRINTS & FIVE PLAIN QUALITIES" (the listed bases are matt nylon, shiny nylon, Alicante net, Life Recycled and velvet) — [Funki FoilMe guide](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/foilme-guide)
- Adhesive bond: "The foil since then has been perfected and the adhesion is much better than the original."; "The stretch is no longer held back by the adhesive, but it might feel slightly tighter until it is worn a few times." — [Snowflake Mystique swatches](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/fabric-swatches/mystique-fabric-swatches.html)
- "Fabric softeners negatively affect the adhesives used in our garments" (the maker adds that this can discolour the foil and loosen embellishments) — [Plum FAQs](https://plumpracticewear.com/pages/faqs-1); GK gives the same explanation for foil discolouring — [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions)
- Coverage levels in one converter's range, as described in its guide: almost full-coverage foil (Lazer), a wet-look subtle finish (Clear), an almost full-coverage iridescent dot foil whose white areas let the base show through (Zing), and dotted metallic silver foil on net (Glint) — [Funki print base guide](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/print-base-guide)
- Pattern vocabulary in current trade use: Activator, Shattered Glass, Clear Dot, Microdot, Sparkle — [The Lineup novelty fabrics](https://thelineup.com/pages/fabrics/novelty); Pattern/Abstract, Paisley, Animal Print, Holographic Dots, Holographic Polka Dots, Shatter Glass, Fish Scale, Stripes and Stars, Snake Pattern, Camo — [Spandex World hologram category](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/browse/1); Sparkle, Opalescence, Prism — [GK catalogue p.126 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/126?viewerID=448752a2); Starlet, Cracked Ice — [Snowflake fabric swatches](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/fabric-swatches.html)
- Dot scale, qualitative only: sparkle-hologram micro dots are "similar in size to a plain mystique lycra"; Liquid Mystique is described with "Extra Twinkle tiny" dots — [Snowflake sparkle hologram swatches](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/fabric-swatches/sparkle-hologram-fabric-swatches.html); [Spandex World product 21269](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/product/21269)
- Colour is two-layer: products are named base/foil, for example "Silver shattered glass hologram on white nylon spandex.", or a royal-blue shattered-glass holographic foil on a purple stretch base — [Spandex World product 5910](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/product/5910); [Solid Stone Fabrics](https://solidstonefabrics.com/?p=2582)
- Fluorescent and UV-reactive versions exist ("Fluorescent Mystique Spandex"; "(Black Light Reactive)") — [Spandex World Mystique category](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/browse/180); [Spandex World product 21269](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/product/21269)
- A matte foil exists (GK "76A Black Matte Mystique"; Pine Crest "matte-foil polyester tricot") — [GK catalogue p.127 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/127?viewerID=448752a2); [Pine Crest Tiger Matte Foil](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/products/tiger-matte-foil-olympus/)
- Rubbing: "Do not rub this fabric, as it will dull the foil." — [Snowflake Mystique swatches](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/fabric-swatches/mystique-fabric-swatches.html); the same maker's washing guide says mystique and sparkly leotards lose their shine if the fabric is rubbed together — [Snowflake washing guide](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/blog/post/how-to-wash-your-gymnastics-leotard)
- Heat: GK says the foil can fade with heat, especially over embellished areas — [GK swatch store data](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gk-swatches/products.json?limit=250); Sylvia P says crystal areas on Mystique may dull slightly from heat application — [Sylvia P fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options); "For foiled fabrics, avoid exposure to heat to prevent the foil from deteriorating." — [Pine Crest FAQ](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/faq/); heat-pressing rhinestones onto metallic mesh dulls its shine — [The Lineup mesh fabrics](https://thelineup.com/pages/fabrics/mesh)
- Chemistry and sweat: GK says deodorant and sweat can cause colour bleed, fading or foil loss, especially where two colours meet under the arms, and that alcohol in hairspray and other aerosols can damage hologram, mesh and foil fabrics — [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions); Sylvia P says antiperspirants, deodorants and perfumes can cause colour transfer and loosen foil at the armpits — [Sylvia P product care](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/product-care-instructions)
- Washing and wear: the "bright shiny/matte foil finish may fade after wear and washing" — [Spandex World product 21269](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/product/21269); GK says repeated use can cause fading or foil loss — [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions)
- Stated country of origin for stocked foil tricot: Korea — [Pine Crest Jewels Foil](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/products/jewels-foil-collection/)

### Inferences
- A renderer should treat mystique as a two-layer material: a dyed diffuse knit plus a mask of small, flat, highly specular metallic (or diffractive) flakes with their own tint. "Matte", "pearl", "sparkle", "opalescence" and "holo" variants are changes to the flake layer (roughness, tint, diffraction), while two-tone names change base and flake colours independently.
- Because the dots are flat foil on a stretch base, stretching should lower flake coverage and expose more base colour rather than deform the dots. Sources describe dulling and loss, not cracking, and none quantifies the change.
- Wear is localised: underarms (deodorant and sweat), areas over heat-set crystals, and anywhere fabric rubs on fabric. A "worn" preset would reduce flake reflectance and coverage in those regions.

### Gaps
- No source found describes the factory process step by step (adhesive printing method, lamination temperature and pressure, carrier film) or the foil's composition; the adhesive-plus-foil account rests on converter and maker statements, not a textile-engineering reference.
- No measured dot diameter, pitch or percentage coverage was found for any mystique or hologram fabric; wholesaler product photos are low resolution and unscaled.
- No source documents foil "cracking" under stretch specifically; the terms used are dull, fade, discolour and loss.

## 5. How does sublimation printing work on polyester-spandex and what are its constraints?

### Takeaway
The design is printed onto transfer paper and heat-transferred into a white polyester-elastane base, which is why sublimated leotards use different base cloths (SubFuse, SoftLux, Motion250, "sublimated matt lycra") from the nylon mystique range. Documented constraints are the polyester requirement, a white or light base (with a restricted dark-ink palette when printing onto a coloured base), reorder colour drift, a quoted 10% colour and placement tolerance, and pilling of the printed polyester on rough surfaces.

### Cited Findings
- Process: a UK printer describes digital sublimation as printing designs onto wide-format paper and transferring them with a heat rotary calender, states that the process is for white polyester-based fabrics only, and calls it a dry process — [Funki print base guide](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/print-base-guide); "The print process we use is Digital sublimation, which offers good performance for swimwear." — [Funki FAQs](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/faqs)
- Temperatures and substrate: "The most common dyes used for sublimation activate at 350 degrees Fahrenheit / 175 degrees Celsius."; "a range of 380 to 420 degrees Fahrenheit / 195 to 215 degrees Celsius is normally recommended for optimal color."; the process "works with polyester and polymer-coated substrates"; the print "will not crack, fade or peel from the substrate under normal conditions." — [Wikipedia, Dye-sublimation printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dye-sublimation_printing)
- Why not nylon: sublimation "is often done on fabrics containing polyester because it can withstand higher temperatures", whereas wet printing is typically done on nylon-based fabric and "Digital ink direct printing, often utilized for nylon and other stretch knit fabrics" — [Pine Crest FAQ](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/faq/); nylon printing "can be done by using an acid ink" — [Wikipedia, Textile printing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_printing)
- GK runs three sublimation offers: Engineered Sublimation (four leotard silhouettes, "35 vibrant designs", on "white SubFuse, Rib, Holotek, or white Opalescence fabric"), SoftLux Sublimation ("10 SoftLux Sublimation prints and 15 base colors") and Custom Sublimation — [GK sublimation program](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/about-sublimation)
- Engineered styles are defined as "Sublimation on white fabric. Select your silhouette, choose your print, and finalize your colors.", and each print is "up to 2/3/4 color artwork" with lettered colour zones (for example Marble: R Electric Blue, S Aqua, T Midnight) — [GK catalogue p.132 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/132?viewerID=448752a2); [GK catalogue p.133 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/133?viewerID=448752a2)
- Printing onto a coloured base restricts the ink palette: GK's SoftLux styles are "Sublimation on a colored fabric", and each base colour has a short list of permitted ink colours. Examples: base 076 Grey allows only Y51 Black; 152 Red allows Black and Candy Apple; 080 Hunter Green allows Black and Kelly; 11G Crimson allows Black and Cardinal; 160 Royal allows Black, Electric Blue and Purple; 148 Purple allows Black, Purple, Royal and Electric Blue; 098 Light Pink allows 17 inks — [GK catalogue p.130 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/130?viewerID=448752a2); [GK catalogue p.131 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/131?viewerID=448752a2)
- SoftLux sublimation print examples name both the base cloth and the ink, for example "Dynamic - Base Fabric Shown: Lavender Primatex; Sublimation Color Shown: Purple" and "Groovy - Base Fabric Shown: Light Pink SoftLux; Sublimation Color Shown: Peony" — [GK catalogue p.130 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/130?viewerID=448752a2)
- Colour repeatability: "Due to the nature of the sublimation process, GK will match the color on reorders to the best of our ability, but cannot guarantee a perfect match. Please be mindful that wear and washing throughout the season will affect the color saturation in your garment." — [GK catalogue p.129 image](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/129?viewerID=448752a2)
- Tolerance: "Due to the nature of sublimation printing, colour and placement can vary by up to 10 percent, which is an accepted industry tolerance" — [Quatro catalogue p.117 image](https://d30tgldqweej78.cloudfront.net/194707/img-8932809-8d27a18d4e7b97d4.jpg)
- Pilling: GK warns that pilling may occur on Primatex and SubFuse fabrics, especially on the back and bottom, from contact with rough surfaces — [GK product 7659](https://gkelite.com/products/7659); Sylvia P says sublimated fabrics are prone to pilling on rough surfaces and that it is "not liable if pilling occurs" — [Sylvia P fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options); same warning for Motion250 — [Velocity product page](https://www.gymnasticsleotards.co.uk/products/personalised-zayla-long-sleeved-gymnastic-leotard)
- Banding: a UK printer warns that fine lines or banding can appear in digital prints, especially in shaded or solid-colour areas — [Funki FAQs](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/faqs)
- Sublimating onto special bases: printable foil bases exist in white polyester (Pine Crest Printable Jewels Foil; Funki's Zing, Lazer, Clear, Hologram and Platinum foiled bases); sublimated mesh is offered by Sylvia P and Quatro; The Lineup sells white crushed velvet, glitter mesh, athletic mesh and pop mesh as dye-sublimation canvases; Funki notes its foiled net can shift colour with certain inks — [Pine Crest Printable Jewels Foil](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/products/printable-jewels-foil/); [Funki print base guide](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/print-base-guide); [Quatro catalogue p.119 image](https://d30tgldqweej78.cloudfront.net/194707/img-8932809-a5ee8eb2c9609ebb.jpg); [The Lineup novelty fabrics](https://thelineup.com/pages/fabrics/novelty); [The Lineup mesh fabrics](https://thelineup.com/pages/fabrics/mesh)
- An ombre leotard is sold in printable Mystique foil fabric (80% polyester / 20% spandex, 200 gsm) with an "Ombre finish graduating from silver to green" — [Velocity ombre Mystique leotard](https://www.gymnasticsleotards.co.uk/products/radiant-silver-to-green-ombre-sleeveless-gymnastics-leotard-in-mystique-foil-fabric)
- Foil can also be applied after printing: custom foiling is "AVAILABLE ON ALL DIGITAL PRINTS" — [Funki FoilMe guide](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/foilme-guide)
- Mill data sheets distinguish "White for printing" cloth from "Plain dyed" cloth with slightly different elongation (nylon Vita 80% / 140% white against 85% / 145% dyed) — [Carvico Vita data sheet](https://cdn.adoratorio.app/carvico/390-Vita_0126.pdf)

### Inferences
- The split in every maker's range between a nylon foil family and a polyester print family follows from the dye chemistry: printable mystique and printable hologram are polyester versions of the nylon originals (GK marks which Mystique colours "Contain polyester"; Pine Crest and Velocity sell printable foil only in polyester).
- The app should enforce three rules: sublimation artwork is only allowed on fabrics flagged as polyester print bases; on a coloured base the ink palette is limited to a per-base list of darker, compatible colours; and since no source lists a white ink, white in the artwork should be treated as unprinted base cloth.
- An ombre "mystique" leotard is a white polyester foil base sublimated with a gradient, so its sparkle sits over the printed colour; the foil layer and the print are independent in the material model.

### Gaps
- No source found states the "grin-through" effect (white base showing when a printed knit is stretched) in those terms or quantifies it; the nearest evidence is that a one-side-printed net shows colour through to the reverse.
- No source found gives a colour gamut or names out-of-gamut colours for sublimation on polyester-elastane, and none explicitly states that white cannot be printed.
- GK does not publish the composition or weight of SubFuse, Holotek or Rib on the pages read.

## 6. How are fabrics combined in one leotard, and what layering or lining is used?

### Takeaway
A competition leotard is typically two to four fabrics: a foil or printed body, a contrast matte or hologram panel or binding, and sheer mesh for sleeves and yokes, with a nude or self-coloured lining under any mesh on the bodice and a front lining as standard. Makers treat mesh and novelty fabrics as accents only and restrict which decorations and trims can use them.

- The app needs per-panel layer stacks, not single materials: outer fabric, optional underlay (nude or coloured lining, or a second mesh layer), with opacity computed from the stack. "Illusion" areas are sheer mesh over skin or over nude lining.

### Gaps

- What Sylvia P's "SP-Hydro Protect" physically is (fabric, finish or panel) is not explained on the pages found, and those pages disagree on whether it is in all competitive leotards or only sleeved ones.

## 7. Which durability and performance properties matter in gymnastics, and what care do makers specify?

### Takeaway
The failure modes makers actually warn about are foil dulling and loss, colour bleed between dark and light panels, pilling of printed polyester and matte nylon on rough surfaces, and snagging or tearing of mesh; matte nylon tricot is rated the most resilient fabric. Care instructions agree on cold water, washing alone and inside out, no softener and no heat, but conflict on hand versus machine washing and on drying.

### Cited Findings
- Resilience ranking from one maker: Matt Lycra is the most resilient with the least fading; Mystique and Diamond Mesh are sensitive and may fade with repeated wear and washing; Mesh and Fog Mesh are delicate and prone to pulling or tearing on rough surfaces; all fabrics carry a "risk of colours fading, bleeding or pilling" — [Sylvia P fabric options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options)
- GK classes Hologram, Mesh and Nylon/Spandex with Foil ("Mystique") as fragile fabrics, says routines with close athlete contact can damage mesh, and does not guarantee mesh's lifespan — [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions)
- Abrasion: Sylvia P says fabrics should not contact rough surfaces or Velcro (it lists Velcro, concrete and gym-mat friction), especially on the bottom of the garment, and will not replace garments affected by pilling — [Sylvia P product care](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/product-care-instructions); Plum says mesh may be damaged by person-to-person contact during routines — [Plum FAQs](https://plumpracticewear.com/pages/faqs-1)

- Care, Snowflake: cold water with 1/4 cup of table salt and no soap or detergent, knead gently (attention to armpits and crotch), rinse in fresh cold water, roll in a towel, air-dry on a plastic hanger; never rub the fabric together or soak — [Snowflake washing guide](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/blog/post/how-to-wash-your-gymnastics-leotard)
- Care, Sylvia P: machine wash inside out with a mild liquid detergent (wool wash) on a short cold cycle, then tumble dry immediately on low or no heat; avoid soaking, bleach, softener, ironing, dry cleaning and direct sunlight — [Sylvia P product care](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/product-care-instructions)
- Care, Quatro: inside out, alone, cold gentle machine cycle, no bleach, detergent or fabric conditioner, remove straight after the spin, and "do not hand-wash"; six-month stone replacement service — [Quatro FAQs](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/faqs)
- Care, Ozone: "Machine wash individually on a delicate cycle (cold water)." with gentle liquid detergent, dry flat, no tumble drying, no bleach, softener or dryer sheets — [Ozone care instructions](https://ozoneleos.com/pages/care-instructions)
- Care, Plum: hand wash cold with mild detergent, pat dry, hang on a thick plastic hanger; "Machine drying with heat and tumbling will cause embellishments to fall off." — [Plum FAQs](https://plumpracticewear.com/pages/faqs-1)
- Development testing for the 2012 US Olympic leotards included athlete wear testing and wash testing, and patterns were engineered per fabric because "every fabric we use reacts differently" — [BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/amyodell/everything-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-team-usa)

### Inferences
- Makers disagree on hand versus machine washing (GK, Plum and Snowflake say hand; Sylvia P, Quatro and Ozone say cold machine; Quatro forbids hand washing) and on drying (Sylvia P says tumble dry on low or no heat; Ozone forbids tumble drying and Plum warns against heat). The common core is cold, alone, inside out, no softener, no soaking, no heat on foil.
- A per-fabric durability attribute for the app could be ranked from the evidence as: matte nylon tricot (best) > shiny nylon and polyester tricot (pilling risk when printed) > velvet > mystique and hologram foil > mesh, foil mesh, lace and glitter (most fragile).
- Chalk is not mentioned on any care page found; the documented chemical threats are deodorant, sweat, hairspray, perfume, lotion and fabric softener.

### Gaps
- No colourfastness grades (wash, perspiration, rubbing) were found for any leotard fabric; the Carvico sheets do not include them.
- No source addresses chalk, or abrasion of foil on the uneven bars specifically, and no abrasion test to failure exists for foil fabrics.
- No maker states an expected service life in wears or washes.

## 8. What are typical prices per yard/metre and fabric consumption for one leotard?

### Takeaway
At 2026 retail cut prices, leotard fabrics cost about $6-10 per yard for lining, mesh and plain tricot, $15-21 for mystique and foil, $16-24 for hologram patterns and $10-18 for velvets, with Carvico cloth in Europe at about 10-17 euros per metre and foil at about 26 euros per metre. A long-sleeve adult leotard takes roughly 1.3-1.6 m of 150 cm cloth in total, so fabric is a small part of a $100-500 finished garment.

### Inferences
- Adding the Jalie figures, an adult three-quarter-sleeve leotard uses about 1.3-1.6 m (1.4-1.75 yd) of 150 cm fabric across two fabrics, and a child's about 0.8-1.3 m. At US retail cut prices that is roughly $15-37 of fabric per adult garment (plain tricot at the low end, liquid mystique at the high end); makers buying full rolls pay less.
- Fabric therefore accounts for perhaps 5-15% of the price of a $250-500 competition leotard; crystals, labour and design dominate cost, which is consistent with makers pricing by embellishment level rather than by fabric.
- A cost estimator in the app can use per-family price bands and a simple area-plus-waste model; an upcharge flag for mystique over plain tricot matches Plum's published policy.

### Gaps
- No maker publishes its own fabric consumption or marker efficiency; the consumption figure comes from one home-sewing pattern and excludes the extra waste of multi-panel competition designs and full-length sleeves.
- Mill-direct prices (Carvico, Korean foil mills) are not public; Pine Crest prices are by quote only.
- Per-fabric upcharges at GK, Sylvia P and Quatro are not published.

### Inferences
- Observed directly in the swatch images above (researcher's own visual reading, not a published description): mystique shows a dense field of sub-millimetre specular glints over a clearly visible base colour, with broad soft highlights along folds; two-tone colourways read as one hue in shadow and another in highlight; Quatro's "Silver Holo", "Magenta Holo" and "Diesel" swatches show rainbow or oil-slick hue shifts across a fold; GK "Sparkle" holograms look like coarse multi-coloured glitter flakes while "Opalescence" looks smoother and pearlescent; wet look shows a continuous satin highlight without glints; fog and fairy mesh show sparse glints on a sheer ground.
- A workable PBR parameterisation from the available evidence: a base knit (albedo, roughness, anisotropy along the wales, normal map at 0.2-0.4 mm pitch), plus a flake layer (coverage, flake size, flake roughness, metallic tint, optional thin-film or diffraction hue shift), plus pile for velvet (sheen with nap direction), plus alpha coverage for meshes. All flake and mesh-coverage values will have to be calibrated from photographs or physical swatches because no measured values are published.
- Arena appearance is not described quantitatively anywhere; the only supplier claims are that hologram is "great for shining on the floor" and that Starlet "shines perfectly on the gym floor".

### Gaps
- No gloss units, BRDF measurements, sparkle density or colour-flop data were found for any fabric.
- No mesh open-area percentage, yarn denier or hole pitch was found for fine stretch nets; no velvet pile height was found.
- No foil dot diameter, spacing or coverage percentage was found; wholesaler product photos are low resolution and carry no usable scale.
- Physical swatch cards (GK: free up to 5; Snowflake: $0.25 each; The Lineup: free) are the practical route to calibrated reference; none could be obtained in this research.
