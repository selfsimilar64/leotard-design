## 1. Silhouettes: which garment types exist, and how do competition leotards differ from training leotards?

### Takeaway
The working garment set is: sleeveless tank leotard, long-sleeve leotard, three-quarter-sleeve leotard, short/cap-sleeve leotard, biketard (short-leg one-piece), and unitard (ankle-length one-piece), plus optional shorts or leggings worn over or under. In US club competition the three-quarter sleeve is now the dominant competition silhouette, long sleeve is the elite/NCAA convention, and the tank is primarily training wear that is also legal (and sometimes issued) for competition.

### Cited Findings
- GK Elite describes two base categories: sleeveless training tank leotards and long-sleeve competition leotards. Tanks are mainly for training but can be worn at some competitions; long sleeves are presented as giving a polished look and clear visual lines that show form; sleeves are not always required at competitions. (Erica Schnebel, GK blog, published 2 Aug 2026, updated 25 Sep 2026) — [GK Elite blog: two types of leotard designs](https://blog.gkelite.com/leotard-styles-the-two-types-of-leotard-designs-for-gymnastics/)
- Snowflake Designs states competition leotards are usually long-sleeved, more glamorous than workout leotards, and should fit slightly more snugly while staying comfortable. — [Snowflake Designs training leotards page](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/gymnastics-leotards.html)
- Wikipedia's summary: competition leotards were traditionally long-sleeved; half-length and sleeveless versions are now permitted under the Code of Points and have been worn at World Championships; practice and podium-training garments are generally sleeveless. — [Wikipedia: Leotard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leotard)
- For Paris 2024, each US long-sleeve competition leotard came with a short-sleeve replica for training. ### Inferences
- The app's base-garment choice can be a small enum: `leotard` / `biketard (short-leg unitard)` / `unitard (ankle length)`, with sleeve length as a separate parameter and `shorts` / `leggings` as optional overlay garments rather than silhouettes.
- A "level preset" would be useful: NCAA (long or sleeveless only), USAG club (three-quarter default, long and tank common), FIG elite (long sleeve convention, any sleeve legal), training (tank default).
- Because one catalogue design is routinely sold in tank, three-quarter and full-sleeve versions, sleeve length should be switchable without redesigning the body panels.

### Gaps
- No quantitative source on sleeve-length shares at FIG elite or NCAA level; the "long sleeve is the elite convention" statement rests on brand and encyclopedia descriptions, not counts.
- The USA Gymnastics attire table names full-sleeve and sleeveless leotards only; three-quarter sleeves are universal in practice and sold as compliant, but I found no rule text that names them. Likewise the NCAA text names only long sleeve and sleeveless tank; how three-quarter or one-shoulder designs are treated is not stated.
- Could not confirm `T-Back` and `Tank-Style` definitions; Snowflake lists them as categories without describing them.
- Alpha Factor, Ozone and Christian Moreau websites could not be reached on 13 Oct 2026, so their category vocabulary is missing.

## 2. Sleeves: full fabric vs mesh/illusion, construction, cuffs, partial and asymmetric sleeves

### Takeaway
Sleeves vary on five independent axes: length, fabric (opaque body fabric, unlined mesh, or sublimated/ombré mesh), cap construction (raglan is named; set-in is implied), symmetry (matched, "opposite sleeve" in a different fabric, or one-sleeve/one-shoulder), and surface treatment (cuff lettering, outer-arm stripes, crystal patterns). Mesh sleeves are now the default at the top level.

### Cited Findings
- Lengths in use and their labels: sleeveless/tank; `cap sleeve`; `Short Sleeve`; three-quarter (`3Q Sleeve`, `3/4 Sleeve`, `3 Quarter Length Sleeve`); long (`Full Sleeve`, `Long Sleeve`, `LS`). — [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog); [Quatro Lite Range](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/lite-range); [GK special order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order)
- Mesh sleeves are standard at elite level: GK design director Jeanne Diaz says mesh has become standard in sleeves and upper bodices, driven by athlete requests. (Breanna Ferreira, Cornell Daily Sun, 31 Jul 2024) — [Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/31/jeanne-diaz-10-designs-leotards-for-womens-olympic-gymnastics-team/)
- GK sleeve vocabulary: `mesh sleeves`, `black mesh sleeves`, `sublimated mesh sleeves`, `mesh sleeves and shoulders`, mesh sleeves with a light crystal scatter, sleeves in sublimated ombré on `SubFuse` fabric, white `Mesh` sleeves sublimated with a colour gradient. — [GK special order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [GK Surge #9661S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/9661s); [GK Tranquil #4959S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/4959s)
- Lining convention: Quatro describes a nude-lined upper bodice paired with unlined, breathable sublimated mesh sleeves; Sylvia P states all mesh body panels are lined with skin-tone lining for zero transparency (sleeves are listed separately from lined body panels). — [Quatro US long sleeve leotards](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/long-sleeve-leotards); [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog)
- Raglan construction is named by Quatro: `3.4 raglan mesh sleeves` (Treille) and raglan-style sleeves credited with better mobility (Vortex); 5 of 405 Quatro descriptions mention raglan. — [Quatro US all leotards](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/all-leotards)
- Asymmetric sleeves are a catalogue feature: four of Sylvia P's 20 designs for 2026-27 (Ruby, Fern, Selene, Trixie) specify one sleeve in the body fabric and an `Opposite sleeve` in mesh. — [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog)
- NCAA examples of sleeve asymmetry: a trend toward one sleeve in a contrasting colour, noted in 2025; Auburn's one-shoulder `Bloom` and Michigan State's `Lambda` with contrasting colour and pattern on sleeves and body in 2026; Alabama's 2009 single mesh sleeve cited as a 2000s hallmark. — [College Gym News roundtable, 6 Jun 2025](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=155167); [College Gym News, 6 May 2026](https://collegegymnews.com/2026/05/06/4-leotard-trends-that-shaped-2026-and-whats-coming-in-2027/); [NCAA.com, 21 Jan 2024](https://www.ncaa.com/news/gymnastics-women/article/2024-01-21/ncaa-gymnastics-leotard-trends-through-decades)
- Cuffs carry branding: in 2026 NCAA teams increasingly put the team name on sleeve cuffs, as graphic print (Kent State, Clemson) or rhinestones (UCLA, Missouri). (Diana Scroggins, 6 May 2026) — [College Gym News](https://collegegymnews.com/2026/05/06/4-leotard-trends-that-shaped-2026-and-whats-coming-in-2027/)
- Sleeve stripes: flagged as an emerging 2027 element (Towson colour racing stripe, Utah State rhinestone stripe, Oklahoma's tapered solid white panel over mesh sleeves); the US `Luminous Legacy` Olympic leotard used crystal stripes along the sleeves. — [College Gym News, 6 May 2026](https://collegegymnews.com/2026/05/06/4-leotard-trends-that-shaped-2026-and-whats-coming-in-2027/); [NBC, 10 Jul 2024](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/paris-2024-summer-olympics/olympic-gymnastics-a-sneak-peek-at-the-leotards-simone-biles-and-team-usa-will-wear-in-paris/3562611/)
- Design caution on sleeve lines: Diaz says the GK team avoids a straight stripe along the sleeve inseam because on uneven bars it would highlight any bend in the arm (a form break judges penalise). — [Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/31/jeanne-diaz-10-designs-leotards-for-womens-olympic-gymnastics-team/)
- Sleeves as a lettering/crystal zone: GK offers team names on either sleeve in crystals (style #4935S) and lists `Brilliance standard crystals on sleeves` on stock styles. — [GK special order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [GK stock competition leotards](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-stock)
- Sleeve length changes crystal count: Sylvia P gives heavy-crystal counts of over 2,220 (long sleeve) vs over 1,880 (three-quarter) for Esmerelda, and over 2,910 vs over 2,520 for Beverly. — [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog)
- A sleeve-wrapping motif: Snowflake's Egypt Olympic leotard has a crystal snake twisting around the right arm. — [Inside Gymnastics, 25 Jul 2024](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/snowflake-designs-outfits-egyptian-gymnasts-for-the-2024-olympic-games/)
- Sylvia P includes its `SP Hydro-Protect` technology as standard in full-length and three-quarter sleeved competitive leotards. — [Sylvia P design options](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/design-with-sylvia-p-teamwear)
- NCAA limits sleeves to long or sleeveless (Rule 1.10.c, 2026-27). — [NCAA 2026-27 Rules Modifications](https://nawgj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-27_WomensGym-Rules-Mod-FINAL.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested sleeve parameters: `length` (none, cap, short, three-quarter, full), `cap construction` (set-in, raglan), `fabric` (body fabric, mesh unlined, sublimated mesh with gradient), `symmetry` (matched, opposite-sleeve fabric swap, single sleeve/one shoulder), `cuff` (plain hem, contrast band, lettered band), `overlay` (outer-arm stripe, tapered opaque panel over mesh, wrap motif), and `crystal zone` (on/off with its own pattern).
- A design-rule warning in the tool would be valuable: flag straight high-contrast lines running along the inner arm, citing Diaz's bars example.
- Crystal budget should recompute when sleeve length changes (roughly 340-390 fewer stones going from full to three-quarter in Sylvia P's two published examples).

### Gaps
- The term set-in sleeve was not found on any brand page; set-in is assumed as the default where raglan is not stated.
- No source describes cuff construction (elastic, binding width, finger loops) or what `SP Hydro-Protect` physically is.
- No source for bell, flutter or detachable sleeves in WAG; none were seen in any 2026 catalogue reviewed.

## 3. Necklines

### Takeaway
Scoop is the default everywhere; V, soft V, high neck (with neckband or collar and a back opening), square, boatneck-scoop, halter-style high neck and asymmetric/one-shoulder all appear in 2026 catalogues. The "illusion" look is built as a high or scooped mesh layer over an opaque sweetheart or V line, and FIG limits how low the opaque-or-open neckline may go.

### Cited Findings
- GK special-order necklines (2026 line, exact labels): `Scoop Neck`, `V-Neck`, `Soft V-Neck`, `High Neck`, `Square neck`. In the Infinite Possibilities programme the buyer chooses `High Neck`, `Scoop Neck` or `V-Neck` for any of the back designs. — [GK special order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [GK Poised #IP005S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/ip005s)

Stock competition styles add a `mock neck` style. — [GK tank leotards](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/shop-all-tank-leotards); [GK stock competition leotards](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-stock)

- Collars: velvet was used on the 2024 US Olympic leotards for binding and high-neck collars (soft against the skin, kept to small areas to avoid overheating); in NCAA 2026 Oklahoma and New Hampshire used a split collar and several schools put their name on the collar, with more collar experimentation predicted for 2027; in 2025 one reviewer said she was tired of high-neck, thick-collar designs. — [Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/31/jeanne-diaz-10-designs-leotards-for-womens-olympic-gymnastics-team/); [College Gym News, 6 May 2026](https://collegegymnews.com/2026/05/06/4-leotard-trends-that-shaped-2026-and-whats-coming-in-2027/); [College Gym News roundtable, 6 Jun 2025](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=155167)
- Front keyholes and notches: Quatro's Eternity has a front neck notch below a high collar; Sylvia P's Paige combines small keyholes with sweetheart paneling. — [Quatro US long sleeve leotards](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/long-sleeve-leotards); [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog)
- Asymmetric necklines: Auburn's one-shoulder `Bloom` (2026); LSU's 2025 design with a cutout running from the back up to one shoulder was criticised as distracting. — [College Gym News, 6 May 2026](https://collegegymnews.com/2026/05/06/4-leotard-trends-that-shaped-2026-and-whats-coming-in-2027/); [College Gym News roundtable, 6 Jun 2025](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=155167)
- Era note: NCAA teams moved from the traditional round neck to high collars and keyhole cutouts in the 1990s, and the 2010s brought a wide variety of necklines. — [NCAA.com, 21 Jan 2024](https://www.ncaa.com/news/gymnastics-women/article/2024-01-21/ncaa-gymnastics-leotard-trends-through-decades)
- Depth limit (FIG, applied at US elite meets): the front neckline may go no lower than half-way down the sternum and the back no lower than the lower line of the shoulder blades. — [USA Gymnastics Elite Program Rules & Policies, Competition Rules 4.e](https://static.usagym.org/PDFs/Women/Rules/rp/rp_elite.pdf)

### Inferences
- Neckline can be modelled as `shape` (scoop, round, V, soft V, high V, square, boatneck-scoop, high/mock, halter-high, racer, one-shoulder/asymmetric) plus `depth`, `finish` (self binding, contrast binding, invisible binding, neckband of set width, stand collar, split collar), and an optional `illusion layer` (mesh from the opaque line up to a higher mesh neckline, lined in a chosen nude tone).
- Any high neck, mock neck or halter-high choice forces a back opening and closure (clasp or zip), so neckline and back style are coupled choices.
- A compliance check can test front depth against the half-sternum line; whether a skin-tone-lined mesh area counts as "covered" for the FIG depth test is not confirmed (see Gaps in section 9).

### Gaps
- "Choker" was not found as a term on any brand page; "boat neck" appears only as GK's `boatneck scoop neckline`.
- No rule text found on whether one-shoulder designs are permitted under FIG, USA Gymnastics or NCAA wording.
- No brand states numeric neckline depths or neckband widths.

## 4. Backs, closures, and the exact option names brands use

### Takeaway
Back style is the most varied structural choice and the one brands name most carefully. The families are: closed/full, scoop (classic, deep, low), V, keyhole (small, standard, large, circular; with or without straps or binding), clasp-closed high back, strappy (parallel, criss-cross, multi-strap, ladder, laced, twisted), open back, racerback, and mesh-filled or double-layer backs. No reviewed storefront offers "back style" as a filter; the names live in product titles, feature bullets and custom menus.

- A workable back taxonomy for the app: `base opening` (closed, scoop with depth, V with depth, keyhole with size and shape, fully open) + `upper closure` (none/pull-on, back-neck clasp, centre-back invisible zip) + `strap set` (see section 5) + `fill` (none, mesh, nude-lined mesh, printed faux straps) + `symmetry`.
- Keyhole-plus-clasp is effectively the consequence of a high neck: the keyhole is the opening that lets the head through. The tool should create it automatically when a high neck is chosen.
- The USA Gymnastics "backless" test is geometric (depth below scapula, width relative to scapular midline, mesh fill exempt) and can be implemented as a live compliance indicator on the 3D back view.
- Training lines default to scoop backs and competition lines to keyhole/clasp or strappy backs, so default suggestions can differ by garment purpose.

## 5. Straps: counts, widths, patterns, attachment and materials

### Takeaway
Straps are specified by count, width, path pattern, what lies beneath them (bare skin, nude mesh, or a printed imitation) and what they are made of (self-fabric binding, rouleau tube, branded elastic, shiny mystique). Thin "spaghetti" straps are banned in USA Gymnastics competition, and strap-heavy backs carry a practical spotting risk.

### Cited Findings
- Rule: leotards with spaghetti straps are inappropriate attire in USA Gymnastics Development and Xcel competition (0.20 after a warning); exposed bra straps may be black, leotard-matching, skin tone or clear. — [USA Gymnastics 2026-2027 Rules & Policies, Ch. 2, B.4, p. 2](https://static.usagym.org/PDFs/Women/Rules/rp/rulespolicies.pdf)
- Counts and patterns named by GK: `double strap open back with criss-cross detail`, `multi-strap criss-cross back`, `ladder straps` on a deep V-back, `criss-cross back straps`, `asymmetrical back with white strap`. — [GK tank leotards](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/shop-all-tank-leotards)
- Width: the only numeric strap width found is GK's Nike collaboration, which uses supportive 1.5-inch branded elastic straps across the upper back. — [GK tank leotards](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/shop-all-tank-leotards)
- Materials named: straps in `SoftLux`, `Mystique` (`mystique straps`, `criss-cross mystique back straps`), polyester/spandex, and branded elastic (GK); `rouleau straps` cut from matt Lycra and a `Straps & Binding` component (Sylvia P); dainty criss-cross elastic straps and delicate thin straps (Quatro). — [GK special order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog); [Quatro US all leotards](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/all-leotards)
- Layering: Sylvia P back straps twist together over a nude mesh underlay for a double-layer look; fine dual criss-cross straps create a double layer (Sibella); Quatro layers cross-straps over nude-lined mesh inside a keyhole. — [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog); [Quatro US open back leotards](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/open-back-leotards)
- Imitation straps: GK uses a sublimated faux criss-cross back print on a tank, and faux straps on a competition style (the latter from a search-result snippet of the GK Interconnected page). — [GK tank leotards](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/shop-all-tank-leotards); [GK Interconnected #9650](https://www.gkelite.com/products/9650)
- Front straps: Sylvia P's Apollo uses delicate front straps to suggest a double-layered neckline; Arizona State's `Princess` leotard had front straps that surprised one reviewer. — [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog); [College Gym News roundtable, 6 Jun 2025](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=155167)
- Decoration: Quatro places crystals along crossover straps. — [Quatro US long sleeve leotards](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/long-sleeve-leotards)
- Attachment: straps meet a back-neck clasp (GK hook clasp; Sylvia P narrow metal clasp; Quatro 20 mm clasp). — [GK special order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog); [Quatro US long sleeve leotards](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/long-sleeve-leotards)
- Aesthetic opinions (NCAA reviewers, 2025): keep one consistent strap width instead of mixing thick and thin; retire lace-up and threaded straps. — [College Gym News roundtable, 6 Jun 2025](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=155167)
- Historical extreme: Arizona State's 2010s `Pitchfork` leotard with a spaghetti-strap trident is cited as one of the most polarising NCAA designs. — [NCAA.com, 21 Jan 2024](https://www.ncaa.com/news/gymnastics-women/article/2024-01-21/ncaa-gymnastics-leotard-trends-through-decades)
- Function: straps can catch a coach's hand while spotting. — [Snowflake Designs training leotards page](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/gymnastics-leotards.html)

### Inferences
- Suggested strap parameters: `count`; `width` (with a minimum that rules out spaghetti straps); `path` (horizontal ladder, vertical, single diagonal, X criss-cross, multi-X lattice, V-converging, twisted pair, laced through loops); `under-layer` (bare, nude mesh, printed imitation); `material` (self-fabric binding, rouleau tube, elastic, mystique/foil); `anchors` (armhole binding, neckband/clasp, side seam, lower back edge); `crystal line` on/off.
- A printed or mesh-backed "faux strap" option gives the strappy look while staying inside the USA Gymnastics backless test and avoiding the spotting hazard, which explains why brands offer it.

### Gaps
- FIG's minimum shoulder-strap width could not be verified from the current Code (see section 9 Gaps); from memory of the previous edition it is 2 cm, unverified.
- No "Y-back" or "lattice back" label found; Quatro uses "lattice" for a crystal or chest pattern, not a strap layout.
- No source for how straps are structurally anchored (stitch types, reinforcement) or for typical strap widths other than the Nike 1.5-inch elastic.

## 6. Leg line and seat

### Takeaway
Brands offer two or three named leg cuts per style (GK: traditional/workout vs modified; Sylvia P: standard vs mid), and rules cap how high the leg opening can rise: USA Gymnastics requires it to sit below the hip bone. Keeping the seat from riding up is treated as a core fit problem.

### Cited Findings
- GK Elite: every 2026 special-order style offers `Modified or traditional leg cut`; older special-order styles list `Modified leg cut` only; tanks list `workout / traditional leg cut` (over 150 mentions across 253 leotard listings) or `modified leg cut` (9). — [GK special order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [GK women's leotards](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gk-gym-women-leotards)
- Sylvia P: `Leg cut` option is `Standard` or `Mid Cut`; 15 team listings carry `(Mid Cut)` in the title. — [Sylvia P Sibella 3Q](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/products/sibella-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Sylvia P Teamwear US storefront](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/all)
- USA Gymnastics (2026-27): the leg cut of the leotard must be below the pelvis/hip bone. — [USA Gymnastics 2026-2027 Rules & Policies, Ch. 2, B.4, p. 2](https://static.usagym.org/PDFs/Women/Rules/rp/rulespolicies.pdf)
- GK's 2026 dress-code guide restates it for customers: the leg opening cannot be cut or rolled above the hip, and bare midriffs are prohibited. — [GK blog: gymnastics dress code, 5 Sep 2026](https://blog.gkelite.com/gymnastics-dress-code-what-to-wear-to-gymnastics-competitions/)
- Wikipedia's summary of the Code: conforming leotards cannot be cut above hip height. — [Wikipedia: Leotard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leotard)
- Fit priorities: Diaz says fabrics are tested so that no leg lines ride up and no seams are weak; GK's company history cites keeping leotard seats down as a signature engineering problem. — [Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/31/jeanne-diaz-10-designs-leotards-for-womens-olympic-gymnastics-team/); [GK blog: history of GK Elite, 1 Sep 2026](https://blog.gkelite.com/our-enduring-legacy-the-history-of-gk-elite/)
- Crystal placement avoids the leg line and underarms for comfort (reported in coverage of the 2024 US Olympic leotards; taken from a search-result summary because the page returned an error when opened directly). — [AOL/wire report on the 2024 leotard design](https://www.aol.com/news/inside-design-intricate-crystal-patterned-133037702.html)
- Coverage alternatives: shorts or leggings may be worn with the leotard in NCAA (Rule 1.10.d), USA Gymnastics, and New Zealand domestic competition; the unitard removes the leg line entirely. — [NCAA 2026-27 Rules Modifications](https://nawgj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-27_WomensGym-Rules-Mod-FINAL.pdf); [USA Gymnastics 2026-2027 Rules & Policies](https://static.usagym.org/PDFs/Women/Rules/rp/rulespolicies.pdf); [Gymnastics NZ attire regulations](https://www.gymnasticsnz.com/attire/)

### Inferences
- Model the leg opening as a curve with a `side height` parameter measured against the hip bone, with a hard ceiling for USA Gymnastics compliance, plus named presets matching brand vocabulary (traditional/workout, modified, standard, mid).
- A "no-crystal zone" mask along the leg opening and underarm is a sensible default for crystal placement tools.

### Gaps
- No brand defines its leg cuts geometrically: I could not confirm whether GK's `modified` cut is higher or lower than `traditional`, or how Sylvia P's `Mid Cut` differs from `Standard`.
- FIG's leg-cut wording for 2025-2028 could not be retrieved. From memory of the previous edition (unverified): the cut may not rise beyond the hip bone, and any leg extension may not go lower than about 2 cm below the base of the buttocks.
- No source on seat construction (elastic type, gripper, back-rise shaping).

## 7. Panel layouts, design lines, piece counts and mesh placement

### Takeaway
Brands describe a leotard as five to seven named fabric zones (body or upper/lower body, sleeves, body panels or inserts, belt, binding and straps, plus underlays or overlays), with design lines drawn as curved or asymmetric panel seams, sweetheart or V overlays, belts and bindings, and printed (sublimated) gradients. Mesh goes on sleeves, the upper bodice, waist and side cut-outs and the back, and wherever mesh sits on the torso it is lined in a skin tone.

### Cited Findings
- Zone vocabulary (Sylvia P Studio SP summary): `Body`, `Inserts & Sleeves`, `Belt`, `Lower Body`, `Straps & Binding`, `Embellishments`. — [Sylvia P Sibella 3Q](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/products/sibella-leotard-3q-sleeve)

Individual designs name between three and seven distinct fabric zones. — [Sylvia P 2026-27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog)

### Inferences
- The app's panel model can mirror the brands' own zones: `upper body`, `lower body`, optional `belt`, `sleeves` (left and right independently), `inserts/body panels`, `binding`, `straps`, plus `underlay` and `overlay` layers. A simple design uses three zones (body, sleeves, binding); elaborate catalogue designs use six or seven plus layered mesh.
- Any mesh zone on the torso should automatically get a lining layer with a selectable nude tone; sleeves default to unlined. Light body colours should prompt a full lining.
- Sublimation makes "panels" optional: gradients, ombré and even faux straps can be printed on one piece, so the tool needs both seam-defined colour blocking and print-defined artwork on the same pattern piece.
- Durability flags are worth surfacing at design time: sublimated or mesh fabric on the seat and lower body will pill or snag sooner.

### Gaps
- No source gives the number of cut pattern pieces in a simple versus an elaborate leotard; the zone counts above are fabric zones named in catalogue copy, not pattern pieces.
- Yoke and piping were not found as brand terms (brands say upper body/upper bodice and binding or trim); princess seam appears only once, as a curved princess line in a Sylvia P description.
- "Waves", "flames" and "chevrons" are rare in 2026 copy (flame appears once at Quatro and as crystal flames on a 2024 US Olympic leotard; chevron twice at Quatro).
- No designer or brand source explains how panel lines are graded across sizes.

## 8. Motifs and trends by era (about 2012-2026), and what designers say makes a leotard work

### Takeaway
The 2010s were defined by strappy backs, varied necklines and ever more rhinestones; the 2020s by nude mesh, sublimated ombré, velvet's return, crystal counts roughly doubling at Olympic level, matte fabrics replacing shiny ones, the unitard as a legitimate elite option, and (in NCAA) branding details such as cuffs, collars, names and campus imagery. Designers consistently put function first, then use line placement, contrast between matte and shine, and crystal density to flatter the body and read from a distance.

### Inferences
- Useful "design lint" rules for the tool, all traceable to the sources above: warn on straight horizontal waist seams, on inner-arm stripes, on mixed strap widths, and on large velvet areas; encourage curved or V-shaped waist lines and lines converging upward toward the face.
- Crystal count is a reasonable proxy for tier: roughly 800-2,900 on a heavily crystalled club leotard, about 5,000 on a 2016 Olympic leotard, about 10,000 on a 2024 Olympic team-final leotard. The app should show a live crystal count and weight estimate (about one pound per 10,000 crystals, from the 2024 figure).
- A renderer needs at least two material classes with different responses to light (matte Lycra versus shiny mystique/foil) plus crystal sparkle, because designers compose with that contrast.

### Gaps
- No sourced crystal counts for London 2012 or Tokyo 2020 US leotards, and nothing found on 2012-2016 Olympic-cycle design trends beyond the Rio crystal figure.
- No designer statement found that addresses arena lighting or television cameras directly (colour choice under LED lighting, moiré on mesh, etc.); the nearest are the matte-versus-shine remark and the CRT-television aside.
- The Fashionista piece on the 2024 design process (the most design-focused article located) could not be opened; no interviews with named designers at Sylvia P, Quatro, Destira, Plum, Ozone, Alpha Factor or Christian Moreau were found.
- Trend evidence is concentrated in one outlet (College Gym News) for NCAA and in brand copy for club level; it is editorial opinion, not survey data.

## 9. Rules: FIG, USA Gymnastics (Development and Xcel), NCAA, and national differences

### Takeaway
Three rule sets constrain design. FIG (2025-2028 cycle) limits neckline and back depth, regulates emblems, logos and advertising by area, bans slogans and athlete names, and allows unitards, leg coverings and head coverings. USA Gymnastics (2026-27) bans backless, racerback and spaghetti-strap leotards and requires the leg cut below the hip bone, with a 0.20 deduction after a warning. NCAA (2026-27) allows only long-sleeve or sleeveless matching leotards, permits names and numbers, shorts and leggings, and applies a 0.2 team deduction. The full FIG leotard article could not be retrieved, so several FIG details remain unverified.

### Cited Findings

**FIG (World Gymnastics), 2025-2028 cycle**

- Location of the attire rule: the WAG Code of Points 2025-2028 places competition attire at Article 2.3.2 (Section 2, gymnast's duties), per a search-result snippet of a German judging site's 2025 recording-sheet header. The Code was approved by the FIG Executive Committee in February 2024 (per the title page of an uploaded copy); the same German site lists an English original dated 23 May 2025, and FIG's February 2026 newsletter carries replacement pages marked November 2025. — [kari-turnen.de recording-sheet header](https://kari-turnen.de/Wertungskoepfe/Frauen/CdP/CdP_F_Kopf_VT25.pdf); [Scribd copy of the WAG Code title page](https://www.scribd.com/document/861031369/en-1-1-WAG-Code-of-Points-2025-2028-1); [kari-turnen.de Code de Pointage page](https://www.kari-turnen.de/Wertungsvorschriften/index.php); [FIG WAG Newsletter, 23 Feb 2026](https://gymogturn.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/WAG_NL_1_en.pdf); [FIG WAG Code of Points 2025-2028 (file too large to open in this research)](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_1.1%20-%20WAG%20COP%202025-2028.pdf)
- Neckline and back depth (current FIG wording as reproduced by USA Gymnastics for its elite meets): the neckline of the front and back of the leotard or unitard must be proper, which the rule defines as no lower than half of the sternum at the front and no lower than the lower line of the shoulder blades at the back. — [USA Gymnastics Elite Program Rules & Policies, Competition Rules 4.e](https://static.usagym.org/PDFs/Women/Rules/rp/rp_elite.pdf)
- Garment type (wording as quoted in 2021 from the then-current Code): the gymnast must wear a correct, sportive, non-transparent leotard or unitard of elegant design; a unitard is a one-piece with full-length legs, hip to ankle; she may wear complete leg coverings in the same colour as the leotard, under or over it. — [Student Independent News, 22 Sep 2021](https://sin.ie/2021/09/22/sexualisation-in-sport-or-functionality-why-german-gymnasts-made-headlines-with-their-unusual-competition-attire/)
- Uniform, not costume: FIG's clothing rules (valid from May 2024; appendix pages are headed 2025-2028) say design must be compatible with a sports uniform and not a costume, and that only national identification, manufacturer's logo, product-technology identification, advertising and bib number may appear. Political slogans or signs, motivational words or logos, and war, violence, sexual or religious themes are prohibited. — [FIG Rules for Competition Clothing and Advertising, valid from May 2024, Introduction](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf)
- National identification (Art. 5): required on competition attire as a flag in official colours and/or the country name (full or FIG three-letter code); minimum total area 30 sq cm; at least once, no maximum; placement optional (anywhere on a women's leotard or unitard); a national emblem or crest may be added; the uniform may be in national colours; the athlete's name may not appear; identical for all members of a team; it does not count as advertising. — [FIG Rules for Competition Clothing and Advertising, Art. 5](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf)
- Manufacturer's logo (Art. 4): one per piece of clothing, maximum 30 sq cm, ideally in its usual retail position (the upper back is allowed if that is the retail placement); a unitard counts as one piece; irregular logos are measured by the rectangle drawn around them. — [FIG Rules for Competition Clothing and Advertising, Art. 4 and Appendix D](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf)
- Advertising on women's clothing (Art. 1.3 and Art. 3): at most three advertising spaces on the athlete, each a different sponsor. On the leotard or unitard: either two spaces of up to 90 sq cm each (background included) at least 3 cm apart, or one space of up to 130 sq cm. On shorts or tights: one space of up to 90 sq cm. Advertising may not be integrated into the overall design (the leotard cannot be a billboard). Team members must show the same sponsor. — [FIG Rules for Competition Clothing and Advertising, Art. 1.3 and Art. 3](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf)
- Product-technology identification (Art. 6): one per garment, maximum 10 sq cm. — [FIG Rules for Competition Clothing and Advertising, Art. 6](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf)
- Head coverings (Appendix E, a modesty/religious provision): permitted in all disciplines if tight-fitting with the face fully exposed and posing no safety risk, in the same colour or colours as the leotard, attached, unattached or part of the leotard, and carrying no advertising, country designation, technology mark or logo. — [FIG Rules for Competition Clothing and Advertising, Appendix E](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf)
- Enforcement (Art. 8): the President of the Superior Jury or a designee checks advertising and national identification before and during competition and applies the penalties listed in the Code of Points; refusal to comply can lead to exclusion from that phase; special manufacturer designs can be sent to FIG for review two weeks ahead. At the Olympic Games IOC guidelines prevail over FIG clothing rules, but Code of Points deductions still apply. — [FIG Rules for Competition Clothing and Advertising, Art. 4.1 and Art. 8](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf)
- No attire changes in the latest FIG technical newsletter: the WAG newsletter of 23 February 2026 covers Code clarifications, personal equipment on the field of play (from 1 June 2026) and new elements, with no mention of attire. — [FIG WAG Newsletter, 23 Feb 2026](https://gymogturn.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/WAG_NL_1_en.pdf)
- Contrast (other FIG disciplines, from search-result snippets of their 2025-2028 codes): rhythmic requires non-transparent material from chest to crotch and uses the same half-sternum and shoulder-blade neckline limits; aerobic requires the crotch area covered up to the hip bones in non-transparent, non-skin-coloured material. — [FIG Rhythmic Code of Points 2025-2028](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_1.1%20-%20RG%20Code%20of%20Points%202025-2028.pdf); [FIG Aerobic dress code](https://www.fig-aerobic.com/3-1-DRESS-CODE_a1591.html)

**USA Gymnastics, Development Program and Xcel, 2026-2027 season (effective 1 Aug 2026)**

### Inferences
- The three rule sets conflict on lettering: FIG forbids the athlete's name, slogans and motivational words; NCAA explicitly allows names and numbers and NCAA teams print slogans and names. A design made for one context can be illegal in the other, so the app should hold a "ruleset" setting and validate text elements against it.
- Compliance checks that can be automated from the verified rules: front neckline depth versus half-sternum and back depth versus lower shoulder-blade line (FIG); backless geometry and mesh-fill exemption, spaghetti-strap minimum width, leg opening below hip bone (USA Gymnastics); sleeve length in {long, sleeveless} and team-wide identity (NCAA); emblem at least 30 sq cm, logo at most 30 sq cm, advertising areas and 3 cm separation (FIG).
- The USA Gymnastics back limit (about 2 inches below the scapula) is slightly more permissive in depth than the FIG limit (lower line of the scapula) but adds a width limit; a design meant for both should satisfy the stricter of each.
- New Zealand's 0.3 and 1.00 penalties mirror what I recall of the FIG structure, which suggests (but does not prove) that FIG's own figures are the same.

### Gaps

- Unverified recollection of the previous (2022-2024) edition of Art. 2.3.2, to be checked against the current text before use: shoulder straps at least 2 cm wide; leotards and unitards with or without sleeves; leg cut not beyond the hip bone, and leg length not below a line about 2 cm under the base of the buttocks; identical leotards for one federation's gymnasts in qualification and team final; no jewellery except small stud earrings; no hip or other padding; bandages beige or skin-coloured; deductions of 0.30 from the final score for incorrect attire, missing emblem, missing number or incorrect advertising, and 1.00 from the team for non-identical leotards. Treat every item in this bullet as a lead, not a finding.
- Whether nude-lined mesh above the half-sternum line satisfies the FIG transparency and neckline tests is not addressed in any document I could open.
- USA Gymnastics 2026-27 Development and Xcel update decks contained no text matching attire terms, but they are slide PDFs and may carry text as images; no 2026-27 attire change was identified.

- Not researched for lack of search budget: British Gymnastics, Gymnastics Canada, Gymnastics Australia, European Gymnastics and other national attire rules; any FIG statement on unitards or shorts since 2021.

## 10. Sizing: ranges offered and how a design is carried across a team

### Takeaway
Leotards are sold in about 10-13 sizes spanning small child to adult plus sizes, sized by body measurement with torso girth as the controlling dimension; brands add long-torso and short-torso variants for made-to-order teamwear and send sizing sets to clubs. Fit sets differ by fabric family because stretch differs. Nothing published explains how artwork and crystal patterns are graded between sizes.

### Inferences
- The size system the app needs: about 13 graded sizes (child XXS to adult 4XL), each with regular, long-torso and short-torso variants, driven by four body measurements with torso girth dominant.
- Because fit sets differ by fabric family, the pattern (or its stretch allowance) should be a function of the primary fabric's stretch, not only of size.
- Catalogue crystal counts are quoted once per design, not per size, which suggests either a fixed stone count laid out to a scaled template or counts quoted for a reference size. The tool should let the designer choose between "scale spacing, keep count" and "keep spacing, vary count" and report counts per size.
- The hip-bone leg-cut rule and neckline rules are anatomical, so compliance must be checked on each graded size (or on an avatar per size), not only on the sample size.

### Gaps
- No brand or designer source describes how panel lines, sublimated artwork or crystal patterns are graded across sizes for a team order.
- Sylvia P's and Quatro's numeric size charts were not captured (PDF/tabbed content); only labels and measuring rules were.
- Adult size ceiling at Sylvia P not confirmed.
- No information on adaptive, maternity or custom-measured competition leotards.

## 11. Market: major brands, signature styles and price tiers

### Takeaway
The market runs from about USD 20-60 training tanks, through USD 100-380 club competition leotards (rising to about USD 700 with heavy Swarovski coverage), to four-figure Olympic pieces. GK Elite (US) is the largest and outfits Team USA; Sylvia P (Australia) and Quatro (UK) are the main international custom teamwear houses with online 3D or kit-builder tools; Snowflake, Plum and Destira serve US clubs at lower price points. Milano Pro Sport, a long-standing UK brand, went into liquidation in July 2026.

### Inferences
- GK's special-order list prices appear to be base prices: product pages describe crystal patterns as optional, so heavily crystalled GK team leotards will cost more than the USD 178-378 shown.
- Price is driven mainly by three things the app can count: sleeve length (tank versus sleeved roughly triples the median at Sylvia P), crystal brand and coverage, and whether the design is a recoloured catalogue style or bespoke. A live cost estimate from those inputs would match how brands quote.
- Existing 3D tools (GK UDesign, Sylvia P Studio SP, Quatro's kit builder) are recolour-and-embellish configurators over fixed styles sold through sales reps, not free-form pattern design tools. That is the gap a professional design application would fill.
- Milano's exit removes a major UK supplier during the 2026-27 season; UK club demand will shift to Quatro and overseas brands.
