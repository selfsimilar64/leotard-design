## 1. Which game is "Dressmaker", and how exactly does building work in it?

### Takeaway
"Dressmaker" is almost certainly the cozy sewing simulation by Cozy Lives / Free Lives, released on Steam on 21 September 2026 (three weeks before this research), which went viral (35,650 peak concurrent players, 98% positive from 9,828 English Steam reviews). Its build flow is a strict pipeline — pick ready-made parts (bodice/neckline, collar, sleeves, skirt) in a sketchbook, assign a fabric per panel, lay out and cut pattern pieces, pin them on a mannequin, sew each seam, then freely place decorations — and its most-cited friction points are exactly the things a professional tool should avoid: no full undo for decoration, a mouse-only interface, and manual steps that players immediately modded away.

#### How building works in Dressmaker (Cozy Lives), step by step
1. **Brief.** A customer names an event and a budget; each has their own taste. The commission card lists target style scores — [Windows Mode guide](https://www.windowsmode.com/?p=47112). Orders list target values for traits such as elegance and romanticism "plus about twenty other indicators", and some orders cap certain traits — [iXBT.games review, 5 Oct 2026](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html). Example: one customer needs Elegant 65, Glamour 40, Quality 75 and no black — [AllThings.How guide](https://allthings.how/?p=207842)
2. **Measure and set the mannequin.** The player drags a tape measure across the client's silhouette for bust, waist and hips; the mannequin has three dials for those and the target is a "100% match" readout — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842)
3. **Design in the sketchbook (choose pieces).** The dress is built from ready-made parts: a bodice, sleeves, a skirt and a collar; each part carries style scores (cute, elegant, romantic…) and the sketchbook shows roughly how many metres of fabric the design needs — [Windows Mode](https://www.windowsmode.com/?p=47112). Another guide lists the choices as "a neckline and bodice, collar, sleeve type, and skirt cut", each shifting style-tag bars that must clear the commission's targets before money is spent — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842). Sketches can be coloured with pencils and fabric samples placed beside them; the album saves designs — [iXBT.games](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html). Official wording: "Design and colour in your sketchbook, adding fabric samples and appliqué." — [press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/highly-anticipated-dressmaker-gets-a-release-date-200k-wishlists)
4. **Buy fabric and trims.** Five fabric families (cotton, linen, wool, silk, velvet) in many prints and colours, each with style tags (formal, cute, simple) that can raise a score the parts miss — [Windows Mode](https://www.windowsmode.com/?p=47112). Fabric is bought by the metre, along with buttons, bows or lace, at a shop that can be filtered by tag; leftover cloth stays in inventory — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842)
5. **Per-panel material assignment.** "Different materials can be assigned to different panels of a dress" — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/games/dressmaker/); the Steam page likewise lists a separate fabric choice for each panel — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4019220/_/). Expensive fabric can be reserved for key details with cheaper material elsewhere — [iXBT.games](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html)
6. **Lay out and cut.** Every pattern piece is arranged on the fabric roll and rotated to sit on the grain; the game shows how much fabric each piece uses before committing; each piece has a grain-line arrow and its outline turns green when aligned; "Misaligned pieces cap your quality score outright." Pieces can be bias-cut, and stripes that do not line up carry through to the finished dress — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842). The part of the fabric under a template becomes part of the finished piece; the player does not trace curves — after selecting the scissors and a piece, the game cuts along the contour automatically — [iXBT.games](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html)
7. **Pin and preview on the mannequin.** Every cut panel is pinned to the form before stitching to check fit and colour blocking; "Rearranging costs nothing at this stage." — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842). The mannequin shows the design in three dimensions — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/games/dressmaker/)
8. **Sew (how pieces attach).** Each seam is a separate minigame along a marked stitch line, steered with keyboard or mouse; quality is scored live, drifting far off the line forces a restart, and a "Sewing Assist" setting guides fabric around curves automatically — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842). Keyboard controls: "Press Space to sew, A and D to rotate the fabric, and W and S to change speed." — [Windows Mode](https://www.windowsmode.com/?p=47112). Bad seams can be unpicked and redone, and cut pieces can be returned to the fabric for re-cutting; thread colour can be changed but seams are never visible on the finished dress — [iXBT.games](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html)
9. **Decorate.** "Buttons can go anywhere, in any quantity"; bows and lace trim can be layered on hems, sleeves, waistlines and open backs; placement affects style stats; "There is no full undo for decoration, so place optional trim slowly." — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842). Appliqué is "fabric shapes sewn on top" — [Windows Mode (AMP)](https://www.windowsmode.com/dressmaker-for-windows/amp/). Accessories "can be positioned freely" — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/games/dressmaker/). A reviewer describes the range as anything from "one brooch" to a dress "covered with feathers" — [iXBT.games](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html)
10. **Deliver, score, archive.** Up to five stars from three inputs: quality (grain alignment and stitching), style-tag match, and budget; each finished dress is archived in the sketchbook — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842)
11. **Photo studio.** The player sets camera angle, lighting intensity and lighting direction and saves a record shot — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842)

#### Reception that bears on UX
- Players praise systems that feel "handmade and tactile", creative freedom, and dresses that come from player choices rather than presets — [GuruGamer](https://gurugamer.com/pc-console/a-tiny-dressmaking-game-is-suddenly-taking-over-steam-27344)
- A reviewer notes that story orders are passed by meeting numerical requirements, so clashing colours or random decorations can still score well ("creativity is optional"), that clients appear only as 2D images so the dress is never seen on the person, and that only dresses in a quasi-Victorian setting can be made — [iXBT.games](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html)
- "Requirement lists can feel like a grading rubric when several traits conflict"; the first patch (Build 403) fixed an invisible cursor in the cutting-room tutorial among other issues; review score 8.4/10 — [GameBrief](https://www.gamebrief.net/blog/dressmaker-review-2026)
- Within three weeks of launch, fan mods appeared that remove manual steps: "AutoSew" automates "sewing seams, measuring, mannequin sizing and laying out pattern pieces"; a mod loader adds a sandbox mode with unlimited money; fabric-pack mods add sheers, laces, watercolour and glitter fabrics — [Windows Mode (AMP)](https://www.windowsmode.com/dressmaker-for-windows/amp/)
- In the prototype, the sewing machine was "a frequent frustration" in player comments, while others called the game "essentially a 1 to 1 to sewing in real life" — [itch.io prototype page](https://elyaradine.itch.io/dressmaker)
- A prototype player discovered that "You can use pieces cut out of the wrong fabric as a pattern piece to cut it out of the right fabric" — [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/14106090)

### Inferences
- The commissioner's phrase "building a design from many smaller pieces" maps onto three distinct layers in Dressmaker, and the target product needs all three but should weight them differently: (1) *part selection* from a catalogue (bodice/collar/sleeve/skirt → for a leotard: front body, back, neckline, sleeve, leg line, inserts/mesh panels); (2) *per-panel material assignment*; (3) *free placement of many small decorations*. Dressmaker's simulation of cutting and sewing is the game's "work", and should not be copied into a professional tool — the AutoSew mod shows that even game players want those steps removed once the novelty fades.
- The single most reusable interaction idea is the **free, zero-cost rearrangement stage on the mannequin** ("Rearranging costs nothing at this stage"): a place where the whole design is visible in 3D and every decision is still reversible. A design tool should make the entire session feel like that stage.
- The clearest anti-pattern is decoration without undo. For a tool where a heavy design carries well over a thousand crystals (see section 3, Sylvia P tiers), unlimited undo and non-destructive editing of placed stones are baseline requirements, not polish.
- Print placement and grain direction carrying through to the finished garment is a strong "what you arrange is what you get" principle. The leotard equivalent is sublimated print and mesh direction per panel.
- The style-score system gives players a goal; the professional analogue is live read-outs that matter to a coach or designer (crystal count, estimated price tier, rule compliance), shown while designing rather than after.
- Dressmaker has no documented symmetry or brush tools, so it is a reference for piece assembly and material assignment, not for bulk rhinestone placement. Section 6 covers better references for that.
- Ambiguity statement: identification rests on name match, timing and fit with the description. It was not confirmed with the commissioner. If they meant something else, the nearest alternative is Roblox's Dress to Impress (section 2).

### Gaps
- No source describes symmetry/mirroring, snapping guides for decorations, or precise mannequin camera controls (orbit/zoom bindings) in Dressmaker. The Steam news hub returned no readable content, and no official manual or wiki was found.
- Whether players can truly draft their own patterns is unclear. A pre-release article says "Create your own patterns and preview your own designs on a mannequin" ([Sims Community, 29 Jul 2026](https://simscommunity.info/2026/07/29/release-info-dressmaker-features/)), but launch reviews describe only ready-made parts. Treat custom pattern drafting as unconfirmed.
- The game engine and technical implementation are not stated in any source found.
- A claim that the prototype had "body sliders and measurement tools" appears only in a search summary ([ingamenews.com](https://ingamenews.com/2026/06/dressmaker-announced-at-wholesome.html), snippet); not confirmed on the itch.io page.
- Console titles named "Dressmaker"/"Dress Maker" were not found, but no storefront-by-storefront check was possible after the search budget ran out.

## 2. Analogous piece-based or free-form garment creation systems in games and consumer apps

### Takeaway
Consumer garment creators fall into four structures: *outfit assembly from whole items* (Dress to Impress, Style Savvy, Infinity Nikki), *recolouring fixed templates* (Fashion Dreamer, The Sims), *painting on unwrapped garment panels* (Animal Crossing pro designs), and *3D items that auto-fit any body* (Roblox layered clothing). The ones praised for fluidity pair a small set of high-level primitives with instant on-body preview and a sharing loop; the ones criticised are those where the design system is too restricted.

### Cited Findings
- **Dress to Impress (Roblox, released 11 Nov 2023, Dress To Impress Group).** Players get a theme and "360 seconds to assemble an outfit that fits the prompt", choosing up to 18 clothing items from a showroom; "The colors of accessories and clothing can also be modified"; others rate each look one to five stars; a freeplay mode has no timer. Over 651 thousand concurrent players in August 2024; played over 2.7 billion times by September 2024. A critic credited its "jankiness" (loose clothing clipping) for part of its appeal — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_to_Impress_(video_game))
- **Roblox layered clothing (platform mechanism).** Layered accessories "stretch and fit on an avatar character's body" using cage meshes: "The inner cage defines the inner surface of the clothing item where the clothes wrap over" and "The outer cage defines the outer surface of the clothing item where any additional clothes can wrap and layer over." Items are authored in external tools and exported as one .fbx or .gltf — [Roblox Creator Docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/accessories/layered-clothing)
- **Fashion Dreamer (syn Sophia, Nintendo Switch, 2–3 Nov 2023).** Players build "Lookits" for characters with colour, garment and style preferences; "Completed Lookits are evaluated according to how well they meet these requirements." The Item Creator lets players customise unlocked patterns with different colours; these form a brand shown in a showroom, and players collect others' designs by giving a "Like". Critics praised collecting and wearing other players' designs but criticised a restricted design system and repetitive play; Metacritic 62/100; nearly 500,000 copies by end of 2023, below forecast. Online support ended in March 2026 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fashion_Dreamer)
- **Style Savvy / Style Boutique (syn Sophia, Nintendo DS, 2008–2009).** "The main gameplay loop consists of buying clothing, then selling it to customers at the player's boutique based on their tastes." Primarily touch-screen input — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_Savvy). The 2015–2016 sequel Fashion Forward sold 1 million copies — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_Savvy:_Fashion_Forward)
- **Infinity Nikki (Papergames/Infold, 5 Dec 2024; macOS 23 Apr 2026).** Outfits are chosen by attributes (Sweet, Fresh, Elegant, Sexy, Cool); clothing is acquired through a gacha system; built on Unreal Engine 5 with emphasis on "detailed visuals, including fabric textures"; over 20 million downloads by The Game Awards 2024 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Nikki)
- **Animal Crossing: New Horizons custom and pro designs.** Designs are "custom-made patterns comprised of 32x32 pixels" with up to 15 colours; in pro designs "Tops can be comprised of separate patterns for the left sleeve, right sleeve, front, and back"; garment templates include tank top, short-sleeve tee, sweater, hoodie, coat, several dress types and hats; the Pro Editor costs 800 Nook Miles; 90 designs plus 90 pro designs per island as of 1.9.0; designs can be displayed for others to copy and up to 130 uploaded for sharing — [Nookipedia](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Custom_design)
- **The Sims 4 Create a Sim.** "Facial and bodily features are adjusted by directly selecting and manipulating the respective area." The colour-and-pattern system from the previous game was dropped: "the 'Create-a-Style' color customization gameplay technologies introduced in The Sims 3 would also be absent from the game." The Gallery lets players "upload and share Sims, Sim households, rooms, and buildings" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims_4)
- **inZOI (Krafton, early access 28 Mar 2025).** "allows for the use of generative AI to create in-game patterns and objects"; creations are shared through the in-game "Canvas", with over 470,000 pieces uploaded; the game received negative user reviews in May 2025 after Krafton announced its use of generative AI — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inzoi)
- **Tiny Glade (Pounce Light, 23 Sep 2024)** — not a garment tool, but the reference for frictionless building and sold in a Steam bundle with Dressmaker ($26.98). A sandbox with no tasks or storyline; built on a custom version of the Bevy (Rust) engine; over 800,000 wishlists before release; IGN called it "a small slice of flow state to pamper your brain" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Glade); bundle — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4019220/_/)

### Inferences
- **Structures compared.** (a) Whole-item assembly is fast but gives no control inside a garment. (b) Template-plus-recolour is the same model the leotard brands' configurators use (section 3) and draws the same complaint — a "restricted design system". (c) Animal Crossing's pro designs are the closest consumer analogue to "a garment made of panels, each with its own surface": front, back and each sleeve are separate canvases. (d) Roblox's cage approach shows how one garment can be made to fit different bodies without re-authoring, relevant to previewing a leotard across sizes.
- **What makes these feel fluid:** a short list of meaningful choices rather than open-ended parameters; the result always visible on a body; direct manipulation of the thing itself (The Sims 4's push-and-pull on the body instead of sliders); cheap experimentation (freeplay modes, no fail state); and a remix loop where other people's work can be copied and altered (Animal Crossing sharing, Fashion Dreamer likes, The Sims Gallery, inZOI Canvas).
- **A brief focuses creativity.** Dress to Impress (theme plus timer), Fashion Dreamer and Style Savvy (customer preferences) and Dressmaker (commission card) all start from a brief. A professional tool can borrow this lightly — for example, starting a design from team colours, a level/age group and a budget band.
- The Sims 3 to 4 change is a caution in both directions: removing free colour/pattern control simplified the tool but was a noted loss. Presets should be the fast path, not the only path.
- Generative AI inside a creative tool carries reputational risk with creative audiences (inZOI's review backlash), so any AI assistance should be opt-in and assistive.

### Gaps
- Infinity Nikki's dye/recolour system and any player-design features are not covered by the source opened. Unverified lead: a dye system was added in the 1.5 update (2025).
- Style Savvy's in-game clothing-design feature (later titles) is not described on the pages opened. Unverified lead: Fashion Forward and Styling Star let players design items from a template, colours and patterns.
- The Sims 3 Create-a-Style specifics (per-channel pattern and colour, drag-and-drop of styles between objects) could not be sourced; the wiki page returned HTTP 402.
- Animal Crossing editor tools (pen sizes, fill, stamps, mirror mode) and code-based sharing are not confirmed on the portion of the page read.
- Dress to Impress layering toggles and per-item pattern/texture controls are not described in the source opened.
- Dedicated consumer fashion-design apps (sketch/flat-drawing apps, mobile dress-design games) were not researched before the search budget ran out.

## 3. Which leotard, gymnastics, cheer, dance and skating brands offer online design tools, and are any of them real 3D?

### Takeaway
Three gymnastics brands offer self-serve design studios: GK Elite (UDesign, self-described "online 3D design studio", requires WebGL, team orders only), Sylvia P (StudioSP, "our 3D product designer", per-zone fabrics/colours plus crystal coverage tiers, shareable link) and Quatro (a "kit builder app" for clubs, minimum 10). All are template-and-zone configurators: none found offers free-form panel drawing or stone-by-stone crystal placement. Everyone else found — Snowflake, Foxy's, Velocity Pro Sport, Varsity, Rebel — still runs custom design through a human designer or sales rep.

Quatro does not say.
- **All three are "template plus zones" configurators.** The user picks an existing style, then swaps fabric/colour per predefined zone and picks a crystal option. In Sylvia P's case the crystal choice is a coverage tier and stone type, i.e. the crystal pattern itself is fixed by the style. No evidence was found of any brand tool that lets a user draw new panel lines, move a seam, or place individual stones.
- **The market gap the target product addresses is therefore specific:** free-form panel design and direct crystal placement (single stones and brushes), in real time, with stunning rendering. The existing tools compete on convenience and ordering integration, not on creative freedom.
- **Pricing is driven by crystals.** Sylvia P's tiers show a roughly 3.5–4x price spread from ~20 crystals to 1,560–1,823+ crystals on the same garment. A live crystal count and cost-band indicator would be meaningful to buyers.
- **Scale target for rendering/placement:** a "heavy" club design is on the order of 1,500–2,000 stones by Sylvia P's own tiers, so the tool must comfortably handle several thousand instances.
- Quatro's "kit builder app" wording matches the name of the UK configurator vendor Kit Builder (section 4), but no source confirms that Quatro uses that platform.
- GK and Quatro restrict self-serve custom design to teams/clubs with minimums; individuals are excluded. Sylvia P's StudioSP shows an "Add to bag" button, suggesting individual purchase is possible, but no minimum is stated either way.

### Inferences
- The teamwear configurator pattern is consistent across vendors: **template → zone colours → logos/text → names and numbers → 3D preview → human check → print-ready file**. - These tools treat the garment surface as flat artwork on a fixed 3D model. They have no concept of physical embellishment, mesh/opaque layering, or panel construction, which are the defining features of a competition leotard. Using an off-the-shelf configurator platform would reproduce what GK, Sylvia P and Quatro already have.

- Technology stacks are mostly undisclosed. The only concrete technical signals found are GK's WebGL requirement (section 3) and Kit Builder's SVG artwork, REST API and Illustrator plugin.

## 5. Professional 3D fashion tools — CLO, Marvelous Designer, Browzwear VStitcher, Style3D, Optitex

### Takeaway
The professional tools all share one model — draw or import 2D pattern pieces, sew them virtually, and simulate the cloth on an avatar — which demands pattern-making knowledge and weeks of learning. They have no purpose-built bulk rhinestone tool: CLO users place crystals by workarounds (custom 3D objects repeated along a topstitch line, or the button tool) and report distortion on curves and mirroring errors. They are excellent for pattern accuracy and production hand-off, and a poor fit for fast, decorative, crystal-heavy leotard design by non-patternmakers.

### Inferences
- **Poor fit, for these reasons:** (1) the entry point is a 2D pattern, so the user must already think like a patternmaker; (2) the tools' central strength — drape simulation of loose cloth — matters little for a skin-tight stretch garment; (3) crystal placement is a workaround with known defects, and a heavy design of 1,500+ stones would have to be authored line by line; (4) the 4–8 week learning estimate is incompatible with a coach or small-brand designer wanting a result in one sitting; (5) licensing (CLO's individual plan excludes companies of two or more people) pushes small manufacturers toward enterprise quotes.
- **Good fit, in these respects:** accurate, graded, production-ready patterns; print layout and sublimation artwork placement; strain/tension maps for stretch fit; avatars in motion for checking a design during movement. A leotard tool could treat these as an *export target* (hand a finished design to a manufacturer's CLO/VStitcher pipeline) rather than something to replicate.
- **The most relevant recent idea is "sketch on the avatar to create panels"** (CLO 2026.0, Marvelous Designer 3D Pencil). Drawing style lines directly on the body and having the software turn the enclosed regions into panels is the natural way to "assemble a leotard from pieces" without touching a pattern window.
- The 80.lv quote captures the right philosophy for the target product: prioritise what the designer wants the garment to look like over what a physics solver says it would do.
- The CLO thread's specific failures define acceptance tests for a crystal tool: stones must keep their shape and spacing on curves, mirrored stones must keep correct orientation, and row ends must not stretch.

## 6. Decoration and painting tools as UX references, and the ingredients of a "frictionless" creative tool

### Takeaway
The best references for crystal placement come from three families: 3D painting tools (non-destructive strokes that can be recomputed, editable paths on the model), sculpting tools (click-drag insertion of repeated instances that slide over a surface, objects repeated along a curve), and rhinestone-template software (area fill, manual single-stone placement, overlap detection). The literature on creative tools reduces "frictionless" to a consistent list: direct manipulation with immediate visible results, trustworthy undo, well-chosen high-level primitives, a low threshold with a high ceiling, and tools that are pleasurable enough to stay out of the way.

### Cited Findings

#### Painting and decoration tools
- **Adobe Substance 3D Painter.** "Painter is non-destructive." "Every action and stroke is recorded and can be recomputed at any time."; "Paint with dynamic brushes, projection tools, particles, including Photoshop brush presets."; "Painter's state-of-the-art viewport lets you view your work in real time, with effects like lighting and shadows."; version 11.0 added "filled 3D paths and enhanced control of 3D path creation and manipulation"; sold in the Substance 3D Collection at US$59.99/month — [Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/products/substance3d/apps/painter.html)
- **Procreate 3D painting.** "the ability to paint directly onto imported 3D models"; painting "without affecting the original textures"; "Break each layer down into its individual materials to work exclusively on color, metallics and roughness."; a "simple yet powerful Lighting Studio"; models can be viewed in AR — [Procreate Handbook](https://help.procreate.com/procreate/handbook/3d-painting)
- **Blender brush stroke methods (5.2 LTS manual)** — a ready-made vocabulary for stone brushes:
  - Space: "Creates brush stroke as a series of dots, whose distance (spacing) is determined by the Spacing setting."
  - Drag Dot: "Leaves only one dab on the canvas which can be placed by dragging."
  - Anchored: a single dab at the brush location, resized by dragging.
  - Airbrush: "Flow of the brush continues as long as the mouse click is held (spray), determined by the Rate setting."
  - Line: dragging defines a straight line with dabs separated by Spacing; Alt constrains to 45-degree steps.
  - Curve: dabs follow an editable Bézier curve saved as a reusable "Paint Curve".
  - Jitter randomly offsets each dab; Stabilize Stroke smooths the path by making the stroke trail the cursor.
  — [Blender Manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/sculpt_paint/brush/stroke.html)
- **Nomad Sculpt.** Insert tool: "Place an object on the surface of another."; "In Instance mode, dragging will create and slide a new instance over the surface."; the size is copied from the first shape so "you can place many same-sized instances" over other surfaces. Instances are linked: "sculpting changes on one object will appear on all the instanced objects." Tube: "Create a tube by drawing a curve.", convertible "into a Curve Repeater", with an option to "add a mirror repeater as a parent of this curve". Stamp: drag adjusts size and rotation, described as suiting rivets — [Nomad Sculpt manual](https://nomadsculpt.com/manual/tools.html)
- **Figma Draw.** Vector brushes; "Dynamic strokes"; variable-width strokes; text on a path; repeat/transform tools for creating patterns from one element; multi-vector editing and lasso — [Figma Draw](https://www.figma.com/draw/)
- **Rhinestone template software (Stitch Era / Hotfix Era, tutorial dated 9 Jul 2019).** Vector shapes are converted into hotfix objects with "single hotfix fill" or "assorted hotfix fill"; fill types named are Uniform, Flexible, Flat, Hexagonal and an inner-ring fill; a "Manual Hotfix" tool adds individual stones; an "Edit hotfix" mode moves and deletes stones; overlaps are flagged and a "Solve overlap" option fixes them; stone sizes use SS numbers ("SS6 = 2mm", "SS20 = 5mm"); at least 1 mm spacing is recommended; text can be filled along the stroke, inside the shape or both; output is SVG/DXF for a cutter — [EduTech Wiki, University of Geneva](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Stitch_Era_-_creating_hot_fix_designs)
- Related production software: Wilcom EmbroideryStudio supports "up to eight (8) sequins and/or eight (8) beads per design" **(snippet)** — [Wilcom docs](https://docs.wilcom.com/embroiderystudio/28/en/OnlineHelp/Mixed/beading/beading-1.htm); TRW Design Wizard builds rhinestone templates, separating a design into one template per stone size and colour **(snippet)** — [The Rhinestone World](https://www.therhinestoneworld.com/Make-Rhinestone-Templates-with-TRW-Design-Wizard-for-CricutCameo_b_306.html)

#### What practitioners identify as the ingredients of a frictionless creative tool
- **Direct manipulation** is defined as an interaction style in which "users act on displayed objects of interest using physical, incremental, and reversible actions"; with continuous feedback "it's easy to check whether each action produced the right result"; it "often enhances users' sense of empowerment over the computer." Its known weakness is bulk and precision work: "If the user needs to perform many actions on many objects, using direct manipulation takes a lot longer" — [Nielsen Norman Group, 2016, reviewed Oct 2024](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/direct-manipulation/)
- **Exploration and undo:** "it must be very easy to try things out, and then backtrack when unsuccessful. This means that the tools must be trustworthy so that users are comfortable trying things. For example, a very good Undo capability is required in the tools." — [Resnick, Myers, Nakakoji, Shneiderman, Pausch, Selker, Eisenberg, "Design Principles for Tools to Support Creative Thinking", 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **Low viscosity:** "we want systems with 'low viscosity' – that make it easy to change all aspects of the design." — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **Self-revealing and pleasurable:** tools should be "'self-revealing' so that it is clear to users what can be done"; "tools must be pleasurable and fun to use. When people are stressed or concentrating too much effort on how to use the tools themselves, then they will have less cognitive resources left over for use on finding creative solutions to their tasks." — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **Partial effort, partial result:** "The goal is to allow a partial effort to get a partial result quickly." — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **Low threshold, high ceiling, wide walls:** "Effective tool designs should make it easy for novices to get started (low threshold) but also possible for experts to work on increasingly sophisticated projects (high ceiling)"; "The low threshold means that the interface should not be intimidating, and should give users immediate confidence that they can succeed." — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **Choice of primitives:** "one of the most important decisions is the choice of the 'primitive elements' that users will manipulate"; "the higher-level the primitives, the easier they are to use, but the less they can do." — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **Restraint:** "We have found that reducing the number of features can actually improve the user experience."; "Often, designs with well-chosen parameters are more successful than designs with fully-adjustable parameters." — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **A warning about CAD:** "Elaborated 3D CAD systems are found effective in helping architects to compose solutions but obstructive to their creative exploration" — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)
- **Diversity as the success test:** "If the creations are all similar to one another, we feel that something has gone wrong."; tools "should foster a community of users to share their creations" — [Resnick et al., 2005](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)

- **Direct manipulation alone is not enough for crystals.** NN/g's stated weakness (many actions on many objects) is exactly the heavy-crystal case, which is why the brief's "one at a time or with brushes" is the right split: single-click placement for accents, and brushes, paths and fills for volume.
- **Presets first, parameters second.** Resnick et al.'s point about well-chosen parameters, the Sims 4 swatch decision, and the configurators' locked palettes all point to curated fabric and crystal palettes as the default, with full control available underneath.
- **"Frictionless" in practice** = every action previewed instantly on the 3D garment, every action undoable, every placed element editable later, sensible defaults for symmetry and spacing, and a first result within the first minute.

## 7. The current custom-order workflow at leotard manufacturers, its pain points, and evidence of demand for self-serve 3D design

### Takeaway
Custom leotards are still mostly ordered through a human loop — enquiry, designer or sales-rep consultation, emailed concepts, revisions, sign-off, sizing kits, then 4 to 13 weeks of production — with team-only eligibility, minimums and non-returnable goods. Evidence of demand for self-serve design is indirect but real on the supply side (three leading brands have built design studios), while direct statements from coaches, booster clubs or parents asking for self-serve 3D design were not found.

### Inferences
- **Unmet needs implied by the workflow:** (1) the creative step is outsourced to the maker's designer, so the club's idea is filtered through emails and revision rounds; (2) lead times of 4–13 weeks plus growth-sensitive sizing compress the window in which design decisions can be made, so anything that shortens the design-and-approval phase has real value; (3) budget is negotiated after the design rather than seen during it; (4) the approved picture and the delivered garment can differ, and custom goods cannot be returned, so preview fidelity is a trust issue; (5) individuals and small groups are locked out of true custom design by team-only rules and minimums.
- From 13 October 2026 to Sylvia P's stated completion date of 14 January 2027 is 93 days, about 13 weeks — the longest lead time found.
- A self-serve tool does not remove the manufacturer; in every existing studio the design is handed to a rep or account manager. The realistic near-term role for a new tool is to produce a design the manufacturer can quote and make — which means outputs they can use (panel map, fabric list, stone count by size and colour, placement artwork).
- Demand evidence is supply-side and adjacent-market. It supports the claim that makers believe customers want to design for themselves, not the claim that coaches or parents are asking for a 3D tool.

### Gaps
- No first-hand coach, booster-club, gym-owner or parent statements could be read: Chalkbucket blocked access and Reddit/Facebook groups could not be searched after the budget ran out. The pain points above rest on two forum snippets and on makers' own messaging. This is the weakest-evidenced part of these notes.
- No data was found on typical numbers of revision rounds, the share of orders that are fully custom versus colour-changed stock designs, typical per-leotard budgets at club level in the US, or re-order rates.
- No explicit customer requests for self-serve 3D design, and no usage or conversion figures for UDesign or StudioSP, were found.
- GK's and Sylvia P's detailed production-time pages returned errors.
