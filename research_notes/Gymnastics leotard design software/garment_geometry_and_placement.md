## 1. Body models and avatars (parametric female body from small child to adult; licensing; measurement-driven morphing; rigging/posing)

### Takeaway
An openly licensed all-ages parametric body now exists — NAVER LABS Europe's **Anny** (Apache-2.0 code/model built on CC0 MakeHuman assets, continuous infant-to-elder age parameter, rigged, v0.6 released 6 Aug 2026) — and it is the only option I could verify that is both commercially usable and continuous from child to adult. The SMPL family is non-commercial by default, its child coverage is a patchwork (SMIL infants, AGORA "kid" interpolation, SMPL+A), and its commercial licensing is in flux after Epic Games' announced acquisition of Meshcapade (Feb 2026).

### Cited Findings

**Anny (open, all ages)**
- Anny is a scan-free, fully differentiable parametric body model; phenotype parameters (gender, age, height, weight, …) drive blendshapes spanning infants to elders; it is calibrated with WHO population statistics; authors claim millimetre-accurate scan fitting. Paper: Brégier, Fiche, Bravo-Sánchez, Lucas, Armando, Weinzaepfel, Rogez, Baradel, "Human Mesh Modeling for Anny Body", arXiv 2511.03589 (submitted 5 Nov 2025; v3 29 Jul 2026; listed as presented at ECCV 2026). — [arXiv 2511.03589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03589)
- Licensing: code Apache-2.0 (© 2025 NAVER Corp.); MakeHuman/MPFB2-derived assets and facial action units are CC0 1.0; the "soma" topology is Apache-2.0; the optional **"smplx" topology is a separate download restricted to non-commercial use**. — [github.com/naver/anny](https://github.com/naver/anny); [PyPI anny](https://pypi.org/project/anny/)
- Technical shape: PyTorch model; default "anny" rig has 104 bones, the full MakeHuman rig (163 bones) is selectable; topology options `anny` (triangulated default), `anny-quads`, `anny-full`, `makehuman`, `smplx`, `smpl`, `soma`; phenotype parameters are in [0,1], plus local shape changes in [-1,1] and facial-action blendshapes; output is vertices + faces; latest release v0.6 dated 2026-08-06 (v0.5 2026-06-03). — [github.com/naver/anny](https://github.com/naver/anny)

### Inferences
- **Recommendation: build on Anny's default topology** (not the "smplx" one). Reasons: permissive licence chain (Apache-2.0 + CC0), one continuous shape space from small child to adult (the product's whole size range), interpretable sliders (age/height/weight/muscle) that map naturally onto a size chart, a ready rig, and active maintenance (releases v0.5 and v0.6 in June and August 2026). MHR is equally permissive but its documentation says nothing about children; SMPL-family models would need a paid licence whose owner and terms are changing in 2026, and their child coverage is an interpolation hack.
- The model is linear blendshapes + linear blend skinning, so runtime evaluation is easy to port off PyTorch (WASM/GPU). Rough sizing if blendshapes were stored dense: 564 × ~13,000 vertices × 3 × 4 bytes ≈ 88 MB float32 — too heavy for a browser as-is, so either exploit sparsity/quantisation or bake a fixed set of bodies (one per size, per pose) offline and ship only those meshes.
- **Measurement-driven morphing**: because Anny is differentiable, target measurements (height, chest, waist, hip, torso girth, inseam…) can be fitted offline by least squares over the phenotype vector, with circumferences measured on the mesh; the result is a lookup table "size → phenotype vector", interpolated at runtime. This needs no runtime optimiser.
- **Fixed topology is the architectural keystone**: every size and pose is the same mesh connectivity with different vertex positions, so anything anchored as (face id, barycentric coordinates) transfers across sizes and poses for free (see section 7).
- Commercial fit avatars (e.g. Alvanon forms, or a factory's own fit-mannequin scan) can still be supported by registering them once to the canonical topology (non-rigid wrap) rather than by using them as the native body.
- WHO calibration grounds height/weight by age in the general population; competitive gymnasts are atypically short and muscular, so default sliders should be validated against real leotard size charts rather than trusted.
- Because the avatars depict minors, a neutral mannequin presentation (no skin detail, no facial realism) is the prudent default; it also sidesteps most asset-licensing questions.

## 2. Garment representation for skin-tight wear (is cloth simulation needed? offset surface vs cut mesh vs pattern-first simulation; tight-fit research; parametric garment programs)

### Takeaway
For a skin-tight leotard, research precedent supports treating the garment as living *on the body surface* — the main published method for skintight clothing (Montes et al., SIGGRAPH 2020) embeds the cloth mesh in the body surface precisely to avoid contact simulation — so a real-time design tool does not need cloth simulation in its interactive loop. Simulation earns its cost only for parts that leave the body (straps across hollows, skirts, gaping cutouts) and for fit feedback (pressure/strain), and can run asynchronously; pattern-first simulation as in CLO is the heaviest route and is unnecessary for the core workflow.

### Cited Findings

**Tight-fitting / stretch garment research**
- "Computational Design of Skintight Clothing" (Montes Maestre, Thomaszewski, Mudur, Popa; ACM TOG 39(4), art. 105, SIGGRAPH 2020; DOI 10.1145/3386569.3392477): an optimisation-driven, physics-based method that models the 2D cloth mesh as embedded in the 3D body surface (a "Lagrangian-on-Lagrangian" approach) which "eliminates contact handling while coupling cloth and body"; sensitivity analysis computes patterns meeting objectives for body shape, pressure distribution and seam traction; demonstrated for personalised patterns on different bodies and complex layouts. — [SIGGRAPH history archive](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=104289); [ETH research collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/items/0a5e5667-082a-4e42-93e9-fbad157fd4b1)
- "Computational Pattern Making from 3D Garment Models" (Pietroni, Dumery, Guenot-Falque, Liu, Vidal-Calleja, Sorkine-Hornung; ACM TOG 41(4), art. 157, 2022; DOI 10.1145/3528223.3530145; arXiv 2202.10272): segments a 3D garment into patches and parameterises each to obtain sewing-pattern pieces; uses a distortion measure modelling the anisotropy of woven fabric, keeps deformation within prescribed cloth-stress bounds via a patch-layout method adapted from quad remeshing; handles seam symmetry, darts and grain alignment; "fast enough to admit user input"; results for both skintight and loose garments; can account for several target poses. — [arXiv 2202.10272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10272); [UTS repository](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/159497)
- "Pattern computation for compression garment" (C. C. L. Wang, K. ### Inferences
- **Recommendation — hybrid of options (1) and (2), no simulation in the loop.** Source of truth: design curves and regions stored on the canonical body surface (option 1). Derived cache: a garment mesh cut along those curves, used for crisp seam rendering, region extraction and flattening (option 2), regenerated whenever curves change. Option 3 (pattern-first + drape simulation) is rejected for the core tool: it needs fabric parameters, a solver, collision handling and seconds-to-minutes of settling, to recover a shape that for a leotard is already known (the body).
- The "offset of the body" needs one refinement: real stretch fabric **bridges concavities** (sternum, lower-back hollow, underarm, gluteal cleft) instead of following them. A one-off "shrink-wrap" per body (smooth the body mesh while constraining it to stay outside the body, i.e. a tensioned envelope) gives a visually correct garment surface; it can be precomputed per size/pose because all bodies share topology. None of the cited papers' abstracts discuss bridging, so this is a design assumption to prototype.
- Where simulation adds real value, in priority order: (a) straps and lattice backs — a strap under tension follows a geodesic on convex regions but spans hollows as a straight chord, which a 1D rod/geodesic-with-lift-off solve handles far more cheaply than cloth; (b) cutouts/keyholes gaping under tension; (c) anything genuinely loose (attached skirts, drapes) — real cloth simulation; (d) fit feedback as pressure/strain maps (Laplace relation p = T/r gives a first-order estimate without simulation; Browzwear exposes this as a tension/pressure map). All four can run on demand or in a background worker.
- If simulation is added: XPBD is the pragmatic choice for an interactive tool (simple, unconditionally stable, strain limiting and long-range attachments are documented for inextensible/tight cloth); Jolt is the only verified permissively licensed engine with both soft bodies and a WASM build; Newton is strong but Python/NVIDIA-bound; ppf-contact-solver is offline quality validation, not interaction.
- **Document-model lesson from GarmentCode and FreeSewing**: sewing patterns work well as *programs with named components, interfaces and measurement parameters* serialised to JSON. For this product the analogue is "leotard = parametric base block (neckline depth, leg-line height, sleeve length, back style) + design curves + embellishment generators", all re-evaluable per size. FreeSewing's Onyx proves a measurement-driven leotard/unitard block can be generated in JavaScript under MIT.
- Lines need **types**, because dye-sublimated leotards decouple artwork from construction: *seam* (cut line, becomes pattern-piece boundary), *edge* (neckline, leg line, cutout boundary — bound with elastic), *print line* (colour boundary inside a printed panel, no seam), *strap centreline*. Colour regions and pattern pieces are therefore two different partitions of the same surface.

## 3. Design lines on a surface (curves on meshes, UV-space curves, cutting, intrinsic triangulations, region fills, cutouts, straps, symmetry)

### Takeaway
Curves should be stored as control points *on the surface* (face + barycentric) and evaluated as geodesic/subdivision splines: edge-flip geodesics (Sharp & Crane 2020) and b/Surf Bézier splines (Mancinelli et al. 2022) are both documented as interactive on meshes with millions of triangles — orders of magnitude beyond a body mesh. Regions bounded by curves can be obtained by explicit arrangement/cutting (BoolSurf, intrinsic common subdivision, mesh-insertion tools) or implicitly by surface winding numbers / signed geodesic distance, which tolerate gaps; UV-space curves are simpler to implement but inherit atlas seams and distortion.

### Cited Findings

**Geodesic paths and curve primitives**
- "You Can Find Geodesic Paths in Triangle Meshes by Just Flipping Edges" (Sharp, Crane; ACM TOG 39(6), art. 249, 2020): FlipOut straightens an edge path into a locally shortest geodesic in the same isotopy class without creating self-crossings, terminates in finitely many steps, runs in a few milliseconds even on meshes with millions of triangles; extends to closed loops, curve networks, and geodesic Bézier curves. — [NSF PAR record](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10216889)
- geometry-central's `FlipEdgeNetwork` implements it: Dijkstra-initialised paths, piecewise paths through marked vertices, closed loops, networks, `iterativeShorten()`, `bezierSubdivide(nRounds)`, `delaunayRefine()`, output as `SurfacePoint` sequences or 3D polylines; requires a manifold triangle mesh; guarantees no new crossings; "runs in milliseconds". Demo app is MIT-licensed. — [geometry-central flip geodesics](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/flip_geodesics/); [flip-geodesics-demo](https://github.com/nmwsharp/flip-geodesics-demo)
- Unreal Engine ships the same algorithm as `FDeformableEdgePath` (DynamicMesh plugin, docs for UE 5.8), noting its implementation does not support closed paths or multiple paths; results can be returned as surface points (vertex, edge crossing, or barycentric). — [Epic API docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/DynamicMesh/FDeformableEdgePath)
- "b/Surf: Interactive Bézier Splines on Surface Meshes" (Mancinelli, Nazzaro, Pellacini, Puppo; IEEE TVCG 29(7):3419–3435; DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2022.3171179; arXiv 2102.05921): direct generalisations of de Casteljau/Bernstein evaluation are fragile and discontinuous for large control polygons; subdivision schemes (recursive de Casteljau bisection, open-uniform Lane–Riesenfeld) are robust; supports curve tracing, point evaluation and point insertion, ordinary 2D editing interactions, and mapping SVG drawings onto meshes; interactive on meshes with millions of triangles; tested on all watertight manifold Thingi10k models. — [arXiv 2102.05921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05921); [project page](https://ggg.dibris.unige.it/papers/TVCG22_bSurf/TVCG22.html)
- "Vector Graphics on Surfaces Using Straightedge and Compass Constructions" (Mancinelli, Puppo; Computers & Graphics 2022; conference version STAG 2021, DOI 10.2312/stag.20211469): ports geometric constructions (circles, polygons, etc.) to surfaces under the geodesic metric and reports that many Euclidean properties cannot all be preserved at once. — [UniGe IRIS](https://iris.unige.it/handle/11567/1087411); [EG digital library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/stag20211469)
- "Stroke Parameterization" (R. Schmidt; CGF 32(2pt2):255–263, 2013) — bibliographic record confirmed only; the paper itself was not read this session (recalled as a method for parameterising the band around a stroke drawn on a mesh). — [DOI 10.1111/cgf.12045](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12045)
- Geodesic distance choices documented in geometry-central: exact polyhedral MMP (Kirsanov implementation; also returns shortest paths, log maps and gradients; no reusable precomputation), Fast Marching (Kimmel & Sethian 1998; supports signed distance to curves, more efficient than the signed heat method for closed curves), and the Heat Method (approximate, fastest for repeated solves after prefactorisation). — [geometry-central geodesic distance](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/geodesic_distance/)
- The Heat Method (Crane, Weischedel, Wardetzky; CACM 60(11):90–99, 2017) needs two sparse linear systems that "can be factored once and subsequently solved in near-linear time". — [CMU project page](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kmcrane/Projects/HeatMethod/)
- Survey: Crane, Livesu, Puppo, Qin, "A Survey of Algorithms for Geodesic Paths and Distances" (arXiv 2007.10430, 2020). — [arXiv 2007.10430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.10430)

**Regions, booleans, inside/outside**
- BoolSurf (Riso, Nazzaro, Puppo, Jacobson, Zhou, Pellacini; ACM TOG 41(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2022; DOI 10.1145/3550454.3555466): boolean set operations between shapes drawn on surfaces of any genus (including open boundaries) by computing and labelling the arrangement of shapes; flags inconsistent arrangements; interactive for up to 100k line segments on meshes of up to 1M triangles. — [Adobe Research](https://research.adobe.com/publication/boolsurf-boolean-operations-on-surfaces); [UniGe IRIS](https://iris.unige.it/handle/11567/1106424)
- "Winding Numbers on Discrete Surfaces" (Feng, Gillespie, Crane; ACM TOG 42(4), art. 36, 2023; DOI 10.1145/3592401): given curves on a surface (possibly with gaps or self-intersections) it determines which bound regions, outputs integer region labels and a completed curve; cost is a Poisson solve, or a sparse linear program on surfaces with non-trivial topology. Code is MIT, but the main branch needs the commercial Gurobi solver (an OR-Tools branch exists). — [SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=132358); [github.com/nzfeng/SWN](https://github.com/nzfeng/SWN)
- "A Heat Method for Generalized Signed Distance" (Feng, Crane; ACM TOG 43(4), art. 92, 2024; DOI 10.1145/3658220), implemented as geometry-central's `SignedHeatSolver`: signed distance to oriented, possibly broken curves on a surface (unsigned for points/unoriented curves), with level-set constraints and shared lazy precomputation. — [geometry-central signed heat method](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/signed_heat_method/)

**Cutting a mesh along curves; intrinsic triangulations**
- geometry-central represents a curve vertex as a `SurfacePoint`: a vertex, an edge + parameter t, or a face + barycentric coordinates, with value interpolation helpers. — [geometry-central SurfacePoint](https://geometry-central.net/surface/utilities/surface_point/)
- Intrinsic triangulations: the *signpost* structure traces intrinsic edges over the input surface but is sensitive to floating-point error; *integer coordinates* (normal coordinates + roundabouts) are more robust and always maintain a topologically valid common subdivision; the *common subdivision* is the explicit polygon mesh obtained by slicing one triangulation along the other, and represents piecewise-linear functions of both exactly. — [signposts](https://geometry-central.net/surface/intrinsic_triangulations/signposts/); [integer coordinates](https://geometry-central.net/surface/intrinsic_triangulations/integer_coordinates/); [common subdivision](https://geometry-central.net/surface/intrinsic_triangulations/common_subdivision/); [arXiv 2106.00220](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00220)
- CGAL's `Seam_mesh` marks a set of edges as a cut graph and virtually duplicates seam vertices by storing UVs per halfedge, so a mesh can be parameterised as a disk without physically cutting it. — [CGAL surface mesh parameterization](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Surface_mesh_parameterization/index.html)
- gradientspace's geometry3Sharp (C#, Boost licence; long hiatus, .NET 8 update in progress) has ready-made curve-insertion tools: `MeshInsertUVPolyCurve`, `MeshInsertPolygon`, `MeshInsertProjectedPolygon`, `MeshFacesFromLoop` (faces enclosed by an embedded loop), `MeshTrimLoop`, plus constrained remeshers. — [github.com/gradientspace/geometry3Sharp](https://github.com/gradientspace/geometry3Sharp)
- "Variational Surface Cutting" (Sharp, Crane; ACM TOG 2018) optimises cut curves that are not restricted to mesh edges. — [CMU RI record](https://ri.cmu.edu/publications/variational-surface-cutting)

**UV-space placement caveats (from tool documentation)**
- Houdini's texture-space scattering keeps points consistent through cuts, subdivision or mesh replacement as long as UVs correspond, but "seams may behave worse" and distortion in the UV map changes physical density. — [Houdini Scatter SOP](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/scatter.html)
- Blender's Sample UV Surface performs a reverse UV lookup and fails (returns default values) where UV faces overlap or no face exists. — [Blender manual, Sample UV Surface](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/mesh/sample/sample_uv_surface.html)

### Inferences
- **Recommendation — curve model.** Store each design line as an ordered list of surface control points (canonical face id + barycentrics) plus tangent handles or a subdivision rule; evaluate to a polyline that has a vertex on every mesh edge it crosses. Start with *piecewise geodesics through control points + corner rounding* (flip geodesics), and add b/Surf-style Bézier subdivision for smooth pen-tool curves. Both are far inside interactive budgets for a ~13k–100k-vertex body.
- **Evaluate on one reference body, transfer by barycentrics.** Geodesics differ slightly per size and pose; re-solving per size would make region topology size-dependent. Evaluate curves once on a reference shape, then carry the polyline to every size/pose through the shared topology; allow optional per-size re-straightening where a truly shortest path matters (straps).
- **Regions (colour blocks, panels).** Explicit arrangement is the right default because crisp seams, pattern extraction and flattening all need a mesh that conforms to the curves: insert all curve polylines into a working copy of the mesh (split edges/faces), flood-fill faces between curves, and label cells. Use winding-number or signed-distance labelling only as a fallback for almost-closed regions (the published winding-number code needs an LP solver on non-simply-connected surfaces, and a leotard surface with neck/arm/leg openings is such a surface). Keep the cut mesh as a derived cache so the curves stay the editable truth.
- **Cutouts and edges** are simply regions flagged "removed"; their boundary curves become *edge* lines (elastic binding). **Straps** are a centreline curve plus width: build the band either as the set of points with |signed geodesic distance| < w/2 (signed heat / fast marching), or by offsetting the polyline in a stroke parameterisation; where the strap leaves the body it should become a separate ribbon mesh between anchor points.
- **Mirror symmetry** should be topological, not geometric: precompute the left↔right map of the canonical mesh (face permutation + barycentric swap) and derive mirrored curves/stones through it; this stays exact in asymmetric poses. Curves crossing the centre line need a "weld at mid-line" rule.
- **UV-space curves**: attractive because ordinary 2D Bézier tooling, polygon booleans (Clipper2) and rasterisation work unchanged, but straight UV lines are not geodesics, widths vary with parametric stretch, and any curve crossing an atlas seam must be split and re-joined. Use a stable front/back "technical flat" atlas as a *view and input device* that converts to surface points, not as the storage format.

### Gaps
- Public code locations and licences for b/Surf and BoolSurf were not confirmed (the b/Surf project page failed TLS; a guessed BoolSurf repo URL returned 404). Both build on Yocto/GL (MIT), but the current Yocto/GL README no longer lists a mesh/geodesic module — check before relying on it.
- Not verified this session: libigl's `cut_mesh`/exact-geodesic wrappers, CGAL Surface_mesh_shortest_path and corefinement, MeshLib's contour-cutting and surface-path functions (the README confirms only booleans/offsets), Panozzo et al. "Weighted Averages on Surfaces", Polthier & Schmies "Straightest Geodesics", Unreal's `FIntrinsicTriangulation`.
- No quantitative comparison was found of surface-curve methods at body-mesh scale (10⁴–10⁵ triangles) in WASM.
- How existing garment tools represent curves that cross UV seams is undocumented in the sources reached.

## 4. Parameterisation and pattern flattening (methods, distortion, stretch/negative ease, accuracy, seam allowance, UV atlas vs production pieces, print pre-distortion)

### Takeaway
Flattening a leotard panel is a solved, fast computation — cut to a disk, initialise with a linear conformal map (LSCM or Boundary First Flattening), refine with an isometric energy (ARAP or SLIM) — and all of it is interactive at garment-piece scale. What is *not* solved by generic geometry processing is the stretch-fabric part: production pieces must be smaller than the body by an anisotropic negative ease (trade guidance: 15–20% in width, 0–5% in length for four-way stretch), and only garment-specific research (Montes 2020; Wang & Tang 2008/2010; Pietroni 2022) models fabric behaviour. No published accuracy figures for leotard-class patterns were found.

### Cited Findings

**General parameterisation methods**
- LSCM: Lévy, Petitjean, Ray, Maillot, "Least squares conformal maps for automatic texture atlas generation", ACM TOG 21(3):362–371, 2002. — [DOI 10.1145/566654.566590](https://doi.org/10.1145/566654.566590)
- ABF++: Sheffer, Lévy, Mogilnitsky, Bogomyakov, "ABF++: fast and robust angle based flattening", ACM TOG 24(2):311–330, 2005. — [DOI 10.1145/1061347.1061354](https://doi.org/10.1145/1061347.1061354)
- ARAP parameterisation: Liu, Zhang, Xu, Gotsman, Gortler, "A Local/Global Approach to Mesh Parameterization", CGF 27(5):1495–1504, 2008. — [DOI 10.1111/j.1467-8659.2008.01290.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2008.01290.x)
- Smith, Schaefer, "Bijective parameterization with free boundaries", ACM TOG 34(4), 2015 — bibliographic record confirmed; recalled (not re-read) as the source of the symmetric Dirichlet energy that SLIM optimises. — [DOI 10.1145/2766947](https://doi.org/10.1145/2766947)
- SLIM (Rabinovich, Poranne, Panozzo, Sorkine-Hornung; ACM TOG 2017): optimises flip-preventing energies such as symmetric Dirichlet by iterating proxy problems that extend ARAP's local/global scheme; converges in about 40 iterations, each a sparse linear solve; starts from a locally injective map and guarantees no inverted elements; scales to millions of faces (a 25M-triangle example took 80 minutes); reference implementation in libigl (`igl/slim.h`). — [SLIM project page](https://igl.ethz.ch/projects/slim/)
- Blender 4.3 (2024) added a "Minimum Stretch" unwrap using SLIM (contributed by Łukasz Czyż, completing work by Aurel Gruber); iterations trade quality for time (5 already beat the older methods, little change beyond ~50), it works even without seams, and a vertex group can weight relative UV area. — [Blender 4.3 manual, UV operators](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.3/modeling/meshes/editing/uv.html); [CG Channel, 25 Sep 2024](https://www.cgchannel.com/2024/09/check-out-blender-4-3s-new-slim-uv-unwrapping-system); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/blender-received-new-uv-unwrapping-algorithm/)
- Boundary First Flattening (Sawhney, Crane; ACM TOG 37(1), art. 5, 2017): linear conformal flattening with direct control of boundary lengths *or* corner angles, automatic minimal-area-distortion mode, cone singularities, disk/sphere maps; described as allowing "interactive editing of meshes with millions of triangles"; application is MIT-licensed (v1.6, May 2023; CLI depends on SuiteSparse); also in geometry-central (requires disk topology; runs on intrinsic geometry). — [BFF repository](https://github.com/GeometryCollective/boundary-first-flattening); [geometry-central parameterization](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/parameterization/); [arXiv 1704.06873](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.06873)
- CGAL's package documents the classical menu and guarantees: fixed-border Tutte, Floater mean-value (default), discrete conformal, discrete authalic (one-to-one when the border is convex), iterative authalic; free-border LSCM (≥2 pinned vertices, no bijectivity guarantee) and ARAP (local/global, λ balances angle vs shape, flips possible); orbifold Tutte for spheres. — [CGAL surface mesh parameterization](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Surface_mesh_parameterization/index.html)

**Cut optimisation**
- AutoCuts (Poranne, Tarini, Huber, Panozzo, Sorkine-Hornung; ACM TOG 36(6), 2017) optimises distortion and cuts simultaneously and is most effective interactively with a user in the loop. — [DOI 10.1145/3130800.3130845](https://doi.org/10.1145/3130800.3130845); [OptCuts supplement](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2018/OptCuts/doc/OptCuts_supp.pdf)
- OptCuts (Li, Kaufman, Kim, Solomon, Sheffer; ACM TOG 37(6), 2018; DOI 10.1145/3272127.3275042) minimises seam length subject to a user distortion bound, yields globally bijective maps, and can honour user seam-placement preferences. — [OptCuts project page](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2018/OptCuts/)
- Later work reports that joint methods are slower than other cutting methods and can get stuck in local minima with long cuts (UV-AT, Weill–Duflos, Coeurjolly, de Goes, Lachaud 2023; Zhu et al. 2020 greedy cut construction). — [UV-AT](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/david.coeurjolly/publication/uv-at/); [Zhu et al., CGF 39(2)](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.1111/cgf13923/v39i2pp191-202.pdf)

**Garment-specific flattening and stretch**
- Pietroni et al. 2022 use a woven-fabric anisotropic distortion measure with bounded cloth stress and tailoring constraints (seam symmetry, darts, grain), with skintight examples. — [arXiv 2202.10272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10272)
- Montes et al. 2020 compute patterns for stretched tight garments by physics-based optimisation with pressure and seam-traction objectives. — [SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=104289)
- Bartle et al. 2016 show a fixed-point iteration that adjusts 2D patterns until the simulated result matches a 3D target. — [UBC project page](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2016/PhysicsDrivenPatternAdjustment)
- Trade guidance for body-wear pattern cutting (M. Müller & Sohn, "Pattern Cutting for Body Fashion", E. Küpper, 12 Jun 2022): for high-stretch or four-way-stretch fabrics deduct as a percentage in both directions — "15 to 20% of the width and 0 to 5% of the length"; example "10% stretch = scale 1:0.9"; scale width and length separately; verify per material because some fabrics lose length when stretched in width; for mixed materials draft to body measurements and reduce each piece by its own fabric's stretch factor. — [M. Müller & Sohn](https://www.muellerundsohn.com/en/allgemein/cutting-technique-body-fashion-for-day-and-night)
- Supporting evidence that reduction should depend on measured fabric stretch per direction: US patent 4,894,919 applies length and width reductions dictated by course- and wale-direction stretch of the specific knit; a Korean knit-bodice study found the appropriate reduction differed by wale/course stretch rate; Watkins' pattern-reduction method has been analysed in the literature. — [US4894919](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4894919); [KoreaScience](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO199811919992435.page); [ModaPalavra study of Watkins' method](https://www.revistas.udesc.br/index.php/modapalavra/article/view/19654)
- CLO's avatar-drawn Flatten yields zero-ease patterns; CLO lets a drawn line be flagged to flatten as a straight edge (e.g. centre front/back); one user test on a cube measured about 3.3 mm discrepancy between source face and flattened pattern. — [CLO Flatten article](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008890627-Flatten); [CLO 3D Pen (Avatar) article](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000344167); [CLO community: flatten tool offset](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/ja/community/posts/360039878693-Flatten-tool-offset)

### Inferences
- **Recommended pipeline per pattern piece** (all steps sub-second for pieces of 10³–10⁴ triangles, by extrapolation from the cited scalability claims): (1) take the region bounded by *seam* and *edge* lines from the cut mesh; (2) conformal initialisation — LSCM (one sparse least-squares solve) or BFF; (3) isometric refinement — ARAP, or SLIM with symmetric Dirichlet when flip-freedom must be guaranteed; (4) stretch compensation; (5) seam-length reconciliation between mating edges; (6) seam allowance by 2D polygon offset (Clipper2), notches and grain line; (7) export.
- **Stretch compensation, three levels.** *Level 1 (industry-equivalent):* scale the flattened piece anisotropically along fabric course/wale axes by measured fabric factors (e.g. ×0.80–0.85 width, ×0.95–1.0 length), exposed as per-fabric presets. *Level 2:* flatten against a *target stretch tensor* instead of the identity (ARAP/SLIM with the rest metric pre-scaled), so the solver distributes strain while respecting seams. *Level 3:* physics-based optimisation in the spirit of Montes et al. (pressure and seam-traction objectives) run offline for premium "pattern-making" output. Level 1 matches what pattern cutters do today and should be the first release.
- For stretch garments the flattening distortion criterion differs from woven tailoring: the fabric must be in tension everywhere (strain > 0, or it wrinkles) and below a comfortable maximum, in each of the two knit directions. Double curvature (bust, seat) is absorbed by stretch rather than darts, so a few percent of residual geometric distortion is immaterial next to the roughly 18–25% working strain implied by a 15–20% width reduction (1/0.85 − 1 to 1/0.80 − 1). The dominant error source is therefore the material model and the chosen ease, not the flattening algorithm.
- By Laplace's relation (p = T/r), a uniform percentage reduction produces higher pressure on small-radius parts (arms, small children's torsos); ease percentages may need to vary by size and by body region. This should be validated with manufacturers.
- **Alternative production route — map onto the factory's existing graded blocks** instead of inventing pattern shapes: parameterise each body region so that its boundary matches the factory block's outline (BFF's direct control of boundary lengths/angles, or a fixed-boundary map), then resample artwork and stone positions into the block's coordinates. This keeps proven fit and grading in the factory's hands and may be the more realistic first integration.
- **Two parameterisations, kept separate.** (a) A *stable design atlas*, fixed for the canonical body topology and independent of any design, used for raster artwork storage, the 2D technical-flat view and anchor fallbacks. (b) A *production atlas* = the flattened, stretch-compensated pattern pieces, regenerated whenever seams change. Artwork moves between them by resampling through the 3D surface.
- **Print pre-distortion falls out of the mapping**: artwork authored to look right on the body is resampled through (pattern piece → body surface) into the smaller unstretched piece, so printing it flat and stretching it on the wearer restores the intended appearance — exactly to the extent that the assumed strain field matches reality. With a 15–20% width reduction, a circle drawn on the body prints as an ellipse about 0.80–0.85× as wide.
- **Stones and stretch interact**: stones are rigid, so positions compress on the flat panel while diameters do not. With 15–20% width reduction, stones designed at pitch *d* on the body sit at 0.80–0.85·*d* on the relaxed fabric; any design whose as-worn gap is under roughly 15–20% of the as-worn pitch (equivalently about 18–25% of the stone diameter) in the stretch direction will touch or overlap when the garment is off the body. The tool should run its overlap check in *both* states.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy numbers (mm or % error) were found for 3D-to-2D flattening of stretch garments; the only figure is an anecdotal 3.3 mm from a CLO forum test on a cube.
- Negative-ease guidance comes from one trade source for lingerie/body wear, not gymnastics leotards; fabric-specific values (e.g. for mystique/foil, velvet, mesh) were not found.
- Pattern exchange formats were not verified (ASTM returned 403): unverified leads are ASTM D6673 / DXF-AAMA and DXF-ASTM as the interchange standard, plus PLT/HPGL for plotters.

- Seam-allowance corner conventions and notch placement rules for stretch sewing were not researched.

## 5. Painting and placement on surfaces (UV painting and seams, per-face textures, local parameterisations for decals/stamps; libraries for native, JS/WASM and Rust)

### Takeaway
For stamping motifs that follow the surface with minimal distortion, a *local geodesic parameterisation centred on the stamp* (logarithmic/exponential map) is the right tool, and it is well supported: geometry-central exposes three log-map strategies including the 2025 Affine Heat Method ("fast & highly accurate near source"), exact MMP log maps, and geodesic tracing for the inverse direction. Per-face texture schemes (Ptex, Mesh Colors) remove UV seams for raster paint but are unnecessary for instanced stones. The mature libraries are C++ (geometry-central MIT, libigl MPL-2.0, pmp-library MIT; CGAL is GPL/commercial); production-grade JavaScript/WASM and Rust equivalents were not found.

### Cited Findings

**Local parameterisations for stamps/decals**
- "Interactive decal compositing with discrete exponential maps" (Schmidt, Grimm, Wyvill; ACM TOG 25(3):605–613, 2006) — bibliographic record confirmed; abstract not retrieved this session (recalled as the paper that introduced Dijkstra-style propagated exponential maps for placing decals on meshes). — [DOI 10.1145/1141911.1141930](https://doi.org/10.1145/1141911.1141930)
- geometry3Sharp's `MeshLocalParam` "computes discrete exponential map UV coordinates around a point on a mesh" (Boost licence). — [github.com/gradientspace/geometry3Sharp](https://github.com/gradientspace/geometry3Sharp)
- The Vector Heat Method (Sharp, Soliman, Crane; ACM TOG 38(3), 2019) computes parallel transport by short-time heat flow and from it the logarithmic map (geodesic polar coordinates about a point), Karcher means and geodesic Voronoi tessellations. — [arXiv 1805.09170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.09170)
- The Affine Heat Method (Soliman, Sharp; CGF 44(5), SGP 2025, best paper) computes log maps with an augmented connection Laplacian that yields direction and distance together; one variant allows precomputation for fast repeated solves, the other resolves the map in detail even near the cut locus. — [EG digital library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf70205); [author page](https://www.yousufsoliman.com/projects/the-affine-heat-method.html)
- geometry-central's `VectorHeatSolver::computeLogMap` offers strategies `VectorHeat` ("fast, but may have some distortion and warping"), `AffineLocal` ("fast & highly accurate near source", very regular coordinates, may need an intrinsic Delaunay triangulation) and `AffineAdaptive` (highest quality, must refactor a matrix per solve); sources may be arbitrary surface points; running on an intrinsic Delaunay triangulation makes log maps "dramatically more accurate". — [geometry-central vector heat method](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/vector_heat_method/)
- Exact polyhedral (MMP) geodesics in geometry-central also return log maps (`getLog`). — [geometry-central geodesic distance](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/geodesic_distance/)
- Newest research (preprint, 9 Sep 2026): iLogMap (Banduc, Pezzuto, Sahli Costabal) recasts the angular part of the log map as a magnetic-Laplacian eigenproblem and claims competitive angular accuracy with reduced metric distortion versus heat-based methods, including anisotropic metrics; code availability unconfirmed. — [arXiv 2609.10503](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10503)
- geometry-central also provides geodesic tracing ("Tracing Geodesic Paths"), geodesic centroidal Voronoi tessellations (vector-heat-based, on an intrinsic Delaunay triangulation by default) and stripe patterns. — [geometry-central index](https://geometry-central.net/); [GCVT](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/geodesic_voronoi_tessellations/)
- "Stripe Patterns on Surfaces" (Knöppel, Crane, Pinkall, Schröder; ACM TOG 34(4), SIGGRAPH 2015) computes globally continuous, evenly spaced stripes aligned to a direction field with controllable (even spatially varying) spacing, via one sparse eigenvector problem; branch points are inserted automatically. — [CMU project page](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kmcrane/Projects/StripePatterns/)

**Per-face texture schemes (seam-free raster paint)**
- Ptex (Walt Disney Animation Studios): a separate texture per face, "no UV assignment is required"; BSD-3-style licence (© Disney Enterprises 2014). — [github.com/wdas/ptex](https://github.com/wdas/ptex); [Ptex LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wdas/ptex/main/LICENSE)
- Mesh Colors (Yuksel, Keyser, House; ACM TOG 29(2), 2010) store colours on vertices, edges and faces with no mapping, hence no seams, with local resolution changes and editing after painting; follow-ups address GPU filtering (Mesh Color Textures, HPG 2017; hardware-filtering and Patch Textures papers). — [Mesh Colors page](http://www.cemyuksel.com/research/meshcolors/)

**Path/stamp tooling in a production painter**
- Substance 3D Painter 9.0 (June 2023) added a Path tool: Bézier guide curves placed by clicking the model's surface; the stroke follows the curve; both the curve and the brush properties stay editable; closed curves give seamlessly repeating patterns; works with symmetry and on masks; presets for stitches, zippers, welds and seams. — [CG Channel, 21 Jun 2023](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/adobe-releases-substance-3d-painter-9-0/)

**Library landscape**
- geometry-central: MIT, modern C++ on Eigen, CMake; halfedge mesh, intrinsic triangulations, heat/vector-heat/signed-heat methods, flip geodesics, geodesic tracing, BFF, Poisson-disk surface sampling, GCVT, stripes; CI on Linux/macOS/Windows. — [repository](https://github.com/nmwsharp/geometry-central); [docs](https://geometry-central.net/)
- potpourri3d: MIT Python bindings to geometry-central (heat distance, signed heat, fast marching, vector heat/log map, edge-flip geodesics, geodesic tracing, point-cloud variants); `pip install potpourri3d`. — [github.com/nmwsharp/potpourri3d](https://github.com/nmwsharp/potpourri3d)
- libigl: MPL-2.0 core (see section 4); CGAL: GPL/commercial (see section 4).
- pmp-library: MIT; decimation, remeshing, subdivision, smoothing, curvature, geodesic distances, hole filling, parameterisation; explicitly supports cross-compilation to JavaScript and hosts a browser demo. — [github.com/pmp-library/pmp-library](https://github.com/pmp-library/pmp-library)
- geometry-processing-js (CMU Geometry Collective): MIT; pure-JS mesh framework with Eigen compiled to asm.js via Emscripten and three.js viewers; positioned for "mobile apps, online demos, and course content". — [github.com/GeometryCollective/geometry-processing-js](https://github.com/GeometryCollective/geometry-processing-js)
- MeshLib: source-available, free for non-commercial/educational use, paid licence for commercial use; C++ core with C, C#, Python and JavaScript/WebAssembly APIs; exact and voxel booleans, offsets, projection/ray queries (v3.1.3.429, 2026). — [github.com/MeshInspector/MeshLib](https://github.com/MeshInspector/MeshLib)

### Inferences
- **Recommendation for stamps (spirals, motifs, single stones with a footprint):** define the motif in a 2D tangent plane at the stamp centre and push each stone to the surface with the **exponential map** — trace a geodesic from the centre in direction θ for length r. It is exact along rays, costs only the triangles crossed per stone, needs no linear solve, and is trivial to port to JS/WASM. Use a **log map** (AffineLocal or exact MMP within a radius) when the opposite direction is needed — e.g. to texture a decal, to test which existing stones fall inside a stamp footprint, or to drag a whole motif while preserving its layout.
- Distortion behaviour: geodesic polar maps are exact radially and distort circumferentially in proportion to the Gaussian curvature enclosed, so they are excellent for motifs small relative to body curvature and degrade for large motifs wrapping bust/hip. For large fills, parameterise the region itself (LSCM/ARAP patch, or a stripe-pattern/field-aligned parameterisation) and follow with a few relaxation steps.
- **Raster painting is secondary for this product.** Colour blocks and gradients are better stored as vector regions and procedural fills; imported artwork is a decal resampled via the stable design atlas. Ptex/Mesh Colors solve seams elegantly offline, but neither has standard GPU texture-filtering support in WebGL/WebGPU pipelines, so a conventional UV atlas with seam padding is the practical choice for any raster layer.
- **Platform strategy.** Native: geometry-central + libigl cover nearly every algorithm in sections 3–6 under permissive licences; avoid CGAL unless buying a commercial licence. Browser: no production-grade JS geometry-processing library was verified, so either compile the C++ stack to WebAssembly (pmp-library documents JS cross-compilation; geometry-processing-js shows Eigen under Emscripten) or hand-implement the small kernel actually needed — halfedge mesh, BVH, geodesic tracing, Dijkstra/flip geodesics, one sparse Cholesky solve for LSCM/ARAP. Rust: mesh-processing crates verified here lack geodesics and parameterisation, so a Rust core would mean writing them or binding C++.

### Gaps
- Not verified this session: Htex (guessed repository URL returned 404), Volume-encoded UV maps, seam-erasure methods, UDIM conventions, texture-space dilation practice in painters.
- Whether geometry-central or libigl have maintained WebAssembly builds or npm packages is unconfirmed; sparse-solver performance in WASM was not measured.
- Unverified Rust leads: `spade` (Delaunay/CDT), `lyon` and `kurbo` (2D paths), `bvh`, `meshopt`, `i_overlay`; no Rust implementation of heat-method geodesics or LSCM was confirmed.
- The magnitude of exp-map distortion on body-scale curvature (e.g. a 10 cm spiral over the bust on a child size) was not quantified.
- How Substance Painter internally parameterises strokes across UV seams is not documented in the sources reached (Adobe HelpX blocked).

## 6. Instance-placement algorithms for brush tools (arc-length sampling, thick strokes, spirals, Poisson-disk and gradient scatter, region fills, size gradation, overlap avoidance, relaxation, snapping, symmetry, stroke smoothing, non-destructive editing; pavé and rhinestone-template software)

### Takeaway
Every brush reduces to three steps: (1) a curve or region on the surface, (2) a sampler — 1D arc-length, a 2D lattice or blue-noise set in a local parameterisation — and (3) collision culling plus optional relaxation. All building blocks are published and cheap: Bridson's O(N) Poisson-disk sampling (with a surface implementation in geometry-central), weighted sample elimination for variable density, Vogel's phyllotaxis formula, the 0.9069 hexagonal packing bound, and Houdini-style tangent-space relaxation. Storing the stroke and deriving the stones (non-destructive) has direct precedent in Substance Painter's path tool. I could not verify how jewellery pavé tools or rhinestone-template packages implement automatic packing.

### Cited Findings

**Blue-noise / Poisson-disk sampling**
- Bridson, "Fast Poisson Disk Sampling in Arbitrary Dimensions" (SIGGRAPH 2007 sketch): background grid with cell size r/√n so each cell holds at most one sample; active list; for a random active sample generate up to k (typically 30) candidates in the annulus r–2r and accept those farther than r from existing samples; O(N) total. — [paper PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/bridson-siggraph07-poissondisk.pdf)
- geometry-central's `PoissonDiskSampler` applies the Bridson scheme on surface meshes: options `minDist`, `kCandidates` (30), `pointsToAvoid` with `minDistAvoidance`, and a switch between 3D-ball and geodesic-ball avoidance; spacing is measured in 3D (extrinsic) distance, with the doc noting geodesic distance is the theoretical ideal and that large 3D radii can suppress samples on the opposite side of thin parts; output is `SurfacePoint`s; manifold meshes only (relies on geodesic tracing). — [geometry-central surface sampling](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/surface_sampling/)
- Yuksel, "Sample Elimination for Generating Poisson Disk Sample Sets" (CGF 34(2):25–32, EG 2015): weighted elimination from an oversampled set down to a target count, no radius needed, works in any sampling domain including surfaces, supports progressive/adaptive sampling; code in cyCodeBase. — [project page](http://www.cemyuksel.com/research/sampleelimination/)
- Corsini, Cignoni, Scopigno, "Efficient and Flexible Sampling with Blue Noise Properties of Triangular Meshes" (IEEE TVCG, 2012). — [VCG publication page](http://vcg.isti.cnr.it/publication/2012/CCS12/)
- Blender Geometry Nodes "Distribute Points on Faces": Random or Poisson Disk; Poisson mode uses *Distance Min* (minimum spacing) and *Density Max*, multiplied by a density factor/attribute; point, corner and face attributes (weights, UVs) are transferred to the points; outputs normal and rotation. — [Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/point/distribute_points_on_faces.html)
- Houdini Scatter SOP: density by scale/attribute (paintable), exact counts via *Force Total Count*, *Relax Iterations* that push points apart toward blue noise using a per-point radius derived from density, with relaxation on surfaces alternating between a 2D space perpendicular to the normal and projection back to the surface; *Max Relax Radius* caps low-density radii; per-primitive seed attribute for stability when topology changes; optional scattering in texture space; outputs source primitive number, primitive UVW and a persistent unique id. — [Houdini Scatter SOP](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/scatter.html)

**Lattices, packings and field-aligned layouts**
- The densest packing of equal circles in the plane is hexagonal, density π/(2√3) ≈ 0.9069 (Lagrange 1773 for lattices; Thue 1890; rigorous proof Fejes Tóth 1942); mixtures of two circle sizes cannot beat this when the radius ratio exceeds 0.742; on the sphere the analogous problem is the Tammes problem. — [Wikipedia: Circle packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_packing)
- Phyllotaxis/sunflower layout: Vogel's model r = c·√n, θ = n·137.508° (golden angle); H. Vogel, "A better way to construct the sunflower head", Mathematical Biosciences 44(3–4):179–189, 1979. — [Wikipedia: Fermat's spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat%27s_spiral)
- "Packing circles and spheres on surfaces" (Schiftner, Höbinger, Wallner, Pottmann; ACM SIGGRAPH proceedings paper, c. 2009): introduces "circle packing" (CP) triangle meshes whose face incircles form a packing, obtained by optimisation, giving hexagon-like circle arrangements covering freeform surfaces. — [paper PDF](https://www.geometrie.tugraz.at/wallner/packing.pdf)
- Instant Field-Aligned Meshes (Jakob, Tarini, Panozzo, Sorkine-Hornung; SIGGRAPH Asia 2015): local smoothing of an orientation field then a position field yields a near-isotropic triangular or quad-dominant sample lattice aligned to features/strokes; brush tools edit the fields; full pipeline under a second on meshes with hundreds of thousands of faces. — [project page](https://igl.ethz.ch/projects/instant-meshes/); [repository](https://github.com/wjakob/instant-meshes)
- Stripe Patterns on Surfaces gives evenly spaced, direction-aligned stripes with controllable spacing from one sparse eigenproblem (see section 5). — [CMU project page](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kmcrane/Projects/StripePatterns/)
- Geodesic centroidal Voronoi tessellation (Lloyd-type relaxation on surfaces) is available in geometry-central (`nSites`, `iterations` default 50). — [geometry-central GCVT](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/geodesic_voronoi_tessellations/)

**Neighbour queries**
- Teschner, Heidelberger, Müller, Pomeranerts, Gross, "Optimized Spatial Hashing for Collision Detection of Deformable Objects" (VMV 2003). — [M. Müller publications page](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/publications.html)

**Stroke smoothing / stabilisation**
- Krita documents four modes: none, basic, *weighted* (distance, stroke ending, smooth pressure) and *stabilizer* (averages inputs, line trails the cursor, optional dead-zone "delay" for sharp corners, finish-line option; sample counts at min/max speed since 5.3). — [Krita manual, freehand brush](https://docs.krita.org/en/reference_manual/tools/freehand_brush.html)
- The 1€ filter (Casiez, Roussel, Vogel; CHI 2012, pp. 2527–2530) is a speed-adaptive low-pass filter with two parameters (minimum cutoff for jitter, beta for lag), with implementations in JavaScript, TypeScript, C++, Python and many others. — [1€ filter page](https://gery.casiez.net/1euro/)

**Non-destructive precedent and jewellery tooling**
- Substance 3D Painter's path tool keeps both the curve and the brush parameters editable after drawing, supports symmetry, and closes paths into seamless repeats. — [CG Channel, 21 Jun 2023](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/adobe-releases-substance-3d-painter-9-0/)
- JewelCraft (Blender add-on, GPL-3.0, v3.0.0 for Blender ≥ 4.5) lets users create gems, prongs and cutters, "manage distances between gems to create compact setting", generate a colour-coded gem map and a design summary. — [github.com/mrachinskiy/jewelcraft](https://github.com/mrachinskiy/jewelcraft)

### Inferences
*Recommended algorithms (all derived stones are a pure function of stroke + parameters + seed):*
- **Along a curve.** Build the cumulative arc-length table of the surface polyline; emit at s₀ + k·d. Closed curves: use d′ = L / round(L/d) so the loop closes evenly. Open curves: offer "fit to ends" (same adjustment) or "fixed pitch" (remainder at the end). Orientation = curve tangent projected to the surface; normal = surface normal.
- **Thick strokes.** Rows are *offset curves* at ±k·h, each re-sampled by its own arc length (not a straight lattice mapped onto the curve, which bunches on the inside of bends). Square grid: h = d, same phase. Hex-packed: h = d·√3/2 with alternate rows phase-shifted by d/2. Cull collisions where offsets self-intersect at tight bends.
- **Spirals.** Archimedean r = b·θ with pitch 2πb = row spacing; equal-spacing steps use Δθ ≈ d / (b·√(θ²+1)) (for large θ, θₖ ≈ √(2kd/b)). Phyllotaxis: Vogel's formula with c ≈ 0.525·d to match the areal density of a hex lattice of pitch d (from π·c² = (√3/2)·d²); its minimum neighbour distance is a little below d, so apply a safety factor or a collision pass. Map both to the surface with the exponential map (section 5).
- **Region fills.** (a) *Lattice fill*: hex/square lattice in a 2D parameterisation of the region, rotated to a user direction, mapped to the surface, culled against the boundary inset by stone radius + edge clearance, then lightly relaxed. Hex lattice count ≈ 2/(√3·d²) stones per unit area. (b) *Contour-parallel fill*: iso-lines of geodesic distance to the boundary at d/2 + k·h, each sampled by arc length — the outline-following look. (c) *Scatter*: Bridson on the surface; for gradients use a spatially varying radius or sample elimination with weights. (d) *Field-aligned fill* for large curved regions: stripe-pattern or Instant-Meshes-style position field gives a globally consistent lattice that bends with the body.
- **Gradient / ombré and size gradation.** Drive density or stone size by a scalar field (geodesic distance from a curve, height, or painted weight). Stones come in discrete sizes, so quantise the size field into the available sizes and pack largest-first with per-size radii; sample elimination's progressive ordering gives a natural "thinning-out" sequence.
- **Overlap avoidance.** Uniform spatial hash with cell ≈ largest stone diameter; distance test in 3D is adequate because stone radii are tiny relative to limb thickness (the caveat geometry-central raises only bites for large radii). Check in both worn and relaxed-fabric states (section 4).
- **Relaxation.** A few Houdini-style iterations (repel in the tangent plane, re-project to the surface) fix parameterisation distortion cheaply; full geodesic CVT is higher quality but heavier — reserve for "tidy up" commands.
- **Snapping, symmetry, stabilisation.** Snap to the nearest design curve via a BVH over curve segments, with optional constant offset. Mirror via the canonical symmetry map. Stabilise freehand input with a 1€ filter or Krita-style trailing average, then fit the cleaned samples to an editable spline.
- **Non-destructive vs baked.** Keep generators (stroke/fill + parameters + seed) as the document's primary objects and derive stones on load/size change; allow sparse per-stone overrides keyed by a stable index (row, ordinal); provide an explicit "expand to individual stones" command for hand finishing, after which that group behaves as baked instances. Houdini's notes on seed stability (changing topology or counts reshuffles points unless seeds are pinned) are the main pitfall to design around.

### Gaps
- Jewellery CAD pavé algorithms were not verified: MatrixGold, 3Design, RhinoGold and Peacock pages were blocked or empty, so how "auto pavé" packs mixed stone sizes on a surface (lattice in UV, dynamic relaxation, or circle packing) remains unconfirmed. JewelCraft's exact spacing overlay and curve-scatter features are recalled but only the README-level description above was confirmed.

## 7. Attachment (keeping stones anchored through body shape, size and pose changes; mesh re-cutting; transfer across garment sizes)

### Takeaway
The production-proven anchor is *source face + parametric coordinates*, re-evaluated on the deformed mesh each frame (Houdini's Scatter + Attribute Interpolate; Blender's curves-on-surface), and its documented weakness is that it "works only if the topology does not change". The remedy used by both tools is a second, topology-independent address — surface UV coordinates or rest positions. For a leotard tool this means anchoring to the canonical body mesh (never re-cut) with a stable-UV fallback. I found no source on how leotard manufacturers scale crystal layouts across sizes.

### Cited Findings
- Houdini: Scatter records the source primitive number and its parametric (u,v,w) location for every point; Attribute Interpolate then moves the points onto the deforming surface each frame — the documented way to keep scattered points consistent with deforming geometry; "this works only if the topology does not change". — [Houdini Scatter SOP](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/scatter.html); [Houdini Attribute Interpolate](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/attribinterpolate.html)
- Houdini's texture-space scattering keeps points consistent "through cuts, subdivision, or higher-resolution replacement, provided texture coordinates correspond". — [Houdini Scatter SOP](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/scatter.html)
- Blender attaches hair curves to a deforming mesh with a per-curve `surface_uv_coordinate` (root location in the surface's UV map) and uses `rest_position` to compute tangents so that curve rotation follows the surface; the node compares the original and evaluated meshes and translates/rotates each curve; subdivision with UV smoothing can break the stored attachment. — [Blender manual, Deform Curves on Surface](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/curve/operations/deform_curves_on_surface.html)
- Blender's Sample UV Surface does the reverse lookup (UV → face → interpolated value) and requires non-overlapping UVs. — [Blender manual, Sample UV Surface](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/mesh/sample/sample_uv_surface.html)
- geometry-central's `SurfacePoint` (vertex / edge + t / face + barycentrics) is the general-purpose surface address used as algorithm input and output; intrinsic triangulations provide exact function transfer between an input mesh and a refined one through the common subdivision. — [SurfacePoint](https://geometry-central.net/surface/utilities/surface_point/); [common subdivision](https://geometry-central.net/surface/intrinsic_triangulations/common_subdivision/)
- Houdini Copy to Points derives each copy's transform from target-point attributes (scale via `pscale`; translation/rotation/scale and normal alignment when "Transform Using Point Orientations" is on) and can pack-and-instance so copies share one source geometry. — [Houdini Copy to Points](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/copytopoints.html)
- USD's PointInstancer carries optional 64-bit `ids` so individual instances keep identity when array positions are reused, plus `inactiveIds`/`invisibleIds` masks for sparse pruning. — [OpenUSD UsdGeomPointInstancer](https://openusd.org/release/api/class_usd_geom_point_instancer.html)
- Research on carrying a garment design to other bodies: styling curves regenerated for different bodies and poses (Kwok et al. 2016); "Design preserving garment transfer" between bodies (Brouet et al. 2012 — bibliographic record confirmed only, content not read this session). — [Kwok et al.](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_paperurl/20160819153646437451267); [Brouet et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/2185520.2185532)

### Inferences
- **Recommended anchor record per stone:** canonical face id + barycentric (u, v); in-plane rotation relative to a canonical tangent field (irrelevant for round stones, essential for navettes, pears, baguettes and sequins); stone type/size id; stable instance id; plus a redundant stable-atlas UV for migration. World transform = barycentric position on the current body, interpolated normal, tangent from the canonical field rotated by the stored angle — computable in a vertex shader or a tight CPU/WASM loop for 100k+ stones.
- **Re-cutting never touches anchors** if the stones reference the canonical body mesh and the cut garment mesh is only a derived cache (section 2–3). What a line edit *can* change is validity: after each edit, test each affected anchor's region membership and flag stones that now sit in a cutout, across a seam allowance, or under an elastic edge.
- **If the canonical mesh itself changes** (new body-model version, subdivision level, different avatar): migrate by stable UV (Blender/Houdini approach) or by closest-point projection on the rest shape; storing both addresses from day one makes this a batch job rather than a data-loss event.
- **Size transfer — offer both semantics, per object:** *(A) keep count, scale spacing* — the same anchors on a smaller body; stones keep physical size, so gaps shrink; for a linear body scale s the pitch scales by s. *(B) keep spacing, change count* — re-run the generator on each size; counts scale ~s for lines and ~s² for area fills (e.g. s = 0.7 gives about half the stones in a fill). (A) preserves the exact motif and is the only option for baked stones; (B) preserves sparkle density and avoids collisions and is free for non-destructive generators. A sensible default is (B) for fills and scatters, (A) for outlines and counted motifs, with optional *stone-size substitution* per size band (e.g. one size smaller on child sizes) and a per-size report of counts, collisions and cost.
- Pose changes are handled by the same mechanism (anchors ride the skinned mesh); stones near joints will visibly converge/diverge, which is a truthful preview of the real garment.

### Gaps
- **No source found on manufacturer practice** for grading crystal layouts across leotard sizes (separate templates per size band? count reduction? smaller stones for child sizes?). The search budget ran out before this could be queried; it should be asked directly of manufacturers or covered by the industry-workflow researcher.
- Whether crystals are applied to flat panels before sewing, to the finished relaxed garment, or to the garment stretched on a board/form is unverified, and it determines which spacing state is binding.
- Maya's rivet/uvPin, Unreal's mesh-surface attachment and other DCC equivalents were not verified.
- Numerical robustness of barycentric anchoring under extreme pose-dependent skin deformation (candy-wrapper artefacts at shoulders) was not investigated.

## 8. Picking and interaction performance; undo/redo; file format; collaboration

### Takeaway
Three standard mechanisms cover the interaction load: a BVH for ray/brush queries on the body (three-mesh-bvh advertises 500 rays against an 80k-triangle model at 60 fps and supports refit, skinned meshes and WebGPU), GPU ID-buffer picking for 100k+ stone instances (IDs encoded in colour, optionally rendering only the pixel under the cursor), and a uniform spatial hash for neighbour queries. For persistence and collaboration, the verified patterns are: manifest + binary buffers (optionally inside SQLite for atomic, incremental saves), glTF `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` / USD PointInstancer as *export* formats, patch-based undo, and Figma-style per-property last-writer-wins or a CRDT such as Yjs.

### Cited Findings

**Picking and spatial queries**
- three-mesh-bvh (MIT): accelerated raycasting (incl. first-hit mode), shapecast and sphere intersection, closest-point and distance queries, bounds *refit* when vertices move, asynchronous/parallel BVH generation in workers, BVH traversal in GPU shaders, a WebGPU API, `SkinnedMeshBVH`, BatchedMesh support, BVHs for points and lines, serialisation, and example sculpting and lasso-selection tools; headline claim "Casting 500 rays against an 80,000 polygon model at 60fps". — [github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh)
- GPU picking: render each object with a unique ID as a flat colour into an offscreen target (32 bits across RGBA gives over 4 billion IDs; 0 = nothing), let the depth buffer resolve occlusion, read back the pixel under the cursor; optimisation: render only a 1×1-pixel frustum under the cursor instead of the full target. — [WebGL Fundamentals: picking](https://webglfundamentals.org/webgl/lessons/webgl-picking.html)
- Spatial hashing for deformable-object collision queries (Teschner et al., VMV 2003). — [M. Müller publications page](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/publications.html)
- Repeated geodesic/heat solves are cheap after a one-off factorisation (heat method: "factored once and subsequently solved in near-linear time"; geometry-central solvers precompute lazily and share precomputation). — [Heat Method page](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kmcrane/Projects/HeatMethod/); [geometry-central signed heat](https://geometry-central.net/surface/algorithms/signed_heat_method/)

**Instance interchange formats**
- glTF `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` (ratified): per-node `TRANSLATION` (vec3), `ROTATION` (quaternion), `SCALE` (vec3) accessors plus custom underscore-prefixed per-instance attributes such as `_ID`; no per-instance materials or animation; no non-instanced fallback (list in `extensionsRequired`); may reduce portability and large batches should be grouped into culled cells. — [Khronos glTF extension README](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Vendor/EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing/README.md)
- USD `UsdGeomPointInstancer`: `prototypes` relationship, required `protoIndices` and `positions`, optional `orientations`, `scales`, `ids`; 32 bytes per instance versus 64 for a 4×4 matrix; designed for billions of instances; `inactiveIds`/`invisibleIds` masks. — [OpenUSD API docs](https://openusd.org/release/api/class_usd_geom_point_instancer.html)

**Document persistence**
- SQLite as an application file format: atomic transactions (crash-safe saves), incremental page-level updates instead of rewriting the whole file, changes can be saved continuously with an undo/redo stack persisted across sessions, cross-platform single file, schema evolution by adding tables/columns, queryable with standard tools; caveats: not universal, must treat untrusted files carefully, advocacy from the SQLite project itself. — [sqlite.org/appfileformat](https://www.sqlite.org/appfileformat.html)

**Undo/redo and collaboration**
- Immer records *patches* and *inverse patches* for every state change (JSON-Patch-like, array paths); documented uses are undo/redo and shipping incremental updates over the network; must be enabled explicitly; patch sets are not guaranteed minimal. — [Immer patches](https://immerjs.github.io/immer/patches/)
- Figma's multiplayer design (E. Wallace, 16 Oct 2019): document = tree of objects, effectively `Map<ObjectID, Map<Property, Value>>`; server-authoritative last-writer-wins per property (CRDT-inspired, not a true CRDT); client-generated object IDs include a client ID; parent link and fractional-index position stored as one property; undo principle — an undo modifies redo history at the time of the undo, so undo-copy-redo leaves the document unchanged. — [Figma engineering blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/how-figmas-multiplayer-technology-works/)
- Yjs (MIT): CRDT shared types (Map, Array, Text, XML), commutative/idempotent binary updates, state-vector sync, offline and peer-to-peer providers, `Y.UndoManager` scoped to shared types with `trackedOrigins` so only a user's own changes are undone; Rust port Yrs (with WASM and other bindings). — [github.com/yjs/yjs](https://github.com/yjs/yjs)

### Inferences
- **Picking stones:** GPU ID pass over the instanced stones (one extra flat-colour draw per stone type into an integer or RGBA8 target), read back a small window around the cursor to tolerate near-misses on 3–5 mm stones. Box/lasso selection: read the ID target over the rectangle (visible-only selection) or project instance centres on the CPU and test against the polygon (select-through); 100k point-in-polygon tests are a few milliseconds in WASM.
- **Picking the body / brush centre:** BVH raycast against the current posed mesh (refit after pose/size change; a 13k–100k-triangle body is far below the library's advertised load). An alternative with no CPU BVH at all is a GPU pass writing face id + barycentrics, which returns a ready-made anchor under the cursor.
- **Brush footprint queries:** surface-space disc = stones within radius r of the hit point via the spatial hash; for wrap-around correctness on thin parts compare normals or use geodesic distance from the hit point (one prefactored heat solve) for large brushes only.
- **Holding 60 fps while emitting thousands of stones:** generate in a worker/WASM, append to instance buffers with partial uploads, O(1) spatial-hash inserts, cap emission per frame, and defer expensive passes (relaxation, both-state overlap checks, per-size validation) to stroke end or idle time.
- **Undo architecture:** operate at the generator level whenever possible — "add stroke", "change spacing" are tiny commands whose inverse is trivial, and stones are re-derived. For baked stones keep structure-of-arrays buffers in fixed-size chunks with copy-on-write, and store undo steps as (ids, before, after) deltas; keep these large typed arrays *outside* any proxy-based immutable tree (patch generation over 100k-element arrays is the wrong tool) and reference them by version/hash from the JSON document.
- **File format:** a container (zip or SQLite) holding a versioned JSON manifest (body parameters, lines, regions, generators, fabrics, sizes) plus binary buffers for baked stones — about 24 bytes per stone (face id, two barycentrics, rotation, type, flags, id), i.e. ~2.4 MB per 100k. Use an explicit schema version with forward migrations and preserve unknown fields. SQLite adds crash-safe incremental autosave and a persistent undo log; zip + JSON is simpler to diff and inspect. Export glTF (with `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` and `_ID`) for visual interchange and USD PointInstancer for pipelines — both store baked transforms and lose anchors and generators, so neither is a source-of-truth format.
- **Collaboration-friendly structure:** object-granular documents with stable client-generated IDs and independent properties merge well under Figma-style per-property LWW; make each curve, region, generator and stone group its own object; order layers with fractional indices. Baked stone sets are the awkward case — treat each set as chunked blobs (LWW per chunk) or as a keyed map of stone records in a CRDT. Yjs is the verified off-the-shelf option if offline-first or peer-to-peer editing is required.

### Gaps
- No measured numbers were obtained for GPU ID-buffer readback latency in WebGPU (asynchronous readback) versus WebGL, or for picking 100k+ instances; the three.js picking manual page returned 404.
- Not verified this session: Automerge, Loro, fractional-indexing libraries, Blender/Krita/Photoshop undo internals, Substance Painter's stroke replay on resolution change, three.js InstancedMesh/BatchedMesh raycast behaviour.
- No public description was found of an editor undo system specifically for very large instance sets.
- Long-term format questions (signing, asset embedding, thumbnailing, partial loading on mobile) were not researched.

## 9. 2D/3D dual views (flat sketches or pattern pieces synchronised with 3D, versus 3D-only)

### Takeaway
Professional fashion CAD is uniformly dual-view — a 2D pattern window and a 3D window kept in sync (CLO, Browzwear) — and both vendors now also let users draw on the 3D avatar and derive patterns from it, while recent research (Bartle 2016; Pietroni 2022; Wolff 2023) pushes further toward designing directly in 3D with patterns computed automatically. I found no study of what leotard designers themselves prefer.

### Cited Findings
- CLO: lines and shapes drawn on the avatar with the 3D Pen are extracted as 2D patterns by Flatten, with sewing created automatically between adjacent pieces; since 2024.2 the 2D pattern position and rotation follow the pattern's placement on the avatar; avatar textures are not carried onto the new patterns (they receive the default or selected fabric). — [CLO Flatten article](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008890627-Flatten); [CLO community: 3D Pen and textures](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/15369170495385-3D-Pen-Avatar-Copy-texture-colour-info-from-imported-obj)
- Browzwear VStitcher: work "in both 2D drafting and 3D draping environments" with changes visible in real time; grading across sizes; production-ready pattern export and tech packs. — [Browzwear VStitcher](https://browzwear.com/products/v-stitcher)
- Research direction — edit in 3D, derive 2D: Bartle et al. 2016 motivate direct 3D editing because hand-editing 2D patterns to achieve a 3D change is laborious; Pietroni et al. 2022 compute patterns from a 3D garment with the user iterating on the layout; Wolff et al. 2023 design on the avatar without starting from a pattern. — [Bartle et al.](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2016/PhysicsDrivenPatternAdjustment); [Pietroni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10272); [Wolff et al.](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14728)
- b/Surf shows that familiar 2D vector interactions (and imported SVG artwork) can be carried directly onto a 3D surface for editing. — [arXiv 2102.05921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05921)
- Substance 3D Painter's path tool is used in the 3D viewport by clicking on the model. — [CG Channel, 21 Jun 2023](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/adobe-releases-substance-3d-painter-9-0/)
- GarmentCode's configurator is a third model: a parameter GUI over a programmatic pattern, with the 3D result simulated afterwards. — [GarmentCode repo](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode)

### Inferences
- **Recommendation — 3D-primary with two synchronised 2D views, all backed by the same surface-anchored data.** (1) *3D view*: the main editing surface; essential because many leotard lines and crystal swirls wrap continuously from front to back and over the shoulder, which no flat view shows without a break. (2) *Technical flat (front/back)*: a stable unwrapped view derived from the design atlas, fully editable — precise symmetric drawing is easier flat, it matches how designs are sketched and approved today, and it doubles as the printable spec sheet. (3) *Pattern-piece view*: production output, primarily for inspection and production annotations (grain, notches, allowances, artwork and stone maps per piece) rather than free design editing.
- Because every curve and stone is stored as a surface address, the views cannot drift apart: each is a different projection, and an edit in any view writes the same record. The only genuinely view-specific problem is 2D editing across the flat's cut lines (side seams, shoulder): strokes that reach a cut should continue on the adjoining panel.
- A pattern-first workflow (CLO-style) should not be the default: it presupposes pattern-making skill and simulation, and the product's users are designers and coaches. It remains valuable as an import path for factories' own blocks (section 4).

### Gaps
- No user research was found on designer preference between 2D flats and 3D for leotards or sportswear; this is a question for usability testing or the UX researcher.
- What existing leotard/sportswear configurators offer (2D front/back templates versus 3D) was outside my verified sources — defer to the competitor-landscape research.
- CLO's and Style3D's full feature pages and manuals were not reachable, so details such as bidirectional live editing while simulation runs are not confirmed here.
- Adobe's documentation of Painter's 2D/3D split view was blocked.

## 10. Prior art for placing things on surfaces (Substance 3D Painter, ZBrush, Blender Geometry Nodes, Houdini, jewellery CAD pavé, CLO/Browzwear trims and rhinestones)

### Takeaway
The general-purpose DCC tools converge on the same architecture this product needs: points generated procedurally on a surface (density + minimum distance + seed), bound to the surface by face/UV coordinates, and rendered as instances with per-point transform attributes (Houdini Scatter → Attribute Interpolate → Copy to Points; Blender Distribute Points → Instance on Points), with Substance Painter's path tool as the closest UX precedent for editable strokes on a 3D model. Dedicated rhinestone features in fashion CAD and the internals of jewellery pavé tools could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **Substance 3D Painter** (9.0, June 2023): Path tool — Bézier guide curves created by clicking on the model; the paint stroke is applied along the curve; curve and brush properties remain editable; closed curves give seamlessly repeating patterns; works on paint and mask layers, with symmetry and Dynamic Strokes (new stroke-length properties); ships presets for stitches, zippers, welds, puckers and seams. — [CG Channel, 21 Jun 2023](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/adobe-releases-substance-3d-painter-9-0/)
- **Blender Geometry Nodes** (manual for current 5.x): Distribute Points on Faces (Random / Poisson Disk with minimum distance and maximum density, density attribute, attribute transfer incl. UVs, outputs normal and rotation); Sample UV Surface (reverse UV lookup); Deform Curves on Surface (UV-anchored attachment with rest-position tangents). — [Distribute Points on Faces](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/point/distribute_points_on_faces.html); [Sample UV Surface](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/mesh/sample/sample_uv_surface.html); [Deform Curves on Surface](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/geometry_nodes/curve/operations/deform_curves_on_surface.html)
- **Houdini**: Scatter SOP (density attributes, relaxation to blue noise, exact counts, texture-space mode, per-primitive seeds, source-primitive/UVW outputs, persistent ids); Attribute Interpolate (sticks scattered points to deforming geometry); Copy to Points (per-point transform attributes such as `pscale`, optional pack-and-instance so copies share geometry, piece attribute to choose which source geometry goes to which point). — [Scatter](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/scatter.html); [Attribute Interpolate](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/attribinterpolate.html); [Copy to Points](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/copytopoints.html)
- **Unreal Engine** ships geometry-processing building blocks relevant here (edge-flip geodesic paths on dynamic meshes with intrinsic meshes and surface-point outputs). — [Epic API docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/DynamicMesh/FDeformableEdgePath)
- **Jewellery (open source)**: JewelCraft for Blender (GPL-3.0, v3.0.0, Blender ≥ 4.5) — gems, prongs and cutters; managing distances between gems for compact settings; colour-coded gem map; design summary. — [github.com/mrachinskiy/jewelcraft](https://github.com/mrachinskiy/jewelcraft)
- **Fashion CAD**: CLO's avatar drawing + Flatten and Browzwear's 2D/3D environment with tension/pressure maps are confirmed (sections 2, 9); Browzwear's VStitcher product page does not mention rhinestone, sequin or embellishment tools. — [CLO Flatten article](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008890627-Flatten); [Browzwear VStitcher](https://browzwear.com/products/v-stitcher)
- **Research prototypes** for vector tools on surfaces: b/Surf (Bézier pen tool on meshes), BoolSurf (boolean shape operations on meshes), straightedge-and-compass constructions (geodesic circles/polygons). — [b/Surf](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05921); [BoolSurf](https://research.adobe.com/publication/boolsurf-boolean-operations-on-surfaces); [Mancinelli & Puppo](https://iris.unige.it/handle/11567/1087411)

### Inferences
- The common pattern across Houdini and Blender — *generate points → store surface address → instance with attributes* — is exactly the non-destructive stone pipeline recommended in sections 6–7; neither tool offers stone-specific logic (discrete sizes, physical gaps, counts/costing, two-state overlap checks), which is where a dedicated product adds value.
- Substance Painter's path tool is the best UX template for the pen-tool brushes: click-to-place control points on the model, live re-evaluation while editing, parameters editable after the fact, symmetry, closed-path handling. It paints texture rather than placing discrete counted objects, so spacing-exactness and per-instance editing are new requirements.
- A jewellery-style "gem map" (colour-coded legend by stone size with counts) is a proven presentation for production hand-off and maps directly onto a per-piece stone map for leotard factories.
- None of the verified tools combine surface vector lines, counted instance brushes and pattern flattening in one application; the pieces exist separately in research code (b/Surf, BoolSurf, geometry-central) and DCC tools.

### Gaps
- **ZBrush** (NanoMesh, InsertMesh/IMM and curve brushes, LazyMouse) could not be verified: Pixologic documentation now redirects to Maxon's help site, which did not serve the pages. Recalled behaviour (instances placed per host polygon; curve-following insert brushes) is unconfirmed.
- **Jewellery CAD pavé tools** (MatrixGold, 3Design, Rhino plug-ins such as Peacock/2Shapes) were unreachable; their packing algorithms and UX remain unverified leads.
- **CLO / Style3D / Browzwear rhinestone, sequin or trim-placement features** were not confirmed (CLO manual blocked; Browzwear page silent). Recalled leads such as CLO's OBJ-type topstitch for repeating 3D objects along a line are unverified.
- **Rhinestone-template software** (see section 6 gaps) unverified.
- Unreal PCG/foliage painting and Blender's hair-curve sculpt/density brushes — further relevant prior art — were not examined.
