## 1. Terminology and reality check — what is actually used on WAG competition leotards, what is avoided, and how brands name it

### Takeaway
On leotards from the market leader (GK Elite, USA Gymnastics' Olympic supplier) the stones are heat-applied flat-back crystals: a CAD layout, a rhinestone-transfer ("jewel") machine that sets stones onto heat-transfer film, a heat press, and hand placement with tweezers for larger stones and pearls. GK's catalogue vocabulary is "crystals"/"jewels" (pattern codes T####/J####), "shaped jewels", "nailheads", "spanglez™", "sequinz™", embroidery and imprinting; other makers say "crystals" (Sylvia P: "Standard" vs "Swarovski®") or "rhinestones" (Snowflake Designs, NCAA press).

### Cited Findings

**How GK actually applies stones [PRESS]**
- "With such a high number of crystals, a computer-aided design program is needed to determine exact crystal placement"; a "precision laser with a presser foot" applies crystals individually; for the 2024 Olympics GK "applied larger crystals by hand using tweezers". — [Foley & Lardner, 14 Aug 2024](https://foley.com/?p=108824)
- A rhinestone-transfer machine places crystals on heat-transfer paper, heat-activated glue sets them onto the fabric, and some crystals are placed by hand with tweezers; crystal usage "has exploded over the past two decades, thanks to improving technology and machinery". — [NAM, 30 Jul 2024 (citing The Washington Post)](https://nam.org/?p=31672)
- "Swarovski jewel motifs are applied by heat transfer, with some applied by hand and other fill-in jewels by laser machine"; "Jewel machines create a crystal embellishment of the goat head on a flat sheet. The embellishment is then sealed onto the leotard using a heat press." — [NBC Sports Philadelphia (Tokyo cycle)](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/)
- Rio 2016 crystals were set "with the help of a machine with robot arms". — [SportsLogos.net, 11 Aug 2016](https://news.sportslogos.net/2016/08/11/bejewelled-leotards-most-expensive-olympic-uniforms-ever/other/olympics/)
- In 2008 Nastia Liukin's leotard had 184 crystals, mostly hand-placed; GK later moved to "specialized bejeweling equipment" that places crystals robotically. — [Jewelry Judge blog, 17 Aug 2016](https://jewelryjudge.thejewelerblog.com/u_s__gymnastics_team_leotards_radiate_with_4_000_swarovski_crystals.html)

**Definitions**
- "Hotfix … are decorations like rhinestones, rhinestuds, nailheads, etc."; a hotfix rhinestone has a faceted front, a flat back usually foil-backed, and a glue layer that melts into the fabric. — [EduTech Wiki "Hotfix stone"](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Hotfix_stone) [HOBBY]
- "A spangle is a sequin without the hole in the middle"; sequin transfers ("Hotfix sequins") remove the thread but still have a hole. — [ColDesi, "What are spangle transfers"](https://shop.coldesi.com/blogs/news/what-are-spangle-transfers) [RETAIL]
- In costuming, sequins have a centre hole, spangles a hole at the top, paillettes are large and flat (note this differs from the decoration trade's "spangle = no hole"). — [Wikipedia "Sequin"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequin)
- Rhinestuds are aluminium hotfix pieces "facet-shaped to emulate rhinestones", "lighter than rhinestones", and "do not contain any lead". — [ColDesi rhinestud listing](https://shop.coldesi.com/products/gold-rstud-3mm-500gr-gold-rstud-10d-500) [RETAIL]
- Preciosa's own component taxonomy: flat-back stones (No Hotfix / Hotfix / Sew-on), nacre pearls, cup chains, plastic bandings, metal bandings, crystal net, hotfix transfers, crystal sheet. — [Preciosa Hotfix Application Manual (PDF, 2019)](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf) [MFR]

**What is avoided or restricted, and why**
- Weight: a ~10,000-crystal Olympic leotard weighs about a pound because of the crystals (designer Jeanne Diaz, via NYT). — [People via Yahoo, Jul 2024](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/team-usa-gymnastics-goes-big-154156319.html) [PRESS]
- "Swarovski has continually been working on developing lighter crystals" for the Olympic leotards. — [Marie Claire, Jul 2024](https://www.marieclaire.com/fashion/team-usa-olympic-gymnastics-leotards-swarovski-crystals/) [PRESS]
- For leotards a UK crystal retailer recommends ss16, ss20 and ss30 as large enough to sparkle but small enough not to weigh the fabric down (paraphrase). — [Crystal Parade, 19 Dec 2023](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-embellish-stretch-fabric-with-rhinestones) [RETAIL]
- Sewn sequins: attached with a single embroidery thread, described as not the most secure, can be damaged in the wash, and the centre hole lets fabric show through. — [ColDesi](https://shop.coldesi.com/blogs/news/what-are-spangle-transfers) [RETAIL]
- Spangles are marketed for dance and cheer uniforms and kids' wear because they are light and non-scratching ("soft, smooth, and kid-friendly"). — [SpangleElite press release, 17 Apr 2016](https://www.pr.com/press-release/667228); [ColDesi spangle machines](https://coldesi.com/spangle-machines/) [RETAIL]
- Heat-applied stones are restricted by fabric: no sequinz on velvet; foil/hologram fabric dulls where jewels are pressed; mesh is fragile and GK "will not guarantee the life span of this fabric" (mesh designs need a signed waiver). — [GK Resilient #4947S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/qxli); [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions) [LEO]

### Inferences
- GK's "spanglez™" is almost certainly the hole-less heat-applied spangle that ColDesi-type machines produce (GK only ever lists it as "silver hologram" and for team names/logos); "sequinz™" appears to be GK's name for heat-applied sequin/spangle patterns on stock and replica leotards (it is heat-sensitive enough that it "cannot be applied to Velvet"). GK does not define either term on the pages I could read.
- "Brilliance crystals" vs "clear crystals" on GK pages most likely map to an iridescent (AB-type) crystal vs plain Crystal; the T/TS codes are probably heat-transfer motif numbers and J codes jewel-pattern numbers. Unconfirmed.
- I found no WAG competition-leotard source using sew-on stones, cup chain/crystal banding, sewn sequins or sequin fabric; every piece of process evidence for WAG is heat transfer. Those items are dance/skating/rhythmic techniques and should be secondary library entries. The likely reasons (snagging on apparatus and hair, stiffness across stretch, hand-labour cost) are my reasoning, not sourced.
- The app's primary object types should therefore be: round hotfix crystal (several grades), shaped hotfix crystal, hotfix pearl, nailhead/rhinestud, spangle (hole-less disc), with appliqué, embroidery, foil/glitter print as surface layers.

### Gaps
- Rules: I could not retrieve the FIG 2025–2028 WAG Code of Points attire article (the PDF URL returned site HTML), and found no USAG/NCAA rule text; no source was found for any rule limiting crystals, so "rules" as a reason for avoidance is unverified.
- GK's own definitions of spanglez, sequinz and nailheads, and its "Leotard Fabrics & Embellishments" article (linked from product pages) were not found; the Premium/Standard crystal wording is unverified (see above).
- Catalogue terminology of Ozone, Destira, Quatro, Milano Pro-Sport, Alpha Factor, Christian Moreau, Agiva, Plum, Rebecca's Mom: not retrieved (search budget exhausted; Ozone's site did not resolve, Destira's pages returned navigation only).
- No source found that explicitly says which embellishments WAG makers refuse to use.

## 2. Crystal brands and grades — Swarovski, Preciosa, alternatives; facets, cut geometry, glass, foiling, glue layer

### Takeaway
Preciosa Chaton Rose MAXIMA (Czech, lead-free, 15 facets in common sizes) is the premium stone that crystal retailers have pushed for performance wear since Swarovski withdrew from DIY/costume components in 2021 (GK still used Swarovski for the 2024 Olympic leotards); one major US retailer says Swarovski's purchase restrictions were lifted on 1 June 2026, but I found no Swarovski statement confirming it. Below the premium tier sit Preciosa VIVA12 (12 facets), private-label "Austrian/European" stones, and Korean/Chinese glass (8–14 facets) used for bulk machine-set transfers.

### Cited Findings
**Swarovski — what happened and who can buy**
- Swarovski said in 2020 it would "withdraw from offering Swarovski® components that are tailored for the DIY and nails retail segment" "in the course of the year 2021", refocusing B2B on luxury, accessories and fashion and exiting "low-margin wholesale"; new components for dance, costuming, DIY and nail customers stopped in November 2021. CEO Robert Buchbauer: "After years of exuberant expansion, Swarovski needs to refocus and learn that less is more." — [Rhinestones Unlimited (archived notice)](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/swarovski-corporate-restructuring-and-what-it-means-for-you/) [RETAIL]
- April 2020: Buchbauer became CEO; the company closed 750 stores and laid off "some 6,000 employees"; Alexis Nasard is listed as CEO from 2022. — [Wikipedia "Swarovski" (edited 26 Sep 2026)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarovski)
- **(search summary only)** From 2021 buyers had to sign a Brand Control Agreement; one distributor's criteria: sign the agreement, do not use the Swarovski name in marketing, buy at least US$2,000 of Swarovski a year, and do not resell loose stones. — [Fire Mountain Gems](https://www.firemountaingems.com/swarovski-inquiry)
- Effective 1 June 2026 the Brand Control Agreement is no longer required (the retailer's paraphrase of the policy change); authorised channels can sell to independent designers, resellers, small businesses, DIY customers, nail artists and dance studios, and businesses may use the Swarovski name under its brand guidelines. The retailer stocks styles 2038, 2058, 2078 and 2088 (Crystal and Crystal AB) and special-orders the rest. This is one retailer's account, with no quoted Swarovski statement. — [Rhinestones Unlimited, 2026](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/swarovski-crystals-available-again-2026) [RETAIL]
- Supply is still thin in October 2026: "Rhinestones Unlimited and Swarovski® offer minimal inventory in the U.S. Please plan for 2-3 weeks before we ship if ordering more than 1-2 packages." — [Rhinestones Unlimited, Swarovski 2078 Crystal 16ss](https://rhinestonesu.com/swarovski-2078-flatback-rhinestones-crystal-16ss/) [RETAIL]

**Swarovski cuts and construction**
- "Flat Backs No Hotfix are loose crystal elements backed with platinum foiling for extra brilliance and protection"; art. 2088 XIRIUS Rose uses "multilayer cut technology [that] creates the inspiring XIRIUS star design"; quality is "underlined by the highest precision in table size and diameter". Size ranges: 2000 Rose ss3–4; 2058 XILION Rose Enhanced ss5–10; 2088 XIRIUS Rose ss12, 14, 16, 20, 24, 30, 34, 40, 48. — [Swarovski "Collection 2018 – Flat Backs No Hotfix" (PDF)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0086/3023/9295/files/Swarovski_Non_Hotfix_Collection.pdf?2186) [MFR, 2018 — pre-restructuring]
- 2078 is the hotfix counterpart of 2088 ("Genuine Swarovski® Xirius Rose (2078) faceting, built for hotfix application"; "Heat-activated hotfix backing"). — [Rhinestones Unlimited 2078 16ss](https://rhinestonesu.com/swarovski-2078-flatback-rhinestones-crystal-16ss/) [RETAIL]
- XIRIUS Rose Hotfix 2078 has 16 cuts — 8 large and 8 smaller — plus the table on top. — [EduTech Wiki "Hotfix stone"](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Hotfix_stone) [HOBBY]
- XILION was introduced in 2004 as a cut "designed to optimize the brilliance of Roses" (flat-backs) and chatons; all Swarovski crystal glass made since 2012 is lead-free. — [Wikipedia "Swarovski"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swarovski)
- Swarovski has pending patents on a gemstone-cutting method (EP4371443A1, filed 2022) and on a coloured crystal coating applied as ink in a polymerizable carrier via plasma (US 2023/0272234 A1, filed 2021). — [Foley & Lardner, 14 Aug 2024](https://foley.com/?p=108824)

**Preciosa**
- Two round flat-back lines, both sold hotfix and non-hotfix: VIVA12 (the original, 12 facets, ss5/1.8 mm to ss48/11 mm) and MAXIMA ("SS5 up to SS20 have 15 facets, and SS30 up to SS48 have 18 facets"). VIVA12 foil is "silver/grey", MAXIMA foil "light silver"; colours match across the two lines. — [Crystal Parade, 4 Jan 2024](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/what-is-the-difference-between-preciosa-viva12-and-maxima-rhinestones); [Crystal Parade, 8 Jul 2022](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/our-expert-guide-to-preciosa-flatback-rhinestones) [RETAIL]
- MAXIMA: 15 facets in most-used sizes, 18 in larger sizes, 12 in micro sizes (market standard quoted as 12 / 8); "reflects up to 264 unique rays of light"; "100+ colors and coatings"; consistent "from ss2 to ss48"; lead-free; "higher refractive index than similar competing products" (no value given). — [Rhinestones Unlimited, "Why Preciosa…"](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/why-preciosa-is-the-leader-in-luxury-crystal-rhinestone-production/) [RETAIL relaying MFR claims]
- Conflict: the same retailer's 30ss MAXIMA product page says "Precision 15-facet MAXIMA cut", not 18. — [Rhinestones Unlimited MAXIMA Rose 30ss](https://rhinestonesu.com/preciosa-maxima-flatback-rose-30ss/)
- Underside: a "Silver Reflective Layer" that "contains genuine silver, not a silver-colored substitute", covered by a bonded "DuraFoiling™" protective layer; hotfix glue "designed for secure adhesion across a wide range of carrier materials"; tested for machine wash, sweat, temperature shock, scratch-and-pull, drop and UV (no numbers published). — [Rhinestones Unlimited, "The Underside Engineering…"](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/the-underside-engineering-of-preciosa-chaton-rose-maxima/) [RETAIL relaying MFR claims]
- Preciosa uses size-specific glass recipes so colour reads the same across sizes; range "engineered for consistency from 3ss to 48ss". — [Rhinestones Unlimited, colour/size consistency](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/preciosa-chaton-rose-maxima-color-shine-and-size-consistency/)
- Preciosa's manual calls the hotfix article "MC Chaton Rose MAXIMA Hotfix"; "The heat-activated glue turns into liquid and back into a solid after cooling"; the Preciosa Group "melts 40 tons of glass every day". — [Preciosa Hotfix Application Manual](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf) [MFR]

**Other grades**
- Retailer tiering (US): Swarovski (lead-free "Advanced Crystal"); MAXIMA by Preciosa (presented as the best crystal quality for flat backs and hotfix); Preciosa VIVA12 (economy); LUX Austrian Crystal® (originally made in Swarovski factories, now Preciosa factories; 12 cuts in a 9+3 arrangement); Starcut Crystal™ (US brand, globally sourced, 2088-style star faceting); PriceLess Crystal™ (10 facets, cheapest, aimed at dancers). — [Rhinestones Unlimited "Choose Your Quality"](https://rhinestonesu.com/choose-your-quality) [RETAIL]
- UK retailer's own budget brand "Zodiac" is positioned for large projects or teamwear and has a wider range of sew-on shapes. — [Crystal Parade, 24 Jul 2025](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-use-hotfix-rhinestones-for-gymnastics-leotards) [RETAIL]
- DMC ("Diamant Machine Cut") is a Korean make with 12 facets; the name is also used loosely for Chinese stones; no-name stones can cost about 10× less than Swarovski but up to one-third may be defective. — [EduTech Wiki](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Hotfix_stone) [HOBBY]
- **(search summary only)** Korean "low lead" hotfix stones have 8–11 machine-cut facets, DMC 12 (another seller says 12–14); "low lead" is not lead-free. — [The Rhinestone World, Korean 5A SS10](https://www.therhinestoneworld.com/hot-fix-korean-5a-low-lead-ss10-rhinestones); [The Rhinestone World, DMC SS10](https://www.therhinestoneworld.com/dmc-hotfix-ss10-rhinestones); [Be Createful](https://becreateful.com/products/crystal-korean-glass-hotfix-rhinestones) [RETAIL]

**Glass optics (general, not brand-specific)**
- Ordinary glass has a refractive index of about 1.5; lead glass about 1.7–1.8 with higher dispersion; density ≤2.4 g/cm³ (soda-lime) vs about 3.1 g/cm³ (typical lead crystal); modern crystal replaces lead oxide with barium, zinc or potassium oxide. — [Wikipedia "Lead glass"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_glass)

### Inferences
- A flat-back "rose" is a faceted crown (a flat table plus one or two rings of facets) on a flat mirrored base — there is no pavilion. Renderable facet layouts supported by the sources: 8+8+table (Swarovski XIRIUS 2078/2088), 15 or 18 (MAXIMA), 12 (VIVA12, DMC, LUX), 10 (PriceLess), 8 (budget/micro sizes).
- The realistic 2026 library for a WAG tool is: Preciosa MAXIMA (default premium), Swarovski XIRIUS/XILION (premium, availability returning), a generic 12-facet "standard" stone, and a generic 8-facet "economy/Korean" stone. GK's unverified "Standard vs Premium (4× facets)" language would fit an 8-facet economy stone versus a multi-layer premium cut, but 4× of any real flat-back facet count does not match a published cut, so treat it as marketing.
- MAXIMA's "264 rays" is a marketing figure with no published method; it should not be used as a physical parameter.

### Gaps
- Serinity and Stellux: no source retrieved (search budget exhausted). Unverified.
- No manufacturer drawing was found giving table diameter, crown angle or height per size, nor published refractive index/Abbe number for Swarovski "Advanced Crystal" or Preciosa crystal. Only hobbyist height measurements exist (see section 3).
- Facet count of Swarovski 2058/2038 XILION Rose, and whether MAXIMA ss30+ is 15 or 18 facets (sources conflict), not confirmed from a manufacturer document. EduTech Wiki also mentions an 8-facet "Chaton Rose MAXIMA 8F" that I could not corroborate.
- Hotfix glue chemistry, thickness and colour: not published in any retrieved source.
- Swarovski's own 2026 statement on reopening components sales: not found.

## 3. Sizes — the ss table, the sizes used on leotards, non-round shapes

### Takeaway
Stone size ("ss") is a shared standard with small brand-to-brand tolerances; the working range for apparel is ss6 (2.0 mm) to ss30 (6.4 mm), with ss16 (3.9 mm) and ss20 (4.7 mm) the workhorses and ss34–ss48 used as focal stones. Non-round flat-backs (navette, pear/drop, square, rivoli, triangle families and more) exist in a few fixed sizes each.

On light stones AB reads as soft pinks and blues; on dark stones (Jet, Emerald) it reads as a brighter metallic hue. Shimmer is a "softer, more subtle version of the AB effect". ### Inferences
- Renderer model per stone: (a) glass body = clear dielectric with an absorption tint (plain colours) or volumetric scattering (opals read milky/translucent); (b) base = mirror (foil) or diffuse coloured lacquer (LacquerPRO-type entries are listed without the foil suffix, so they read as solid opaque colour with a glassy top, little sparkle); (c) crown coating = angle-dependent thin-film reflectance on about half of the crown facets for AB, all facets for AB 2X, weaker for Shimmer; metallic effects (Dorado, Light Chrome, Hematite, Rose Gold) behave as a near-opaque coloured metal on the crown.

## 5. Sequins and spangles — materials, shapes, sizes, finishes, machines, attachment

### Takeaway
What gymnastics makers call spangles are hole-less plastic discs punched from reels by a "spangle transfer" machine onto transfer tape and then heat-pressed — the flat, light, cheap cousin of the rhinestone transfer. Sizes are 2–5 mm (up to 6 mm on a four-head machine), in holographic, metallic, matte and glossy finishes; sewn sequins are essentially a dance/costume technique.

### Cited Findings
- Spangle transfers are "produced and output onto transfer tape, like a rhinestone transfer", applied with a heat press, with "no threads or holes". — [ColDesi, "What are spangle transfers"](https://shop.coldesi.com/blogs/news/what-are-spangle-transfers) [RETAIL]
- ProSpangle: sizes 2, 3, 4 and 5 mm; a 5 mm spangle is "equivalent in visual impact to an SS20 rhinestone"; 6 colour/size reels per design (stock load: two 2 mm, three 3 mm, one 4 mm); 600–1,000 spangles per minute; working area 13.5" × 17.5"; "50+ … holographic, matte, and glossy" reel options; bundled with Sierra HotFix Era; US$21,995. — [ColDesi ProSpangle](https://shop.coldesi.com/products/prospangle-spangle-transfer-machine-prospangle) [RETAIL]
- ProSpangle 4-Head: four heads, eight colours/sizes per head, 1,000 spangles per minute per head, sizes 2–6 mm, working area 24" × 20", US$64,999; spangles are solid holographic discs with no centre hole. — [ColDesi spangle machines](https://coldesi.com/spangle-machines/) [RETAIL]
- Supply: reels at US$8.25 each, "1 Reel holds 80 METERS of material". — [ColDesi](https://shop.coldesi.com/blogs/news/what-are-spangle-transfers)
- **(search summary only)** Reel stock is 6 mm wide (so 2–4 mm spangles, 5 mm "with careful planning"); a 2013 trade article quoted spangles from 2 mm to 9 mm with a heat-activated adhesive that needs no sewing. — [ColDesi reel listing](https://shop.coldesi.com/products/turquoise-235-holographic-prospangle-reel-pst-6h-turquoise-235); [Impressions magazine](https://impressionsmagazine.com/news/coldesi-introduces-new-spangle-machine/16982/)
- SpangleElite (desktop): 2, 3 and 4 mm; 400–900 spangles per minute; material "comes in 17 colors of holographic, metallic and matte"; aimed at dance and cheer uniforms and kids' wear; spangles are cheaper than rhinestones because they are punched from reels. — [ColDesi press release, 17 Apr 2016](https://www.pr.com/press-release/667228)
- Hotfix sequin motif machine (AASP-402): sets sequins onto transfer tape at up to 600 pieces per minute; three to four cartridges, each a different shape/size/colour — circles, stars, hearts, squares; can place a "2mm sequin INSIDE a 7mm sequin". — [All American Print Supply, 6 Oct 2011](https://aaprintsupplyco.com/blogs/news/4-colors-4-sizes-at-once) [RETAIL — dated]

### Inferences
- For the app, a spangle is a flat disc (or simple die shape) lying in the fabric surface with one planar reflective face; parameters are diameter (2/3/4/5/6 mm), finish (holographic, metallic, matte, glossy) and colour. Because it is flat and hole-less it behaves like a single mirror facet that follows the fabric normal.
- Spangles suit lettering, logos and dense fills where weight and cost matter (team names); crystals carry the main design. This matches GK offering spanglez specifically for "team names and logos".

## 7. Application — transfers, presses, wands, glues, machines, and practical constraints

### Takeaway
Industrial practice is: stones set face-down in a layout onto transfer film (by brushing into a cut template or by a pick-and-place machine), pressed at roughly 160–170 °C for 15–25 s at medium pressure with the stretch fabric held slightly stretched and flat, cooled 5 minutes and cured 24 hours. Hand tools (heated wand, tweezers, flexible gem glue) cover repairs, large stones and one-offs.

### Cited Findings
**Manufacturer parameters — all from the [Preciosa Hotfix Application Manual (PDF, 2019)](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf) [MFR]**
- Base fabric must withstand at least 120 °C / 250 °F and be absorbent (water-drop test); unsuitable: Teflon, water-repellent, silicone, fluorocarbon, softener and dirt-resistant finishes, dyes with metal pigments, smooth leather. Softener residue "can be sometimes eliminated by washing the material prior to Hotfix application". Minimum surface tension 38 mN/m.
- Timeline: about 5 minutes to cool, 24 hours to cure ("Do not handle or wash the textile material during this period").
- Single stones, heat through the stone (front side), ss10, seconds at 120/130/140/150/160/170/180/190/200 °C: cotton/silk/linen (50–200 g/m²) 48/40/34/28/22/16/12/10/8; wool/cashmere (200–300 g/m²) 55/45/35/30/25/20/16/13/10; "PES, lycra" (150–250 g/m²) 52/44/48/32/25/18/15/12/9 (the 48 at 140 °C is as printed and looks like a typo); suede/embroidery/denim (250–400 g/m²) 60/50/42/34/26/20/16/13/10.
- Single stones, heat through the fabric (back side), same temperatures: cotton 15/13/10/9/7/5/4/4/3; wool 24/22/17/14/11/7/6/6/5; PES/lycra 20/18/15/12/9/6/5/5/4; denim group 25/23/20/15/11/7/6/6/5.
- Time grows with stone size: on cotton at 160 °C from the front, roughly 15 s for ss4, 22 s for ss10 and 60 s for ss48 (read from a chart whose labels were partly garbled in text extraction).
- Hotfix transfers: "Adjust the Heat-press to 160-170°C … Start with 160°C first, 20 second"; "Press for 15-20 seconds with medium pressure"; PES/lycra 25 s at 160 °C or 18 s at 170 °C; if stones are loose raise by 3–4 °C and 3–4 s; after peeling the film, cover with cotton and press a further 15 s. The transfer film "is made of PVC" and deforms without a cloth or Teflon cover.
- "For the application of motifs composed of different rhinestones size or of oversize rhinestones (larger than ss16) it is recommended to carry out the application on the reverse side (stones facing down) with increased pressure"; use a compensation pad (silicone foam) "when applying stones of different heights next to each other".
- Stretch: "If stretch material is used for the hotfix application it is recommended to secure the material slightly stretched on a pad prior to the application."
- Seams: "Special attention should be paid to application near buttons, zippers, seams and other protrusions"; a listed fault is "The stone/applications does not hold on seams or layered materials". "Materials that bend or move easily under application pressure are not suitable for applying big stones."
- Thin fabric: a stone "is correctly applied when the glue soaks a little through the base material and is visible on the reverse side"; put Teflon or silicone foil inside the garment so the glue does not stick front to back.
- Correct result: "a tiny amount of glue extrudes around the stone … not visible to the eye unless viewed under a magnifying glass"; too much heat or pressure makes a visible glue ring and a weak bond.
- Other methods: ultrasound (glue activated by friction heat while the stone is pressed; machine picks and places stones automatically, two items at once); hand applicator (adapter per stone size); iron at 150 °C / 300 °F.
- Care: hotfixed textiles "can be washed at 60 °C, tumble-dried, dry-cleaned and carefully ironed"; coated stones gentle cycle 30 °C, inside out, in a wash bag.

### Inferences
- Minimum spacing rule for the tool: template-based production needs a hole about 0.5–0.6 mm larger than the stone and a web between holes, giving a practical minimum centre-to-centre pitch of about stone diameter + 0.5 to 1.0 mm (e.g. ss16: 4.4–4.9 mm). Machine pick-and-place onto tape has no web constraint, but no source gives its minimum gap.
- Stones are rigid and the glued footprint cannot stretch, so on the body the centre-to-centre spacing grows with local fabric stretch while stone size stays fixed: a layout designed flat looks sparser where the fabric is most stretched, and dense fills locally stiffen the garment. Preciosa's advice to press with the fabric "slightly stretched" exists to pre-compensate. The app should preview spacing in the worn (stretched) state and can warn where stretch × stone coverage is high.
- Seams, elastic and layered areas are weak-bond zones (uneven pressure): the tool should flag stones that straddle a seam or sit on elastic casings, and support "keep-out" offsets from seams. Stones are normally pressed on flat panels before final side-seam sewing (NAM describes panels being sewn together after crystal application), so layouts crossing side seams need a hand-applied finishing pass.
- Mesh: glue passes through open fabric, so mesh needs a release sheet inside during pressing and a lining for comfort (GK requires a nude lining under mesh bodices); large stones on unlined mesh are a durability risk.
- Mixed sizes in one transfer need either reverse-side pressing or a soft compensation pad because stones of different heights do not receive equal pressure — a reason production layouts separate large hand-set stones from the machine-set field.
- Throughput: at 170 stones per minute a 6,359-stone leotard is about 37 minutes of machine setting and a 10,000-stone leotard about 59 minutes (single head, excluding loading and pressing); at 600 per minute, about 11 and 17 minutes.

### Gaps

- No source for hand-stoning labour rates (stones per hour), the make/model of GK's machines, or machine minimum stone spacing and placement accuracy.

## 9. Layout vocabulary — how stone patterns are designed, with rules of thumb

### Takeaway
Designers work with a small, recognisable set of moves: outline a seam, neckline, cut-out or logo; fill a shape or lettering; scatter with a density gradient ("cascade", "rainfall", "meteor shower"); build dense geometric or clustered zones; and mix two or three stone sizes and one to six colours. Published numeric rules are few — use 2–3 sizes, keep stones at least about 0.4–1 mm apart, and prefer scatter over rigid lines when a pattern has to cross a curved, stretching body.

- Plan first: sketch the design or mark dots where each stone will go for even spacing; use placement grids for straight or diagonal lines. — [Crystal Parade, 24 Jul 2025](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-use-hotfix-rhinestones-for-gymnastics-leotards) [RETAIL]
- Spacing numbers: ≥1 mm between stones for cut templates; contour rows ≥4.4 mm apart for 4 mm stones; 4.6–5 mm rows for ss16; hexagonal arrangement is the denser fill; stones sit on the border of a shape by default with an adjustable distance from the border. — [EduTech Wiki, Stitch Era hotfix](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Stitch_Era_-_creating_hot_fix_designs); [EduTech Wiki, Ink/Stitch hotfix](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/InkStitch_-_hotfix) [HOBBY]
- ss16 is the smallest size that makes "fairly good filled lettering" comfortable by hand. — [EduTech Wiki](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Hotfix_stone) [HOBBY]
- Fill families offered by layout software (which reflect what designers ask for): island fill, path fill, hatch fill, scatter effects, contour, knock-out (**search summary only**, [TRW Design Wizard Pro 2026](https://www.therhinestoneworld.com/trw-design-wizard-2026-software)); uniform, hexagonal, concentric, flexible, flat, inner-ring ([Sierra Hotfix Era](https://www.sierra-software.com/Sites/Products/HotfixEra/HotfixEra.aspx); [EduTech Wiki](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Stitch_Era_-_creating_hot_fix_designs)); edge, linear fill, radial fill ([Silhouette School](https://www.silhouetteschoolblog.com/2014/07/how-to-make-custom-rhinestone-designs.html)).

### Inferences
- A workable taxonomy for the app's brush and fill tools, each grounded in the descriptions above:
  1. **Outline / path** — single or multi-row line following a seam, neckline, keyhole/open back, cuff, colour-block edge or artwork contour (skyline outline, stones "surrounding the open back", racing stripe).
  2. **Lettering and logo fill** — team name on a cuff or sleeve, stoned logo on the back; outline plus interior fill.
  3. **Region fill** — grid, hex ("brick") or contour-following rows inside a closed shape; hex for maximum density, contour rows when the shape edge must read cleanly.
  4. **Gradient scatter** — random, non-overlapping placement whose density (and often stone size) falls off with distance from an anchor edge: the "necklace cascade", "rainfall", "meteor shower", ombré.
  5. **Clusters** — dense local groups of mixed sizes with sparse surroundings ("heavy clusters", "clustering … on the cuffs").
  6. **Radial motifs** — "bursts", stars, sunbursts; radial fill from a centre.
  7. **Linear repeats** — stripes, chevrons, lattice; these are the layouts most sensitive to distortion on the body (the reviewers' preference for scatter over linear designs is consistent with that).
  8. **Flourishes** — swirls and paisley drawn as curved paths with tapering stone size.
  9. **Accent stones** — individually placed large or shaped stones and pearls at focal points (neckline centre, sternum, cuffs).
  10. **Alternation** — two colours or sizes alternating along a path ("alternating orange and clear").
- Symmetry: classic designs are mirror-symmetric about the centre front/back; 2026 NCAA trends show deliberate asymmetry (one-shoulder, single waist cut-out), so mirror symmetry should be a toggle per element, not a global assumption.
- Size-gradation default: largest stones nearest the anchor (neckline, focal point), stepping down through 2–3 sizes as density thins — inferred from the "necklace cascade" and "rainfall" descriptions plus the 2–3 sizes rule; no source states the order explicitly.

- Dance/skating/rhythmic stoning guides (forums, video transcripts) that might give scatter-density and size-mix ratios were not retrieved because the search budget ran out.

## 11. Optical behaviour — brilliance, fire, scintillation of flat-backs and flash of sequins, for a renderer

### Takeaway
No measurements of flat-back rhinestones or sequins under arena or broadcast lighting were found; the sources give only qualitative mechanisms. Those mechanisms are enough to specify a renderer: a flat-back is a small faceted glass crown over a mirror, so each stone throws a handful of discrete, direction-dependent glints (scintillation) with slight prismatic colour (fire), AB adds angle-dependent thin-film colour on about half the crown, and a spangle is a single flat mirror that flashes all at once.

### Cited Findings
- Standard gem vocabulary: brightness = "The total internal and external reflection of white light"; fire = "The dispersion of light into the colors of the spectrum"; scintillation = "The pattern of light and dark areas, and the sparkle when moved". — [GIA 4Cs, cut](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/diamond-cut/)
- "A rhinestone sparkles because of how its facets bounce light back to your eye"; three drivers: facet count, cut design, glass quality/refractive index; MAXIMA's cut is said to be designed to look brilliant from many angles, not only straight on, and to reduce dead zones. — [Rhinestones Unlimited, science of sparkle](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/what-makes-a-rhinestone-sparkle-the-science-of-crystal-brilliance/) [RETAIL]
- Facet counts by grade: 8+8+table (Swarovski XIRIUS), 15/18 (MAXIMA), 12 (VIVA12, DMC, LUX), 10 (PriceLess), 8–11 (Korean low-lead). — [EduTech Wiki](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Hotfix_stone); [Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/what-is-the-difference-between-preciosa-viva12-and-maxima-rhinestones); [Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/choose-your-quality); [The Rhinestone World (search summary only)](https://www.therhinestoneworld.com/hot-fix-korean-5a-low-lead-ss10-rhinestones)
- The back is a mirror: Swarovski "platinum foiling for extra brilliance and protection"; Preciosa a genuine-silver reflective layer under a protective layer; weak foil can cloud, dull or separate under heat, moisture and wear. — [Swarovski 2018 collection (PDF)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0086/3023/9295/files/Swarovski_Non_Hotfix_Collection.pdf?2186); [Rhinestones Unlimited, underside](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/the-underside-engineering-of-preciosa-chaton-rose-maxima/); [Rhinestones Unlimited, washing](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/do-rhinestones-survive-the-washing-machine/)
- Glass index: about 1.5 for ordinary glass, 1.7–1.8 for lead glass (higher dispersion); lead-free crystal uses barium/zinc/potassium oxides; Preciosa claims a "higher refractive index than similar competing products" without a number. — [Wikipedia "Lead glass"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_glass); [Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/why-preciosa-is-the-leader-in-luxury-crystal-rhinestone-production/)
- AB: thin-film interference from a vacuum-deposited, "transflective" metal layer on about 50% of the surface (100% for AB 2X); pastel pink/blue shift on light stones, metallic on dark stones; said to stand out under stage lights. — [OD's Jewellers](https://www.odsjewellers.com/pages/swarovski-aurora-borealis-coating-guide); [Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-use-hotfix-rhinestones-for-gymnastics-leotards)
- Size effect: "The bigger the stone, the flashier the shine"; many small stones give a "dazzling effect"; random size mixes make light bounce unpredictably. — [Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/rhinestones-101-what-size-rhinestone-do-i-need/)
- Equivalence used by the trade: a 5 mm spangle has the "visual impact" of an ss20 rhinestone. — [ColDesi](https://shop.coldesi.com/products/prospangle-spangle-transfer-machine-prospangle)
- Sequins stitched at one point dangle and catch more light; faceted sequins are made to increase reflectivity. — [Wikipedia "Sequin"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequin)
- Context: heat-pressing dulls foil/hologram fabric under the stones, and designers now pair crystals with matte fabrics. — [GK Resilient #4947S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/qxli); [Marie Claire](https://www.marieclaire.com/fashion/team-usa-olympic-gymnastics-leotards-swarovski-crystals/)

### Inferences
- **Flat-back stone model.** Light enters through the table or a crown facet, reflects off the planar mirror base, and exits through the same or another facet. Table-in/table-out behaves like a tilted plane mirror under glass (bright, white, tight highlight that tracks the fabric normal — the "brightness" term). Paths that enter and leave through differently inclined facets act as a prism: they are deviated by tens of degrees and slightly spread by wavelength, giving small coloured glints ("fire") at angles away from the mirror direction. With N crown facets there are on the order of N² such in/out combinations, so each stone has a sparse set of discrete bright directions; as the athlete moves, individual stones switch on and off (scintillation). More facets mean more, smaller, more frequent flashes; fewer facets mean fewer, bigger flashes. Fire is weak in lead-free crystal (index near 1.5–1.6) and short path lengths, so colour fringes should be subtle except with AB.
- **Practical real-time approach.** At close range, render true facet geometry (table plus 8–18 crown facets) with a mirror base and screen-space reflection of light sources; at garment scale, replace each stone with a small set of precomputed lobe directions per stone type (a "glint lobe table") evaluated against the arena's point-like light sources, with a per-stone random rotation about its normal so neighbouring stones do not flash in unison. Brightness should be allowed to exceed display white so bloom/lens glare reads as sparkle, as it does on broadcast cameras.
- **AB.** Apply an angle-dependent thin-film tint (magenta/blue/gold shifting with incidence) to roughly half of the crown facets and to reflections from them; on dark body colours treat the coated facets as coloured metal.
- **Spangles and sequins.** A heat-applied spangle is a flat disc in the fabric plane: one specular lobe along the mirror direction of the local fabric normal, so whole areas flash together as the body surface sweeps through the mirror angle (a "sheet" flash rather than a crystal's pin-point twinkle). Holographic finishes add diffraction: the flash is rainbow-coloured and visible over a wider angular range. Sewn, cupped or dangling sequins add normal variation and therefore more frequent, smaller flashes.
- **Arena lighting.** Sparkle needs small, intense, well-separated sources; under very diffuse light stones look like flat mirrors of the ceiling. The renderer's lighting presets should therefore include arrays of discrete arena fixtures rather than only an environment dome.
