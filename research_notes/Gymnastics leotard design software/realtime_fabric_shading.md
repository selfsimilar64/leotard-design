## 1. PBR foundation — is metallic-roughness GGX enough, where does it fail for cloth, and what do the major material models provide?

### Takeaway
A GGX metallic-roughness base with multiple-scattering compensation is the correct foundation but is not sufficient alone: every production model surveyed adds a separate sheen/fuzz lobe, tangent-driven anisotropy, a coat, thin-film Fresnel and (for sheers) thin-walled diffuse transmission or coverage. By 2026 these have converged on the OpenPBR Surface layer stack (v1.1.1, April 2026); the glTF KHR extensions cover the same ground except diffuse transmission (still Release Candidate, not ratified) and an LTC-based fuzz.

### Cited Findings

**Baseline microfacet model and its energy problem**
- [Engine] Filament's standard ("lit") model: `D_GGX = α² / (π((n·h)²(α²−1)+1)²)`, height-correlated Smith visibility `V = 0.5 / (NoL·√(NoV²(1−α²)+α²) + NoV·√(NoL²(1−α²)+α²))`, Schlick `F = f0 + (f90−f0)(1−v·h)⁵`, Lambert diffuse, `α = perceptualRoughness²`. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Engine] Single-scattering microfacet BRDFs lose energy at high roughness. Filament compensates by scaling the specular lobe by `1 + f0·(1/r − 1)`, `r` being the DFG lookup's second channel; the document presents this as a simplification (credited to Lagarde and Golubev) of Kulla and Conty's compensation lobe. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Engine]/[PR] three.js uses Fdez-Agüera's image-based formulation: `FssEss = F·a + f90·b`, `Ems = 1 − (a+b)`, `Favg = F + (1−F)/21`, `Fms = FssEss·Favg / (1 − Ems·Favg)`, citing "A Multiple-Scattering Microfacet Model for Real-Time Image Based Lighting" (JCGT 8(1), 2019). — [three.js physical BRDF source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js); r186 (24 Sep 2026) reworked and optimised this path and fixed iridescence IBL multi-scattering — [three.js r186 release notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186)
- [Course] Kulla and Conty's talk "Revisiting Physically Based Shading at Imageworks" (SIGGRAPH 2017 Physically Based Shading course) is the origin of the compensation approach; the sheen paper is its supplemental. — [SIGGRAPH 2017 PBS course page](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/)

**Why a hard-surface BRDF fails for cloth, and Filament's cloth model**
- [Engine] Filament provides a dedicated cloth shading model built on Ashikhmin and Premože's velvet work: the masking-shadowing term is replaced by a smoother denominator and the Fresnel term is removed, with the specular reflectance given directly by a `sheenColor` (which allows two-tone fabrics). — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Engine] Filament cloth specular: `f_r = D / (4(NoL + NoV − NoL·NoV))` (Neubelt visibility) with the "Charlie" NDF `D = (2 + 1/α)·sin(θ)^(1/α) / (2π)`; the document says it omits the Estevez–Kulla shadowing term for cost. Earlier options it lists: Ashikhmin velvet NDF `D ∝ 1 + 4·exp(−cot²θh/α²)` and Neubelt–Pettineo's normalised form `D = (1 + 4·exp(−cot²θh/α²)/sin⁴θh) / (π(1+4α²))`. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Engine] Filament cloth diffuse: Lambert, optionally an energy-conserving wrap term `saturate((NoL + w)/(1+w)²)` with `w = 0.5`, multiplied by `saturate(subsurfaceColor + NoL)`; in that path N·L is applied separately to the specular lobe. Shipped shader: `Fr = D·V·f0` with `f0 = sheenColor`. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html); [surface_shading_model_cloth.fs](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/main/shaders/src/surface_shading_model_cloth.fs); [surface_shading_lit.fs](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/main/shaders/src/surface_shading_lit.fs)
- [Engine] Cloth model parameters: standard properties minus `metallic` and `reflectance`, plus `sheenColor` and `subsurfaceColor`; iridescence is not available with the cloth shading model; subsurface colour is costly and can interfere with shadows. The two Filament documents disagree on the `sheenColor` default (0.04 in the design document, square root of base colour in the materials guide). — [Filament materials guide](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html); [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Engine] Cloth under IBL: a third "DG" channel is added to the DFG lookup (uniform sampling, no Fresnel), reusing the same prefiltered environment maps, which the document itself calls a compromise. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Engine] Filament's lit model separately supports a sheen layer (`sheenColor`, `sheenRoughness`) on top of the base, attenuating the base by `sheenScaling = 1 − max3(sheenColor)·sheenDFG`. — [surface_shading_lit.fs](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/main/shaders/src/surface_shading_lit.fs)

**Disney principled BRDF (Burley 2012)**
- [Course] Presented as "Physically Based Shading at Disney" (Brent Burley, SIGGRAPH 2012 PBS course). — [SIGGRAPH 2012 PBS course page](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2012-shading-course/)
- [Course] Reference implementation parameters: baseColor, metallic, subsurface, specular, roughness, specularTint, anisotropic, sheen, sheenTint, clearcoat, clearcoatGloss. Sheen is an additive Schlick-weighted grazing term `Fsheen = FH·sheen·mix(1, Ctint, sheenTint)`; anisotropy uses `aspect = √(1 − 0.9·anisotropic)`, `ax = r²/aspect`, `ay = r²·aspect`; clearcoat is a fixed GTR1 lobe. — [Disney BRDF Explorer source (disney.brdf)](https://github.com/wdas/brdf/blob/main/src/brdfs/disney.brdf)

**Estevez–Kulla "Charlie" sheen (2017) — lead confirmed**
- [Course] "Production Friendly Microfacet Sheen BRDF", Alejandro Conty Estevez and Christopher Kulla (Sony Pictures Imageworks), supplemental to the SIGGRAPH 2017 PBS course. BRDF `f = F·G·D / (4|ωo·n||ωi·n|)` with `D(m) = (2 + 1/r)·sin^(1/r)θ / (2π)`, `r ∈ (0,1]`, replacing Ashikhmin's inverted Gaussian. — [paper PDF](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/imageworks/s2017_pbs_imageworks_sheen.pdf)
- [Course] Shadowing: `G = 1/(1 + Λ(ωo) + Λ(ωi))`, `Λ(θ) = exp(L(cosθ))` for `cosθ < 0.5`, else `exp(2L(0.5) − L(1 − cosθ))`, `L(x) = a/(1 + b·x^c) + d·x + e`; fitted constants for r = 0: a 25.3245, b 3.32435, c 0.16801, d −1.27393, e −4.85967; for r = 1: a 21.5473, b 3.82987, c 0.19823, d −1.97760, e −4.32054; interpolate with weight `(1−r)²`. A deliberately non-physical terminator softening `Λ'(θi) = Λ(θi)^(1 + 2(1−cosθi)⁸)` makes the BRDF non-reciprocal. — [paper PDF](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/imageworks/s2017_pbs_imageworks_sheen.pdf)
- [Course] Layering is by albedo scaling: the base is scaled by one minus the sheen's directional albedo (from a table), using the minimum over incoming and outgoing directions; the authors accept the small energy loss. — [paper PDF](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/imageworks/s2017_pbs_imageworks_sheen.pdf)
- [Course] The cheaper visibility term used by most real-time engines comes from Neubelt and Pettineo, "Crafting a Next-Gen Material Pipeline for The Order: 1886" (SIGGRAPH 2013 PBS course, Ready At Dawn). — [SIGGRAPH 2013 PBS course page](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2013-shading-course/)

**LTC sheen (2022) — lead confirmed**
- [Course] "Practical Multiple-Scattering Sheen Using Linearly Transformed Cosines", Tizian Zeltner, Brent Burley, Matt Jen-Yuan Chiang, SIGGRAPH 2022 Talks: a volumetric sheen BRDF approximating scattering from normally oriented fibres, added because the earlier measured-cloth-based model lacked backward scattering; low roughness gives a fuzzy look, high roughness a dusty look. — [project page](https://tizianzeltner.com/projects/Zeltner2022Practical/)
- [Course] Implementation: the cosine-weighted BRDF is an artist colour × fitted reflectance `R` × an LTC lobe; only `R` and two matrix entries `A`, `B` are tabulated on a 32×32 grid over roughness and cos θ; reference model is an SGGX fibre-flake volume with multiple scattering; code (pbrt-v3 plugin, fitting code, tables) is Apache-2.0. — [tizian/ltc-sheen](https://github.com/tizian/ltc-sheen)

**How shipping specs and engines implement sheen**
- [Spec] glTF `KHR_materials_sheen` (ratified): `sheenColorFactor` (default 0,0,0), `sheenRoughnessFactor` (default 0), textures; Charlie NDF with `α = sheenRoughness²`; two visibility options (full Estevez–Kulla Λ fit, or the simpler `1/(4(NoL + NoV − NoL·NoV))`, which is cheaper but not energy-conserving with albedo scaling); layering `sheen_material = sheenColor·sheen_brdf + base·(1 − max3(sheenColor)·E(VdotN))`; clearcoat sits on top of sheen. — [KHR_materials_sheen](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_sheen/README.md)
- [Engine] three.js: `D_Charlie` (α = roughness², citing Estevez–Kulla), `V_Neubelt` (citing Neubelt–Pettineo), an analytic curve fit for the sheen's hemispherical albedo under IBL, and base attenuation `1 − max3(sheenColor)·max(albedoV, albedoL)` for direct light; r186 improved energy conservation for diffuse and sheen lighting. — [three.js physical BRDF source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js); [r186 notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186)
- [Engine] Unity HDRP Fabric has two material types: Cotton/Wool (described as inspired by the Imageworks cloth model) and Silk (anisotropic GGX). In source, Cotton/Wool uses `D_Charlie` with `V_Ashikhmin` (the exact Charlie visibility is commented out as too expensive), no Fresnel (F0 used as a tint), and a "FabricLambert" diffuse; Silk uses joint anisotropic GGX with Schlick Fresnel and Disney diffuse; area lights use a dedicated Charlie LTC table; cloth multiple scattering is an open TODO. — [HDRP Fabric master stack reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/fabric-master-stack-reference.html); [Fabric.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Runtime/Material/Fabric/Fabric.hlsl)
- [Engine] Unreal's legacy Cloth shading model adds a thin fuzz layer via two extra inputs, Fuzz Color and Cloth. — [Unreal shading models](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine)
- [Engine] Unreal Substrate (docs for UE 5.8; enabled by default for new projects): the Slab BSDF exposes Diffuse Albedo, F0, F90, Roughness, Anisotropy (−1…1) and Tangent, SSS mean free path and phase anisotropy, Second Roughness plus weight (a second, "haze" specular lobe), Fuzz Amount / Fuzz Color / Fuzz Roughness (described as usually used for fabric), glints, and a thin-film helper node that outputs F0/F90; operators are Horizontal Blend, Vertical Layer, Coverage Weight and Add (Add flagged as not physically plausible). Cost: evaluating two BSDFs is stated to be twice as slow as one; mobile falls back to parameter blending. — [Substrate overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine)
- [Engine] Substrate graduated to production-ready in UE 5.7 (coverage dated 12 Nov 2025), with cloth named among the layerable material types and advice to benchmark shaders. — [Digital Production, 12 Nov 2025](https://digitalproduction.com/2025/11/12/unreal-engine-5-7-foliage-pcg-and-in-editor-ai/); [GameFromScratch](https://gamefromscratch.com/unreal-engine-5-7-released/)

**OpenPBR Surface, Standard Surface, MaterialX, Blender**
- [Spec] OpenPBR Surface v1.1.1 (page dated 2026-04-17). Layer structure: `M_surface = layer(coated-base, fuzz, fuzz_weight)`; `coated-base = layer(base-substrate, coat, coat_weight)`; `base-substrate = mix(dielectric-base, metal, base_metalness)`; dielectric base mixes opaque and translucent bases; opaque base mixes glossy-diffuse and subsurface. Diffuse is energy-preserving Oren–Nayar (EON); metal Fresnel is "F82-tint". — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Spec] OpenPBR fuzz: `fuzz_weight` (0), `fuzz_color` (1,1,1), `fuzz_roughness` (0.5); recommended BRDF is Zeltner et al. 2022 (SGGX fibre microflakes fitted with LTCs); `μi·f_fuzz = fuzz_color · E_fuzz(μo, α) · D_LTC`; layering by albedo scaling `F·f_fuzz + (1 − F·E_fuzz)·f_coated-base`; the fuzz layer is non-absorbing and transmits grey, so it does not tint the base. — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Spec] OpenPBR anisotropy: `αt = r²·√(2/(1 + (1−a)²))`, `αb = (1−a)·αt` (keeps `αt² + αb² = 2α²`). Coat: `coat_weight`, `coat_color`, `coat_roughness`, `coat_roughness_anisotropy`, `coat_ior` (1.6), `coat_darkening` (1). — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Spec] OpenPBR thin-walled mode: the subsurface slab becomes an infinitesimal scattering sheet with albedo `S = subsurface_color` split into reflection `½S(1−g)` and transmission `½S(1+g)` by `subsurface_scatter_anisotropy`; `geometry_opacity` acts as a presence (cut-out) weight in thin-walled mode; shadow transmittance `T = 1 − α(1 − (1−M)·T_dielectric)`. — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Preprint] Companion paper "OpenPBR: Novel Features and Implementation Details" (Portsmouth, Kutz, Hill; arXiv 29 Dec 2025, tied to a SIGGRAPH 2025 course) documents thin film, coat darkening, fuzz and thin-walled mode, and names hazy specular and retroreflection as planned extensions. — [arXiv:2512.23696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23696)
- [Spec] Autodesk Standard Surface v1.0.1.1: `sheen` (weight), `sheen_color`, `sheen_roughness` (default 0.3), based on Estevez–Kulla; `thin_film_thickness` (nm) and `thin_film_IOR` (1.5) based on Belcour–Barla; `thin_walled` turns subsurface into diffuse transmission; `specular_anisotropy` / `specular_rotation`. — [Standard Surface specification](https://autodesk.github.io/standard-surface/)
- [Spec] MaterialX 1.39 PBR spec (document dated 5 June 2026 when fetched): `sheen_bsdf` with `mode` = `conty_kulla` or `zeltner`; thin-film via `thinfilm_thickness` (nm) / `thinfilm_ior` on `dielectric_bsdf`, `conductor_bsdf`, `generalized_schlick_bsdf` (citing Belcour 2017); `oren_nayar_diffuse_bsdf` with `energy_compensation` switching to EON; `translucent_bsdf`; `subsurface_bsdf`; a `retroreflective` flag (citing Portsmouth 2026); shading models listed include Standard Surface, UsdPreviewSurface, glTF PBR and OpenPBR Surface. — [MaterialX PBR specification](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/MaterialX/blob/main/documents/Specification/MaterialX.PBRSpec.md)
- [Engine] Blender (manual for 5.2 LTS): the Principled BSDF is based on OpenPBR; order is base (metal / diffuse / subsurface / transmission) → specular → thin film → coat → sheen on top; sheen is described as very small fibres or dust; thin film, diffuse roughness and anisotropy are marked Cycles-only. — [Blender Principled BSDF manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/shader_nodes/shader/principled.html)

### Inferences
- GGX alone fails for cloth in seven specific ways that matter here: (1) no grazing-angle forward/back-scatter from fibres (needs a sheen lobe); (2) fabric highlights are tinted by the dye rather than following dielectric Fresnel (needs a colour-driven sheen/spec tint); (3) rough single-scatter GGX darkens matte fabrics unless compensated; (4) thread or pile direction makes highlights anisotropic; (5) porous and sheer fabrics need coverage and diffuse transmission; (6) sub-pixel yarn relief aliases unless the NDF is filtered; (7) filament fabrics show a sharp highlight on a broad one, which one lobe cannot express (hence Substrate's second roughness and OpenPBR's planned hazy specular).
- Smooth nylon/spandex tricot is closer to a hard surface than the cotton/wool cases these cloth models were built for: continuous filaments, little protruding fibre. The expected best fit is therefore GGX-dominant (with anisotropy and a low-weight sheen), not a sheen-dominant cloth model; velvet is the opposite.
- The safest authoring parameterisation is OpenPBR's (it is the only one where base, fuzz, coat, thin film and thin-walled transmission are all defined in one energy-consistent stack), stored so that a glTF-extension subset can be exported; the Charlie lobe is the pragmatic runtime choice because every web engine already ships it, with Zeltner's LTC fuzz as an upgrade for velvet.
- Filament's own docs disagreeing on the sheenColor default is a reminder that cloth defaults are not standardised; material presets must be calibrated per renderer, not copied between engines.

## 2. Cloth-specific appearance models from research (woven / knitted, real-time, neural) — are any needed for smooth warp-knit tricot lycra?

### Takeaway
Research cloth models are either woven-only, offline, or real-time only on high-end discrete GPUs (the best neural woven model reports roughly 60 fps at 1080p on an RTX 3090 and explicitly excludes knits). I found no published model or measurement specific to nylon/elastane warp-knit tricot; a well-parameterised general model (anisotropic GGX + sheen + scanned normal/roughness maps) is the practical choice, with research models useful only as offline references.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] "Real-time Neural Woven Fabric Rendering", Xiang Chen, Lu Wang, Beibei Wang (SIGGRAPH 2024 Conference Proceedings): a lightweight network for range queries of fabric BSDFs built on the Zhu et al. 2023 surface-based model; one network covers plain, twill and satin weaves; about 60 fps at 1920×1080 on an RTX 3090; network under 5 MB; training about 4 days on one RTX 3090. — [arXiv:2406.17782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17782); [HTML version](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17782v1)
- [PR] Stated limitations of that method: knitted fabrics are not supported; only three weave types; no importance sampling; energy conservation not guaranteed; delta transmission and glint-like perturbation excluded; point/directional lights only. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17782v1)
- [PR] Models that paper builds on or cites (all verified as cited there): Adabala et al. 2003 "Real-time rendering of woven clothes"; Irawan and Marschner 2012 "Specular reflection from woven cloth"; Sadeghi et al. 2013 "A practical microcylinder appearance model for cloth rendering"; Zhao et al. 2011 (micro-CT volumetric fabric models); Khungurn et al. 2016 "Matching Real Fabrics with Micro-Appearance Models"; Aliaga et al. 2017 "An Appearance Model for Textile Fibers"; Montazeri et al. 2020 "A practical ply-based appearance model of woven fabrics"; Jin et al. 2022 "Woven Fabric Capture from a Single Photo"; Wang et al. 2022 "SpongeCake: A Layered Microflake Surface Appearance Model"; Zhu et al. 2023 "A Realistic Surface-Based Cloth Rendering Model" and "A Practical and Hierarchical Yarn-based Shading Model for Cloth"; neural prefiltering: NeuMIP (Kuznetsov et al. 2021), MIPNet (Gauthier et al. 2022), Zeltner et al. 2024 "Real-Time Neural Appearance Models". — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17782v1)
- [PR] Knits: "Practical Ply-Based Appearance Modeling for Knitted Fabrics" (Montazeri, Gammelmark, Jensen, Zhao; EGSR 2021 DL-only track) stores knit plies with fibre detail in normal and tangent maps and names level-of-detail for interactive use as future work. — [arXiv:2105.02475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.02475)
- [PR] "A Texture-Free Multi-Scale Model for Surface-Based Rendering of Knitted Fabrics" (Khattar, Aubry, Yan, Montazeri; Computer Graphics Forum vol. 45, 2026): stitches as parametric thick curves mapped onto the mesh, curvature-aware parallax mapping, multi-scale aggregation over the texture footprint. — [Eurographics Digital Library](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf70356)
- [Preprint] "Neural Appearance Model for Cloth Rendering" (Soh, Montazeri; arXiv Nov 2023, revised Aug 2024): neural aggregated BSDF for woven and knitted fabric aimed at accelerating path tracing of fibre-level models, not at 60 fps rasterisation. — [arXiv:2311.04061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04061)
- [PR] Real-time yarn/fibre-level rendering: Wu and Yuksel, "Real-time Fiber-level Cloth Rendering" (I3D 2017) and "Real-time Cloth Rendering with Fiber-level Detail" (IEEE TVCG 2017); "Real-Time Knit Deformation and Rendering" (Huang, Shi, Wang, Qiu, Yang, Wu; SIGGRAPH 2025) reports 1.05 ms simulation and 23.6 ms rendering on an RTX 3090 and roughly 120,000× speed-up over a path-traced reference. — [Kui Wu publications](https://kuiwuchn.github.io/); [project page](https://kuiwuchn.github.io/rtstitch.html)
- [Course] Film practice: "Volumetric Skin and Fabric Shading at Framestore" (Walster, SIGGRAPH 2017 PBS course). — [SIGGRAPH 2017 PBS course page](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/)

### Inferences
- None of the verified research models is both knit-capable and cheap enough for integrated graphics: the neural woven model needs an RTX 3090 for ~60 fps and excludes knits; the yarn-level knit renderer spends 23.6 ms per frame on the same card, which already exceeds a 16.7 ms frame.
- For tricot, loop structure is sub-millimetre and sub-pixel at garment-viewing distance, so its contribution reduces to (a) a mean NDF with some anisotropy and (b) a normal/roughness detail map visible only in close-up. That is exactly what a general model with LEAN-style roughness filtering represents, so a specialised knit BSDF is not required for the interactive view.
- The research models remain valuable as offline ground truth: render a tricot swatch with a knit model (or photograph a real swatch on a cylinder under a known light) and fit the general model's roughness, anisotropy, sheen and normal-map amplitude to it.
- If close-up "macro" inspection becomes a product requirement, the Khattar et al. 2026 texture-free parametric-stitch approach is the most relevant direction (surface-based, multi-scale, no stored yarn geometry), but its real-time cost is unreported in what I could read.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed BRDF measurement or dedicated appearance model for nylon/spandex warp-knit tricot was found. With the search budget exhausted this was not exhaustively searched; treat as "not found", not "does not exist".
- Zhu et al.'s 2024 multi-scale surface-based cloth model (a lead from memory) and the full text of Zhu et al. 2023 could not be fetched (ACM DL returned 403); both are known to me only through citations.
- The Eurographics page for Khattar et al. 2026 returned 403 on direct fetch; details above come from the search-result abstract, and its performance and deformation handling are unconfirmed.

## 3. Shiny lycra — specular sheen, anisotropy, the "wet look", and stretch-dependent appearance

### Takeaway
Shiny lycra is best modelled as a low-to-mid-roughness anisotropic GGX dielectric (optionally with a second, broader lobe), and wet-look as the same base under a smooth coat; all the needed lobes are standard and cheap. Stretch-dependent lightening and show-through have essentially no rendering literature and no documented support in the material formats checked: the only relevant research changes yarn geometry with strain, so this must be an in-house model calibrated against stretched swatches.

### Cited Findings
- [Engine] Anisotropic GGX parameterisations in use: Filament `αt = α(1 + anisotropy)`, `αb = α(1 − anisotropy)` with anisotropy in [−1, 1]; glTF `αt = mix(α, 1, strength²)`, `αb = α`; OpenPBR `αt = r²√(2/(1+(1−a)²))`, `αb = (1−a)αt`. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html); [KHR_materials_anisotropy](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_anisotropy/README.md); [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Engine] Anisotropy under IBL is approximated by bending the reflection vector: `aT = cross(bitangent, v)`, `aN = cross(aT, bitangent)`, `bentN = normalize(mix(n, aN, anisotropy))`, `r = reflect(−v, bentN)`; Filament calls this a rough approximation, and glTF recommends extra samples along the tangent for shinier bases. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html); [KHR_materials_anisotropy](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_anisotropy/README.md)
- [Engine] Precedent for shiny filament fabric as plain anisotropic GGX: Unity HDRP's Silk fabric type, with a tangent input that stretches highlights along a chosen direction. — [HDRP Fabric master stack reference](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@17.0/manual/fabric-master-stack-reference.html)
- [Engine] Dual-lobe specular is available as Substrate's Second Roughness and weight, and Filament's `secondRoughness` / `secondRoughnessWeight` properties. — [Substrate overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine); [Filament materials guide](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html)
- [Engine] Clear coat is the documented way to get a wet surface without a second mesh: three.js lists wet surfaces among clearcoat use cases; Filament's coat is an isotropic dielectric (IOR 1.5, F0 0.04) with GGX `D`, Kelemen visibility `1/(4·(l·h)²)`, base response attenuated by `(1 − Fc)`, base F0 remapped for the coat interface, and base roughness raised to at least the coat's; three.js and Filament both allow a separate coat normal map. — [MeshPhysicalMaterial docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html); [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html); [surface_shading_lit.fs](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/main/shaders/src/surface_shading_lit.fs)
- [Spec] OpenPBR models coat absorption and a `coat_darkening` control, i.e. the darker, more saturated look of a coated substrate is part of the standard model. — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [PR] Stretch in research is handled geometrically: "Mechanics-Aware Deformation of Yarn Pattern Geometry" (Sperl, Narain, Wojtan; SIGGRAPH 2021) uses triangle strains to interpolate precomputed yarn geometry, reproducing knit loops tightening under stretch, and can run in real time on top of a mesh simulation; code is public. — [project page](https://visualcomputing.ist.ac.at/publications/2021/MADYPG/)
- [PR] "Real-Time Knit Deformation and Rendering" (SIGGRAPH 2025) likewise changes yarn-level structure under stretching and shearing, at 23.6 ms render time on an RTX 3090. — [project page](https://kuiwuchn.github.io/rtstitch.html)
- [Spec] The apparel material format U3M 1.1 has no strain-dependent visual property: its per-side properties are alpha, anisotropy value/rotation, basecolor, clearcoat (value, normal, roughness), displacement, ior, metalness, normal, roughness, sheen value/tint, specular value/tint, subsurface (colour, radius, value) and transmission, each a constant plus optional texture. — [U3M 1.1 JSON schema](https://github.com/vizoogmbh/u3m/blob/master/u3m1.1/spec/u3m_schema.json)
- [Vendor] A Style3D article states that performance knits with high spandex content need tension-recovery simulation and often manual calibration against physical swatches; it does not describe appearance changing with stretch. — [Style3D blog](https://www.style3d.com/blog/?p=11922)

### Inferences
- Proposed starting parameters (to be calibrated, not measured): matte nylon/spandex — roughness 0.55–0.75, anisotropy 0–0.2, sheen weight 0.1–0.3; shiny tricot — roughness 0.30–0.45, anisotropy 0.3–0.6 aligned with the knit's wale/course direction in pattern space, optional second lobe (roughness ≈ 0.6, weight ≈ 0.3), little or no sheen; wet-look — the shiny base at roughness 0.25–0.35 under a coat of weight 0.6–1.0, roughness 0.05–0.15, with the knit normal map on the base and a much smoother coat normal.
- The tangent field should come from the pattern piece's UV (grain line), not from mesh tangents, so anisotropy follows the fabric when a designer rotates a panel on the bolt.
- Stretch model (un-sourced engineering proposal). Take the per-triangle deformation from pattern space to the draped body, principal stretches λ1 ≥ λ2 and area ratio J = λ1·λ2 (available from the drape solver, or in-shader from screen-space derivatives of position and UV). Then: cover factor `c = clamp(c0·J^(−k))` with k fitted between 0 and 1; opacity `α = c` at normal incidence; colour `= α·fabric + (1−α)·T·underlayer`; normal-map amplitude scaled by 1/λ along each principal direction; anisotropy strength increased with λ1/λ2 − 1 along the λ1 direction; roughness reduced slightly as yarns straighten. A second term should lerp printed colour toward the undyed core colour with strain, because sublimation dye sits on the fibre face. Every coefficient needs fitting from photographs of swatches at known strain (for example 0, 25, 50, 75 %) over black and skin-tone backings.
- Because a leotard is skin-tight, the under-layer is known (skin or lining at essentially zero gap), so show-through can be composited in the fabric's own shader rather than with real transparency (see section 7).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or vendor documentation was found that models colour lightening or opacity loss of knit fabric as a function of strain for rendering. The search budget ran out before this could be searched from more angles (textile-science literature on cover factor and "grin-through" would be the next place to look).
- Whether CLO, Browzwear VStitcher or Style3D alter material appearance from their strain/tension maps could not be checked (their help-centre pages were not reachable without search). Recalled, unverified: these tools visualise strain as diagnostic heat maps rather than feeding it into the material.
- The trade meaning of "wet look" (a high-gloss, often coated, nylon/spandex) and "Mystique" (foil-dot printed nylon/spandex) is assumed from general textile knowledge, not verified from a mill source this session.
- No measured BRDF for nylon/spandex tricot (relaxed or stretched) was found.

## 4. Metallic foil-dot fabrics and holographic foil — patterned metal over a dyed base; diffraction models and cheaper approximations

### Takeaway
Foil-dot fabric is a spatial mix of a metal BRDF and the dyed-fabric BRDF driven by a mask, which every surveyed model supports directly (metalness mix in OpenPBR, horizontal blend in Substrate). Holographic foil is a spatially varying one-dimensional diffraction grating: peer-reviewed work shows it can be captured as per-point grating orientation and period maps and rendered in real time, and the classic first-order diffraction shader (Stam 2004) is cheap enough for WebGL2; the common game "rainbow ramp" tricks are ad hoc.

### Cited Findings
- [Spec] In OpenPBR the base substrate is a statistical mix of dielectric and metal weighted by `base_metalness`, so a metalness mask texture is the standard representation of a patterned metallic layer; the metal uses F82-tint Fresnel driven by `base_color` and `specular_color`. — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Engine] Unreal Substrate's Horizontal Blend linearly mixes two slabs across a surface by a mask, and Coverage Weight controls partial coverage; each additional BSDF per pixel adds proportional lighting cost. — [Substrate overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine)
- [PR] "Acquiring Spatially Varying Appearance of Printed Holographic Surfaces" (Toisoul, Dhillon, Ghosh; ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH Asia 2018): holographic surfaces (the paper's examples include gift bags, holographic paper, clothing and security holograms) are one-dimensional gratings whose period and orientation vary across the sample; two capture methods (1–5 flash photographs assuming a repeated tile, or ≥ 4 polarisation images) recover per-point orientation and relative period, which then reproduce the effects in real time, including under environment lighting. — [project page](https://wp.doc.ic.ac.uk/rgi/project/acquiring-spatially-varying-appearance-of-printed-holographic-surfaces/)
- [PR] "Practical acquisition and rendering of diffraction effects in surface reflectance" (Toisoul, Ghosh; ACM TOG 36(5), 2017): data-driven real-time rendering from a first-order Huygens–Fresnel approximation with one precomputed diffraction lookup table, measured from a flash photograph; shown under complex lights and environments. — [project page](https://wp.doc.ic.ac.uk/rgi/project/practical-acquisition-and-rendering-of-diffraction-effects-in-surface-reflectance/)
- [Course/Book] Jos Stam, "Simulating Diffraction", GPU Gems (2004): for grating spacing `d`, tangent `T` along the grooves and un-normalised half vector `H = L + V`, reinforced wavelengths are `λ = d·|u|/n` with `u = T·H`; colour is the sum over orders n (about 7–8) of a three-bump "rainbow" function; at `u = 0` an anisotropic Ward-style highlight is added. — [GPU Gems chapter 8](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-8-simulating-diffraction)
- [Ad hoc] Alan Zucconi's 2017 tutorial series implements the same grating equation `d·|sinθL − sinθV| = n·λ` in a Unity surface shader, sums orders 1–8, converts wavelength to RGB with a cheap fitted spectral function and adds the clamped result to standard PBR shading; it is explicitly game-oriented. — [CD-ROM shader, part 1](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2017/07/15/cd-rom-shader-1/)
- [PR] Image-based lighting of iridescent microfacet BRDFs is addressed by Kneiphof, Golla and Klein (2019), cited in the glTF iridescence specification's references. — [KHR_materials_iridescence](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_iridescence/README.md)
- [Spec] A per-texel direction field already has a standard encoding: the glTF anisotropy texture stores a tangent-space direction in RG and a strength in B. — [KHR_materials_anisotropy](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_anisotropy/README.md)
- [PR] LEAN mapping (Olano and Baker, I3D 2010) filters bump distributions so that sub-pixel ridges become anisotropic highlights and several bump layers can be combined, which is the relevant theory for foil micro-wrinkles and dot edges minifying. — [LEAN Mapping page](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/olano/papers/lean/)

### Inferences
- Foil-dot ("mystique") recipe: mask texture M (dots) in pattern space; `material = mix(dyed fabric BRDF, foil BRDF, M)` where foil = metalness 1, F0 = foil colour, roughness 0.2–0.35, its own fine crinkle normal map; add a small bevel in the normal/height at dot edges. In close-up the mask is binary so only one lobe set is evaluated per pixel; when the dots minify, the filtered mask becomes fractional and the correct result is a weighted sum of both lobes (two specular evaluations) rather than one lobe with averaged roughness and metalness. Evaluating both lobes when 0 < M < 1 costs roughly one extra specular lobe and one extra environment lookup.
- Dots do not stretch with the fabric; under strain the gaps between dots widen and coverage drops. In the stretch model of section 3 this is a mask-coverage scale of about 1/J, plus optional crack masks for all-over foil.
- Holographic recipe on top of the foil lobe: per-texel grating orientation (RG) and period (B or a constant, on the order of a micrometre). Analytic lights: Stam's sum over 4–8 orders. Environment light: for K wavelength samples and orders ±1, perturb the reflection direction along the grating tangent by about ±λ/d and sample the prefiltered environment there, accumulating with spectral weights; K = 3–6 gives 6–12 extra cube-map taps. This follows the first-order model of the cited papers but the IBL shortcut itself is my approximation.
- "Shattered glass" and similar patterns are tessellations with a different random grating orientation per cell; orientation maps for those can be generated procedurally (cell noise) and stored in the same RG encoding.
- The cheapest look-alike — a hue ramp indexed by N·V or T·H plus noise — is not physically based and will not respond correctly when the user moves lights, which is the product's stated requirement; it should be at most a low-end fallback.
- All of this is fragment-shader arithmetic and texture lookups, so it is feasible in both WebGL2 and WebGPU; cost class is medium (iridescence-like) for analytic lights and medium-high with the multi-tap IBL term.

### Gaps
- No measurement or model specific to holographic or foil-printed stretch textiles was found; the Toisoul papers cover rigid planar samples (clothing is mentioned only as an example category).
- The Bonn publication page for Kneiphof et al. 2019 did not render, so its technique and cost are unconfirmed.
- Stam's original "Diffraction Shaders" (SIGGRAPH 1999) and other leads (Werner et al. 2017 scratch iridescence) are recalled, unverified.
- No verified frame-time numbers for real-time diffraction shading on integrated GPUs.

## 5. Thin-film iridescence — the Belcour–Barla model, implementations, and parameterising pearlescent, colour-shift and dichroic films

### Takeaway
Belcour and Barla (SIGGRAPH 2017) is the universal real-time thin-film model: it is the reference for the glTF iridescence extension, OpenPBR, Standard Surface and MaterialX, and it ships in three.js, Babylon.js, Filament, Unity HDRP and Blender Cycles. Three numbers control it — film thickness (nm), film IOR and the base's Fresnel — plus a weight; pearlescent and colour-shift looks are parameter choices, whereas true multilayer dichroic films exceed a single-film model.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] "A Practical Extension to Microfacet Theory for the Modeling of Varying Iridescence", Laurent Belcour and Pascal Barla, ACM TOG (SIGGRAPH 2017): anti-aliases the thin-film spectrum so RGB real-time renderers avoid spectral aliasing, and integrates it into microfacet theory; the authors' page lists implementations in Unity's HD Render Pipeline, a community Unity port, Unreal and Blender. — [authors' page](https://belcour.github.io/blog/research/publication/2017/05/01/brdf-thin-film.html)
- [Spec] glTF `KHR_materials_iridescence` (ratified): `iridescenceFactor` (0), `iridescenceIor` (1.3), `iridescenceThicknessMinimum` (100 nm), `iridescenceThicknessMaximum` (400 nm), intensity texture (R) and thickness texture (G). The non-normative code follows Belcour–Barla: optical path difference `OPD = 2·η_film·d·cosθ₂`, interface phase shifts of 0 or π, spectral integration done analytically in Fourier space as a DC term plus harmonics m = 1, 2 evaluated through Gaussian fits of the XYZ sensitivity curves, then XYZ→Rec.709. The result replaces the base Fresnel for both dielectric and metal; the clearcoat/film interface is ignored. — [KHR_materials_iridescence](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_iridescence/README.md)
- [Engine] three.js implements that code (the shader chunk links Belcour's page; two harmonics; film IOR is blended toward the outside IOR as thickness approaches zero); properties `iridescence`, `iridescenceIOR` (1.3), `iridescenceThicknessRange` ([100, 400]) and maps; the rect-area-light path does not apply iridescence. — [iridescence_fragment.glsl.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/iridescence_fragment.glsl.js); [MeshPhysicalMaterial docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html); [physical BRDF source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js)
- [Engine] Filament lit model: `iridescence`, `iridescenceIor` (1.3), `iridescenceThickness` (400 nm); not available on the cloth model. — [Filament materials guide](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html)
- [Spec] OpenPBR: `thin_film_weight` (0), `thin_film_thickness` (0.5 µm), `thin_film_ior` (1.4); a non-absorbing film applied to both the metal and dielectric Fresnel terms; Belcour–Barla recommended. Standard Surface: `thin_film_thickness` (nm), `thin_film_IOR` (1.5). MaterialX: `thinfilm_thickness`, `thinfilm_ior` on the dielectric, conductor and generalised-Schlick BSDFs. — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/); [Standard Surface](https://autodesk.github.io/standard-surface/); [MaterialX PBR specification](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/MaterialX/blob/main/documents/Specification/MaterialX.PBRSpec.md)
- [Engine] Blender: thin film sits on the metal, transmission and specular components, thickness in nm, default IOR 1.33, Cycles only. Babylon.js: iridescence block with intensity, IOR, min/max thickness (nm) and textures; OpenPBR thin-film maps onto it. Unreal Substrate: a thin-film helper node feeding F0/F90. — [Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/shader_nodes/shader/principled.html); [Babylon PBR guide](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/masterPBR); [Babylon OpenPBR status](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/OpenPBR/); [Substrate overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine)
- [Engine] Recent correctness work: three.js r186 fixed iridescence IBL multi-scattering and a transposed XYZ→Rec.709 matrix. — [r186 notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186)

### Inferences
- Parameter recipes (starting points): pearlescent/"shimmer" — film over a pale dielectric base with a strong diffuse component, weight 0.2–0.5, thickness 250–400 nm modulated by a low-frequency noise map, film IOR 1.3–1.5, so colour appears only in the highlight; colour-shift ("chameleon") foil — film at full weight over a metallic base, constant thickness chosen for the target hue pair (roughly 300–600 nm), low roughness; oil-slick/rainbow — thickness ramped across UV by a gradient texture.
- A single-film model produces broad, pastel interference bands. Commercial dichroic and many "iridescent film" laminates are multilayer stacks with narrow, saturated bands and a transmitted colour complementary to the reflected one. Options: (a) accept the single-film approximation; (b) fit a small view-angle colour table (reflectance vs. cos θ) from photographs and use it in place of Fresnel — data-driven but ad hoc; (c) for sheer dichroic film, combine thin-film reflection with thin-walled transmission tinted by the complement.
- Cost: one evaluation per pixel for IBL (at N·V) plus one per analytic light (at V·H), each a handful of exponentials and cosines per channel — low-to-medium, fine in WebGL2.
- The film also needs spatial variation in thickness (stretch thins the laminate, wrinkles change the angle) to avoid looking like uniform plastic; a thickness texture in the G channel is already standard.

### Gaps
- No measured data or published parameters for commercial iridescent/holographic lycra laminates were found.
- A real-time multilayer (dichroic) interference model was not searched for (budget exhausted); none is cited above.
- Unity HDRP's exact iridescence parameter names were not re-fetched; its use of the model is confirmed only by the authors' page.

## 6. Velvet (smooth and crushed stretch velvet) — sheen/fuzz lobes, pile direction, flow maps, shells and fins

### Takeaway
Velvet is the one family where the sheen/fuzz lobe carries the appearance and GGX is secondary: use a strong Charlie or (better) LTC fuzz lobe over a dark diffuse base, tilt the lobe by a pile-direction field, and drive that field with a flow map for crushed velvet. Shell rendering (Lengyel et al. 2001) is the established real-time way to show actual pile in close-up but multiplies fill cost, so it should be a close-up-only option.

### Cited Findings
- [Course] The Estevez–Kulla sheen targets velvet-like materials whose response comes from micro-fibres mostly oriented along the normal, giving back-scattering and a soft grazing highlight; lower roughness concentrates the lobe at grazing angles. — [Estevez and Kulla 2017](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/imageworks/s2017_pbs_imageworks_sheen.pdf)
- [Engine] Filament's cloth model descends from Ashikhmin's velvet distribution and exposes a sheen colour precisely so that two-tone fabrics can be reproduced; its wrap-diffuse "subsurface colour" softens the terminator. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Course/Spec] The LTC fuzz adds the multiple scattering and backward scattering the earlier model lacked; in OpenPBR's wording low `fuzz_roughness` corresponds to thin normal-aligned fibres (high-sheen fabric) and high roughness to a dusty look. — [Zeltner et al. 2022](https://tizianzeltner.com/projects/Zeltner2022Practical/); [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Engine] Unity's Cotton/Wool type and Unreal's fuzz inputs are the shipping-engine equivalents; Unity additionally fits a Charlie LTC so the lobe works with area lights. — [Fabric.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Runtime/Material/Fabric/Fabric.hlsl); [Substrate overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine)
- [PR] "Real-time fur over arbitrary surfaces" (Lengyel, Praun, Finkelstein, Hoppe; I3D 2001): fur is drawn as concentric semi-transparent shells over the surface, with fin polygons added near silhouettes, following a global direction field; view, lighting, colour, length and direction can be changed interactively. — [project page](https://hhoppe.com/proj/fur/)
- [Engine] Babylon.js ships a shell-based Fur material: the mesh is drawn multiple times with a shader offset, with spacing, density, gravity and a quality (shell count, 30 in the example) parameter; cost rises with shell count and mesh complexity. — [Babylon Fur material](https://doc.babylonjs.com/toolsAndResources/assetLibraries/materialsLibrary/furMat)
- [Spec] A standard texture encoding for a direction field exists (glTF anisotropy texture: RG direction, B strength). — [KHR_materials_anisotropy](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_anisotropy/README.md)

### Inferences
- Smooth stretch velvet recipe: diffuse base at the dye colour (kept dark, since most brightness comes from the fuzz), fuzz/sheen weight near 1 with roughness 0.3–0.5 and a sheen colour lighter than the base, weak rough GGX (roughness ≥ 0.8), and no clearcoat. With Charlie this is two lobes; with the LTC fuzz it is one table lookup and a small matrix transform per light.
- Pile-direction dependence (un-sourced technique): evaluate the fuzz lobe with a tilted normal `n' = normalize(n + k·p)`, where p is the pile (nap) direction in the tangent plane and k the lean (about 0.2–0.6). This makes the fabric lighter or darker depending on whether one looks with or against the nap, the defining visual property of velvet, and lets a designer flip nap per pattern piece.
- Crushed velvet: replace the constant p with a flow map (RG direction, B lean amount) containing patchy, high-contrast direction changes, generated procedurally (domain-warped noise) or painted; lower fuzz roughness and add a little anisotropic GGX for the glassy streaks.
- Shells for close-ups: 8–16 shells over a sub-millimetre pile height with an alpha-tested fibre-density texture; fragment cost scales with shell count, so enable only when the camera is within a threshold distance and only on velvet panels; on integrated graphics cap at about 8 shells or substitute parallax occlusion. Fins are only needed at silhouettes.
- Stretch velvet thins its pile when stretched; reuse the section 3 coverage term so the backing shows through under strain.
- Browser feasibility: sheen/LTC and tilted normals are trivial in WebGL2/WebGPU; shells are feasible in both but are the most fill-rate-hungry technique in this survey.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed real-time model for crushed velvet or for pile direction; the tilted-normal and flow-map approach is game-art practice that I could not source this session.
- Whether any web engine ships the Zeltner LTC fuzz table was not confirmed (Babylon lists OpenPBR fuzz as "to be evaluated"; three.js uses Charlie). It would be a custom node/shader.
- No performance numbers for shell rendering on integrated GPUs were verified.

## 7. Mesh and sheer fabrics — partial coverage, transparency methods, view-dependent opacity, sorting over skin, moiré, layering, shadows

### Takeaway
Treat mesh as coverage, not as blended glass: alpha-to-coverage under MSAA (or hashed alpha with temporal accumulation) writes depth, needs no sorting and survives self-overlap, while order-independent methods (weighted-blended OIT, now built into three.js r186; dual depth peeling in Babylon.js) are the fallback for genuinely translucent sheers. Because a leotard is skin-tight, the most robust option of all is to composite mesh over skin inside one shader, avoiding transparency entirely.

### Cited Findings
- [Engine] three.js material options: `alphaTest` (hard cut-out); `alphaToCoverage` (MSAA contexts only; smooths alpha-tested and clip edges); `alphaHash` (discard against a random threshold; approximates blending without sorting at the cost of grain, which TAA reduces); `transparent`/`opacity` (sorted blending); double-sided transparent objects are drawn in two passes, back faces then front faces, unless `forceSinglePass` is set. — [three.js Material docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Material.html)
- [PR] "Hashed Alpha Testing" (Wyman and McGuire, I3D 2017): a hash-based per-fragment threshold gives stable noise, keeps distant alpha-mapped geometry from vanishing, and extends to alpha-to-coverage and stochastic transparency. three.js's implementation links this paper and derives its hash scale from screen-space derivatives of position. — [NVIDIA Research page](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2017-02_hashed-alpha-testing); [three.js alphahash chunk](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/alphahash_pars_fragment.glsl.js)
- [PR] "Alpha Distribution for Alpha Testing" (Cem Yuksel, I3D 2018): preprocesses a texture's alpha so that plain alpha testing reproduces semi-transparent coverage at coarser mip levels, with no run-time change, and extends to alpha-to-coverage. — [project page](http://www.cemyuksel.com/research/alphadistribution/)
- [Ad hoc] "Computing Alpha Mipmaps" (Ignacio Castaño, 2010): alpha-tested textures lose coverage in lower mips; fix by rescaling each mip's alpha to preserve the base level's coverage (implemented in NVIDIA Texture Tools); the same issue affects alpha-to-coverage. — [article](https://www.ludicon.com/castano/blog/articles/computing-alpha-mipmaps/)
- [PR]/[Engine] Weighted, blended OIT (McGuire and Bavoil, JCGT 2013) is implemented in three.js as `OITPassNode` (merged 15 Aug 2026, in r186): opaque pass, then accumulation of weighted colour and revealage into multiple render targets, then composite; default weight is the paper's equation 9 and can be overridden; it relies on WebGPURenderer features (MRT, per-attachment blend state, shared depth); a follow-up improved MSAA support. — [three.js PR #34253](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/pull/34253); [r186 notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186); paper: [JCGT 2(2)](https://jcgt.org/published/0002/02/09/)
- [Engine] Babylon.js OIT uses dual depth peeling (since 5.0): default 5 passes supporting at most 10 transparent layers, WebGL 2 and WebGPU only, transparent meshes are rendered many more times, it does not work with refraction textures, and anti-aliasing post-processes are listed as incompatible. — [Babylon transparent rendering](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/advanced/transparent_rendering)
- [Engine] Babylon.js's non-OIT guidance for self-overlapping transparent meshes: draw order is depth-pre-pass, opaque, alpha-tested, then alpha-blended sorted back-to-front; fixes for concave self-transparency are a depth pre-pass, a separate culling pass (back faces then front faces), or facet depth sorting (correct but CPU-expensive). — [Babylon transparent rendering](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/advanced/transparent_rendering)
- [PR] "Moment-Based Order-Independent Transparency" (Münstermann, Krumpen, Klein, Peters; I3D 2018): stores log-transmittance as a few power or trigonometric moments per pixel, needs two additive passes, is fully order-independent, and also applies to shadows from transparent casters; shader code is published. — [project page](https://momentsingraphics.de/I3D2018.html)
- [Engine] Filament: blending modes opaque / transparent / fade / masked (masked enables alpha-to-coverage, threshold default 0.4) / add / multiply / screen; `transparency: twoPassesOneSide | twoPassesTwoSides` to reduce sorting artefacts; `alphaToCoverage` only meaningful with MSAA; transparent and coloured shadows are listed features. — [Filament materials guide](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html); [Filament README](https://github.com/google/filament)
- [Spec] WebGPU exposes `alphaToCoverageEnabled` in the pipeline's multisample state, and multisampled textures must have a sample count of 1 or 4. — [MDN createRenderPipeline](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUDevice/createRenderPipeline); [MDN createTexture](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUDevice/createTexture)
- [Engine] Shadows from partially transparent casters: Babylon.js requires `transparencyShadow` on the generator and offers soft transparent shadows by writing a dither pattern into the shadow map from fragment alpha (patterns can be visible; blurred exponential filtering hides them best); three.js has a `webgpu_shadowmap_opacity` example. — [Babylon shadows](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/lights/shadows); [three.js examples index](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)
- [Spec] Physically meaningful thin-sheet semantics exist in the standards: glTF diffuse transmission (RC) for thin scattering sheets; OpenPBR thin-walled subsurface split by scatter anisotropy and `geometry_opacity` as presence weight. — [KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_materials_diffuse_transmission/README.md); [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/)
- [Engine] Temporal AA and MSAA are mutually exclusive in three.js's TRAA node (MSAA must be off), which matters for choosing between alpha-to-coverage and hashed alpha. — [TRAANode source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/tsl/display/TRAANode.js)

### Inferences
- Two regimes. When holes are larger than a pixel, the fabric is a cut-out texture (alpha mask). When holes are sub-pixel, it is a homogeneous partial coverage α. A single material must cross-fade between them using the pixel footprint (`fwidth(uv)·textureSize`): mip-mapped alpha with coverage-preserving mips does this automatically, provided alpha-to-coverage or hashing (not a hard alphaTest) consumes the filtered value.
- Recommended default: 4× MSAA + alpha-to-coverage, with the coverage value dithered or hashed to get more than the five levels four samples provide. It writes depth (so AO, shadows and depth-based effects work), needs no sorting, and handles sleeve-over-torso and front-over-back overlap for free. Fallback where MSAA is unavailable or TAA is required: hashed alpha plus temporal accumulation.
- View-angle-dependent opacity (derived, not sourced): for a thin layer of thickness-bearing yarns, effective coverage rises with obliquity; `α(θ) = 1 − (1 − α₀)^(1/max(cos θ, ε))` is the Beer–Lambert-style form, which goes opaque at grazing angles as real mesh does. Apply it with the view angle for visibility and again with the light angle for the shadow the mesh casts on what is beneath.
- Layered mesh over skin or lining in one shader: `colour = α(θv)·L_mesh + (1 − α(θv))·(1 − α(θl))·L_under`, with `L_under` the skin (or lining fabric) BRDF evaluated at the same point. For a skin-tight garment the gap is negligible, so this is accurate, costs one extra BRDF evaluation, and removes the sorting problem completely; it also makes stretch-dependent show-through (section 3) a parameter change rather than a transparency effect.
- True multi-layer sheer (floating skirt panels, overlays with air gaps) needs OIT: weighted-blended OIT is one extra pass and fits integrated GPUs but mis-weights strongly coloured, nearly opaque layers at different depths (known limitation of the method; recalled, see Gaps); depth peeling is exact but costs one full garment pass per layer pair, so reserve it for a "final quality" still.
- Moiré at distance arises when hole pitch approaches pixel pitch. Mitigations in order of value: trilinear + anisotropic filtering of the alpha map with coverage-preserving mips; the footprint-based cross-fade to homogeneous α; MSAA with alpha-to-coverage; idle-frame supersampling. Avoid screen-door dithering patterns that are themselves periodic.
- Shadows cast by mesh: hashed/dithered depth writes into the shadow map with PCF or PCSS filtering give fractional shadows cheaply; for image-based lighting only, the angle-corrected coverage term above already darkens the under-layer.

### Gaps
- Per-pixel linked lists (Yang et al. 2010), depth peeling's original papers (Everitt 2001; Bavoil and Myers 2008), stochastic transparency (Enderton et al. 2010) and adaptive/multi-layer alpha blending (Salvi et al.) are recalled, unverified this session. A WebGPU "a-buffer" sample exists in the official samples repository ([webgpu-samples/sample/a-buffer](https://github.com/webgpu/webgpu-samples/tree/main/sample/a-buffer)) but I could not read its implementation; whether linked-list OIT is practical in WebGL2 (which lacks storage buffers and atomics, as I recall) is unverified.
- The JCGT page for weighted-blended OIT did not render, so its stated limitations are from memory.
- Whether three.js's `OITPassNode` runs on the WebGL2 fallback backend is not stated in the pull request.
- No measured BTDF or angle-dependent transmittance data for stretch mesh/power-net was found.

## 8. Fine detail — fabric normal/detail maps, seams, topstitching, elastic edges

### Takeaway
Fine fabric relief is carried by tiling normal (and roughness/displacement) maps authored at true physical scale, blended with garment-level wrinkle normals using Reoriented Normal Mapping; seams, topstitching and elastics are rendered as geometry edges plus texture-space decals with their own normal/height. Every one of these high-frequency normal sources must be paired with specular anti-aliasing (section 12).

### Cited Findings
- [Ad hoc, widely adopted] "Blending in Detail" (Barré-Brisebois and Hill, 2012) compares linear, overlay, partial-derivative, whiteout and UDN blending with Reoriented Normal Mapping (RNM): with base normal t and detail normal u, `t' = (t.x, t.y, t.z + 1)`, `u' = (−u.x, −u.y, u.z)`, `r = t'·(t'·u')/t'.z − u'` (normalise). RNM preserves detail on tilted bases at a cost close to whiteout blending. — [Blending in Detail](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/blending-in-detail/)
- [Spec] The apparel interchange format carries physical scale: each U3M image has width and height in millimetres and a dpi, plus normal, displacement, roughness and alpha maps per fabric side (front/back). — [U3M 1.1 JSON schema](https://github.com/vizoogmbh/u3m/blob/master/u3m1.1/spec/u3m_schema.json)
- [Engine] Separate normal maps per layer are supported where needed (Filament `clearCoatNormal` and `bentNormal`; three.js `clearcoatNormalMap`). — [Filament materials guide](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html); [MeshPhysicalMaterial docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)
- [PR] LEAN mapping supports combining several bump layers per pixel while filtering their combined distribution. — [LEAN Mapping page](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/olano/papers/lean/)
- [PR] Surface-based knit models use curvature-aware parallax mapping to keep stitch relief coherent at grazing angles. — [Khattar et al. 2026](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf70356)

### Inferences
- Texture strategy: one tiling detail set per fabric (normal + roughness + optional height/alpha) mapped in pattern space at real-world scale, RNM-blended with the mesh-level normal; because leotard fabric relief is tiny, the detail normal should fade out with distance into increased roughness (section 12) rather than shimmer.
- Seams: a seam is a geometric crease between panels; render it with (a) a slight indentation in the normal across the seam line, (b) an ambient-occlusion stripe, and (c) for flatlock/coverstitch seams typical of stretchwear, a decal strip carrying the stitch pattern's normal, height and thread colour. Decal strips are generated along seam curves in each panel's UV space so they follow pattern edits.
- Topstitching: at normal viewing distance a textured strip (procedural dashes from a signed-distance function along the seam's arc length, with a rounded-thread normal profile) is indistinguishable from geometry and costs nothing extra; switch to instanced thread meshes or parallax only for macro close-ups.
- Elastic edges and bindings: model as separate narrow panels with their own material (often shinier, ribbed); ribs are a one-dimensional normal/height pattern that is cheap to generate procedurally and benefits from anisotropy along the rib.
- All stitch and rib detail is thin and high-contrast, so author it with mip-safe widths and rely on roughness-from-normal-variance filtering to stop sparkle.

### Gaps
- How CLO, Browzwear and Style3D render topstitching and seam puckering (recalled: selectable texture-based versus 3D-geometry stitches, plus puckering normal maps) could not be verified; their documentation URLs were not reachable without search.
- I found no academic source specific to real-time seam or stitch rendering.
- Babylon.js's detail-map feature and Unreal's detail-texturing documentation are recalled, unverified.

## 9. The body under the garment — appropriate skin shading for a mannequin or avatar

### Takeaway
For a garment design tool the body is a supporting surface: a pre-integrated or wrap-diffuse skin approximation (or a neutral mannequin material) is sufficient and cheap, while screen-space diffusion methods (separable SSS, under 0.5 ms on the authors' hardware) are an optional quality tier. What must be right is skin colour, because it shows through mesh and stretched fabric.

### Cited Findings
- [Course] "Pre-Integrated Skin Shading" (Eric Penner, SIGGRAPH 2011 Advances in Real-Time Rendering): scattering is pre-integrated into a lookup so that non-local subsurface effects are captured with purely local shading. — [SIGGRAPH 2011 Advances course](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2011/index.html)
- [PR] "Separable Subsurface Scattering" (Jimenez et al., Computer Graphics Forum 2015): a single separable kernel replaces sum-of-Gaussians diffusion; two 1D convolutions, seven samples per pixel, under 0.5 ms per frame, implemented as a post-process; code on GitHub. — [project page](http://www.iryoku.com/separable-sss/)
- [Engine] Unreal's tiers illustrate the trade-off: Subsurface, Preintegrated Skin (cheaper, less accurate) and Subsurface Profile (highest quality). — [Unreal shading models](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine)
- [Engine] three.js: `MeshSSSNodeMaterial` is an experimental extension of the physical node material adding a thickness-based subsurface term (thickness colour, distortion, ambient, attenuation, power, scale); examples include `webgpu_materials_sss`, `webgpu_postprocessing_sss` and `webgl_materials_subsurface_scattering`. — [MeshSSSNodeMaterial docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshSSSNodeMaterial.html); [three.js examples index](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)
- [Engine] Babylon.js sub-surface scattering is a screen-space pre-pass with up to five diffusion profiles, documented as WebGL 2 only and adding significant GPU cost. Filament offers an approximated translucent (subsurface) shading model. — [Babylon PBR guide](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/masterPBR); [Filament README](https://github.com/google/filament)
- [Spec] Standards: OpenPBR subsurface (`subsurface_weight`, `_color`, `_radius`, `_radius_scale` default (1, 0.5, 0.25), `_scatter_anisotropy`); MaterialX `subsurface_bsdf`; glTF `KHR_materials_subsurface` is only an initial draft. — [OpenPBR specification](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/); [MaterialX PBR specification](https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/MaterialX/blob/main/documents/Specification/MaterialX.PBRSpec.md); [glTF extensions registry](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/README.md)

### Inferences
- Offer two body materials: a neutral mannequin (mid-grey or soft white dielectric, roughness about 0.5–0.6, no subsurface) that keeps attention on the garment and costs nothing, and an avatar skin (pre-integrated or wrap diffuse with a reddish subsurface tint, plus a rough specular lobe) for presentation.
- Skin tone presets must be colour-managed albedos because they feed the mesh-over-skin composite and the stretch show-through term; errors there read as wrong fabric colour.
- Screen-space SSS is a full-screen post-process and competes with ambient occlusion for the integrated-GPU budget (compare the 2.39 ms GTAO figure in section 10); make it optional and off by default.
- Most of the body is covered by an opaque garment, so body shading cost can be cut further by discarding body fragments under opaque panels (depth pre-pass or a per-panel visibility mask).

### Gaps
- No source was found on what skin model fashion-CAD tools use for avatars.
- The Burley/Christensen normalised-diffusion profile and the fast translucency approximation that three.js's SSS material resembles (Barré-Brisebois and Bouchard, GDC 2011) are recalled; the three.js docs page does not cite a reference.

## 10. Lighting — HDRI image-based lighting, analytic area/spot lights, soft shadows, ambient and specular occlusion, multiple scattering, ray/path tracing

### Takeaway
Use image-based lighting from prefiltered HDR environments as the primary light (rotation is free; switching costs a re-prefilter that can be precomputed), add a handful of LTC rectangular area lights or spot lights for arena rigs, shadow one or two key lights with PCF/PCSS-style filtering, and add GTAO-class ambient occlusion with specular occlusion. Area lights cast no shadows in three.js or Babylon.js, and path tracing in the browser is a progressive "final render" mode (now WebGPU compute), not an interactive one.

### Cited Findings
- [Engine] IBL in Filament: diffuse from 2–3 band spherical harmonics; specular from a mip-mapped prefiltered cubemap (`lod` proportional to perceptual roughness) combined by the split-sum `L ≈ (f0·DFG1 + f90·DFG2)·LD(r, α)`, with DFG in a 2D lookup indexed by N·V and roughness; clear coat adds a second lookup scaled by its Fresnel. Filament names Karis's analytic DFG fit as an alternative to the lookup; Karis's talk "Real Shading in Unreal Engine 4" is in the SIGGRAPH 2013 course (talk title verified; that it is the origin of the split-sum approximation is recalled, not re-read). — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html); [SIGGRAPH 2013 PBS course page](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2013-shading-course/)
- [Engine] three.js `PMREMGenerator`: builds a cube render target whose mips hold GGX-filtered radiance from mirror to roughness 1; minimum face size 256, mips down to 8 px; sharp levels use 256 Hammersley samples with visible-normal GGX sampling, the roughest three levels integrate a 16×16 source; entry points `fromScene`, `fromEquirectangular`, `fromCubemap`, plus shader pre-compilation; r186 replaced the separable blur with a spiral blur. — [PMREMGenerator source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/extras/PMREMGenerator.js); [r186 notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186)
- [Engine] Runtime control in three.js: `scene.environment`, `environmentIntensity` and `environmentRotation` (an Euler rotation applied to physical materials' environment lookups), with matching `background`, `backgroundRotation`, `backgroundBlurriness`, `backgroundIntensity`. — [three.js Scene docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Scene.html)
- [PR] Area lights: "Real-Time Polygonal-Light Shading with Linearly Transformed Cosines" (Heitz, Dupuy, Hill, Neubelt; SIGGRAPH 2016) approximates GGX by a linearly transformed clamped cosine that integrates analytically over polygons; code and a WebGL demo are published; line and disk lights followed in the 2017 course. — [project page](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/415-2/); [SIGGRAPH 2017 PBS course page](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/)
- [Engine] three.js `RectAreaLight`: LTC-based, PBR materials only, requires LTC table initialisation, and has no shadow support; clearcoat under rect lights was added in r183; the LTC lookup is parameterised by √α and √(1 − cos θ) on a 64-texel table. — [RectAreaLight docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/RectAreaLight.html); [three.js releases](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases); [physical BRDF source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_pars_fragment.glsl.js)
- [Engine] Babylon.js: rectangular area lights arrived in 8.0 (27 Mar 2025) and gained emission textures in 9.0; they do not cast shadows; only point, directional and spot lights do; a material handles four lights by default (`maxSimultaneousLights` raises it), and 9.0 adds clustered lighting on WebGPU and WebGL 2. — [Babylon lights introduction](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/lights/lights_introduction); [Babylon 8.0 announcement](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2025/03/27/announcing-babylon-js-8-0/); [Babylon 9.0 announcement](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2026/03/26/announcing-babylon-js-9-0/)
- [Engine] Fabric under area lights needs its own fit: Unity HDRP samples a Charlie LTC matrix for the Cotton/Wool specular term. — [Fabric.hlsl](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/Graphics/blob/master/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition/Runtime/Material/Fabric/Fabric.hlsl)
- [Engine] Soft shadows: Babylon.js offers Poisson, exponential (plain/blurred/close), PCF and contact-hardening PCSS (both WebGL 2 only; PCSS aimed at desktop, softening with distance from the caster) and cascaded maps for directional lights. Filament lists EVSM, PCSS, DPCF and PCF plus cascades and screen-space contact shadows. three.js r186 removed the PCF-soft code path (use `PCFShadowMap`; `VSMShadowMap` remains), added a `SunLight` with cascades, and has PCSS, VSM and CSM examples. — [Babylon shadows](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/lights/shadows); [Filament README](https://github.com/google/filament); [r186 notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186); [three.js constants](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/constants.js); [three.js examples index](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)
- [Engine] Shadows from the environment itself: Babylon.js 8.0 introduced IBL shadows (contributed by Adobe) and 9.0 made them update dynamically with lighting changes. — [Babylon 8.0 announcement](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2025/03/27/announcing-babylon-js-8-0/); [Babylon 9.0 part 3](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2026/04/02/part-3-babylon-js-9-0-openpbr-and-additional-engine-updates/)
- [PR] Area-light shadows with ray tracing: "Combining Analytic Direct Illumination and Stochastic Shadows" (Heitz, Hill, McGuire; I3D 2018) splits shadowed lighting into exact analytic unshadowed lighting times a stochastic, separately denoised shadow ratio, so noise is confined to shadows. — [project page](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/705-2/)
- [PR]/[Engine] Ambient occlusion: XeGTAO (Intel, MIT licence, now archived) implements Jimenez et al. 2016 "Practical Real-Time Strategies for Accurate Indirect Occlusion"; at 1920×1080, High preset: about 0.56 ms on an RTX 2060 and about 2.39 ms on Intel Core i7-1195G7 integrated graphics; optional bent normals add roughly 25 %; a 5×5 depth-aware denoiser, better with TAA. three.js r186 updated its GTAO node and added an SSAO node with depth-aware blur. — [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO); [r186 notes](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186)
- [Engine] Specular occlusion in Filament: Lagarde's approximation `saturate(pow(NoV + ao, exp2(−16·roughness − 1)) − 1 + ao)` applied to indirect specular only, a bent-normal variant (more accurate, more expensive), horizon occlusion `min(1 + dot(r, n), 1)²`, and a multi-bounce AO option that adds coloured inter-reflection (on by default on desktop). — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html); [Filament materials guide](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html)
- [Engine] Photometric units and exposure: Filament uses luminous intensity (cd) for lights and luminance (cd/m²) for IBLs, `EV100 = log2(N²/t) − log2(S/100)`, exposure `= 1/(1.2·2^EV100)`, and pre-exposes lighting so half-float buffers suffice. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Engine] Path tracing in the browser: three-gpu-pathtracer (Garrett Johnson) now states it uses WebGPU compute shaders and requires WebGPU; it supports MeshStandard/MeshPhysical materials, environment lighting, point/spot/directional/area lights with multiple importance sampling, progressive per-frame sample accumulation and a denoise/upscale example. three.js also carries a `webgl_renderer_pathtracer` example. — [three-gpu-pathtracer](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer); [three.js examples index](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)
- [Spec] No ray-tracing feature appears in Chrome's WebGPU release notes through Chrome 155–156 or on the WebGPU implementation-status page (last edited 2 Oct 2026). — [Chrome WebGPU news](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/webgpu/news); [gpuweb implementation status](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status)

### Inferences
- Rotation versus switching: rotating an HDRI only transforms lookup directions (free, every frame); switching HDRIs requires a new prefiltered cubemap. With 256-sample GGX filtering over six mip levels this is a burst of work on integrated GPUs, so ship environments pre-filtered (compressed prefiltered cubemaps) or prefilter once and cache, and cross-fade between two environments to keep the change seamless.
- IBL alone gives no cast shadows and makes fabric look flat; combine it with (a) GTAO plus specular occlusion for contact, and (b) one or two shadow-casting key lights extracted from, or matched to, the brightest regions of the HDRI.
- Arena look: a ring of rectangular area lights (LTC) reproduces the long, soft highlights of overhead sports lighting on shiny lycra far better than point lights; because web-engine area lights are shadowless, pair each with a co-located spot light's shadow map filtered with a wide PCSS kernel, or use the ratio-estimator idea with the shadow map supplying the shadow ratio.
- Sheen and fuzz lobes under area lights need a Charlie LTC (as Unity has) or the Zeltner LTC fuzz, which is natively an LTC and therefore integrates over polygonal lights with the same machinery; three.js's rect-light path appears to cover only base diffuse/specular and clearcoat (my reading of the source summary), so sheen under area lights is a custom addition.
- Budget on integrated graphics: one high-quality full-screen pass costs on the order of 2.4 ms at 1080p on Iris Xe-class hardware (the XeGTAO figure), i.e. about 14 % of a 16.7 ms frame. That supports roughly AO plus one more full-screen effect alongside forward shading; everything else should be evaluated in the material shader.
- "Final render" mode: when the camera and scene are idle, accumulate jittered frames (supersampling, soft-shadow and AO refinement) or hand off to a WebGPU path tracer for a progressive still. Custom lobes (diffraction, pile tilt, stretch) would need re-implementation in the path tracer or the still will not match the viewport.
- WebGPU is not universal (see section 14: Linux and Firefox-on-Android gaps), so the interactive pipeline should not depend on compute shaders; keep compute-only features (path tracing, clustered volumetrics) optional.

## 11. Colour and tone — linear workflow, colour management (sRGB, Display P3, HDR), tone mappers, exposure, bloom, neon/fluorescent colours

### Takeaway
For product-colour fidelity the Khronos PBR Neutral tone mapper (May 2024) is the purpose-built choice: it reproduces base colours 1:1 up to about 0.8 and only compresses highlights, whereas ACES is documented to wash out saturated colours and AgX deliberately drives bright colours toward white. Wide-gamut (Display P3) output is Baseline in WebGL and available in WebGPU, HDR output exists in WebGPU on Chrome, and fluorescent "neon" fabrics are physically outside what a reflectance-only RGB albedo can represent.

### Cited Findings
- [Engine] Reference pipeline order (Filament): scene-referred luminance → exposure normalisation → white balance → colour grading → tone mapping → transfer function (OETF) → display. — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)
- [Spec] Khronos PBR Neutral tone mapper, released 16 May 2024 for e-commerce, architecture and CAD: 1:1 colour reproduction up to a set maximum, the remaining range used to compress highlights, analytically invertible, 13 lines of GLSL; currently sRGB output only. Khronos's guidance: no tone mapping for assets without HDR lighting; filmic operators (ACES, AgX) for strongly HDR scenes or artistic looks; PBR Neutral for well-exposed photoreal PBR with accurate sRGB base colours. Adopted in model-viewer, Babylon.js, Filament, three.js and the glTF Sample Viewer. — [Khronos press release](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/khronos-pbr-neutral-tone-mapper-released-for-true-to-life-color-rendering-of-3d-products)
- [Spec] PBR Neutral formula: with `F90 = 0.04`, `Ks = 0.8 − F90 = 0.76`, `Kd = 0.15`: `x = min(R,G,B)`; `f = x − x²/(4·F90)` if `x ≤ 2·F90` else `F90`; `p = max(R,G,B) − f`; if `p ≤ Ks` output `c − f`; otherwise `pn = 1 − (1−Ks)²/(p + 1 − 2Ks)`, `g = 1/(Kd·(p − pn) + 1)`, output `(c − f)·(pn/p)·g + pn·(1 − g)`. Hue is preserved (changes stay in the plane of the colour and the white axis); every channel between 0.08 and 0.8 maps to itself minus 0.04, which reproduces a shiny dielectric's base colour exactly under unit white light; an OpenColorIO config and LUT are provided. — [PBR Neutral specification](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md)
- [Spec] ACES is stated to wash out bright yellow, green and cyan hues on sRGB displays, and omitting tone mapping causes hue skew and loss of form on shiny objects. — [Khronos press release](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/khronos-pbr-neutral-tone-mapper-released-for-true-to-life-color-rendering-of-3d-products)
- [Engine] AgX became Blender's default view transform in 4.0, replacing Filmic; it handles over-exposure better and moves bright colours toward white as cameras do; Blender notes that Filmic and AgX target the SDR 0–1 range and are unsuitable for its HDR display mode. — [Blender 4.0 colour management notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/4.0/color_management/)
- [Engine] Tone mappers available: three.js — None, Linear, Reinhard, Cineon, ACES Filmic, AgX, Neutral, Custom; Filament — GT7, PBR Neutral, AgX, ACES, filmic and a generic customisable operator. — [three.js constants](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/constants.js); [Filament README](https://github.com/google/filament)
- [Engine] three.js colour spaces: core defines sRGB and linear-sRGB; an add-on defines Display P3, linear Display P3 (usable as a working space, with P3 texture unpacking and P3 drawing buffer), linear Rec.2020, and an "extended sRGB" output that requests extended-range canvas tone mapping; r184 made render targets use the working colour space; there are `webgpu_hdr` and Ultra HDR texture-loader examples. — [three.js ColorSpaces add-on](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/math/ColorSpaces.js); [three.js releases](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases); [three.js examples index](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)
- [Spec] Browser output: WebGL `drawingBufferColorSpace` accepts `srgb` or `display-p3` (Baseline, newly available since October 2024). WebGPU canvas configuration accepts `colorSpace` `srgb` or `display-p3`, formats including `rgba16float`, and `toneMapping.mode` `standard` (clamp to SDR) or `extended` (display's HDR range); the HDR mode shipped in Chrome 129 (page updated September 2024), and Chrome 155–156 adds linear sRGB and linear Display P3 canvas colour spaces. — [MDN drawingBufferColorSpace](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGLRenderingContext/drawingBufferColorSpace); [MDN GPUCanvasContext.configure](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUCanvasContext/configure); [Chrome 129 WebGPU notes](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-129); [Chrome WebGPU news](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/webgpu/news)
- [PR] Fluorescence and gamut: Jung, Wilkie, Hanika, Jakob and Dachsbacher, "Wide Gamut Spectral Upsampling with Fluorescence" (EGSR / Computer Graphics Forum 2019): the gamut of physically valid reflectance spectra is smaller than the spectral locus because of energy conservation, so very bright saturated colours cannot be produced by reflectance alone; the print, plastics and textile industries add fluorescent dyes to extend the gamut, and the paper models colour as reflectance plus a fluorescent component. — [paper page](https://rgl.epfl.ch/publications/Jung2019Wide)
- [Engine] Exposure and bloom are defined photometrically in Filament (`EV100`, bloom threshold expressed in EV). — [Filament design document](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)

### Inferences
- Default view: linear working space, PBR Neutral, exposure calibrated so a white diffuse surface under the reference environment lands near 0.8 (the top of the operator's linear range). Under that condition a fabric swatch's on-screen colour equals its authored base colour minus the 0.04 specular floor, which is what a designer matching dye lots needs. Offer AgX as a "photographic" alternative and keep ACES out of the default path.
- A tone mapper only preserves colour under neutral white light. Coloured arena lighting or a tinted HDRI will legitimately shift product colour, so provide a neutral "colour check" lighting preset alongside the dramatic ones.
- Neon fabrics: three compounding problems. (1) Their chromaticities sit outside sRGB, so use a wide-gamut working space and output Display P3 where the display supports it, with explicit gamut mapping for sRGB screens. (2) Fluorescent dyes re-emit absorbed short-wavelength light, so apparent reflectance in the emission band exceeds what a conserving albedo allows; emulate with a "fluorescence gain" term (an albedo-like colour allowed above the conserving limit in the emission band, or an emission proportional to incident irradiance) rather than a fixed emissive, so it still responds to lighting. (3) On SDR displays the result must be compressed; on HDR-capable WebGPU canvases the extra luminance can be shown directly.
- PBR Neutral is specified for sRGB/Rec.709 SDR output only, so the wide-gamut and HDR paths need a different or extended operator; this is an open design point rather than a solved one.
- Bloom should be off or very subtle in the accurate mode (it changes local colour and contrast); if used for presentation, keep it energy-conserving and tied to exposure.
- Sublimation prints: artwork arrives in sRGB or a CMYK print profile; convert once to the linear working space via ICC, and consider a soft-proof toggle that applies the printer/ink/fabric profile so designers see the achievable gamut. Texture compression must not band or block smooth gradients, so use high-quality modes of the ratified Basis Universal texture extension or uncompressed textures for hero prints.

### Gaps
- Safari and Firefox support for WebGPU `toneMapping: extended` and for P3 canvases was not confirmed (MDN's compatibility table did not load); the status above is verified for Chrome only.
- ACES 2.0 and its behaviour for saturated colours were not checked.
- No verified source quantifies how far standard neon/fluorescent textile colours fall outside sRGB or Display P3.
- Whether three.js or Babylon.js apply PBR Neutral correctly with a Display P3 output was not verified.

## 12. Anti-aliasing and temporal stability — specular aliasing on high-frequency normals; MSAA vs TAA vs SMAA/FXAA

### Takeaway
Fabric is a worst case for specular aliasing (dense normal detail, glossy lobes, moving highlights), and the fix is to widen roughness from normal variance — baked into roughness mips for normal maps and computed from screen-space normal derivatives for geometry (Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan; shipped in Filament and three.js). For edge and coverage anti-aliasing, 4× MSAA suits this content better than TAA during interaction (and enables alpha-to-coverage); temporal accumulation is best reserved for idle-frame refinement.

### Cited Findings
- [Engine] Filament's geometric specular anti-aliasing: `variance = σ²·(|dFdx(n)|² + |dFdy(n)|²)`, `kernel = min(2·variance, threshold)`, `α² ← saturate(α² + kernel)`, with material options `specularAntiAliasingVariance` (default 0.15) and `specularAntiAliasingThreshold` (default 0.2); the shader cites Kaplanyan 2016 ("Stable specular highlights"), Tokuyoshi 2017 and Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan 2019, and applies the same filter to clear-coat and sheen roughness. — [surface_shading_lit.fs](https://github.com/google/filament/blob/main/shaders/src/surface_shading_lit.fs); [Filament materials guide](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html)
- [PR] The underlying papers: "Improved Geometric Specular Antialiasing" (Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan, I3D 2019); "Stable Geometric Specular Antialiasing with Projected-Space NDF Filtering" (Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan, JCGT 10(2), 2021, with HLSL code); "Error Reduction and Simplification for Shading Anti-Aliasing" (Tokuyoshi, Square Enix technical report 2017). — [Tokuyoshi publications](https://yusuketokuyoshi.com/)
- [Engine] three.js always applies a simpler geometric term: `dxy = max(|dFdx(n)|, |dFdy(n)|)`, `geometryRoughness = max component`, `roughness = min(max(roughness, 0.045) + geometryRoughness, 1)`, using the un-perturbed geometric normal; the same is applied to clearcoat roughness. — [three.js lights_physical_fragment](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_fragment.glsl.js)
- [Engine] Babylon.js exposes `enableSpecularAntiAliasing` for flickering highlights on glossy surfaces. — [Babylon PBR guide](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/masterPBR)
- [PR] Normal-map filtering: LEAN Mapping (Olano and Baker, I3D 2010) stores linearly filterable statistics of the bump distribution so standard mip and anisotropic filtering preserve highlight shape and turn unresolved ridges into anisotropic shading. — [LEAN Mapping page](https://userpages.cs.umbc.edu/olano/papers/lean/)
- [Course] "Rock-Solid Shading: Image Stability Without Sacrificing Detail" (Hill and Baker, SIGGRAPH 2012 Advances in Real-Time Rendering) covers shader anti-aliasing theory, pipeline implications and deferred integration; Neubelt and Pettineo's course entry links a public demo comparing normal-map filtering techniques for specular aliasing. — [SIGGRAPH 2012 Advances course](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2012/index.html); [SIGGRAPH 2013 PBS course page](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2013-shading-course/); [SpecularAA demo](https://github.com/TheRealMJP/SpecularAA)
- [Engine] Temporal AA in three.js (`TRAANode`): 32-sample Halton jitter, velocity reprojection using the closest depth in a 3×3 neighbourhood, variance clipping, depth-based disocclusion rejection; MSAA must be disabled; a documented square-pattern artefact can appear under motion with sub-pixel correction. Examples exist for TRAA, TAA upscaling, FXAA and SMAA. — [TRAANode source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/tsl/display/TRAANode.js); [three.js examples index](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)
- [Engine] Filament offers MSAA, FXAA and TAA together with dynamic resolution (with FSR upscaling). — [Filament README](https://github.com/google/filament)
- [Spec] WebGPU multisampling is limited to a sample count of 4 (or 1). — [MDN createTexture](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUDevice/createTexture)
- [Engine] Interactions to plan around: alpha-to-coverage requires MSAA; hashed alpha relies on TAA to hide grain; GTAO noise is designed to be resolved by TAA when present but has a stand-alone spatial denoiser; Babylon's depth-peeling OIT is incompatible with anti-aliasing post-processes. — [three.js Material docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Material.html); [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO); [Babylon transparent rendering](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/advanced/transparent_rendering)

### Inferences
- Three-layer specular anti-aliasing for fabric: (1) at asset import, convert each normal map's lost variance into its roughness mip chain (Toksvig/LEAN-style) so knit, crinkle and stitch detail fades into a broader highlight instead of sparkling; (2) at run time add geometric specular AA from screen-space normal derivatives for folds and seams; (3) clamp minimum roughness (three.js and Filament use 0.045) so analytic lights never produce sub-pixel highlights. Foil and wet-look, the glossiest families, benefit most.
- MSAA versus TAA for this product: the scene is a single object with thin silhouettes, fine printed detail and frequent lighting/material edits. TAA blurs fine print slightly and ghosts when shading changes without motion vectors (a light or colour change is exactly that), whereas MSAA is stable and sharp but does not address shader aliasing — which layers (1)–(3) handle. Hence: 4× MSAA + specular AA while interacting; SMAA or FXAA as the low-end fallback; no TAA in the default interactive path.
- Idle refinement: when nothing has changed for a few frames, accumulate jittered frames (16–64) for near-reference anti-aliasing, soft shadows and AO, resetting on any input. This gives "flawless" stills without temporal artefacts during interaction, and also resolves hashed-alpha grain.
- Printed designs need 8–16× anisotropic texture filtering and a slightly negative mip bias to stay sharp at grazing angles; that is independent of the edge-AA choice.
- If TAA is ever required (for example to use hashed alpha everywhere), write motion vectors for the garment and reset history on material or lighting edits.

- Toksvig's "Mipmapping Normal Maps" (2005) and CLEAN mapping (Baker 2011) are recalled leads that I did not fetch; LEAN is verified.
- The lead "Kaplanyan et al. 2016, Filtering Distributions of Normals for Shading Antialiasing (HPG)" was not confirmed; Filament's source cites the Kaplanyan 2016 work under the title "Stable specular highlights". The two may be the paper and the talk; unverified.

## 13. Material acquisition — how digital fabric libraries are produced, what maps they contain, whether stretch mills publish digital twins, DIY capture

### Takeaway
The apparel industry digitises fabrics two ways: optical scanners or scan-plus-AI services that output standard PBR maps (base colour, normal, roughness, displacement, alpha, sometimes metalness/specular), and separate mechanical testers for stretch, bend and thickness; the open U3M format bundles both. These pipelines capture ordinary matte fabric well but have no channel for diffraction, thin-film, pile direction or strain dependence, so the special leotard fabrics need hand-authored parametric materials. At least one major stretch mill (Eurojersey) publishes a digital-twin library for CLO and Browzwear.

### Cited Findings
- [Spec] U3M (Unified 3D Material), hosted by Vizoo on GitHub under BSD-3-Clause, versions 1.0 and 1.1, describes itself as a bridge between apparel software vendors. The 1.1 schema fixes the shader to "principled" and defines, per fabric side (front/back/side), constants plus optional textures for: alpha, anisotropy value and rotation, basecolor, clearcoat value/normal/roughness, displacement, ior (default 1.4), metalness, normal, roughness (default 0.7), sheen value and tint, specular value and tint, subsurface colour/radius/value, and transmission; images carry physical width/height in mm and dpi; a physics block holds thickness, weight (g/m²), fibre composition, construction and a link to a Fabric Analyzer measurement file. — [U3M repository](https://github.com/vizoogmbh/u3m); [U3M 1.1 schema](https://github.com/vizoogmbh/u3m/blob/master/u3m1.1/spec/u3m_schema.json)
- [Vendor] Vizoo: xTex hardware ("PBR texture scanners") and xTex software (capture, tile, recolour), a physX physical-property database, an on-demand digitisation service, and a U3M extension on Blender's official extensions platform. — [Vizoo](https://www.vizoo3d.com/)
- [Vendor] X-Rite: the TAC7 scanner captures colour, texture, gloss, translucency and transparency of physical samples (synthetic fabrics are mentioned) into AxF, a vendor-neutral appearance format whose renderings comprise diffuse colour, normal, specular colour, roughness, Fresnel, anisotropic rotation, alpha, height and clear-coat normal maps, with SVBRDF, car-paint and BTF variants; a Virtual Material Service offers scanning. — [X-Rite TAC7](https://www.xrite.com/categories/appearance/tac7); [X-Rite AxF](https://www.xrite.com/axf)
- [Vendor] Browzwear's Fabric Analyzer (FAB) measures physical properties — bend and drape, thickness, stretch and recovery — and applies them to the digital twin; Browzwear lists the U3M file format and a send-in digitisation/lab service producing ready-to-use digital fabrics. It measures nothing optical. — [Browzwear Fabric Analyzer](https://browzwear.com/products/fabric-analyzer)
- [Vendor] SEDDI Textura: cloud service that turns images from ordinary desktop scanners into digital textiles with "science-based" fabric physics; subscription pricing with a free starting tier; tiling and imperfection-removal tools. — [SEDDI Textura](https://textura.ai/)
- [Vendor] Frontier.cool (TextileCloud): fabrics scanned on an office scanner are converted by AI into "3D maps and physics" in a stated 3–5 minutes; testimonials mention CLO and Browzwear plugins, digitisation for adidas's mills, and one mill with more than 2,000 digitised materials. — [Frontier.cool](https://www.frontier.cool/)
- [Vendor] Swatchbook: a material marketplace and library for fashion with supplier-provided digital materials, "true to size" scans, recolouring, and 3D/AR visualisation. — [Swatchbook](https://www.swatchbook.us/)
- [Vendor] Adobe Substance 3D Sampler: AI-powered Image to Material removes shadows and generates albedo, roughness, normal and displacement; intelligent tiling; photogrammetry capture; works with or without a scanner; also builds HDR environment lights from 360° images. — [Substance 3D Sampler](https://www.adobe.com/products/substance3d/apps/sampler.html)
- [Vendor] Stretch-mill digital twins: Eurojersey (Sensitive Fabrics warp knits) runs a "3D Digital Library" presenting its fabrics as digital twins compatible with Browzwear and CLO, with access on request. Carvico's home page, by contrast, mentions no 3D library or digital twins. — [Eurojersey 3D Digital Library](https://www.sensitivefabrics.it/en/3d-digital-library/); [Carvico](https://www.carvico.com/en/)

### Inferences
- Coverage of the leotard fabric families by standard capture: matte and shiny solid lycra, sublimation prints and (with a backlit alpha capture) mesh map well onto U3M/AxF-style maps. Foil dots are partly covered (metalness + roughness masks). Holographic foil, iridescent film, velvet pile direction and anything strain-dependent have no slot in U3M and are not what flatbed-plus-AI pipelines infer, so those materials should be parametric presets tuned against reference photographs or video under a moving light.
- U3M's physics block (weight, thickness, composition, stretch test data) is useful beyond drape: stretch-test curves give the strain range over which the section 3 appearance model must be calibrated.
- Mill-published twins are tied to CLO/Browzwear ecosystems and access-controlled; a new tool should plan to import U3M (and ideally read AxF-derived maps) but cannot assume leotard-specific fabrics (mystique, hologram, stretch velvet) are available as twins.
- DIY capture that would actually work for a small team: (a) flatbed scan at known dpi for base colour and scale (flat matte fabrics only); (b) photometric stereo — fixed camera, 4–8 light directions, cross-polarised for diffuse/normal separation — for normal and roughness; (c) backlit shot for alpha/coverage of mesh; (d) repeat (a)–(c) with the swatch clamped at known strains to calibrate show-through; (e) a short video of the swatch on a cylinder under a moving point light to fit anisotropy, sheen, thin-film or grating parameters by eye or by optimisation; (f) a colour target in every shot. Then tile, de-light and build roughness mips with variance.

### Inferences
- Off-the-shelf versus custom, for the fabric families: off the shelf — matte and shiny lycra (GGX + anisotropy + sheen), wet-look (clearcoat), all-over foil (metal), thin-film iridescence, sublimation print, basic mesh (alpha-to-coverage / hash / OIT). Custom — two-lobe foil-dot mixing under minification, diffraction grating lobe and its IBL approximation, LTC fuzz and pile-tilt/flow-map velvet, shells, strain-driven appearance, mesh-over-skin single-shader compositing, variance-to-roughness mip baking, fluorescence gain.
- Every custom item is expressible as fragment-shader arithmetic plus ordinary textures, so none forces WebGPU; the features that do require WebGPU today are compute-based (path tracer, some volumetrics) and three.js's new OIT pass (stated to rely on WebGPURenderer capabilities).
- Given WebGPU's remaining gaps (Linux outside recent Chrome, Firefox on Linux/Android), a design that needs "flawless" behaviour on mainstream machines should treat WebGL 2-class features as the baseline tier and WebGPU as the enhanced tier.
- Filament's cloth shader, Unity's Fabric.hlsl and three.js's physical chunk together form a complete, permissively licensed reference set for the Charlie path; tizian/ltc-sheen is the only public reference for the LTC fuzz and would need porting to GLSL/TSL.

### Gaps
- Unreal's shader source (cloth BxDF, Substrate fuzz) is not publicly fetchable; only documentation was verified.
- Babylon.js's current patch version is not verified first-hand (a search result mentioned a 9.22.0 patch fixing area-light and OpenPBR anisotropy IBL issues).
- I did not verify whether three.js's node-material (TSL) path has feature parity with the GLSL chunks quoted above for sheen, anisotropy and iridescence, beyond the existence of matching WebGPU examples.
- The three.js WebGPURenderer manual could only be read through a search-result summary (direct fetch returned 404).

## 15. Objective synthesis — recommended technique per material family, with real-time cost and browser feasibility

### Takeaway
All eleven fabric families can be rendered at 60 fps in a browser with one forward-shaded material built from a GGX base plus optional lobes (anisotropy, sheen/fuzz, coat, thin film, diffraction) and a coverage term; no family requires a research cloth model, neural network, ray tracing or WebGPU-only features. The expensive options — velvet shells, depth-peeled transparency, multi-tap diffraction IBL, screen-space skin scattering, path tracing — should be close-up or idle-time tiers.

### Cited Findings
- Cost anchors that are actually published: evaluating two BSDFs per pixel costs twice one (Unreal Substrate); three.js's physical material costs more per pixel and each enabled feature adds cost; GTAO at 1080p High costs about 2.39 ms on Core i7-1195G7 integrated graphics and 0.56 ms on an RTX 2060; separable SSS costs under 0.5 ms on its authors' hardware; depth-peeled OIT defaults to five extra passes for ten layers; the best neural woven-cloth model reaches about 60 fps only on an RTX 3090; real-time yarn-level knit rendering takes 23.6 ms on an RTX 3090. — [Substrate overview](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine); [MeshPhysicalMaterial docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html); [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO); [Separable SSS](http://www.iryoku.com/separable-sss/); [Babylon transparent rendering](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/advanced/transparent_rendering); [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17782v1); [Huang et al. 2025](https://kuiwuchn.github.io/rtstitch.html)
- Both renderers of three.js already demonstrate the standard lobes (sheen, anisotropy, iridescence, clearcoat, transmission, alpha hash, rect area lights), which establishes WebGL 2 and WebGPU feasibility for them. — [three.js examples index](https://threejs.org/examples/files.json)
- Multisampling with alpha-to-coverage is available in WebGL (antialiased contexts) and WebGPU (4 samples). — [three.js Material docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Material.html); [MDN createTexture](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUDevice/createTexture)

### Inferences

**Pipeline recommendation (same estimate caveats).**
- Lighting: prefiltered HDRI IBL with free rotation and cached/precomputed environments; up to about four analytic lights of which some are LTC rectangles; one or two shadow-mapped key lights with PCF/PCSS-style filtering; GTAO-class AO with specular occlusion. On integrated graphics allow roughly one to two full-screen passes.
- Colour: linear wide-gamut working space; PBR Neutral default with calibrated exposure; AgX optional; Display P3 output when available; HDR canvas as an enhancement; fluorescence gain for neon fabrics; bloom off in accurate mode.
- Anti-aliasing: 4× MSAA + variance-to-roughness mips + geometric specular AA while interacting; SMAA/FXAA fallback; jittered accumulation when idle; no TAA by default.
- Quality tiers: (1) baseline WebGL 2-class — everything in the table without shells, depth peeling or multi-tap diffraction IBL; (2) enhanced — shells in close-up, WBOIT for floating sheers, bent-normal AO, screen-space skin; (3) idle/final — accumulation AA and soft shadows, depth-peeled transparency, WebGPU progressive path tracing where custom lobes have been ported.
- Worst case for the budget is a full-viewport close-up of a multi-lobe fabric (wet-look, holographic, mesh-over-skin); test that case on integrated hardware first, since the whole frame is then covered by the most expensive shader.

### Gaps
- None of the per-family cost multipliers is measured; they must be replaced by profiling on target integrated GPUs before being quoted in a report as facts.
- The suggested parameter ranges are starting points from general shading experience, not from measurements of real leotard fabrics.
- Three items central to the product have no literature behind them and are design risks rather than solved problems: strain-dependent appearance, holographic/foil stretch laminates, and fluorescent dye colour on wide-gamut/HDR displays.
- Because the web-search budget was exhausted early, recent (2025–2026) papers on real-time diffraction, sheer-fabric transmission or stretch-dependent appearance may exist that this survey did not find.
