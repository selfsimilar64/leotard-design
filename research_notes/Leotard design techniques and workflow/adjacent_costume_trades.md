# Adjacent costume trades: construction techniques and how the makers work

Reading key. [S] = seen only as a search-result snippet or search summary; the page itself was not opened. A bullet with one link is single-source unless it says otherwise. Everything linked to Spandex Simplified is one maker, Marie Porter, documented across many garments: rich and internally consistent, but one person's method. Findings are paraphrased; translations from Russian and Spanish press are mine. Project files are cited by path under `knowledge/`. Inferences appear only under the Inferences headings.

## Working hypothesis: do these makers fit a base garment, then arrange and sew shapes on it, on a form or the wearer?

### Takeaway

Only partly; it does not hold as the default. The best-documented route in skating and synchro is flat: fit lives in a paper pattern, the design is drawn on that paper, and appliqué goes onto flat pieces before the seams close. Only the stones reliably happen on a form. The on-body route is real but used for particular jobs: lines that must relate to the bust, large panels added to a finished dress, stone layouts, and cup placement. The hypothesis fits best in Latin and ballroom, where a physical fitted base exists and decoration is tried on it at fittings. "Fit first, decoration after" is well supported everywhere; "arrange shapes on the body" is not the norm.

### Cited Findings

**Against: flat paper is the default**
- Porter's skating and synchro builds all run the same way: base pattern traced to exam-table paper, design drawn on it in pen then marker, each colour section traced as its own piece, pieces glued flat onto lining and mesh, appliquéd, seams closed, stones last. None of these write-ups describes a wearer or form before the stones — [Flames](https://spandexsimplified.com/flames-skating-dress/); [Pink and black](https://spandexsimplified.com/pink-black-skating-dress/); [80s dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/80s-figure-skating-dress/); [Jazz team suits](https://spandexsimplified.com/jazz-team-synchro-suits/)
- For her Reputation bodysuit she drew everything, sweetheart line included, on flat paper; the dress form appears only for crystalling — [Reputation part 1](https://spandexsimplified.com/taylor-swift-reputation-outfit/)
- Her basic skating dress puts appliqué on cut pieces before any structural seam, and the page describes no mid-build fitting on the wearer. Fit is checked in the pattern, with a practice run in cheap fabric recommended — [Basic skating dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-ice-skating-dress/)
- Patterns get reused: she traced an earlier dress made for the same client instead of fitting anew, then mailed the finished base to the client, who stones it herself — [Art Deco dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-i-made-that-art-deco-skating-dress/)
- Many top makers cannot reach the body at all: Stephanie Miller and Luanne Williams run weekly fittings in Detroit, but out-of-state and foreign skaters rely on mail and email — [Traverse Ticker](https://www.traverseticker.com/amp/news/costume-designer-to-the-stars-on-ice). Mariia Ignateva's fittings with her dressmaker are online, and she marks up photos of the costume on mannequins — [Ice-Dance.com, May 2026](https://www.ice-dance.com/site/mariia-ignateva-sketching-the-performance/)

**For: the body or form is used when position matters**
- A skater-maker built her base dress first, then draped a mesh peacock panel on it while wearing it, pinned it, took the dress off to stitch, and spent hours trimming the excess mesh. She did this alone, with no helper — [Doctor T Designs, 2013](https://doctortdesigns.com/2013/09/28/2013-figure-dress-2-the-galloping-peacock/)
- Porter herself leaves paper for bust-framing lines: she made a quick unlined muslin, pinned the wearer into it, drew the design on one half, then moved the lines to her paper master and cleaned them up. Her reason: "I just didn't want to mess around with trying to place the lines" on a flat pattern — [Hela costume, 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/mcu-hela-costume-cosplay/)
- For an all-over stone design she taped exam paper over a finished bodysuit on a dress form, traced the side seam and elastic edges, and drew and colour-coded the colour changes there, because a bodysuit changes shape once a body is inside it. The paper sections then guided the stones — [Lover bodysuit](https://spandexsimplified.com/taylor-swift-lover-outfit/)
- Nataliya Desheva (Latin and ballroom): every dress has a skin-colour bodysuit inside a stretch net dress; it must fit perfectly before decoration; decoration is tried at fittings; stones are placed by eye without marks — [PBS Fit 2 Stitch, listing dated Jan 2026](https://video.wpsu.org/video/beaded-dance-dresses-mOFOmK/)
- Ballroom dresses start as a sketch and are then crafted by hand on mannequins matched to the dancer's dimensions [S] — [Gulf Shore Business](https://www.gulfshorebusiness.com/?p=15151); they grow from a bodysuit foundation, with the sketch as an artistic guide rather than a blueprint [S] — [Gulf Shore Life](https://www.gulfshorelife.com/?p=7796)
- In theatrical costume, most contoured patterns are cut by working calico onto a dummy padded to the performer's shape, partly because the cutter can stand back and judge the balance and flow of style lines. These authors study non-stretch cloth and note that stretch garments need less accurate fit — [Garland and Almond, Second Skin, Costume 50(1), 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110947/3/research%208.pdf)
- Literal drawing on a form exists in contour training: De Montfort students cover a silicone body form with masking tape, draw style lines, cut the pattern off with a knife, and grade from a standard 34B — [Garland and Almond](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110947/3/research%208.pdf)
- Cup position is found on the wearer: the front lining is pinned to a bra or tank being worn, cups are pinned to the lining only, then the lining comes off for stitching — [Peekaboo](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/our-blog/adding-bra-cups-to-a-swimsuit)
- Cosplay makers wear a unitard and draw style lines on it with a marker, or cut a marked bodysuit apart and flatten the pieces onto card [S] — [Technochic](https://technochic.net/blogs/tech-craft-tutorials/lady-gaga-cosplay-spandex-bodysuit-tutorial); [Popverse](https://thepopverse.com/bodysuit-cosplay-tutorial-pattern-fit-beginner-pro)

**Physical forms are not a clean answer**
- A home ballroom maker found the built-in bodysuit bunches on a dress form because its leg openings do not work there, producing bumps in the bodice; she fitted on her own body instead — [Quintessential Clothes Pen, 2012](https://thequintessentialclothespen.com/2012/03/08/berry-ballroom-dress-update/)

**Fit first, decoration after**
- Stated directly by Desheva — [PBS](https://video.wpsu.org/video/beaded-dance-dresses-mOFOmK/) — and by Porter, who wants the base pattern fitting before any style change and treats a clean fit as the precondition for design — [Basic skating dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-ice-skating-dress/); [Design principles](https://spandexsimplified.com/spandex-costume-design-principles/)
- Exceptions under time pressure: Dancing with the Stars outfits are often first worn about two hours before the show, with fitting and trimming late — [Cheat Sheet, 2021](https://www.cheatsheet.com/entertainment/dancing-with-the-stars-6-surprising-facts-costumes-show-including-1-thing-common.html). Ignateva's costumes often change after first fittings, and she once redesigned an appliqué mid-build — [Ice-Dance.com](https://www.ice-dance.com/site/mariia-ignateva-sketching-the-performance/)

### Inferences
- Restated hypothesis that the evidence supports: makers go to the body whenever a line's position relative to the body matters (bust, hip, all-over stone layout), and go to flat paper whenever they need to cut. Most of the time they get by on paper, partly because a body or a good form is often unavailable.
- Drawing on a 3D mannequin still fits, for a different reason than hypothesised. It replaces the muslin-on-wearer and paper-on-form steps makers already improvise, and could spare the many who skip them. It must still hand back the flat master they cut from, because that is what they work with at the bench.
- "Base garment" means different things by trade: in ballroom a physical bodysuit plus net dress; in Porter-style skating a paper pattern plus a lining-and-mesh layer with no separate garment. A tool should not assume either.
- A virtual mannequin avoids a specific physical failure: bodysuits with legs behave badly on legless dress forms.

### Gaps
- The sample skews to home ateliers and press pieces. No top-tier skating atelier's bench routine was found, so high-end practice could be more on-form than this suggests.
- No source measures how common each route is.

## Figure skating: what are the techniques and how is each built?

### Takeaway

A skating dress is briefs, bodice and skirt joined at a waist seam, with decoration built into flat bodice layers and stones added at the end. Beige lining plus flesh mesh makes the base layer, and coloured fabric is appliquéd on top. No tutorial uses the name "floating appliqué"; the closest methods are below.

### Cited Findings

**Structure and order**
- Order: cut, appliqué on flat pieces, skirt side seams, brief and bodice seams, elastic, skirt to briefs, bodice over skirt, waist seam through three layers, then hem and embellish — [Basic skating dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/basic-ice-skating-dress/)
- Waterfall skirts are spiral-cut ruffles: a wider ruffle cascades more and needs fewer pieces; outside-in starting circle diameter = rounds × width × 2 + width (three rounds of 2 in gives 14 in). Layers are pinned longest first, overlapped by a third to a half, zigzagged every two rows, unhemmed, in non-fraying fabric, after bodice and brief are finished. An adult XS used about 1.5 m each of three colours — [Waterfall skirts](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-design-waterfall-skirts/)
- A centre-back seam can be moved: she redistributed it into two diagonal seams (a moving-darts idea), adding allowance only to the new edges and appliquéing some back sections before their seams — [Art Deco dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-i-made-that-art-deco-skating-dress/)

**Flesh mesh bodices with applied fabric**
- Nearly all designers use flesh-toned mesh; under ISU rules it does not count toward the 50 percent upper-body coverage — [New York Post via Fox, 2018](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/designers-spill-secrets-of-olympic-figure-skating-outfits.amp)
- Layer stack: beige swim lining and natural stretch mesh are cut from the uncut master and glued together; coloured sections go on top and are checked by laying the translucent paper pattern over them, then pinned — [Flames](https://spandexsimplified.com/flames-skating-dress/); [Pink and black](https://spandexsimplified.com/pink-black-skating-dress/)
- Yoke method: shoulder and upper-back areas are traced separately and cut from beige mesh with greatest stretch across, with 1 to 2 in extra at the lower edge for the solid pieces to overlap — [Ursula dress, 2024](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/)
- Mesh types: stretch mesh is matte, open, nearly invisible and delicate, and can be tinted darker with tea or coffee; illusion (glistenette) has a shimmer that looks less like skin; power mesh is for inner support, not skin effects — [Types of stretch fabric](https://spandexsimplified.com/types-of-stretch-fabric/)
- Skin match: Josiane Lamond picks carefully, sometimes custom dyes, suggests a Pantone chart, and tests samples under different lighting — [Skate Canada, 2024](https://skatecanada.ca/2024/08/07/ask-an-expert-costume-design/). Miller says fabric colour against skin tone is always her first check — [Traverse Ticker](https://www.traverseticker.com/amp/news/costume-designer-to-the-stars-on-ice)
- Thread: every seam containing mesh is sewn in the mesh colour so it disappears on skin — [How to sew spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-sew-spandex/). Where a beige serged seam runs through black sections, a close straight stitch inside, sewn while stretching, keeps the beige from showing — [Ursula dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/)
- Colour shift by layering: green mesh under blue mesh on sleeves — [Doctor T Designs](https://doctortdesigns.com/2013/09/28/2013-figure-dress-2-the-galloping-peacock/)

**Floating appliqué on mesh (closest evidence)**
- Appliqué on mesh needs pins as well as spray, since spray works poorly on mesh; a washable glue stick dabbed along the edge and fully dried is the alternative; keep everything flat. A mesh body piece should be patterned as though it were a large appliqué — [How to appliqué spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-applique-spandex/)
- Temporary adhesive bonds lining to mesh only weakly — [Reputation part 2](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-2/)
- To stop feed dogs distorting stretch fabric, sandwich the costume between appliqué and paper and tear the paper away; one poster hand-sewed a pinned motif around its perimeter so it could come off for washing [S] — [Threads forum](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/forum/sequined-applique-on-skating-dress)
- A cosplay tutorial irons tear-away on both outer faces and never between layers [S] — [Patreon archive](https://www.patreon.com/posts/8101286). Stitching over open mesh or lace can cause problems and benefits from a stabiliser [S] — [Marcy Tilton](https://marcytilton.com/blogs/news/stable-sewing-with-mesh-lace-net)

**Cutting away behind an appliqué**
- Two approaches: lift the upper layer and trim the one beneath as you go, which needs repositionable spray; or, once accurate, cut the patterns to reduce bulk from the start — [How to appliqué spandex](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-applique-spandex/)
- The pre-planned version adds a small lip to whichever layer goes underneath — [Flames](https://spandexsimplified.com/flames-skating-dress/); [Jazz team suits](https://spandexsimplified.com/jazz-team-synchro-suits/) — or a tab on mesh pieces so they tuck under the spandex — [Art Deco dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-i-made-that-art-deco-skating-dress/)
- Where snakes cross, the top one is cut and its ends tucked under the one below — [Reputation part 2](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-2/)
- Reverse appliqué: see `knowledge/domain/construction/applique-and-overlays.md`; not repeated here.

**Other appliqué variants not yet in the project bundle**
- Bordered appliqué: cut the motif, glue to white fabric, appliqué in the motif colour, then trim the white to a 1/8 to 3/16 in border — [Reputation part 2](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-2/)
- Quilted padded motif: gold Mystique over thin neoprene or bra foam, edge stitched, then a spiral quilting stitch — [Ursula dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/)
- Foil lycra spray-bonded to thin stretch neoprene as the appliqué material, with narrow pieces cut so greatest stretch runs along their length — [Hela costume](https://spandexsimplified.com/mcu-hela-costume-cosplay/)
- Crossing a closing seam: stop stitching about 1 in short, pin the loose end back, sew the seam, smooth the end across, trim and stitch — [Reputation part 2](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-2/)
- Keep sleeve designs to the middle third to half of the pattern area — [Reputation part 2](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-2/)

**Lace motif appliqué**
- Naomi Brunson makes her own lace — [CNN via KTVZ, Feb 2026](https://ktvz.com/entertainment/cnn-style/2026/02/18/from-sketches-to-sequins-how-olympic-skating-costumes-are-made-2/). No skating-specific method was found; the best first-hand account is ballroom, in the dance section. Lamond names beading, embroidery and appliqué as the most time-consuming handwork — [Skate Canada](https://skatecanada.ca/2024/08/07/ask-an-expert-costume-design/)

**Illusion necklines and backs**
- Method: draw the false neckline on the bodice pattern, cut along it, keep both pieces; the smaller piece plus 1 in becomes the mesh; glue-stick line on the bodice wrong side, dry fully, zigzag from the right side. Where the appliqué meets the neckline, leave a notch equal to the elastic width, because neck elastic goes on after the mesh — [Neck and back variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)
- Closures: butterfly clasps are sewn in backwards so they are less likely to open — [Neck and back variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/). Pat Pearsall sews and knots closures repeatedly after a hook failed mid-skate — [Fox](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/designers-spill-secrets-of-olympic-figure-skating-outfits.amp)
- Synchro swimmers prefer flesh mesh showing skin on the back — [Design principles](https://spandexsimplified.com/spandex-costume-design-principles/)

**How a shape is positioned, and how the sweetheart line is built**
- Flat method: with the front bodice pattern folded, sketch the sweetheart on one half, flip and trace for a mirror — [Ursula dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/). Or draw sweetheart and new armhole flat, trace yoke pieces to new paper, spray them to lining and main fabric, and appliqué the edges — [Reputation part 1](https://spandexsimplified.com/taylor-swift-reputation-outfit/)
- Continuing motifs: tape front and back yoke patterns together at the shoulder to draw a necklace across, and cut it on the fold so both sides match — [Ursula dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/). Tape front to back pattern to carry designs across the side seam — [Reputation part 2](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-2/)
- Seam matching: design lines must meet where the seam allowances touch, not at the paper edges, or a sloped line jumps at the seam — [Ursula dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/); [Colour blocking](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)
- Bust stand-ins: she used rolls of tape on the flat pattern to indicate rough chest placement, then got client approval on the rendering — [Art Deco dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-i-made-that-art-deco-skating-dress/)
- On-body methods: see the hypothesis section above.

**Stretch velvet**
- Cut with greatest stretch around the body and all pieces laid the same way, or it reads as two fabrics; Porter prefers nap smoothing downward — [Types of stretch fabric](https://spandexsimplified.com/types-of-stretch-fabric/). A retailer recommends it for sleeves, collars and flowing skirt panels — [Evoli](https://evoli-shop.com/en/the-ulimate-cuide-to-proffesional.html)
- Stones on velvet may need testing; if glue fails, pipe into the pile, let it partly dry, re-pipe, then set — [Crystalling](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-on-spandex/)

**Dye, airbrush and paint**
- Evgenia Kosygina's instructions to seamstresses list specifics such as chiffon skirt, biflex insert, stones and airbrush painting — [Sports.ru, 2020](https://www.sports.ru/figure-skating/1088425018.html)
- Juju Designs describes a skirt hand-dyed with acid dyes and airbrush paints, with ombré waves individually pinned, hand-sewn and trimmed so the transition runs top to bottom [S] — [Instagram mirror](https://imginn.com/juju.designs/)
- Sk8 Gr8 Designs lists airbrushed, dip-dyed, custom-dyed and hand-painted fabric as services, with no method given — [Patch, 2014](https://patch.com/illinois/channahon-minooka/custom-figure-skating-dresses-sk8-gr8-designs_f1e84744)
- The ISU's costume award text describes mesh as a sheer skin-like layer between paint and stone patterns [S] — [ISU](https://isu.org/figure-skating/isu-skating-awards/best-costume)
- Stretch paint: about 2 parts clear water-based caulk to 1 part acrylic; garment stretched to worn size and pinned to cardboard; brushed or piped; stones or sequins can be pressed into wet paint; stays stretchy, hand wash only. Not airbrush — [Stretch fabric paint, 2021](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-make-stretch-fabric-paint/)

**Hand stoning**
- Stretch the garment to within an inch or two of the wearer's measurements on a dress form, cardboard cut to half-body size, a PVC frame or a stretch board, with parchment underneath. This shows true placement and lets glue cure under worn tension — [Crystalling](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-on-spandex/). Jazz team suits went on cardboard so stones stayed consistent across the team — [Jazz team suits](https://spandexsimplified.com/jazz-team-synchro-suits/)
- Glue and bonding: E-6000 Fabri-Fuse piped from the bottle with roughly five minutes' working time; press so glue rises around the stone and grips the clear part; keep stones as separate dots so fabric can stretch; cure a couple of days. HotFix adhesive sits only on the foil back, so she rates it less secure — [Crystalling](https://spandexsimplified.com/crystalling-on-spandex/)
- Layout logic in her dresses: a line of stones from the sweetheart, then fill; larger stones high and smaller, wider-spaced ones lower; tiny stones tracing stitch lines; front cures days before the back — [Ursula dress](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/). Start from the densest area and work out, left to right if right-handed; smaller, closer dots along borders — [Reputation part 3](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-3/)
- Largest stones go first to set the layout, then smaller ones fill; cheap stones work when dense — [$20 challenge](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-20-rhinestone-challenge/)
- Vendor guidance: two or three sizes, with 20ss and 30ss most popular; bold placement first, then graduated density, then texture on sheer panels; varied density reads as motion — [Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/how-to-rhinestone-a-skating-costume-for-competition/)
- Colour ombré in stones: block rows with visible edges, or a merge where the next colour is seeded in with increasing frequency; the merge has no fixed pattern and is done by eye — [Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-create-an-ombre-effect-using-rhinestones)
- Marking conflicts: The Line Up marks every stone with washable marker or chalk over cardboard, doing about 10 drops at a time — [The Line Up, 2016](https://blog.thelineup.com/how-to-rhinestone-flat-back-method). Desheva marks nothing — [PBS](https://video.wpsu.org/video/beaded-dance-dresses-mOFOmK/)
- Counts and weight: about 5,000 crystals on a standard dress, 2,200 on Mirai Nagasu's to save weight for a triple axel — [Fox](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/designers-spill-secrets-of-olympic-figure-skating-outfits.amp); up to 10,000 by hand on some designs — [Diari de Tarragona, 2024](https://www.diaridetarragona.com/economia/194620/nana-disseny-esportiu-disenadoras-de-campeones-de-patinaje-artistico-mi19881692.html); Brunson estimates over 120,000 placed in a year — [CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/entertainment/cnn-style/2026/02/18/from-sketches-to-sequins-how-olympic-skating-costumes-are-made-2/)
- Heavy stones reportedly threw Gracie Gold off balance in a Brad Griffies dress — [Medill, 2016](https://news.medill.northwestern.edu/chicago/figure-skating-dress-design-with-an-edge). A dropped stone counts as a deduction, so Yumi Barnett-Nakamura checks stones constantly — [WHDH, 2014](https://whdh.com/news/costumes-make-the-difference-in-olympic-ice-skating/)
- Effort: about 10 hours of research, sketches, materials and fittings plus 10 to 40 hours' build (Brunson) — [CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/entertainment/cnn-style/2026/02/18/from-sketches-to-sequins-how-olympic-skating-costumes-are-made-2/); longest dress about 72 hours (Lamond) — [Skate Canada](https://skatecanada.ca/2024/08/07/ask-an-expert-costume-design/); 20 to 30 hours for the peacock dress — [Doctor T Designs](https://doctortdesigns.com/2013/09/28/2013-figure-dress-2-the-galloping-peacock/); 4 to 6 weeks per dress (Barnett-Nakamura) — [WHDH](https://whdh.com/news/costumes-make-the-difference-in-olympic-ice-skating/)

### Inferences
- The stack is a data model: lining and mesh from the uncut master; each colour a separate piece with lips at overlaps. This sits naturally on the project's line types in `knowledge/domain/construction/design-line-realisations.md`, adding a per-overlap lip value.
- Porter's seam-allowance rule is maker confirmation of the bundle's inferred advice to match artwork along the stitch line (`knowledge/domain/construction/rule-decoration-crossing-seams.md`).
- The bust is where flat drawing most often fails: she improvises tape rolls on paper or abandons paper for a muslin.
- Stitch order is design-specific, not universal. Flames stitches red first because it holds the others; Jazz stitches the largest area first and red last.

### Gaps
- No source names and teaches floating appliqué on unbacked mesh, and none says how much unbacked mesh a floating shape can span.
- No stretch-velvet appliqué method, no dye recipe, and no source states whether airbrushing happens on a form, on flat pieces or on bolts.
- No first-hand method pages from Brad Griffies, Delica, Elite Xpression, Mondor or Sew Much More. Griffies coverage gives only consultation and several sketch options, plus Bernina machines — [WeAllSew, 2014](https://weallsew.com/brad-griffies-creates-dresses-for-usa-olympic-figure-skaters/). The sk8gr8.com domain is an unrelated school, and Sk8 Gr8 Designs says it is not taking orders — [Sk8 Gr8 Designs](http://sk8gr8designs.com/). Jalie's blog returned rate-limit errors.
- No skating book was consulted.

## Rhythmic gymnastics: layering, draping, lace, skirts, asymmetry, dense stoning and named makers

### Takeaway

Low confidence. Named makers describe their work very little in public; eleven searches in English, Russian, Spanish and Italian found no step-by-step account. What holds: fabric sewn over a base, very high stone counts, heavy elements sewn rather than glued, and multi-layer skirts in some periods. Draping, gathers, foil bases and asymmetry were not documented by any source.

### Cited Findings
- Bolortsetseg "Crystal" Tamsagbaatar (Crystal Leotards, Torrance, 2019) began after a custom leotard from Russia disappointed; she taught herself by studying clothes she liked, later took a college fashion programme, machine-sews the outer fabric onto an inner lining, and places about 3,000 to 4,000 Swarovski crystals in planned layouts on larger pieces — [ECC Union](https://eccunion.com/features/2019/05/29/this-has-been-edited-needs-headline/)
- Nina Altshuler, a former obstetrician, started by making individual designs for her daughter's team, compares a leotard to a painting, finds repeat orders of identical costumes mechanical, uses Swarovski crystals, sequins and shiny appliqués, and studies how fabric moves in competition footage on YouTube (2011) [S] — [Jewish Independent](https://jewishindependent.ca/oldsite/archives/april11/archives11april29-06.html)
- Svetlana Gerasimova, maker for the Averina sisters, began sewing for her daughter and her daughter's sports-school friends; top gymnasts often get several variants a season (2021) [S] — [Sport24](https://sport24.ru/news/gymnastics/2021-12-02-khudozhestvennaya-gimnastika-kupalniki-sester-averinykh-intervyu-svetlany-gerasimovoy-o-viner-averinykh-masterskaya-po-poshivu-kupalnikov)
- A Russian retailer's care guide says the leotard base is a nylon mesh with coloured synthetic fabrics sewn onto it; 10,000 to 20,000 crystals of various sizes are glued on; the largest elements are sewn because glue may not hold their weight; stones can lift even in a cold wash; a wet leotard sags under stone weight [S] — [Sportmaster](https://www.sportmaster.ru/media/articles/kak-stirat-kupalnik-dlya-hudozhestvennoj-gimnastiki/)
- History, with no designers named: supplex base today; rhinestones from about 2001; multi-layer fringe skirts in the 2013 to 2016 cycle; lace, feathers and velvet among materials; up to 2 kg in weight — [SkyGrace](https://skygrace.ru/en/news/est-vopros/istoriya-kupalnika-ot-prostogo-triko-do-naryada-vesom-dva-kilogramma)
- A custom seller notes larger appliqués are hand-sewn rather than glued; a buyer calls the piece very heavy [S] — [Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/listing/892704407). Vinkova, a Spanish maker, sells made-to-order leotards with stone quantity as a customer variable [S] — [Dealroom profile](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/vinkova)
- Rules, briefly: FIG Rhythmic requires non-transparent material chest to crotch (snippets, in `knowledge/domain/rules/fig-attire-rules.md`); the lower part must be solid without nude mesh, with lace allowed as trim or connection [S] — [Sport24, 2021](https://sport24.ru/news/other/2021-10-12-khudozhestvennaya-gimnastika-kupalniki-gimnastok-seksualnyye-gimnastki-sestry-averiny-v-kupanikakh-gimnastika-izmeneniya-v-pravilakh-kupalnikov-foto-analitika)
- Conflict to flag: Sportmaster's nylon-mesh base sits awkwardly with the opaque lower-body rule. They may describe different regions; no source resolves it.

### Inferences
- The little evidence suggests rhythmic construction sits nearer Latin (fabric and stones on a base, heavy items sewn) than factory artistic work (heat-transfer motifs on flat pieces).
- Stone counts in rhythmic overlap ballroom more than skating, though the figures are thin and from a retailer.

### Gaps
- No named maker's account of layering, draped or gathered fabric, foil or printed bases, or asymmetric construction. Asymmetry appears only as a general spandex variation — [Neck and back variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/).
- Sport24 and Sportmaster refused direct fetches (401); the Jewish Independent page returned 404; a Ukrainian design study returned 502 repeatedly ([KNUTD](https://jrnl.knutd.edu.ua/index.php/artdes/article/download/1779/1707)); vinkova.com is a parked domain.
- A search summary reported an Etsy listing with 140 mirrors, 81 sewn-on stones and 4,635 hotfix stones, but did not identify which listing, so it is unverified.
- Most likely unlocks: Russian-language video interviews, or the About sections of Etsy makers; these were not reachable here.

## Ballroom, Latin and competitive dance: base garment, applied fabric, cut-outs, fringe, draping

### Takeaway

This is the trade where the hypothesis works best. The Latin dress is a stretch net garment with a skin-colour bodysuit inside, fitted before decoration, with decoration tried at fittings and stones set by eye. Fringe is sewn because it is heavy. How fabric is pinned or draped onto a stretch base remains undocumented.

### Cited Findings

**Nataliya Desheva, the fullest single account** — [PBS Fit 2 Stitch, "Beaded Dance Dresses", S14 E1411](https://video.wpsu.org/video/beaded-dance-dresses-mOFOmK/)
- The client chooses a sketch and they settle every line first, including openings, slits and length.
- The whole dress is mesh netting, stretching in both directions: sleeves single layer, body double so it is not too see-through; a skin-colour bodysuit sits inside; straps are mesh with elastic inside.
- Seams: a straight stitch hidden inside the serged stitch; the straight stitch holds the pieces and the serger backs it up if it breaks.
- Colour can shift by changing the mesh itself, for example purple mesh at the bottom of a blue top to blend the two.
- Stones: glue rather than hotfix, which she says does not hold under heavy handling; no placement marks; neckline first, sleeves from the bottom up, then downward. Bigger stones mean fewer stones and less labour at about the same stone cost.
- Beaded fringe is sewn because glue is not strong enough for heavy items.
- About 28,000 stones took about three months; stones about $2,000; base fabric $200 to $300; about 4 lb; dresses from about $1,500 to $7,000; hand wash only.

**Other first-hand and museum accounts**
- Home-built standard gown: nude power mesh, stretch knit bodice and a built-in bodysuit; bodice sewn and fitted repeatedly on the maker; quarter-circle godets; order was bodice, godets, zip, serged seams, hems, top edge and straps, about 3,000 hand-glued stones, then chiffon floats and neck decoration — [Quintessential Clothes Pen, 2012](https://thequintessentialclothespen.com/2012/03/08/berry-ballroom-dress-update/)
- Lace appliqué: already-stoned lace reused from an abandoned dress was hand-sewn to a stretch bodice base, with much piecing to fit the new shape; slow but invisible when finished. The stretch base also carried darts; charmeuse drapes and bra cups were added, then chiffon floats from the arms — [Quintessential Clothes Pen, 2013](https://thequintessentialclothespen.com/2013/02/26/distraction-from-historical-sewing/)
- A 1979-80 world champion's dress: two layers of net (about 25 yards) with 10 m of chiffon; most of its value in rhinestones; the only foundation is flesh-coloured nylon-covered bra cups — [London Museum](https://londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/v/object-527947/dress-ballroom-dance-dress)

**Patterning and fabric**
- A German pattern dossier covers Latin dresses with inserted bodies, tapering front inserts, cut-out fronts and sides, tapered and round panel seams, asymmetric ruffled skirts and built-in pants, in lycra, mesh, stretch satin, velvet and gabardine with georgette or organza (a shop listing; the dossier itself was not read) — [M. Mueller & Sohn](https://www.muellerundsohn.com/en/?p=2746)
- The dress pattern is cut horizontally at the waist, then panties and skirt are attached [S] — [Threads forum](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/forum/ballroom-dance-costumes)
- Lycra is the base of nearly every Latin and ballroom couture dress and suits leotard bases and shaping panels [S] — [Chrisanne Clover](https://www.chrisanne-clover.com/news/performance-lycra/). Stretch net is a prominent Latin fabric [S] — [Chrisanne Clover](https://www.chrisanne-clover.com/news/stretch-net-2021/)
- A counter-example to net and Lycra: a gown built on stretch suiting instead of satin or chiffon, relying on structure and controlled drape, with restrained stones, from close dialogue between dancer and head designer (brand PR) — [DSI London, 2026](https://dsi-london.com/news/article/when-tailoring-meets-the-ballroom/1560)
- Cut-outs backed by mesh use the same method as skating illusion necklines — [Neck and back variations](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)

**Television schedule as a stress test**
- Sharna Burgess's costume went from Tuesday creative meeting to Wednesday fabric buying, first fitting Friday or Saturday and last on Sunday; dancers are taped in with double-sided tape — [ET, 2018](https://www.etonline.com/sharna-burgess-takes-et-inside-the-dancing-with-the-stars-wardrobe-department-exclusive-112495)
- Daniela Gschwendtner: at most five days for all outfits, about half a day per costume before stones; everything has stretch; about 20 costume staff. One costume carried 25,000 rhinestones; an average costume can cost $5,000 — [Cheat Sheet, citing TV Insider and Parade](https://www.cheatsheet.com/entertainment/dancing-with-the-stars-6-surprising-facts-costumes-show-including-1-thing-common.html)
- The final fitting comes after couples block routines for camera [S] — [Threads, 2009](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/2009/05/15/interview-with-dancing-with-the-stars-costume-designer)
- Fringe elsewhere: beaded strands stitched along leg openings with varied drop lengths — [Lover bodysuit](https://spandexsimplified.com/taylor-swift-lover-outfit/)

### Inferences
- In a Latin net dress a "cut-out" is the absence of appliqué, not a hole in fabric. That inverts the leotard logic of a solid piece with a mesh-backed hole, and would make a useful second mode of thinking in a design tool.
- Because Desheva places stones by eye on a fitted garment, a tool that insists on pre-computed placement would not match how this kind of maker works.

### Gaps
- No source gives pinning order, ease on stretch, or how draped panels attach to a stretch base. General fashion-school posters describe drape on form, pattern, sample, fit, final garment, beading last, but are not stretch-costume specific — [CWU poster](https://digitalcommons.cwu.edu/source/2014/posters/76).
- Blocked: Chrisanne Clover (403), Threads including its Erin Jean Kaya profile (403), TV Insider (403). Gulf Shore pages were not retrieved: one returned the homepage, the other rate-limited. A page calling the DWTS base a leotard base looked low quality and is not relied on ([People's Pharmacy mirror](https://library.peoplespharmacy.com/dancing-stars-costumes)).

## Acrobatic, aerial and circus: reinforcing, grip, durability, decoration under movement, printed bodysuits

### Takeaway

These makers start from the apparatus and where stress falls, and they strip or swap decoration when it hurts or hinders. Stretch direction is a failure-level decision. Custom colour comes from dyeing and printing white Lycra in-house. Actual reinforcement patterns are not public.

### Cited Findings

**Where design starts**
- Libby McDonnell (Circa) studies each apparatus to locate strain, finds acrobats rougher on costumes than dancers, and reduces designs to the least information needed. She sketches and uses mood boards with the director, adds elastic gussets so shirts stay tucked, takes in oversized trousers at the waist to leave thigh room, dyes for theatrical effect, and tours at least two of every item — [StageLync, 2022](https://stagelync.com/news/the-fabric-of-circa)
- Jenny Leigh Du Puis puts safety and function as the foundation, because the performer must not be thinking about the costume mid-act; body-to-body and body-to-apparatus contact shape the work — [StageLync](https://stagelync.com/news/circus-costume-magic-with-jenny-leigh-du-puis). Her DearHeart unitard came from interviews with circus designers, technicians and performers — [Cornell lab](https://performancewear.human.cornell.edu/post/circus-costume-2)
- Clothilde Laude tests toiles in neutral fabric for cut, fit and movement before cutting real cloth. Full-scale tests at 40 m showed mock-ups did not read, so shapes were reworked. Durability covers humidity, sweat, repeated handling and cast changes, so every element should be adjustable, repairable and replaceable without the designer present. Acrobat costumes approach custom tailoring, with graded patterns where the cast rotates — [Transe Express](https://transe-express.com/en/news/adn-when-costumes-reach-new-heights)
- A company brief for acrobats' costumes names ease of movement, material slipperiness and the willingness to test ideas before committing — [Mimbre, 2016](https://mimbre.co.uk/?p=6316)

**Stretch direction**
- Leotards cut with vertical stretch for a water-feature show failed on opening night, and all had to be rebuilt the next day cut across the body (Belinda Long-Hodsden) — [Cornell, Leotard's Legacy](https://exhibits.library.cornell.edu/flights-of-fancy/feature/leotard-s-legacy). Du Puis treats up-the-body versus around-the-body as a core unitard decision — [StageLync](https://stagelync.com/news/circus-costume-magic-with-jenny-leigh-du-puis)

**Decoration under movement**
- Veronica Blair's inherited rhinestone costumes were heavy and sharp, leaving small cuts and bruises and limiting her early trapeze tricks; she moved to lighter, durable shimmer fabric with selective sparkle, and has patched lost rhinestones with similar sequins. Fishnets enhance grip on the bar and read as bare leg from a distance. Fitted garments and weight distribution change how an artist adjusts a trick — [Cornell, Aerialists Aloft](https://exhibits.library.cornell.edu/flights-of-fancy/feature/aerialists-aloft)
- Sequins secured at four cardinal points and tied off keep sparkle without costing stretch — [StageLync](https://stagelync.com/news/circus-costume-magic-with-jenny-leigh-du-puis). Porter likewise sews onto an already stretched garment and ties off individually or in batches, so thread has room when worn — [Beading and sequins](https://spandexsimplified.com/beading-and-sequins-on-spandex/)
- Friction acts such as aerial hoop and Spanish web need more repair and replacement; costumes last 6 months to 2 years, with two spares of each and daily washing of anything touching skin — [Cirque Fascination](https://www.cirquefascination.com/?p=483)
- Gold elastic chains for a water show kept breaking until thickness, glue and technique were all changed — [Neon Museum](https://neonmuseum.org/fabrics/)

**Printed and dyed bodysuits**
- Cirque dyes and treats about 80 percent of its fabric in-house, starting mostly from white: bath dye, silk-screen, hand painting, digital printing and newer sublimation — [Neon Museum](https://neonmuseum.org/fabrics/); [Cirque Fascination](https://www.cirquefascination.com/?p=483). Most fabric starts as Lycra, with printing, silk-screening and detailing in-house — [KATU, 2022](https://int.katu.com/news/local/a-back-stage-tour-at-cirque-du-soleils-alegria)
- Each Cirque design goes through at least 10 drafts — [Cirque Fascination](https://www.cirquefascination.com/?p=483). Plaster head casts are standard — [La Nouba notes](https://www.cirquefascination.com/?p=381). Its workshop has a research and development arm for new materials and techniques — [VCUarts Qatar, 2018](https://qatar.vcu.edu/event/cirque-du-soleil-innovation-collaboration-at-the-heart-of-costume-creation)
- A 1990 bodysuit by Becky Truman combines Lycra with paint, mesh, trims and sequins, painted and stitched — [V&A](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O1294886)

### Inferences
- A map of apparatus-contact zones would be the circus equivalent of the bundle's crystal keep-outs (`knowledge/domain/construction/rule-crystal-keep-outs.md`): same idea, driven by friction, grip and safety rather than comfort alone.
- Modularity and repairability are design goals here that the gymnastics sources do not mention; a seasonal gym leotard may simply not need them.

### Gaps
- No source gave rub-panel, gusset or seam-reinforcement patterns for acrobatic bodysuits, grip-zone specifications, or how print is engineered onto pattern pieces.
- Blocked: Du Puis's ITAA paper and a 2022 circus apparel framework paper, both on the Iowa State site behind Anubis ([DearHeart DOI](https://doi.org/10.31274/itaa.8419); [framework](https://www.iastatedigitalpress.com/itaa/article/id/13826/)). Nora Nikitin's interview is paywalled ([StageLync](https://stagelync.com/news/pro-exclusive-think-like-an-acrobat-lets-design-for-ourselves-the-art-of-the-circus-costume-with-master-costumer-nora-nikitin/amp)).
- Nothing was found on stones catching on silks or the inner thigh.

## Cheer: panels, printing, sublimation against cut and sew

### Takeaway

Cheer offers the clearest statement of what each decoration method can and cannot do, and its business runs on reps, prototypes and team orders much like gymnastics. First-hand construction writing is scarce and most sources are vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Appliqué and embroidery carry a backing that does not stretch easily, which is a problem on the bust; logos often need simplifying and ombré is impossible. Vinyl stretches but has few colours and little fine detail. Sublimation cannot exactly match non-sublimated fabric, so sublimate the whole garment or use deliberate contrast. Spangles and stones look pixelated, so spangles suit bold lettering over 2 in and can fall off; teams often mix a few accent stones with embroidery to save cost — [The Line Up, 2018](https://blog.thelineup.com/5-ways-to-add-logo-and-lettering-to-your-cheer-uniform)
- Pressing a finished garment leaves faint white lines at folded seams; printing flat panels first gives edge-to-edge colour, but alignment can shift slightly at seams, so critical elements stay in safe zones. Needs at least about 65 percent polyester — [Custom Ink](https://www.customink.com/blog/sublimated-jerseys-guide/)
- Sublimating finished garments can leave crushed fabric and white streaks; one maker's online builder produces a PDF order form and shareable designs — [ASI, 2017](https://mgmt.asicentral.com/news/how-to/may-2017/the-ins-and-outs-of-sublimated-uniforms)
- A patent describes printing raw fabric before assembly, then cutting and sewing with overlock joins [S] — [Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20100212059)
- Varsity's sequence: a personal design session, a prototype that must be approved, athletes personally fitted, then the order. School looks stay clean-lined, all-star looks are one-offs, and the trend was specialty fabric combined with sublimation (2016) — [FloCheer](https://flocheer.com/articles/5050402-inside-the-world-of-varsity-spirit-fashion)
- Vendors market all-over patterns, gradients and 4-way polyester, with online preview studios and no test data — [Wooter](https://wooter.com/articles/?p=15103); [Cheerleading.com](https://www.cheerleading.com/uniforms/cc-fusion-sublimation.html). Process steps for Quatro, Rebel and Varsity are already in `knowledge/domain/market/custom-order-process.md`.

### Inferences
- Sublimated cheer uniforms print panel illusions rather than sew them, the same separation of colour regions from pattern pieces the bundle records for leotards.
- Online builders are normal in cheer, so a design tool would meet an existing expectation there, though only 2D builders were seen.

### Gaps
- No source on sewn-panel sequence, braid application or Varsity's factory. Rebel's site was rate-limited and its crystal and sublimation methods remain unconfirmed.

## Performance swimwear: ruching, power mesh, bonded edges, lining, cups, cut and sew against seamless

### Takeaway

Swimwear documents shaping methods that the project's notes so far hold only as looks or not at all: elastic-driven gathers, hidden power mesh, cups set into lining, and glued raw edges. The evidence is hobby tutorials and patents rather than factory accounts, and two suggested sources were unreachable.

### Cited Findings

**Ruching and shirring**
- Clear elastic on a side seam: mark the ruched length with clips while trying the garment on inside out; elastic is about 0.65 of that length; mark midpoints; long narrow zigzag, stretching only the elastic. This is from a top, not a swimsuit — [5 out of 4](https://5outof4.com/rouching-side-seams-with-elastic/)
- Thread gathers instead: a 1/4 in running stitch between notches on both front side seams, gathered to match the lining — [Mood, Aeron swimsuit](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=78136)
- Ruching over a foam cup (Beverly Johnson): thin two-way stretch fabric at least as wide as the cup and about 3 times its depth; two gather lines along the neckline curve; pull and pin until tension gathers neatly without collapsing the cup; zigzag close to the foam and trim. Preformed foam is needed because cut-and-sew cups are too soft to hold the gathers — [Bra-Makers Supply, 2017](https://braandcorsetsupplies.com/?p=513211)
- Ruching the centre front with elastic can create a sweetheart effect [S] — [Threads, 2019](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/2019/02/21/pattern-review-cashmerette-ipswich-swimsuit)
- Elastic tension: 1:1 stabilises without gathering; test on a 10 in scrap — [WeAllSew, Coralie](https://weallsew.com/how-to-sew-a-swimsuit-for-summer/). About 90 percent of opening width for leg and shelf elastics in one pattern — [Mood, Aeron](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=78136)

**Power mesh and lining**
- Power Mesh has more give and suits the bra area; Power Net is firmer for the tummy; use only one in the front or back or the suit cannot be pulled on — [Peekaboo](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/our-blog/adding-bra-cups-to-a-swimsuit)
- Trim mesh 1/4 in slimmer than the tricot at each side seam and stay-stitch so it carries slight tension — [Mood, Phoenix, 2018](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=22714). Elsewhere it is cut the same shape as the outer, basted 1/8 in from the edge, with darts closed on both layers first — [Mood, Speedwell, 2021](https://blog.moodfabrics.com/?p=55696)
- Colour-block main pieces first, since the lining does not need those seams; swim elastic outlasts fold-over or binding in chlorine — [WeAllSew](https://weallsew.com/tips-for-sewing-swimsuits-using-lingerie-patterns/)

**Cups and support**
- On-body placement method, then zigzag around the cup at full width and trim away the lining inside it — [Peekaboo](https://peekaboopatternshop.com/our-blog/adding-bra-cups-to-a-swimsuit); [Threads, 2011](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/2011/06/13/how-to-install-bathing-suit-bra-cups) [S]
- Underwire bodysuit: mirror cup pairs in fabric and lining or foam, wire about 3/4 in from centre front, channel on the lower cup edge, wires in after assembly; fit is judged on a trial suit and the wires moved — [Underwire bodysuit](https://spandexsimplified.com/underwire-bodysuit/)

**Bonded, laser-cut and seamless**
- Raw-edge patent (Eleven18, granted 2025): inner layer about 110 to 180 gsm, outer about 190 to 260 gsm, both typically 80/20 nylon spandex; polyurethane adhesive along edges, continuous or as dots or lines, which also stiffens the fabric; laser-cut raw edges with no fold or stitching; semi-tacky silicone strips 0.3 to 1 mm thick and 6 to 18 mm wide on the inner layer to hold position. Purpose: avoid skin indentation from seams and elastic. Bust darts may still be stitched — [US12357039B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12357039B2/en)
- Seamless-suit patent (granted 2011, lapsed): panels ultrasonically cut, moulded to a 3D form, joined with polyurethane glue plus heat pressing and ultrasonic jointing — [US7882569B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7882569)
- Machinery is established: Framis tapes, Macpi hot-air tape-and-trim, Belsonic hemming, Vetron ultrasonic. Welding only works on man-made thermoplastic fibres; curves and highly stretchable materials are the main challenges (2011) — [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/?p=60892)
- A university study on ladies' swimwear found sewn seams consistently stronger and better looking than ultrasonic welded seams, with synthetic-only fabrics suiting the method better [S] — [SEALS repository](https://vital.seals.ac.za/vital/access/manager/Repository/vital:34048)
- Claims conflict and are commercial: one brand bonds two layers instead of using tight seams [S] — [onewith](https://onewithswim.com/blogs/1w/bonded-vs-seamless-swimwear-what-s-the-difference); a lingerie retailer says laser-cut edges curl under hot water and tumble drying and cut-and-sew lasts longer and allows more fabrics, embroidery, appliqué and trims [S] — [Lingerie Museum](https://www.lingeriemuseum.com/exhibits/bikini-brief-vs-seamless)

### Inferences
- Ruching cannot be represented as a seam or appliqué line. It needs a region with a gather ratio, plus an elastic or thread path, so it would be a new realisation type alongside those in `knowledge/domain/construction/design-line-realisations.md`. The app's fold stamps could then carry construction meaning rather than being looks only.
- Laminating two layers stiffens fabric, which would counteract the high recovery gymnastics needs and adds a failure mode at raw edges under chalk and repeated stress. This is reasoning, not tested data.

### Gaps
- Sew Sew Def's domain did not resolve and melissamora.com redirects to a parked page, so neither was read; searches did not surface their teaching elsewhere. Two Threads pattern pieces were blocked. No swimsuit-specific clear-elastic ratio was found, and no data on how bonded edges survive high stretch or abrasion.

### Takeaway

More is already in use than might be expected. Catalogues and the owner's moodboard show mesh yokes to a sweetheart, lined mesh shapes, gathers, twists, wraps and lace, so for many of these techniques the gap is not whether the look exists but how it is built, which factories do not publish and the adjacent trades do. Most "why not" answers are my inference; no gymnastics maker was found explaining the absences. Rule mentions are kept brief since rule-checking is deferred. ### Inferences
- Why the absences: bars, beam and floor punish loose parts and sharp edges; tumbling needs full recovery and low bulk; factory leotards at team prices cannot absorb hand placement measured in months. Desheva's 28,000-stone dress at about three months makes the cost point vivid.
- The most useful transfers are build methods for looks already on the moodboard: yoke overlaps and thread colour, mesh shell over shaped lining, gather ratios behind the fold stamps, lace piecing. After those come low-bulk additions: bordered appliqué, layered colour-shift mesh, density-graded stone layouts. Cups, sewn fringe and skirts matter mainly if rhythmic or dance users are ever in scope. This is a prediction.

### Gaps
- No gymnastics maker was found explaining why any of these are avoided, or how catalogue gathers and twists are actually built. Another researcher covers leotard construction, so this comparison rests on the bundle.

## How do these makers think? Where do they start, do they use a form, the body or sketches, and how does a shape get from the body to a flat piece?

### Takeaway

They start from music and the athlete, not from cloth or body. A sketch communicates; a paper pattern positions; fittings correct. The designer is often not the sewer. Moving a shape from body to flat is handled by a family of low-tech tricks, with the taped second skin as the closest physical analogue to drawing on a 3D mannequin.

### Cited Findings

**Typical sequence**

**What the second-skin research found** — [Garland and Almond](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110947/3/research%208.pdf)
- A four-piece princess layout would not lie flat because the body has too many curves; many more seams were needed. Redrawing on a mannequin in a different pen colour gave better balance, and mistakes were fixed by taping the mould back together.
- It showed proportions far more effectively than flat cutting, allowed lines more fluid than cutting on the stand, and let fit come first and style lines take priority. Flowing lines were produced that the authors say drafting and stand work could not achieve.
- Limits: restricted arm movement, posture consistency, flesh compression, consent and invasiveness when working on a person, and extra time.
- Students who had struggled with pattern drafting produced accurate complex patterns and were less afraid to experiment. Of 160 emailed delegates, 38 replied, all supportive; freelancers under time pressure stayed reluctant.
- The work is explicitly about non-stretch cloth.

### Inferences
- Drawing on a virtual mannequin is a digital second skin without the cling film, the consent problem or the posture worries. Stretch cloth should reduce its worst flaw, because fabric absorbs curvature that a stiff tape shell cannot; how much is the project's fit model to decide.
- The sketch stage comes first and is often done by someone who does not sew. A 3D canvas will either replace the sketch or follow it; mood-board and sketch-first users should be able to work roughly and quickly, before any seam logic appears.
- Two distinct papers are in use: the illustrative sketch and the positional master. Merging them is the main thing a 3D tool can change.

### Gaps
- No source measures how flat-drawn motifs distort at worn stretch; negative ease means they must, but makers do not discuss compensation beyond stoning at worn size. No documented use of masking tape on a form by a stretch-costume maker was found, though it probably exists.

## Is there anything a digital tool could make far easier, in the makers' own words?

### Takeaway

Nobody was found asking outright for a 3D design tool, so this answer is by implication. Their complaints and workarounds point at seam matching, placing lines relative to the bust, carrying a design across sizes, seeing the back, weight, and remote approval. **Cautions in their own terms**
- Desheva places stones by eye with no marks, and Crystal Parade's merged ombré has no fixed pattern — [PBS](https://video.wpsu.org/video/beaded-dance-dresses-mOFOmK/); [Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-create-an-ombre-effect-using-rhinestones)
- In 2016, computer use in theatrical costume was limited by budget and application, an experienced manual cutter often overrode the computer, and costumes are usually one-offs — [Garland and Almond](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110947/3/research%208.pdf)
- Freelancers under time pressure resist new pattern methods when familiar ones work; one maker's view was that any method is valid if it works — [Garland and Almond](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110947/3/research%208.pdf)
- The project's market notes found no dedicated 3D tool for leotards, dancewear or skating dresses (`knowledge/domain/market/unoccupied-gap.md`).

### Inferences
- The most valuable single promise, judged by what makers actually improvise, is: draw on the body once, and get seam-matched flat pieces, per-size regrading and stone templates back.
- The tool should accept freehand, by-eye stone work as a first-class mode alongside computed layouts, or it will lose makers like Desheva.
- Skaters and designers already sketching on iPads, like Ignateva, are the natural early users, and the project's iPad target suits them.

### Gaps
- No direct wish-list quotes from any maker; no maker survey. A short set of interviews with practising dressmakers would answer this better than more web research. Instagram and YouTube, where many of them talk about work, were not covered: a fetch of one Instagram mirror was refused by the permission system, so that source stands as a snippet only.
