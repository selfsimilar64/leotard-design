## From the moodboard catalogue, which distinct constructions appear, how often, and in which images?

### Takeaway
Finished edges and collars are near-universal; about 83% of garments use mesh, and about 60 use mesh in a shaped region. Separate straps and applied strips are about 30 garments, closures at least 17, wraps and overlaps about 10, linings about 5, ruching about 4. The catalogue almost never says whether a shaped panel is pieced or applied, so the size of the "mesh bodice with applied plates" family is a range, not a count.

### Inferences
- **[inferred]** Three of the items the brief lists (mesh with plates, mesh-filled cut-outs, reverse appliqué) are one question photos cannot settle: how was a shaped mesh boundary sewn. The app must therefore offer the choice, not guess it.
- **[inferred]** `e49647c7` is probably the owner's reference design in [the decision][dec]: racer, high neck, mauve wet look, two plates between a waist seam and the armhole, which is also the shape the test uses ([materials.test.js:154-156][testmat]).

### Gaps
- Pieced versus applied cannot be read from the catalogue. A share of the ~23 family needs a maker's answer or close photo reading.
- 76 of ~106 garments have no visible back, so back counts are floors, and backs are photographed when interesting [cp §2 caveat].

### Takeaway
Pieced panels, cut-outs, block silhouettes, paired asymmetry and the new overlay pieces are real construction. Straps render truly but are unknown to the pattern. Linings and hardware exist only as script commands. Wraps, printed seams and ruching are looks. Reverse appliqué and any backed cut-out are not expressible. Documents lag the code in several places listed below.

Where documents lag the code (each checked against the code):
- [The tutorial][tut] describes a tool rail and a pen option "Mirror: One side". There is no rail ([toolbar.js][toolbar]), and `PenOptions` has only "Draws" ([ShapeTab.jsx:137-144][shape]).
- [studio-interface][studio] says a selected line's page has "mirror"; `LineEditor` has no mirror control ([ShapeTab.jsx:161-189][shape]), though `curve.set` accepts it. It also omits the overlay button.
- [construction-model][cm] says "Stones are not yet tied to overlay deletion". This is half true: `dropOverlaysOn` removes stones on the piece ([commands-garment.js:136][cmd]); `overlay.remove`, which the Fabric tab's trash calls ([FabricTab.jsx:84][fabric]), does not.
- [The roadmap][road] places surface stamps in P7 as T14; they already ship as decal looks. The `details` list its schema anticipates stays unused.
- A comment at [engine.js:157-158][engine] says the Shape tab's list reaches a strap's line; that list filters strap paths out ([ShapeTab.jsx:116][shape]).

### Inferences
- **[inferred]** The app has one strong concept, a cell of the shell, and everything that does not fit one (a strap, a strip, an overlapped piece, a print boundary) sits beside the piece system or is forced into it.
- **[inferred]** [design-line-realisations][real] proposes a line type per line (seam, closing seam, edge, appliqué outline, print line, strap centreline). The code has `role` plus a separate overlay flow; a line cannot change between seam and appliqué without being redrawn, because no command converts one.

### Gaps
- `band.add`, `layer.add`, `hardware.add`, `panel.cut` and `patternPieces` have no test (grep of `app/test`), so their behaviour here rests on reading alone. Straps have one incidental use ([blocks.test.js:154][testblocks]).

## For each construction the app can express, what are the exact steps, where is the friction, and do they match how it is really made?

### Takeaway
A mirrored plate costs about N + 12 steps and is the closest match to real practice, but it passes through a hidden seed check, a mirror rule that drops the second side, and a shared-material trap. Straps are quick to draw but cannot be reshaped afterwards. Everything else multiplies per line, because finishes have no multi-select and there is no offset or duplicate.

### Cited Findings
A step is one click, key, slider drag or typed entry; turning the view is not counted. N is the number of free control points. Start: a block is chosen, Mirror on, Select tool, nothing selected. Labels are the interface's own [code].

**1. Mesh body + mirrored plate bounded by seams (`e49647c7`): N + 12 steps.**
Click Front (1). Separate from the others (1). Mesh tile (1). Colour (1). Sheerness (1). Press O, or Shape tab then Add overlay piece (1-2). Click first seam (1), N points with Shift for corners, click second seam (1). Click a side (1); the tool exits and the Fabric tab opens. New material (1). Fabric tile (1). Colour (1). A second plate in that material: N + 5.
- *Internal knowledge needed:* (a) **Hidden seed.** Only the cell holding the cut panel's `seed`, or a sliver, counts as fabric, because `strokeRegions` runs `buildGarment` and never `inheritPanels` ([build.js:118-136, 191-195][build]). So `addRegionOverlay` refuses the other cells with "Choose a side that has fabric" ([commands-garment.js:535-537][cmd]), and `previewRegion` shows no highlight there ([tools.js:581][tools]). This is the refusal in the brief, still at HEAD. A Front seeded at x = 7 cm can only be filled on the side holding that seed, and the wearer's right plate of a mirrored stroke is refused ([blocks.js:396][blocks]). The test covers only an opening and a seeded side ([materials.test.js:160, 170-173][testmat]).
  (b) **Mirror rule.** Any control point within 6 mm of the centre, or points on both sides, makes the line one part: `penMirror` returns `none` ([tools.js:37, 245-248, 565][tools]). A plate line starting at a CF seam point therefore yields one plate with Mirror on. This is the asymmetry in the brief, and [mirror-toggle][mirror] records it as a deliberate decision ("The centre stays one part").
  (c) **Shared material.** A new overlay starts in the base's material ([commands-garment.js:518-521, 544][cmd]) and `panel.set` routes look fields to the material ([commands-garment.js:359-362][cmd]). Pressing a fabric tile before New material re-fabrics the base as well **[test]** ([materials.test.js:54-66][testmat] for shared pieces).
  (d) **One cell only.** `at` picks one cell of the shell cut by all its lines ([commands-garment.js:535][cmd]). A plate spanning two cells, such as across a yoke seam, cannot use line-to-line mode: the outline "ends on the next line it meets" ([tools.js:547][tools]). The user removes the seam or falls back to free-drawing, which is not caught in any seam ([cm known gaps][cm]). **[inferred]** This is the "cut twice" case that remains after commit `2e72750`, which fixed the simple case of one cell.
- *Tracing:* none when both ends lie on lines, an improvement over the free outline.
- *Tab switches:* 2 (Fabric, Shape, Fabric), the last automatic.
- *Real making:* good. A shape on a whole base, zigzag edge, placement line, on flat pieces ([applique-and-overlays][appl], [assembly order][assem]). Edges along the armhole or waist are caught in that seam.

**2. Free appliqué or sash (`IMG_4527`): N + 5.** O (1), N ≥ 3 points, click first point or Enter (1), New material (1), fabric (1), colour (1). A mosaic such as `094859`'s 14 strips: about 14 × 6, no copy. Real: matches, but overlays stacked one on another ("built one layer at a time" in [appliqué][appl]) cannot be made in the interface.

**3. Underlay (`TREILLE`, `095021`): not in the interface.** Script: `layer.add`, `curve.add` per edge, `panel.add`; the piece then appears among the Fabric chips. Real: a lining cut from the unaltered pattern, basted to the shell ([rule-lining][lining]).

**4. Wrap or surplice (`113423`, `DAPHNE`): LOOK, 2N + 10 or more.** Pen (1), two lines from side seam to side seam across the centre, each N + 2 and hand-matched because neither is mirrored, then per cell: click, Separate or chip, fabric. Bind each edge per item 10. Real: two longer pieces that overlap. Not matched: the export gives pieces edge to edge.

**5. Floating strap becoming an applied band (`1dfae2a5`, `105148`): N + 6.** Draw a strap (1), Width (1), Colour (1), Material (1), click start on a line (1), N points, click end on a line (1). Then Stacking (1).
- *Friction:* path cannot be reshaped. `Drawn` hides `strap-path` lines ([ShapeTab.jsx:116][shape]), `hitAt` returns the strap, not its line ([engine.js:159-164][engine]), and `pointAt` needs a `curves` selection ([tools.js:251-253][tools]); no route selects it, so delete and redraw. The look is set before drawing, from a swatch also used by bands ([tools.js:44, 611-613][tools], [ShapeTab.jsx:234][shape]).
- *Real:* a separate strip caught in seams at each end ("sewn into the side seams", `093908`). The drawing matches; the record of the catch does not exist.

**6. Strap cage.** 6-strap fan (`EllaZwart`): opening by block (2) or closed line (N + 4), then 3 mirrored strokes, about 3N + 12. X or double-X (6 of 30 backs): a leg across the centre is not mirrored, so 2 or 4 separate strokes, symmetric by eye only. A mirrored X's two halves share one stacking height, because `order` is per strap id ([render/straps.js:47, 54][straps]), so they meet in one plane. **[inferred]** They would z-fight at the crossing unless split with Mirror off. Clicking a strap in Rows puts stones on its path at lift 0 ([tools.js:311-313][tools], [generators.js:246][gen]); `isFabric` drops those over an opening ([generators.js:611-613][gen]) and the rest lie below the ribbon's height ([engine.js:576][engine]) **[inferred]**. Moodboard norm: one stone row on each strap [cp §2].

**7. Narrow band over a seam (`f035dc11`): 5 per line.** Click line (1), Flat tape (1), Width (1), Colour (1), Material (1). Each line must first exist as a seam, which cuts the piece; `523f80ff`'s free strips would become piece boundaries. Real: band laid on and zigzagged along both long edges ([appliqué][appl]). Not matched where the fabric under it is whole.

**8. Ruching, gather, twist-knot: about 6, look only.** Sparkle tab (1), Lettering (1), Look: Gathers, Ruching or Twist knot (1), click body (1), Width (1), Turn (1). Discoverability is poor: it lives under lettering. Real: extra length gathered onto a tab or seam; pieces are unaffected here, so no fullness is cut.

**9. Mesh-filled window (`videoframe_7861`): N + 7. Open cut-out: N + 4.** Draw a seam (1), N ≥ 3, click first point (1), click the new piece (1), then Separate (1), Mesh (1), Sheerness (1), Colour (1); or for an opening, scissors (1). Mirrored automatically off-centre. Real: often a hole backed with mesh overlapping 1 in and zigzagged ([appliqué][appl], [realisations][real]); the app's seam is a different build. A line drawn as a seam keeps role `seam` after cutting, so Add a band is hidden on it ([ShapeTab.jsx:234][shape]).

**10. Piping or binding: 5 for the first line, 3 for each next.** Click line (1), tile (1), Width (1), Colour (1), Material (1); next line: click, tile, material chip. `trim.set` accepts a list ([commands-garment.js:470-472][cmd]) but `FinishSection` handles one line ([ShapeTab.jsx:213-237][shape]). A new finish starts white shiny ([ShapeTab.jsx:218][shape]). Real: matches, per line.

**11. Keyhole by block: 2.** Back row, Keyhole tile. Binding: 5 more. Shapes outside the seven backs: item 9. Ladder (`Taraaubergine_5`) or stacked (`48732520`): one closed line per opening, bridges by eye.

**12. Collar or cuff: 2 for one-piece high neck; 5 for a separate band.** Click line (1), Add a band on this edge (1), click band (1), fabric (1), colour (1). Height is fixed in the interface: the button passes only the swatch ([ShapeTab.jsx:234][shape]), so 1.5-4 cm cuffs mean dragging every control point. The band starts in the hidden swatch colour (default dark indigo mystique, [tools.js:44][tools]) and is not selected afterwards. Real: a band sewn on, usually folded, open at CB for a hook; no fold or opening is recorded. Its new edge is not attached to the line it came from ([commands-garment.js:668-670][cmd]), so a later neckline change leaves it behind **[inferred]**.

**13. Closures: not in the interface.** Script `hardware.add`. [Tutorial][tut] notes the same.

**14. Asymmetric sleeve: 4.** X (1), click sleeve (1), fabric tile (1), X (1). Splits and separates automatically. An asymmetric seam: X, then N + 3. A silhouette element applies to both sides, so one-side sleeve length means dragging the cuff's points. Real: matches, `Cut 1` each.

**15. Band wrapping front to back (`6b3ad2ff`): 4(N + 2) + 1 plus fills.** Two lines on the front and two on the back, each from side seam to side seam, or stopping at the seam. No "continue from here"; the back line's start is placed by eye on the side seam. The block `waist` gives only the front ([blocks.js:347-391][blocks]). [The tutorial][tut] notes that arms hide the side seam from behind in the mannequin stance **[doc]**. Real: two pieces joined at the closing seam, which matches, though without notches.

**16. Printed fake seam (`220432`): N + 4 + fills.** Draw a seam (1), Draws: Print line (1), N + 2, then per cell click and colour. Looks right; export is wrong (next question).

**17. Parallel lines (mesh waist band, nested chevrons, corset strips).** Each edge is drawn by hand; no offset. `0c2159b3`-style stacks cost one line per edge.

### Inferences
- **[inferred]** The closest match to real making is the overlay flow, and it is also the only flow with a side-choosing step. The friction is concentrated in what the user must know: which side holds a seed, whether a point is within 6 mm of centre, and that a new plate shares its base's material.
- **[inferred]** Most repeated cost comes from three absent generics: offset a line, apply to several lines, duplicate a piece.
- **[inferred]** Choosing the construction is a one-way decision at drawing time (seam tool or overlay tool), which is the opposite of how [design-line-realisations][real] frames it.

### Gaps
- Step counts come from reading the components, not timing a user. Scrolling to a control is not counted.
- Whether side seams really are hidden in the current mannequin stance is a tutorial statement that I did not check.

## What does the pattern output get right or wrong for each?

### Takeaway
It gets the new overlay model right: the base stays whole, the overlay has no allowance, caught stretches are dashed and named, and placement is dotted on the base. It is silent on straps, closures and default elastic, gives every opening a seam allowance, turns print lines into seams, and adds a slit to any piece with an interior hole.

## Do the drawing tools behave consistently in how lines start, end, snap, mirror, get previewed and get finished?

### Takeaway
No. Seam, strap and overlay share one click handler but differ in ending, closing, options and what happens afterwards, and Rows of crystals follows a different model again. The largest gaps: no snap preview, no line mirrored up to the centre, attached ends that cannot be dragged, and strap paths that cannot be edited.

### Cited Findings
Functions are `ToolController` methods in [ui/tools.js][tools] unless named. All [code].

Further inconsistencies:
- **Attached ends do not drag.** `curve.movePoint` writes `sp` only ([commands-garment.js:308][cmd]) and `controlPoints` prefers `on` ([evaluate.js:144][eval]). There is no detach or slide command in the interface, so the second hidden anchor in the app, after the panel seed. **[inferred]** The drag gives no visible result and no message.
- **Refusal wording.** "Choose a side that has fabric" is shown for a side that does have fabric but no seed ([commands-garment.js:537][cmd]).
- **Kinds that are not kinds.** The finish list depends on `isOpening`, not on the line's kind ([ShapeTab.jsx:198-201, 216][shape]); `Edge` and `Seam` both cut and both show a seam groove ([fields.js:20][fields]), and the kind only decides whether Add a band appears ([ShapeTab.jsx:234][shape]). `Print line` drops the groove but still cuts ([build.js:12][build]).
- **Finish controls.** Guides and print lines have no finish section ([ShapeTab.jsx:214][shape]), strap edges offer two options while `strap.set` accepts a third ([ShapeTab.jsx:253][shape], [commands-garment.js:628][cmd]).
- **Rows and edges.** Stones within a keep-out of an opening are dropped silently ([generators.js:611-613][gen]); the group shows (0) and the tutorial explains by hand ([tutorial][tut]). The side of `offset` depends on line direction.
- **Mirror off on overlays [inferred, not run].** `splitPanel` copies `outline` without splitting the line ([symmetry.js:119-127][sym]). Deleting one side of a free-drawn mirrored overlay runs `overlay.remove`, which deletes the shared line ([commands-garment.js:556-568][cmd]) and leaves the twin panel with no outline, its seed free to claim the layer's remaining cell. This needs a run to confirm; it could cover the body.
- **Crossing overlays [inferred, not run].** All free overlays share one layer ([commands-garment.js:509][cmd]), and a layer's lines cut each other. So two crossing free-drawn bands fragment; the second takes the smallest unclaimed cell ([commands-garment.js:514][cmd]). Line-to-line pieces have a layer each, so they do not slice **[test]** ([materials.test.js:185-190][testmat]), but they share one height ([commands-garment.js:545][cmd]) and would fight where they overlap ([cm][cm]).

### Inferences
- **[inferred]** One shared line-drawing behaviour, with the tool deciding only what the finished line becomes, would remove most rows of this table. The end rule, close rule, preview and finish could be common.
- **[inferred]** The `crosses` rule answers a real need (a neckline is one part) with a test on point positions. What the designer needs in addition is a half-line mirrored to the centre, which meets its own image at CF.

### Gaps
- Behaviours marked [inferred, not run] need one run in a tab nobody designs in; the brief excluded that here.
