# Leotard construction techniques: a catalogue by what the designer wants to achieve

Scope: gymnastics leotards. Rule-checking is deferred by the owner and is not covered here. Written 2026-10-31.

## What other techniques are we missing?

### Takeaway

The owner's discovery (a plate sewn on top of a larger mesh piece) is one of at least four different builds for the same look, and the app cannot yet express the others. The biggest missing ideas are: a layer that sits *under* a see-through layer, a seam enclosed between shell and lining, a layer with its own free finished edge, gathered or twisted fabric, a binding that continues as a strap, and named strap-end joins.

### Cited Findings

**How to read the evidence grades used in every section**

- **Strong:** two or more independent how-to sources, or factory reporting plus a how-to.
- **Moderate:** one detailed how-to (Jalie or Marie Porter) plus brand copy showing the device on gymnastics leotards.
- **Weak:** brand copy only, one search snippet, or one how-to from an adjacent garment.
- **[PHOTO]:** my own reading of a product photo at about 620 px wide. This is always an inference, never a confirmed build.
- **[SNIPPET]:** seen only in a search summary, page not opened.
- **[SINGLE]:** one source only.
- Moderate cards usually rest on one maker (Jalie or Porter), so treat them as one line of evidence. [SINGLE] marks cards where even that maker shows the method only once. Brand-copy facts are each from the linked listing alone.

### Inferences

Suggested priority for the app, highest value first:

1. **A layer stack per region with an explicit "seen through" relation.** Without it, Lunette-style builds (shine under mesh) and Hera-style builds (plate on mesh) look the same in the model but cut differently.
2. **A join-type property on each line:** pieced seam, enclosed seam, applique edge (stitch type, margin, what happens underneath), basted edge (layers treated as one), floated edge (finished by hand across a closing seam).
3. **Lining as its own piece set,** not a copy of the shell. Jalie's lining pieces differ from shell pieces in most patterns.
4. **A free-edged layer:** one that is attached on some edges only and finished along the rest.
5. **Gather as a seam property:** cut length, finished length and gather method.
6. **Edge-to-strap continuity,** and named strap-end joins with a "set at fitting" flag.
7. **Thread colour per seam segment,** because seams show through mesh.

## How is a bodice divided into pieces in practice?

### Takeaway

Commercial patterns give the first real piece counts: roughly 8 to 18 cut pieces including trims and lining, usually with seams at shoulders and crotch, side seams unless side panels replace them, and a front on the fold unless the design is asymmetric. Backs split between on-the-fold and a centre-back seam. Decoration goes on before those closing seams, which confirms the project's order but adds that the crotch is often sewn first.

- **A3. Waist seam, straight or V.** A V waist needs a guide stitch 6 mm in and a clip at the point on both pieces, then the point is sewn without stretching, the result checked from the right side, and only then is the rest of the waist sewn, this time stretching: [3910 Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf), [3673 Violeta](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3673.pdf). **Where closing seams sit and in what order**

- Jalie's order across gymnastics patterns: appliques first, then yokes, then **crotch with crotch lining**, then shoulders (stitched without stretching on Kate), then sides or sleeves, then elastics: [3901 Kate](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3901.pdf), [3464 Lillie](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3464.pdf).
- A ChalkBucket maker also sews the crotch first so it can be top-stitched flat, then chooses side or shoulder next according to how tricky the applique is: [ChalkBucket](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/latest) [SNIPPET].
- The crotch lining is enclosed with a roll: lining right side to the back's wrong side, stitched over the crotch seam, then brought forward and basted along the leg edges: [3464 Lillie](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3464.pdf), [3910 Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf).
- GK reportedly fastens pieces with temporary glue before sewing, with logos embroidered afterwards: [NAM](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672). This confirms project knowledge.
- Allowance on all of these is 6 mm, with exceptions at strap joins (1 cm), sleeve hems (2 to 2.5 cm), leg hems (2 cm) and clasp folds (about 1.5 cm): [3241](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3241.pdf), [2792](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_2792.pdf).
- Pattern sheets state minimum fabric stretch for the pattern: 50% both ways on [Kate](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3901.pdf), 60% on [Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf) and [Tessa](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3891.pdf).

### Inferences

What the tool must capture, by card:

- **All cards:** each piece with fabric, count (1, mirrored pair, on fold), greatest-stretch direction, notch set and role. The fold is only offered when the piece is symmetric across its centre line.
- **A3:** the V point as a special operation: guide stitch, clip, sew point unstretched, then the rest stretched. An operation list, not only a line.
- **A5/A6:** a garment with no side seam, so "side seam" cannot be assumed as a closing seam. The closing seams become whichever seams are sewn last.
- **A7:** lining pieces with their own, simpler division.
- **A10:** overlapping pieces joined only at seams, with a free fold or bound edge, so two pieces occupy one surface region.
- **A11:** a wrap panel crossing a side seam is two cut pieces unless the side seam is deleted. Tool choice: split at the side seam and match at the stitch line, or remove the seam.
- **Order:** the project's "closing seams last" holds, but the crotch is not necessarily last. Store order per pattern.

- No factory piece counts or marker data. Zone counts in brand copy remain fabric zones, not pieces.
- No source shows how a front-to-back wrap panel is actually cut on a gymnastics leotard.

## What do the Sylvia P and GK terms physically mean?

### Takeaway

Brand words describe the look, not one build, and the same word covers different constructions. Only Sylvia P's fabrication lists are close to a piece list. Fabric-by-zone statements in that copy are reliable; anything about how the layers join is my reading of photos and is marked so.

### Cited Findings

- **`Underlay`.** Whatever lies beneath the visible feature, in two opposite roles. An opaque plate under mesh: `Upper body underlay panels` in mystique under a `Mesh overlay` on [Lunette](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/lunette-leotard-3q-sleeve), and matt Lycra under gathered mesh on [Kamira](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/kamira-leotard-3q-sleeve). Or a sheer panel under straps: `Back twist underlay` on [Hazel](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/hazel-leotard-3q-sleeve). Evidence: moderate for zones. [PHOTO] Lunette's close-up shows mesh haze continuing over the mystique from neckline to waist seam, so the plate really is under the mesh.
- **`Overlay` / `mesh overlay`.** Usually mesh over a body fabric: [Lunette](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/lunette-leotard-3q-sleeve), [Quatro Torch](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/torch), [Quatro Bluebell](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/bluebell), where the overlay also forms the high neck and racer shape while the Lycra base sits beneath. But GK's [E5407](https://www.gkelite.com/products/e5407-distributor) uses overlay for mesh straps lying over a deep scoop back, GK's [4958S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/4958s-sales-rep) says overlaying panels, and Destira's [Infinite Shine](https://destira.com/products/infinite-shine-blush-leotard) uses it for a gold foil layer. Evidence: moderate that the word is unreliable.
- **`Double layer` / `double layer look`.** At least five builds in one catalogue: mesh over mystique ([Lunette](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/lunette-leotard-3q-sleeve)); gathered mesh over matt ([Kamira](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/kamira-leotard-3q-sleeve)); fine front straps that merely suggest a second neckline ([Apollo](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/apollo-leotard-3q-sleeve)); a mesh panel under twisted back straps ([Myla](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/myla-leotard-3q-sleeve)); and an opaque printed halter front over a mesh yoke, each with its own neck binding ([Soraya](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/soraya-leotard-3q-sleeve), which lists `Underlay neck binding` separately). Evidence: moderate for zones. [PHOTO] Soraya shows two distinct neck edges.
- **`Double layer neckline`.** The Kamira and Apollo builds above. Kamira's copy also calls it engineered, with no stated meaning. Evidence: weak.
- **`Wrap illusion`.** On [Paige](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/paige-leotard-3q-sleeve): a mesh `body panel overlay` over a mystique `Accent underlay body panel`, on a matt `Main body`, with `Neckline & panel binding` in matt. Nothing wraps; the copy says it gives the illusion. [PHOTO] A bound diagonal edge runs from a front keyhole to the opposite side, reading as the edge of a wrapped layer. Evidence: moderate for zones, inference for joins.
- **`Wrap mesh` (GK).** Listed on [9652S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/9652s-distributor) with mesh sleeves and cut-outs. [PHOTO] Shows stacked, gathered bands crossing the torso and gathering into the side seam, with sheer areas above and below. In the colourway shown the bands look opaque, so the name may refer to the design rather than a fabric. Evidence: weak.
- **`Gathered overlay panels`.** Named on [Kamira](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/kamira-leotard-3q-sleeve), cut from mesh over an opaque `Upper body underlay`. [PHOTO] Mesh gathers fan out from the shoulder and neck, cross at the chest, and leave crisp free edges lying over the underlay. ### Inferences

- The app should not model `overlay`, `underlay` or `double layer` as single features. They resolve to B3, B4, B6, D3 or a strap arrangement.
- `Underlay` plates behind mesh are cut as shaped pieces, and because they are seen through mesh, their edges are visible design lines. The app should draw them as lines even though they are under another layer.
- A useful test for reading a photo: mesh haze over the shiny area means the plate is under; a narrow stitched edge with no haze means the plate is on top; a soft ridge and no haze means a pieced seam.

### Gaps

- No brand states how `invisible binding`, `set-in neckband`, `accent panel lines` or `back twists` are sewn. Inspecting an actual garment inside-out would settle most of these in minutes.

## Given a mesh upper bodice carrying two opaque plates, is it a whole mesh piece with plates on top, or pieced panels?

### Takeaway

All four builds are documented or named, and catalogue copy does not usually say which was used. A maker decides mainly on how many sides of the plate touch mesh, whether the shape can be pieced, and whether seam allowances would show through. When mesh surrounds a plate on several sides, whole mesh with the plate on top is the practical build; when mesh only sits above a line, piecing with the seam enclosed by lining is the clean build.

### Cited Findings

**The builds**

- **B1. Pieced mesh yoke, seam enclosed by lining.** Jade: yoke is sewn to the front; the front lining is then sewn over the same seam with the yoke sandwiched between shell and lining; shell and lining are brought wrong sides together and basted at the raw edges. The mesh stays unlined and the lining stops at the seam: [3910 Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf). [PHOTO] Sylvia P's Myla looks pieced, with a soft curved seam where mesh meets body and no haze: [Myla](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/myla-leotard-3q-sleeve). Evidence: moderate.
- **B2. **What makers say that bears on the decision**

- Lines too intricate to seam are overlaid. Simple lines are seamed and coverstitched; swirly designs are overlaid and zigzagged: [ChalkBucket](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/post-274958) [SNIPPET]. Confirms project knowledge.
- Porter says a mesh body piece behaves like one large applique: [How to Applique](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-applique-spandex/).
- Mesh seams show. Porter sews mesh-area seams in beige because an allowance can flip under a sheer section and show through dark thread: [Art Deco](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-i-made-that-art-deco-skating-dress/). - Overlaps need a planned lip. ### Inferences

**Decision guide for two plates on a mesh bodice (mine, built from the sources above)**

| Question | Points to |
|---|---|
| Does mesh touch the plate on only its top edge? | Pieced (B1), because the seam is one line and can be enclosed by lining |
| Does mesh touch the plate on two or more sides, or run between plates? | Whole mesh (B3) or under-mesh plates (B4), because the piecing would need L-shaped openings |
| Does the plate have tight curves, sharp inside corners or points? | Applique on top (B3) |
| Should the plate look shiny through a haze? | Under the mesh (B4) |
| Is the mesh sheer in a colour unlike the allowances? | Avoid an exposed pieced seam: enclose it, or use B3 or B4 |
| Is the plate velvet or the base velvet? | Avoid stitching onto velvet; piece it instead |
| Is the plate fragile foil, or will stones be pressed on it? | Consider applying stones before applying the plate |
| Must the mesh stay stretchy across the bust? | Prefer fewer stitched perimeters; no numbers exist |

**What the tool must capture**

- **B1:** shell, mesh yoke and lining pieces; lining shape stops at the yoke seam; a "sandwich" seam type; lining basted to shell at raw edges; 6 mm allowance; no trim.
- **B2:** whole shell piece plus inset; the inset's overlap; placement on the wrong side; cut-away line defined as "inside the stitch"; order: cut-away before shoulders.
- **B3:** full-pattern mesh plus full-pattern lining cut as a bonded pair; plates with no allowance; a lip on any edge that tucks under another; stitch order per plate; trim-behind option.
- **B4:** plate pieces between lining and mesh; mesh on top with no stitching visible on the plate unless chosen; plate edge as a visible line. How the plate is held is unknown, so the tool should flag it as a manufacturer choice.
- **B5:** window contour, backing 1 in larger, stitch in main-fabric thread.
- **B6:** two neck edges with two finishes; layers joined at shoulder, side and waist only; a free edge finish on the upper layer.
- **Every overlay:** "under treatment" with values left whole, trim to margin (default 10 mm), cut close, or no overlap (lip only).
- **C2/C3:** strip width, cut direction, stitch (coverstitch or zigzag), and whether it covers a raw edge or lies free. Strips crossing closing seams need the float treatment.
- **C5:** the band's outer edge is caught in a seam, so only one edge needs surface stitching, and the band must be applied before that seam.
- **C6:** a two-piece plate with a border offset.
- **C7:** an explicit over/under order at each crossing, and cut top pieces with tuck allowance.

**Shape limits and stretch by build (my summary; where no source is linked, it is my inference)**

| Build | Shape limits | Stretch and comfort |
|---|---|---|
| Pieced seam | Project's rules hold; V or sweetheart point needs guide stitch, clip and pivot ([Olivia](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4455.pdf)) | Stretch across a seam is not measured; flat seams are advertised for comfort ([Plum](https://plumpracticewear.com/products/plum-swirly-gymnastics-unitard-for-girls)) |
| Enclosed yoke seam (B1) | As pieced seam; lining shape must follow the same line | No raw allowance against skin along that line |
| Inset from behind (B2) | Any stitchable outline; Tessa's is a simple V | One extra stitched perimeter; overlap varies from close trim to 1 in |
| Whole mesh with plates (B3) | Any stitchable shape; overlaps need a lip | Each plate adds a stitched perimeter and a second layer; plate must stretch as much as the base ([3138](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3138.pdf)) |
| Plates under mesh (B4) | Any cut shape; edges show through | Extra layer, likely warmer; heat limits on fabric are noted only for velvet, per the project's Diaz source |
| Strips (C3) | Set by the binder width (¾ in here) and curve tightness | Depends on cut direction; bias trim is described as stretchy ([Ovation](https://babylock.com/ovation-gymnast-leotard)) |
| Rolled strip (C4) | Any traced line | Strip must stretch like the main fabric ([3134](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3134.pdf)) |
| Gathers (D1-D3) | Fans from one seam; opposite edge flat or also gathered | Spare fabric may allow more give; a second layer is needed under sheer gathers |
| Pinch front (D4) | Short, at centre front only | Stays snug at the neckline ([2916](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_2916.pdf)) |

### Gaps

- No maker or brand states which of these a specific catalogue leotard uses, so the frequency of each build is unknown.
- How under-mesh plates are held on a Lunette-style build (stitched through the mesh, held only in seams, or bonded to the lining) was not found.
- No guidance on seam choice for sheer mesh in stretch. French seams are the general answer for sheer fabrics ([Chalk and Notch](https://www.chalkandnotch.com/blogs/news/sewing-tips-french-seams) [SNIPPET]) but nothing addresses their stretch.

## Shaping and volume: ruching, shirring, gathers, twists, drapes, lacing, pleats, padding

### Takeaway

Volume is rare but real at the top of the market: gathered mesh appears on 10 Quatro styles and on Sylvia P's Kamira, and GK offers ruched bodices and waistbands. How-to evidence comes from adjacent garments, so build details are moderate at best. Pleats, knots, drapes and shirring are not named in current gymnastics copy apart from GK's Shirred style name.

### Cited Findings

- **D1. Gathered seam.** Names: gathers, `Gathered`. Build: extra cut length is reduced by two rows of gathering stitches inside the allowance, between notches, then the bobbin threads are pulled and gathers adjusted to fit the mating piece: [3350 view B](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3350.pdf), a swimsuit. Limits: no maximum ratio is given. Stretch: not stated. Evidence: moderate.
- **D2. Clear-elastic ruching along a seam.** Names: ruching, rouching. Build: clear elastic is cut to 65% of the span and zigzagged along the seam, stretching only the elastic: [Rouching with elastic](https://5outof4.com/rouching-side-seams-with-elastic/) (a shirt, not a leotard, [SINGLE]). A second method uses clear elastic sized to the mating seam plus tails: [Ready to Sew](https://readytosew.fr/en/journal/froncer-du-jersey-a-la-laminette-b115.html). Build: string or tube threaded through 2 in loops, straight-stitched through both layers at marked spots; pattern allows string width plus one elastic width each side; lacing is done after elastic and the ends trimmed and knotted: [Porter](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/). Drape appears only for crystals or beading, not fabric: [Maeve](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/maeve-leotard-3q-sleeve), [Plum Dynamic](https://plumpracticewear.com/products/plum-simply-elegant-dynamic-competition-leotard). Evidence: negative, from the tally above.
- **D12. Padding.** Padded applique is in the project already. Bust pads are covered under added pieces. No new shaping padding found.

### Inferences

What the tool must capture:

- **D1/D3:** cut length versus finished seam length (a gather ratio per seam), where the gather starts and stops, whether the opposite edge is flat, fabric. Crystals on gathers cannot be placed by flat geometry, so warn or disallow machine motifs there.
- **D2:** elastic length, position (usually along a seam), "stretch elastic only".
- **D4:** pleat count, depth and the distance down from the edge; taken after the neck finish, so the cut pattern has no dart.
- **D5/D6:** a twist cannot be represented as a flat region. It needs a piece with a twist point, a fixed turn direction, and a note that it is set by hand. The cut shape needs cutting in full, with extra length, and cannot be mirrored blindly.
- **D8:** loop count and spacing; string length long, trimmed at fitting; ends knotted.
- **Stretch:** a gathered panel has spare fabric, so it may stretch more before going taut. No source confirms this.

### Gaps

- No cut-length ratios for gathered mesh on leotards. GK's Shirred style could not be matched to a current listing to inspect.
- How free edges of gathered mesh overlays are finished was not found.
- No how-to for a Hazel or Myla back twist.

## Edges and joins

### Takeaway

Jalie documents three distinct binding methods, two with elastic inside, which is much more specific than the project's "binding wraps the edge". Bound edges keep a 6 mm seam line inside, contradicting the project's inferred zero allowance. Picot, lettuce and raw or laser-cut edges have no evidence on current gymnastics leotards, and decorative topstitching appears mainly in makers' descriptions, not brand copy.

### Cited Findings

**Bindings and bands**

- **F1. Binding wrapped from the right side, elastic inside (`binding`, `contrast binding`).** Binding is joined into a loop, elastic loop basted to its wrong side along one raw edge, binding pinned right side to right side at the opening, zigzag on the elastic while stretching the band, guide stitch, binding brought to the wrong side around the elastic, topstitched from the right side through all layers, the excess trimmed along the stitch on the inside: [3901 Kate](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3901.pdf). The inside shows a raw trimmed edge. Evidence: moderate.
- **F2. Binding wrapped from the wrong side, elastic inside.** Free raw edge of the binding goes against the wrong side of the opening, stitched while stretching, turned out, elastic folded under, topstitched on the outside: [3354](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3354.pdf). Elastic equals binding length in this method and the stretch comes from pinning to the opening. Evidence: moderate.
- **F3. Binding with no elastic.** Right side of binding to wrong side of fabric, stretched to fit, seam allowance wrapped by the binding, folded to the right side, topstitched: [3138](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3138.pdf), [3136](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3136.pdf). Evidence: moderate.
- **F4. Folded band or neckband.** Band folded lengthwise, raw edges basted, pinned to the right side of the neck and stretched to fit, seam allowance then turned: [2792](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_2792.pdf), [4349 Emma](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4349.pdf). Leg bands also exist as an alternative to leg elastic: [3910 Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf). Evidence: strong.
- **F5. Collars.** Mock neck: collar folded lengthwise, pinned raw edges to right side of neck and stretched to fit: [4237 Fiona](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4237_67cd0454-765d-4a67-b5cc-d17fc4116150.pdf). Fold-over elastic.** Attached to the wrong side, folded over, stitched from the right side: [Made Everyday](https://www.madeeveryday.com/sewing-with-spandex-for-the-little-gymnast/), [Seamwork](https://www.seamwork.com/articles/mastering-lingerie-elastic); an option on [4021 Camille](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4021.pdf). ### Inferences

What the tool must capture:

- **Edge types as separate objects:** turned elastic; wrap binding from outside (F1); wrap binding from inside (F2); binding with no elastic (F3); folded band (F4); collar (F5). They differ in which side shows a seam, the visible width, whether the edge is trimmed and whether elastic is inside.
- **For each binding:** cut width and length (per size), seam allowance at the edge (6 mm kept inside, not zero), elastic length, join position (usually a seam or centre back), trim operation, what shows on the inside. Interior raw edges are not visible through opaque fabric but are visible through mesh.
- **Corner treatment:** V lap, mitre, separate bands with tacked corners.
- **Closures:** type, location, the fold allowance they require, extra length (Porter's ½ to 1 in), and the elastic they interrupt.
- **Zip:** a mandatory centre seam, an order constraint (before side seams and decoration), and a warning when combined with enclosed lining.
- **Thread:** seam-segment colour list, plus optional second-run stitch.
- **Piping:** split into set-in piping (needs a seam) and surface piping effect (needs a line only).

### Gaps

- No measured binding widths for brands. Jalie cut widths are on pattern pieces I did not measure.
- No source on the visual difference between F1 and F2 at gymnastics brands.
- No build for split collars. A photo of a real one would help.

## Straps

### Takeaway

The project's largest gap is now filled: there are at least eight documented ways to attach a strap end, and they fall into before-elastic and after-elastic groups. Length is usually finalised at a fitting, and designs that cross or braid straps are simply left to the sewer. Nothing documents how crossings are fixed in place.

Confirms the project.

### Inferences

What the tool must capture:

- **Strap object:** construction (string, turned, rolled, part of back, separately bound, binding continuation), width, greatest stretch along the length, fabric.
- **Each end:** one of the eight join types, order relative to the elastic, allowance (6 mm, 1 cm), and whether the edge elastic passes over it. This decides whether the end must exist before or after the elastic step.
- **Length:** target length plus surplus, and a "set at fitting" flag. That length should not drive machine stones placed along the strap.
- **Crossings:** order (who is on top), whether free or tacked. Default to free unless the maker says otherwise.
- **Applied strips against free straps:** look alike at design scale but cut differently. A strap crossing an opening is free; a strip crossing fabric could be free (over mesh) or stitched down. The tool should ask.
- **Shoulder width formula** from the project still applies and should count the join.

### Inferences

A simple chooser for the tool (mine):

1. Is the base white polyester? If not, print is out.
2. Is the line straight or gently curved with no inside corners? If yes, a seam works; prefer it when the two sides differ in fabric anyway.
3. Does the line separate sheer from opaque? Choose B1, B2 or B3 using the plate guide above.
4. Does it have to read as a raised or textured line? Applied strip or stones, not print.
5. Does it cross a closing seam? Print and floating applique cope; a pieced line needs matching at the stitch line.
6. Does it cross high-wear zones? Avoid mesh and foil there per the project's durability rule.
7. Budget: print, then seam, then applique, then twist and lacing, in rising hand operations. This order is my estimate, not sourced.

Since the exported pattern changes with the choice, the design should keep the look (line) separate from its realisation, so the user can switch without redrawing. That matches the project's line-type idea and extends it with B1, B2, B4 and C3 as new realisations.

**Contradicts or corrects**

- `seams-stitches-and-allowances.md` and `edge-finishing-elastic-and-binding.md` infer a bound edge has zero allowance.
