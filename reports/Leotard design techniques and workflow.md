# Draw first, decide how it sews

**Let the user draw the line first and decide afterwards how it is made: that one change answers most of what you asked.** The public record shows professional leotard designers starting from a reference photo, a client's drawing or a catalogue style, drawing in 2D software, handing fit and cutting to a pattern department, and changing backs and stones late or separately. What you found by copying a design, a plate sewn onto a larger piece, is one of at least four documented builds for the same look, and the wider truth is that nearly every visible line on a leotard can be seamed, laid on top or printed, with current catalogues mixing all three on one garment and photos rarely showing which was used. The app offers two of those four builds. It cannot express a window backed with mesh; it has no button for a plate under mesh, a lining or a closure; it shows wraps, gathers and printed fake seams without truly building them; and it draws straps that the pattern never hears about. On our analysis these are not forty missing tools, because about seven general ideas cover nearly all of them. Our reading of your moodboard is that the app can usually be made to look right already, so the gaps that matter are truth (your 31 October rule that the model must match what is really cut and sewn) and effort (users must know hidden rules to succeed). We recommend one grammar for the drawing tools: lines land, the areas they enclose light up, a tap picks an area, and how it is made stays changeable. Two cautions govern everything below: no brand publishes stitch-level construction, so every account of how something is built comes from pattern makers and costume makers standing in for factories, and no source explains how a designer decides what a line becomes, which only a conversation with a working designer can settle.

*How to read this report.* Fact, single-source claims and inference are kept apart throughout. A plain statement with a link is **fact**: independent sources agree, or a maker is describing its own product. Where several linked examples support one point, the point is the fact and each example rests on its own link. **One source** marks a claim resting on a single source or a single maker, with a note when that source is sponsored, dated or was seen only as a search excerpt. **Our inference** marks this report's own reasoning; no source says it, and it must not be read as trade practice. Statements about what the app does today come from reading its source code at the current commit (2e72750, 31 October 2026); the app was not run for this research. A "script command" is one only an agent or script can reach, with no button. Moodboard counts are estimates from photo teardowns, about 106 garments in 138 photos with only 30 backs visible, good to about ±10%. Competition-rule checking is left out, as you deferred it.

## Designers remix someone else's picture in 2D and hand the cutting to others

**A leotard is rarely one person's idea.** In college gymnastics the coaching staff usually originates the design and asks athletes for inspiration (one source: [College Gym News 2025](https://collegegymnews.com/2025/03/03/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-college-gymnast-leotards/)), and named coach-designers include Heather Brink at Nebraska, Geralen Stack-Eaton at Minnesota and Erin Dooley at Maryland ([College Gym News 2020](https://collegegymnews.com/2020/01/15/nebraska-leos-a-labor-of-love-for-head-coach-heather-brink/); [College Gym News 2024](https://collegegymnews.com/2024/03/28/behind-the-design-a-look-at-leotards/)). Sales staff sit inside the loop: Stack-Eaton takes her ideas to a GK representative, who turns them into mockups ([College Gym News 2024](https://collegegymnews.com/2024/03/28/behind-the-design-a-look-at-leotards/)), and OZONE describes advisors and designers building a client's idea together (one source, sponsored: [Inside Gymnastics](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/interview-with-ozones-vice-president-lea-brown/)). The designers themselves are insiders with apparel or graphic training who stay for years: GK's design director Jeanne Diaz was hired straight out of Cornell's textiles programme in 2010 ([Cornellians](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/diaz-olympics)), and OZONE's Lea Brown studied graphic design, was a gymnast from age three through college and coached before joining (sponsored: [Inside Gymnastics](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/interview-with-ozones-vice-president-lea-brown/)). *Our inference:* the app is entering a three-way loop of originator, translator and go-between, in which the person with the idea often does not hold the pen. That supports your plan for a coach view over the same file, and suggests the designer release will often be used with a client watching.

**The working canvas is flat.** GK sketches custom leotards in Adobe Illustrator ([NBC](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/paris-2024-summer-olympics/jordan-chiles-2024-paris-olympics-gymnastics-beyonce/)), and Elite Sportswear's job ads ask for Illustrator and Photoshop, print-ready artwork and only basic knowledge of construction and patterns (search excerpts of closed postings: [Paylocity](https://recruiting.paylocity.com/recruiting/jobs/Details/3435444/Elite-Sportswear/Associate-Designer---Gymnastics-Apparel); [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/c/Elite-Sportswear/Job/Associate-Designer-Gymnastics-Apparel/-in-Reading,PA?jid=871e234a862e0e23)). Crystal layout at GK runs on computer-aided design software it does not name ([Cornellians](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/diaz-olympics); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/?p=108824)). Coaches and athletes draw by hand ([College Gym News 2020](https://collegegymnews.com/2020/01/15/nebraska-leos-a-labor-of-love-for-head-coach-heather-brink/); [FloGymnastics](https://www.flogymnastics.com/article/60156-winner-lovely-leos-from-the-2017-u-s-classic)). The only 3D found is built for customers, such as GK's UDesign360 and Moreau Sport's configurator ([Mann Publications](https://www.mannpublications.com/fashionmannuscript/?p=12153); [Le Journal des Entreprises](https://www.lejournaldesentreprises.com/article/amelie-rivoire-cultive-sa-fibre-entrepreneuriale-2056615)), and the brand studios described on their makers' own pages swap options inside fixed zones, with none found that lets a user draw a new panel line ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/products/nylah-leotard-full-sleeve); [GK](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/udesign); [Quatro](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/teamwear-design-studio)). No leotard maker was found naming CLO, Browzwear or Optitex, which is absence of evidence and not proof of absence.

**Documented work almost never starts from a blank body.** GK pulled Jordan Chiles's photo of a Beyoncé outfit into its files and built the leotard from it ([NBC](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/paris-2024-summer-olympics/jordan-chiles-2024-paris-olympics-gymnastics-beyonce/)). Clients with no idea ask OZONE for proofs in their colours, and clients with several pictures get them merged into one concept sketch (sponsored: [Inside Gymnastics](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/interview-with-ozones-vice-president-lea-brown/)). Brink brings a drawing, a dress or a belt ([College Gym News 2020](https://collegegymnews.com/2020/01/15/nebraska-leos-a-labor-of-love-for-head-coach-heather-brink/)); Dooley browses catalogues and adds a twist ([College Gym News 2024](https://collegegymnews.com/2024/03/28/behind-the-design-a-look-at-leotards/)); national collections begin with an athlete survey and mood boards (search excerpt: [Fashionista](https://fashionista.com/2024/07/olympics-2024-gymnastics-team-usa-uniforms-leotards-design-process); also [NBC News](https://www.nbcdfw.com/paris-2024-summer-olympics/olympic-gymnastics-a-sneak-peek-at-the-leotards-simone-biles-and-team-usa-will-wear-in-paris/)). One report says a new design at GK then starts as a line drawing (one source: [NBC](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/paris-2024-summer-olympics/jordan-chiles-2024-paris-olympics-gymnastics-beyonce/)), but whether that drawing sits on a figure or a flat, and whether it shows seams, is unknown, because no professional base file has been published. *Our inference:* an empty mannequin matches none of these openings, while a base style in team colours, a pinned photo and an imported sketch all do.

**No source supports a fixed order of silhouette, then lines, then fabric, then crystals.** The evidence shows constraints and references first; colour early at clubs, where it is usually fixed and the style changes (search excerpt: [ChalkBucket](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/team-leotards-who-chooses-them.33616/)); function before decoration ([Vogue Scandinavia](https://www.voguescandinavia.com/articles/team-usa-olympic-uniform-is-made-by-swarovski)); swatches after the mockup, on our reading of one composite article ([College Gym News 2024](https://collegegymnews.com/2024/03/28/behind-the-design-a-look-at-leotards/)); and stones adjustable to the end, down to Brown adding jewels in a hotel room before a meet (sponsored: [Inside Gymnastics](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/inside-look-ozones-exclusive-why-i-chose-ozone-presented-by-ozone-leotards/)) and Sylvia P selling heavy and light stone versions of one design (search excerpt: [Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/lexi-leotard-3q-sleeve)). The back behaves as its own part: Brown says a new back changes the whole look while the front art and stones stay the same, and OZONE aims for four to five new back patterns a year (one source, sponsored: [Inside Gymnastics](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/interview-with-ozones-vice-president-lea-brown/)), while GK's 2025 catalogue pitches choosing a back and neckline first (search excerpt: [GK](https://cdn.abicart.com/shop/ws57/101257/files/Catalouges/2025_GK_Catalog_Pages_LR.pdf)). *Our inference:* a typical working order runs who wears it and where, what is fixed, a reference or base, function, front lines and art, back, fabric, stones, a sample on a body, then last stone changes. It can be suggested but must never be enforced, and each of those parts has to change without disturbing the others.

**The designer's drawing is not the pattern.** At GK the line drawing goes to a pattern department; fabric is laser cut for fit, art is printed and heat-transferred, appliqués (shapes stitched on top) are hand-placed, glued and stitched, crystal motifs are pressed on, and only then are front and back closed ([NBC](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/paris-2024-summer-olympics/jordan-chiles-2024-paris-olympics-gymnastics-beyonce/); [NAM](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672); [NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/)). One GK designer's job in 2016 included the paperwork that carries sewing and embellishment information to pattern makers and the floor (one source: [UDaily](https://www.udel.edu/udaily/2016/august/olympic-apparel-designs/)), and pattern staff engineer each style heavily because every fabric reacts differently (one source, 2012: [BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/amyodell/everything-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-team-usa)). No leotard design sheet or tech pack is public. *Our inference:* the designer owns the look, line placement, print art, stone motifs and fabric choice, and the pattern department owns fit, allowances, stretch and sizing, which is why the app's export has to carry intent as well as shapes.

**Iteration is client-led and repeats at two stages, drawing and sample, and the most concrete failure happens between them.** Nebraska's samples matched what was requested, yet seeing them on a body prompted changes that delayed delivery ([College Gym News 2020](https://collegegymnews.com/2020/01/15/nebraska-leos-a-labor-of-love-for-head-coach-heather-brink/)), and Oklahoma's athletes try samples to see where everything lands (search excerpt, 2017: [Sooner Sports](https://soonersports.com/news/2017/1/4/211382289)); that is two accounts, both from GK clients. Stack-Eaton tests each sketch with GK for practicality and price as a separate step ([College Gym News 2024](https://collegegymnews.com/2024/03/28/behind-the-design-a-look-at-leotards/)). OZONE runs live calls in which coaches watch colours change and stones move and ask for changes on the spot (one source, sponsored: [Inside Gymnastics](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/interview-with-ozones-vice-president-lea-brown/)). Sources describe several rounds and months of elapsed time, and never give a count of rounds or designer hours. Stated visual concerns are thin and dated: symmetry against busyness and designs that chop up the body (one source, 2012: [BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/amyodell/everything-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-team-usa)), a taste for shine that reversed by 2024 ([Vogue Scandinavia](https://www.voguescandinavia.com/articles/team-usa-olympic-uniform-is-made-by-swarovski)), and crystals carrying design detail ([Cornellians](https://alumni.cornell.edu/cornellians/diaz-olympics)). Your moodboard adds one point: the commonest scheme is one colour in several finishes (about 27% of garments), and about 80% use two or three fabrics. *Our inference:* a designer starts from who wears it and the room it will be seen in, protects the body's line, decides where the eye travels, lets the back carry a second idea, and treats stones as drawing material as well as sparkle. No source says this outright.

**The intuitive picture, in which a maker fits a base garment on a form and then arranges shapes on it, is only partly true.** Factories decorate flat pieces ([NAM](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)). The best-documented route in skating and synchro is also flat: a pattern traced to paper, the design drawn on it, pieces glued flat and stitched, seams closed, stones last (one maker, the costume maker Marie Porter, across many garments: [Flames](https://spandexsimplified.com/flames-skating-dress/); [Jazz suits](https://spandexsimplified.com/jazz-team-synchro-suits/)). Makers leave the paper when a line's position relative to the body matters. Porter pins the wearer into a quick test garment and draws bust-framing lines on it ([Hela](https://spandexsimplified.com/mcu-hela-costume-cosplay/)) and tapes paper over a bodysuit on a form to plan stones ([Lover](https://spandexsimplified.com/taylor-swift-lover-outfit/)); a skater pinned a mesh panel onto herself ([Doctor T](https://doctortdesigns.com/2013/09/28/2013-figure-dress-2-the-galloping-peacock/)); a Latin dress maker fits a bodysuit first and tries decoration at fittings (one source: [PBS](https://video.wpsu.org/video/beaded-dance-dresses-mOFOmK/)). Theatre researchers who drew style lines on taped shells found that proportions read better than in flat cutting, and that students who struggled with drafting produced accurate patterns and were less afraid to experiment, though their work concerns non-stretch cloth ([Garland and Almond](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/110947/3/research%208.pdf)). *Our inference:* drawing on the 3D mannequin is the digital form of these improvised steps, a stronger reason for it to exist than any claim that leotard designers already work that way. It has to hand back the flat pieces makers cut from, and designers should be expected to ask for flats beside it.

## Your plate is one of at least four builds for the same look

### Four builds, and a photo that rarely says which

Take the design that started this: a mesh upper bodice carrying two opaque plates. **Four builds produce that look, and catalogue copy usually does not say which was used.** They differ in what is cut, what is stitched and what shows through the mesh, so the pattern changes even when the picture does not. Because no brand publishes stitch-level construction, the how-to evidence comes from Jalie's home-sewing pattern sheets, some of them gymnastics patterns, and from Marie Porter's costume write-ups, standing in for factory methods.

| Build | What it is, and what it is good for | Evidence | App today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seam enclosed by lining | Mesh is sewn to the body along one line and the lining is sewn over the same seam, sandwiching it, so the seam's raw edges are enclosed. Suits mesh sitting above a single line. | One pattern maker ([Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf); [Olivia](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4455.pdf)). Factory use unknown. | The seam, yes. The enclosure and the lining, no. |
| Window from behind (reverse appliqué, illusion inset) | Mesh lies behind the body fabric and is stitched in place, and the body is cut away inside the stitching, or cut first with the mesh overlapping about 1 in. Any stitchable outline becomes see-through with no piecing. | Fact ([Tessa](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3891.pdf); [Porter](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)). Brands sell mesh cut-outs ([GK 7682S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/7682s-distributor); [Sylvia P Fern](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/fern-leotard-3q-sleeve)). | Cannot be expressed: a lining is clipped to the fabric, so it cannot show through an opening. |
| Plate on a whole base | Mesh and lining are cut whole and the plate is stitched on top round its edge. Any shape; seams then pass through all layers. This is your find. | One maker, in skating and costume work, the only step-level source ([Flames](https://spandexsimplified.com/flames-skating-dress/); [Reputation](https://spandexsimplified.com/reputation-leotard-part-2/)). | Yes, and the closest match to real practice. |
| Plate under the mesh | A shaped opaque plate lies beneath a mesh layer, giving shine through a haze. Its edge reads as a design line though nothing is stitched on top. | Look is fact ([Sylvia P Lunette](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/lunette-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Paige](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/paige-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Quatro Cobalt](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/cobalt-long-sleeve); [Violetta](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/violetta)). No how-to found; how the plate is held is unknown. | Script command only. |

What each build is good for is our reading of these sources, apart from the shine under mesh, which is Sylvia P's own description. A fifth build, a true double layer with two separately finished necklines, rests on one product listing and a photo ([Sylvia P Soraya](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/soraya-leotard-3q-sleeve)).

How a maker chooses is among the least documented parts of the trade. Two reasons are stated: simple lines are seamed and swirly ones overlaid (search excerpt: [ChalkBucket](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/post-274958); also [Porter](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)), and fabric rules some builds out, since engineered printing needs a white base ([GK](https://www.gkelite.com/products/sbe100-dealer)) and sewing appliqué onto stretch velvet is strongly discouraged ([Jalie 3026](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3026.pdf)). The table below is **our inference**, assembled from those sources. No maker has confirmed it. It is a suggestion the app could offer, never a rule.

| If the line or plate... | It points to |
|---|---|
| has mesh touching it along one top edge only | A seam enclosed by lining: one line, easy to enclose |
| has mesh on two or more sides, or mesh running between plates | A plate on a whole base or under the mesh, because piecing would need L-shaped openings |
| has tight curves, sharp inside corners or points | A plate on top |
| should read as shine through a haze | Under the mesh |
| must read as a raised or textured line | An applied strip or stones, not print |
| crosses a side, shoulder or crotch seam | Print, or a plate finished by hand across the seam; a sewn line has to match at the stitch line |

**What happens beneath a plate matters too, and it refines your decision record.** That record says a shape sewn on top leaves the fabric under it whole, which holds for what is cut and is incomplete for what is finished. Jalie's gymnastics pattern trims the base about 1 cm from the stitching to reduce bulk ([Kate](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3901.pdf)); Porter lifts and trims each layer beneath as she stacks colours, or cuts pieces in advance so overlaps are only a small planned lip, with darker fabric on top so it does not show through ([How to appliqué](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-applique-spandex/); [Flames](https://spandexsimplified.com/flames-skating-dress/)); the window build removes the layer altogether ([Tessa](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3891.pdf)). Her bonded mesh-and-lining base does stay whole, as do bases under many small appliqués ([Jalie 3138](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3138.pdf)). *Our inference:* every plate needs an "under" setting (left whole, trimmed to a margin, cut away, lip only), because it changes weight, stretch and, under sheer fabric, what shows.

For your recreation test, one reading aid follows. *Our inference from product photos, unconfirmed by any maker:* mesh haze over a shiny area means the plate is under the mesh, a narrow stitched edge with no haze means it is on top, and a soft ridge with no haze means a pieced seam. It will not settle every garment. Only 2 of the roughly 23 moodboard garments in this family have catalogue entries that state the build, a photo cannot separate a backed window from a pieced inset, and your own reference design was judged to be a plate on a whole base from a photo, unconfirmed by its maker.

### About seven general ideas cover nearly every other missing technique

*Our inference:* the useful question is not which tools are missing but which few properties a design needs to hold, because each of the following unlocks several techniques at once. Brand-copy counts below come from a keyword tally of about 1,200 current styles at [Quatro](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/all-leotards), [GK](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order), [Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog), [Destira](https://destira.com/collections/all) and [Plum](https://plumpracticewear.com/); they count mentions, not garments or sales.

**A stack of layers, with a rule for what shows through.** A leotard is not only pieces side by side but also pieces on top of one another, which is what makes shine through mesh, a shaped lining visible under a mesh shell (about 5 moodboard garments) and lace over lining possible. It is also why brand words mislead: overlay, underlay and double layer each name a look and not a build. One Sylvia P catalogue uses double layer for at least five different constructions, and Quatro calls the same plate under mesh a lining ([Kamira](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/kamira-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Apollo](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/apollo-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Myla](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/myla-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Cobalt](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/cobalt-long-sleeve)). The brands' fabric zone lists are fact; how the layers join is read from photos and is inference. These words should never become single features in the app, which today can stack layers only by script.

**A way of joining on every line.** A line can be a seam, an enclosed seam, a stitched plate edge, a print boundary or a strip lying on the surface, and each cuts differently. The belt shows this best: named on 50 Quatro styles, it comes in at least four builds, a pieced band, an appliqué, a mesh panel, or no fabric at all with print or stones ([Olivia](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4455.pdf); [Arcadia](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/arcadia); [Saffron](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/saffron); [Elegance](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/elegance); [Omnia](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/omnia-black-pink)). Two members of this family are missing or wrong in the app. Narrow applied strips, sold as trim or accent panel lines, lie on the fabric without cutting it (build from one source: [Baby Lock](https://babylock.com/ovation-gymnast-leotard); use: [Sylvia P Aurora](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/aurora-leotard-3q-sleeve)), yet in the app a strip must be drawn as a seam, which cuts, as a strap, which the pattern ignores, or as a hand-traced outline. Printed fake seams are a named category (faux criss-cross, faux raglan, faux sweetheart, faux strapping), and one maker says they give the look without the discomfort of real straps ([GK E5484](https://www.gkelite.com/products/e5484-sales-rep); [GK 5857ST](https://www.gkelite.com/products/5857st); [GK 7597](https://www.gkelite.com/products/7597-dealer); [Destira](https://destira.com/products/tiger-lily-leotard)), yet the app's print line still cuts, so it exports two pieces and two allowances for what is one printed piece.

**Lining as its own set of pieces.** Lining gives modesty, and competition leotards normally have it: Sylvia P lines the full front of its competition range ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/the-sylvia-p-teamwear-difference)) and Jalie's gymnastics patterns carry a crotch lining ([Kate](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3901.pdf)). It is often not a copy of the outer fabric: it stops at the yoke seam on one pattern and is cut in fewer pieces on another ([Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf); [Odette](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/4566.pdf)), and shelf linings or cup pads appear on some styles and in larger sizes ([Sylvia P Prevail](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/prevail-tank-leotard-shelf-bra); [Quatro Laguna](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/laguna)). In the app a lining needs a script, the export does not label it, and no crotch lining is ever generated.

**A layer with its own free edge.** A wrap or a second neckline needs a layer attached on some edges and finished along the rest. A real wrap front, called a surplice, is two overlapping pieces held by the shoulder seams, neckband and waist seam (one pattern maker, on a general leotard: [Emma](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4349.pdf)); Sylvia P's Paige gets a similar read from a mesh layer with a bound diagonal edge, and its own copy says the wrap is an illusion ([Paige](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/paige-leotard-3q-sleeve); the edge is read from a photo). The app draws these as pieces meeting edge to edge. About 10 moodboard garments need true overlap.

**Fullness as a property of a seam.** Gathering puts extra fabric into a seam so that it fans out, which gives real softness and volume. It is rare but sits at the top of the market: gathered mesh on 10 Quatro styles and Sylvia P's Kamira, ruched bodices and waistbands at GK ([Tiffany](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/tiffany); [Kamira](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/kamira-leotard-3q-sleeve); [GK 4935S](https://www.gkelite.com/products/4935s-distributor)). No gymnastics source says how it is built. The nearest how-tos draw up extra cut length with rows of stitching on a swimsuit ([Jalie 3350](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3350.pdf)) or sew in clear elastic at 65% of the span on a shirt (one source: [5 out of 4](https://5outof4.com/rouching-side-seams-with-elastic/)). A pinch front, two small pleats at centre front that make a sweetheart dip, is better documented ([Jalie 2916](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_2916.pdf); [Quatro Starfall](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/starfall)); back twists have brand copy and no how-to ([Hazel](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/hazel-leotard-3q-sleeve)). In the app gathers, ruching and twist knots are a stamp, found under Lettering, that changes nothing in the cut. About 4 moodboard garments clearly need them. *Our inference:* stones on gathers cannot be placed by flat geometry, so this needs a warning.

**Straps as real pieces with ends, crossings and a link to their edge.** Straps carry the design on 12 of the 30 visible moodboard backs, and every strap end has to be attached somehow. At least eight joins are documented, from a strap seamed to the shoulder with the edge elastic folded over it to one threaded through a casing ([Jalie 3241](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3241.pdf); [Porter](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/); [Jalie 3672](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3672.pdf)), though none comes from a gymnastics factory. The join decides whether the strap goes on before or after the elastic and how much allowance it needs, and tension is often set at a fitting ([Jade](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/3910.pdf)). A binding can also run on and become the strap, one long piece that binds the opening and continues past it (one pattern maker: [Violeta](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3673.pdf); [Camille](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_4021.pdf)). *Our inference:* that gives an unbroken line from neckline to strap, and GK's strappy binding backs are probably built this way ([GK IP002](https://www.gkelite.com/products/ip002-sales-rep)). Whether straps cross or braid is left to the sewer ([Jalie 3354](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3354.pdf)), and nothing documents how crossings are fixed. In the app a strap renders well, but its path cannot be edited after drawing, the pattern does not list it, and a row of stones on it, the moodboard norm, needs a script.

**Thread and show-through.** Mesh is on about 83% of moodboard garments, and anything under it shows. Makers sew seams in the mesh colour and run a second line of stitching in the fabric colour where the first would show, and trim neckband edges so they cannot be seen through a sheer yoke (one maker each, the thread method from synchro and skating: [Jazz suits](https://spandexsimplified.com/jazz-team-synchro-suits/); [Jalie 2792](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_2792.pdf)). The app has no notion of seam thread, or of what lies under a sheer area.

**Closures and collars are the plain omissions outside these ideas.** A back-neck closure shows on about two-thirds of visible moodboard backs. A centre-back zip needs a seam to sit in and goes in before side seams and decoration, and Porter avoids invisible zips in performance wear even though brands and Jalie still use them ([Porter](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-sew-an-invisible-zipper/); [Kamira](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/products/kamira-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Jalie 3356](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3356.pdf)). The app has a script command for closures, with no button, no opening in the collar and nothing in the pattern. Collars and cuffs, on about 50 garments, exist as separate pieces but at a fixed height and with no back opening.

**Some techniques can wait.** Picot and lettuce edges, raw or laser-cut edges, pleats, skirts, sewn fringe, ruffles, peplums and godets get no mentions at all in the five brands' copy, moodboard entries have no skirts, and the moodboard's fringe is stones, which the app already generates. Underwire was not found on gymnastics leotards. *Our inference:* these matter only if rhythmic or dance users come into scope, because bars, beam and floor punish loose parts and sharp edges and team prices cannot absorb the hand work. No gymnastics maker was found explaining the absences.

**This research also corrects our own notes in five places.** Bound edges do not have zero allowance: Jalie's binding methods keep the fabric edge wrapped inside the binding ([Kate](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3901.pdf)), so the app's flat 6 mm is not the error we had listed for them, though it is wrong for sleeve hems of about 2 cm, clasp folds of about 1.5 cm and strap joins at 1 cm ([Jalie 2792](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_2792.pdf); [Jalie 3241](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3241.pdf)). Crossover backs are not unusual: Porter's remark that they are not typical for competition suits ([Porter](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)) sits against 65 of 411 Quatro and 25 of 153 GK listings that mention them. A lining is not simply a copy of the outer fabric, as above. The crotch is not always closed last: pattern sheets sew it before the shoulders and sides ([Kate](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_printing_guide_3901.pdf)), while factory reporting lists it among the last seams ([NAM](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)), which looks like a difference in practice. And stones on mesh are widespread at the brands ([GK 5857ST](https://www.gkelite.com/products/5857st); [Quatro Astralis](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/products/astralis)), though nothing confirms how single-layer sleeves are treated, so our inferred ban on unsupported mesh should become a warning.

## Today's friction lives in what the user must secretly know

**A mirrored plate takes about a dozen clicks plus one for each point on its line, and the count is not the problem.** What hurts is four things the user has to know while nothing shows them. A hidden anchor decides which side counts as fabric: each piece remembers one point, and only the area holding that point can be chosen, so the app can refuse a perfectly good side with the message "Choose a side that has fabric", including one of the two plates of a mirrored stroke. A mirror rule drops the second side: a line with a point within 6 mm of the centre counts as one part, so a plate line starting at a centre-front seam gives one plate with Mirror on. A new plate starts in its base's material, so pressing a fabric tile before New material changes the base as well. And the tool demands a side the moment the first line lands, then returns to Select, so a plate with two new edges has to be cut twice or traced by hand. That is the case you hit, and the latest commit fixed only the simpler one, a plate bounded by a single new line.

**The four line tools follow four sets of rules.** A seam or strap ends on any line it reaches; an overlay begun on a line ends there and asks for a side, while one begun in the open runs past lines. A seam honours a click on its first point and a strap silently ignores it. After finishing, a seam stays in its tool with the line selected, an overlay drops back to Select, and rows of crystals can only start from a line that already exists. Nothing shows where a click will snap until after it lands; the tutorial's remedy is to undo and click closer. Attached ends do not drag, and a strap's path cannot be reshaped at all, so it has to be deleted and redrawn.

**Three general commands are missing, and their absence multiplies cost everywhere.** There is no parallel copy of a line, no way to apply a finish to several lines at once, and no way to duplicate a piece. A mosaic of 14 appliqué strips costs about 84 steps, a band wrapping front to back means four separately drawn lines placed by eye, and nested chevrons cost one hand-drawn line per edge.

**How something is made is decided once, before drawing.** You pick the seam tool or the overlay tool, and no command converts one to the other, so changing your mind means redrawing. *Our inference:* since even an expert looking at a photo often cannot tell which build it is, the app asks for this decision at the moment the user knows least.

**Starting from a photo and working on a flat, the two habits designers bring, have no way in.** Reference photos exist as commands, with camera solving and colour matching, but no button starts them. The flat view shows pattern pieces and cannot be drawn on, so the front-and-back flat synced with the body in your frictionless-flow requirement does not exist yet. On a tablet the picture is harder still: a finger's pick commits at the moment of contact, with nothing shown first, because the previews are driven by hover.

## One drawing grammar: lines land, areas light up, a tap picks, the making stays open

Research on creativity tools reduces "effortless" to acting directly on the thing with immediate results, undo that can be trusted, being able to change any aspect of a design later, and a few well-chosen building blocks ([Resnick et al.](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)). The proposal below is built to meet those four tests.

**Treating enclosed areas as things you can simply pick, with no tracing, is proven in 2D illustration and untested on a 3D body.** Fashion CAD asks for the labour you objected to: in CLO's Trace you select the outlines and internal lines yourself and then convert (search excerpt: [CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012381528-Trace)), and Flatten, the closest thing to your idea, needs you to hover until a closed shape highlights and press Enter (search excerpts: [Marvelous Designer](https://support.marvelousdesigner.com/hc/en-us/articles/47358340247193-Flatten); [CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115012223847-Flatten)). The 2026 sketch features let you draw freehand on a locked front or back view before converting ([CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/55461410696345); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/04/15/marvelous-designer-2026-0-adds-3d-pencil-and-lacing/)), which we read as a new layer in front of that older step.

Illustration software solved the same problem differently. Illustrator's Live Paint keeps lines whole and derives areas from them, and with experienced users it gave the expected result about 98% of the time by its authors' own count, a figure they qualify because those users knew which cases to avoid; inexperienced users made more errors but undid no more than with traditional tools ([Asente et al.](https://perso.lisn.upsaclay.fr/mbl/FundHCI/papers/Asente-SIGGRAPH07.pdf)). Figma's author reports that cutting holes through negative space confused people until a toggle on the enclosed area replaced it (one source, vendor author: [Wallace](https://madebyevan.com/figma/introducing-vector-networks/)). Affinity on iPad fills an enclosed area with a tap and no prior selection ([Affinity](https://affinity.help/designer2ipad/en-US.lproj/pages/ObjectControl/floodingAreas.html)). SketchUp keeps its line tool on, splits a face when a line crosses it and heals it when the line is erased ([SketchUp](https://help.sketchup.com/en/sketchup/dividing-splitting-and-exploding-lines-and-faces); [SketchUp](https://help.sketchup.com/en/sketchup/introducing-drawing-basics-and-concepts)), though its reputation for ease is anecdotal: no controlled study could be read. The one garment precedent is DressUp, a research system using tracked tools on a physical mannequin, where an open cut line extends to the nearest cloth border and the user decides whether it removes fabric or inserts a seam; five people tried it, so it is suggestive at most ([Wibowo et al.](https://www.jst.go.jp/erato/igarashi/projects/dressup/dressup_tei2012.pdf)). Consumer garment tools make construction easy by removing it, with catalogue parts and templates ([Tailornova](https://tailornova.com/)). *Our inference:* your silhouette catalogue should stay the fast path and drawing the deeper one.

**The strongest objection is that professionals do not design in 3D.** Small qualitative studies keep 2D drawing at the creative centre, treat 3D as a specialist role and find pattern-first designers misaligned with 3D tools ([Martin and Seo 2026](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/26648/); search excerpt: [Wallace](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1336934/a-study-of-3d-cad-and-implications-for-training-and-skills-in-the-fashion-industry)). But those studies concern pattern-and-simulation software, which takes four to eight weeks to reach productive use by one estimate and 6 to 12 months to reach proficiency by another (one source each: [KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/clo-3d/); [Martin and Seo 2025](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/24804/)). A designer who co-authored a sketch-on-mannequin system says it removed the need for pattern-making knowledge, as testimony and not a study ([Turquin et al.](https://cs.brown.edu/~jhughes/papers/Turquin-ASI-2007/paper.pdf)). *Our inference:* a skin-tight garment drawn directly on a body avoids most of what those studies complain about, and the trade's most concrete failure is the surprise of seeing a flat design on a body. So keep the body as the canvas, add a flat beside it, and test with real designers early, because the case remains unproven.

**The proposal, tested against your plate.** The whole table is *our design, to be prototyped*. No source has tried it on a 3D body.

| Step | Today | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Shape tab or the O key, which fixes what the line will mean | Draw, from a control on the stage, or simply Pencil contact |
| First new edge | Lands, and the tool immediately demands a side | Lands, snaps visibly to the seam, and the enclosed areas re-tint. The tool stays on |
| Second new edge | Not possible without a second cut or a traced outline | Lands, and the areas re-tint. No seam is traced and nothing is cut |
| Mirror | A line touching the centre drops the second side, and one side can be refused | Both sides appear; a half-line meets its own image at the centre |
| Pick | A click commits at press; on touch, with no preview | A tap lights the area during contact and commits on lift. Tap another to add it, tap again to remove it; neighbours merge into one piece |
| How it is made | Fixed by the tool chosen | Offered after the pick: plate, separate piece, cut out, window, under the mesh. Changeable later; one undo returns to the lines |
| Material | A new plate shares its base's material | A new plate gets its own, so a fabric tile cannot change the base |

That brings your plate to **two lines and one tap with Mirror on**, and the number of body pieces does not change. There are two forms of different risk. The smaller keeps two starting commands, seam (cuts as it lands) and plate (cuts nothing). The larger has a single Draw, with unused lines becoming seams when the tool ends. Tesler's argument favours the larger, since when you pick the thing first and the action second, a wrong action is cheap to change ([Tesler](https://trisquel.info/files/larry_tesler_modeless_copy_paste.pdf)), while SketchUp's appeal favours an immediate visible effect. We recommend building the smaller first, because it answers your complaint directly, and testing the larger on you. Either way, how a line is made should be stored on the line from the start, because that is what lets the user change their mind.

**A "how is this made?" control turns the catalogue of builds into something usable.** *Our inference:* selecting any line or area should show the builds physically possible there as cross-section pictures, as your finish tiles already do, with a suggested default from the table earlier and a plain statement of what changes in the export. It would also teach. Brink says touring GK's factory shaped how she will design ([College Gym News 2020](https://collegegymnews.com/2020/01/15/nebraska-leos-a-labor-of-love-for-head-coach-heather-brink/)), and you found overlays by trying to copy a garment.

**One behaviour for every line.** *Our inference:* all drawing tools should share one way of starting, ending, closing, snapping, previewing, mirroring and editing, with the tool deciding only what the finished line becomes, which removes most of the inconsistencies above. Three additions matter most. A half-line that snaps to the centre and meets its own mirror image serves the commonest edges on the moodboard; Blender's visible merge distance and hard stop at the plane are the models to compare ([Blender](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/mirror.html)), and a line ending just outside the 6 mm rule needs testing for thin slivers. A "continue from here" across the side seam serves the waist bands, and the app can do automatically what Porter says makers must do by hand, matching design lines where the seam allowances meet instead of at the cut edge (one maker: [Ursula](https://spandexsimplified.com/the-ursula-dress/); [Colour blocking](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)). Parallel copy, apply-to-several and duplicate give the most relief per change of anything in this report.

**iPad is a first-class target by your own decision, so each job that hovering a mouse does today needs a replacement.** A touch surface senses only contact or none ([Buxton](https://billbuxton.com/3state.html)), hover hardware is limited, and Apple says hover should preview and never act ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pencil-and-scribble)). *Our inference from those sources:* show what can be picked with faint, always-on tints while the tool is active, which also signals that a line has closed, as Shapr3D's shaded fill does (forum: [Shapr3D](https://discourse.shapr3d.com/t/convert-a-sketches-lines-of-this-type-to-be-able-to-extrude-it/26977)). Act on lift instead of press, so touch-down shows the point or area, a slide adjusts it and lifting commits, close to the touch-drag-lift mode SketchUp recommends for beginners ([SketchUp](https://help.sketchup.com/en/sketchup-ipad/getting-started)). Let the Pencil draw while a finger turns the view and picks, as Nomad allows ([Nomad](https://nomadsculpt.com/manual/interface)). Keep a generous tap tolerance: today 5 pixels of movement counts as turning the view, and Shapr3D users report selection failing from small Pencil movements (forum: [Shapr3D](https://discourse.shapr3d.com/t/apple-pencil-selection-on-ipad/29270)). Let a fabric swatch be dragged onto an area, which gives continuous contact and a highlight with no hover (precedents: [Procreate](https://help.procreate.com/procreate/handbook/colors/colors-interface); [Sims 3](https://modthesims.info/article.php?p=3932645)). Your removal of the tool rail can stand: Tesler accepts a brush-like mode when it is shown where the user is looking ([Tesler](https://trisquel.info/files/larry_tesler_modeless_copy_paste.pdf)), and Nielsen Norman Group advises two indicators, here the bar on the stage with a prompt that changes as you work and the tints on the body ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modes/)). But the iPad tools whose tool models could be read keep a visible home for creation, and on a touch screen with no keys the Shape tab is the only route, so a Draw control on the stage is worth testing.

**Most of the failure modes are already known.** SketchUp's worst reports are a tiny gap that gives no face and no explanation, with one user losing an hour on a simple shape ([SketchUp forum](https://forums.sketchup.com/t/edge-not-separating-faces/138704); [Fine Woodworking](https://finewoodworking.com/2010/03/14/why-cant-i-get-a-face)), so snap generously, extend an open end to the nearest line as DressUp does, and say in the bar when a line has not closed. Thin slivers are a recognised problem for any such system ([Asente et al.](https://perso.lisn.upsaclay.fr/mbl/FundHCI/papers/Asente-SIGGRAPH07.pdf)), so absorb them instead of offering them as picks. Later edits have no perfect rule: Live Paint's authors treat this as a problem with no always-right answer and aim for results people can understand, matching each area by stable line identity and by which side of the line it lies on. Onshape's silent best-match behaviour confused its users, one of whom put the lesson this way: "Complex is fine. Magic is problematic." (traveler_hauptman, [Onshape forum](https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/1116/anyone-else-having-unexpected-things-happen-to-solids-when-adding-entities-to-a-previous-sketch), 2015). So a plate should follow its seams when they move, and the app should say what happened when one is deleted. Today the plate is deleted with the seam, reported only when a silhouette change causes it. *Our inference:* keep the plate's shape, turn that stretch into its own edge and show a notice, since a designer rarely means to lose a piece; this one is your call.

**Two risks stay open.** Lines here are drawn by placing points, so a tap means both "add a point" and "pick this area". The workable rule is that a tap adds a point while a line is in progress, a tap on or near a line starts one, and a tap inside an area picks it, which leaves lines that begin in the open needing another start. This is the largest interaction risk in the proposal and should be prototyped first. And Live Paint's largest failure class was people expecting stacking from art that merely looks stacked ([Asente et al.](https://perso.lisn.upsaclay.fr/mbl/FundHCI/papers/Asente-SIGGRAPH07.pdf)), which suggests some users will want to outline a plate, so the free outline must stay obvious.

**Drawing alone does not create the conditions for arbitrary designs; the openings, the second view and the freedom to change anything late matter as much.** *Our inference throughout this paragraph, drawn from the practice described earlier.* Open on a base style that recolours in one step or on a pinned reference photo, the real starting points; the photo machinery already exists and needs only an interface. Keep front and back flats synced with the mannequin, for the habit designers bring and for seeing what crosses a closing seam, and export them as vector files that open in Illustrator, because that is where the next person works. Make the back a part that swaps without touching the front art or stones. Allow several versions side by side and a set view, because colleges buy three or four differing leotards (sponsored: [Inside Gymnastics](https://www.insidegymnastics.com/news-features/interview-with-ozones-vice-president-lea-brown/)) and makers sell heavy and light versions. Keep stone placement by eye as a first-class route beside generators, because at least one maker places stones with no marks at all ([PBS](https://video.wpsu.org/video/beaded-dance-dresses-mOFOmK/)). Add a view from a distance, because fine detail vanishes there ([Porter](https://spandexsimplified.com/spandex-costume-design-principles/)). Run a live count of stones and stitched edges and not a price, because no per-operation cost data was found at any maker. Use the trade's words (body, sleeves, V, straps, back; proof for the image, sample for the object), keeping in mind that some of these come from press and not from makers. Keep construction warnings gentle, never blocking.

## What to build first, and the three checks that would replace guesswork

The order below is **our inference**, weighing moodboard reach against how often each problem recurs. Steps one and three should be designed together, because the way a line is made has to be a property of the line from the start.

| Order | Build | Why here | Moodboard reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One line behaviour: tool stays on, areas tint, half-lines to the centre, ends that drag, acting on lift | Your direct complaint, and it recurs in almost every drawn line | about 50 centre edges; about 23 plate designs |
| 2 | Parallel copy, apply to several lines, duplicate, continue across the side seam | Most relief per change | Parallel lines about 30 to 45; finishes about 35; waist seams about 36 |
| 3 | "How is this made?": strips and print lines that do not cut, backed windows, plates under mesh, enclosed seams, an "under" setting, all changeable without redrawing | The core truth gap | Shaped mesh up to about 60; strips about 18; fake seams up to about 15 |
| 4 | Buttons for what only scripts can reach: lining, closures, stones on a strap, reference photos | Half built already, and the photo is the real starting point | Closures at least 17; strap rows about 12; lining every garment once exported |
| 5 | Pattern truth: straps and strips as pieces, finishes located on their lines, real hem and clasp allowances, notches and folds, lining pieces, no false slits, what joins what and in what order | What a factory actually reads | about 106 |
| 6 | Free-edged layers, crossing order, gathers with real fullness, thread and show-through | Rarer and harder | Overlap about 10; gathers about 4 |

**Three inexpensive checks would replace most of the remaining guesswork.** First, talk to one working designer. **The most important unknown for the product is how a designer decides whether a line becomes a seam, a print, a plate or a crystal line**, and whether the pattern maker can change that call; no source addresses it, and the decision guide above is only a stand-in. Second, turn two or three real competition leotards inside out, one each with a plate on mesh, a plate under mesh and a strap back. No brand says how it builds invisible binding, set-in neckbands, back twists, accent lines or under-mesh plates, and inspection should settle most of that quickly. Third, give three to five designers the plate task with only the bar's prompt to guide them, the format DressUp used. No source tests picking areas on a body with non-engineers, and it is unknown whether designers read a plate as an area or instinctively want to outline it.

**The rest of what this research could not establish.** Nearly everything public is celebratory press, sponsored features or job ads: no source shows a real leotard design sheet, and no working designer criticises the process on the record. The evidence skews to GK, which supplied over three-quarters of college programmes in 2020 ([College Gym News 2020](https://collegegymnews.com/2020/01/15/nebraska-leos-a-labor-of-love-for-head-coach-heather-brink/)). Several sources could be read only as search excerpts, including the CLO and Marvelous Designer manuals, ChalkBucket and some Sylvia P pages, and Reddit could not be searched. No figures were found for how much a seam, plate, gather or strap reduces stretch, or for how often each build is used. The moodboard's back counts are floors, since 76 of about 106 garments show no back and backs get photographed when they are interesting, it does not separate garments served by a silhouette option from those needing a drawn line, and it may not be limited to artistic competition wear. Finally, four suspected problems in the app were read from code and need one run in a tab nobody designs in: crossing free-drawn plates slicing each other, deleting one side of a mirrored free-drawn plate possibly covering the body, a line ended on a non-cutting line leaving a slit, and the false slits in pieces with holes.

## Conclusion

Your discovery was really a discovery about the app's central idea. It treats a leotard as a surface divided into areas, and that idea is excellent for seams and is the source of most of these gaps, because a real leotard is a stack of layers with a chosen join on every line. Truth and ease look opposed, since more construction seems to mean more to learn, and separating the line from its making resolves the conflict: the user draws what they see, the app suggests a build, the model records it, and nobody has to know pattern-making to begin or be trapped by a choice they made too early. Arbitrary design then comes from combining a few general ideas, not from a longer list of tools. That combination, drawing on a body where existing seams count automatically and real pieces come back, was not found in any tool examined, and it is what makers improvise today with test garments, tape and paper.

One implication goes beyond the tools. Your acceptance test judges form and appearance, and the app can pass it while still recording the wrong garment, so the construction rule needs a test of its own. One already exists in public: Jalie's leotard patterns publish their complete cut lists, so rebuilding Kate, Jade and Lillie in the app and comparing its export against those lists, piece by piece, would measure truth as directly as the moodboard measures looks. It would also keep the honest limit of this research in view: those lists show how a careful pattern maker builds a leotard, and what a factory does differently remains something only a factory can say.
