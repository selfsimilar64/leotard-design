# Borrow the engine, build the sparkle

The software should be built on an existing open-source 3D engine with a hand-written layer for the three things no engine supplies: truthful sparkle from thousands of editable crystals, the special leotard fabrics, and tools for drawing lines and brushing stones directly onto a body. If its users include coaches and clubs on ordinary laptops, as this report assumes, that engine should be three.js running in the browser. Physically based rendering (PBR) is the right foundation and is what "accurate lighting" takes, but the plain version every engine ships was designed for hard surfaces, so each fabric family needs one or two extra, well-understood shading layers, and glitter, sequins and crystals each need a treatment of their own. The domain itself proved small and well catalogued: every fabric the researchers found in the 2026 GK Elite, Quatro, Sylvia P and Snowflake ranges is one of about eight base cloths carrying one of about seven surface finishes, the stones are heat-pressed flat-back crystals in a handful of standard sizes, and a design is a short list of named choices (sleeve, neckline, back, straps, leg cut, panels) bounded by three rulebooks that software can check as the user works. Nothing found does what is proposed: the brand design studios that could be examined recolour fixed templates and sell crystals by coverage tier, the professional fashion programs examined document no dedicated stone tool, and the rhinestone layout programs examined are flat and 2D. The game Dressmaker is a sound model for assembling a garment from catalogue pieces with a fabric per panel, and an equally useful warning, because its decoration has no full undo and players automated its manual steps within three weeks of release. Three cautions run through everything below: the research was cut short when the shared web-search allowance ran out, no measured optical data exists for any leotard fabric or stone, and nobody has measured these rendering techniques on the ordinary laptops and tablets a coach would use. The first thing to build is therefore one stress-test scene, judged against physical fabric swatches, before any commitment to the wider product.

## A competition leotard reduces to a small, well-catalogued kit of parts

The reassuring finding from the domain research is that modern leotard design is finite. Makers publish their fabric lines, colour lists, stone menus and option names, and the same few dozen terms recur from brand to brand. For the software, most "leotard knowledge" is therefore data to load into libraries and rule checks, not behaviour to invent.

### Eight base cloths and seven surface finishes cover every catalogued fabric

**The material library should work as "choose the cloth, then choose the finish".** Fifteen-plus fabric families appear in live 2026 catalogues, but almost all share one construction: a warp-knit tricot (a smooth, fine knit that stretches in both directions) of roughly 78–87% nylon or polyester with 13–25% elastane, weighing 180–250 g/m². What separates them is the surface: dyed matte or shiny, printed with dots of metallic or holographic foil, covered in continuous foil, glittered, coated, or coloured by sublimation printing. Velvets, light meshes of 65–115 g/m², lace, rib knits and thin linings complete the set. Every fabric the researchers catalogued fits a scheme of about eight base constructions and about seven finishes, with base colour and foil colour as independent settings.

The catalogues you expected do exist and are detailed. **GK Elite's 2026–27 catalogue runs to 144 pages**, with fabric pages at 126–129 and a sublimation guide after them, backed by an online swatch store ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-view-catalog-online)). Quatro's 2026/27 catalogue is 124 pages ([Quatro](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/brochures)), Sylvia P publishes its 2026–27 fabric and colour range online ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options)), and Snowflake sells 98 swatches at 25 cents each ([Snowflake Designs](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/fabric-swatches.html)). They give named fabric lines, full colour lists (GK prints 39 mystique colours and a 72-colour sublimation palette; Quatro lists 46 mystique colours), fibre compositions and explicit decoration restrictions ([GK catalogue p.127](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/127?viewerID=448752a2); [GK catalogue p.129](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/129?viewerID=448752a2); [Quatro catalogue p.118](https://d30tgldqweej78.cloudfront.net/194707/img-8932809-de531f8d6c2fb198.jpg)). They do not give weight, thickness or measured stretch. Those appear only on mill and wholesaler sheets, and the Italian mill Carvico is the one source found that publishes full technical data: its matte Vita tricot is 78% recycled nylon and 22% elastane at 190 g/m² and stretches 85% across the width but 145% along the length under the same test load ([Carvico](https://cdn.adoratorio.app/carvico/390-Vita_0126.pdf)). "Four-way stretch" on a catalogue page does not mean equal stretch both ways.

Make-up and weight figures come from mill and wholesaler sheets and maker catalogues ([Carvico Vita](https://cdn.adoratorio.app/carvico/390-Vita_0126.pdf); [Carvico Panama velvet](https://cdn.adoratorio.app/carvico/361-Panama_0126.pdf); [Carvico Alicante mesh](https://cdn.adoratorio.app/carvico/210-Alicante_0126.pdf); [Spandex World mystique](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/product/1916); [Pine Crest Jewels Foil](https://pinecrestfabrics.com/products/jewels-foil-collection/); [GK swatch store](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gk-swatches/products.json?limit=250)).

**Mystique is the fabric to get right first.** It is a dyed tricot carrying micro-dots of bonded metallic foil, which is what produces the "wet look" sparkle ([The Lineup](https://thelineup.com/pages/fabrics/novelty)), and a GK executive was already calling it the industry's standard fabric in 2012 ([BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/amyodell/everything-you-ever-wanted-to-know-about-team-usa)). The dot pattern and the foil are separate choices at the converter: one UK supplier offers 37 glue patterns with 5 hologram and 11 metallic foils ([Funki Fabrics](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/foilme-guide)). Two-word colour names such as Navy/Royal encode a base colour and a foil colour. Hologram fabrics swap in a foil that throws rainbow colour. The foil is also the weak point: makers warn that rubbing, heat, fabric softener, deodorant and sweat dull or lift it ([Snowflake Designs](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/fabric-swatches/mystique-fabric-swatches.html); [GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions)), and GK states that foil and hologram fabrics dull slightly wherever stones are heat-pressed onto them ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/products/qxli)). Taste is moving too. GK's design director Jeanne Diaz reports that athletes now prefer matte fabrics under crystals and that shiny fabrics are becoming less of a default ([Vogue Scandinavia](https://www.voguescandinavia.com/articles/team-usa-olympic-uniform-is-made-by-swarovski)), and the fabrics GK marks as new for 2026–27 are a high-shine cire and a stretch satin, not new foils ([GK catalogue p.126](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/126?viewerID=448752a2)).

**Sublimation printing splits every maker's range in two.** The artwork is printed on paper and heat-transferred into the cloth, which works only on white, polyester-based fabric ([Funki Fabrics](https://www.funkifabrics.com/page/print-base-guide)). That is why printed leotards use different base cloths from the nylon mystique range, and why printable foil fabrics are polyester versions of the nylon originals. The documented limits are worth encoding as rules. On a coloured base only a short list of darker inks is allowed: GK's grey base accepts black alone, and its red base accepts black and one red ([GK catalogue p.130](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/130?viewerID=448752a2)). GK does not guarantee a colour match on reorders ([GK catalogue p.129](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/129?viewerID=448752a2)), Quatro quotes a colour and placement tolerance of up to 10% ([Quatro catalogue p.117](https://d30tgldqweej78.cloudfront.net/194707/img-8932809-8d27a18d4e7b97d4.jpg)), and printed polyester pills where it rubs on mats and Velcro ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/fabric-options)). An ombré mystique leotard is a white polyester foil cloth with a gradient printed into it, so in the software the print and the foil are independent layers.

**A competition leotard typically combines two to four fabrics, and the makers' rules for combining them are explicit enough to automate.** GK codes each special-order style as a fabric set, for instance a red mystique body with red mesh and a compulsory nude lining under the mesh bodice ([GK Elite](https://gkelite.com/products/7659)). It recommends mesh for accents only and requires a signed waiver for it ([GK care instructions](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-care-instructions)). Sylvia P lines the full front of every competitive leotard ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/design-with-sylvia-p-teamwear)). Nude lining and mesh come in three to five fixed skin tones per maker ([Quatro](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/tw-fabrics); [The Lineup](https://thelineup.com/pages/fabrics/mesh)). GK will not apply its heat-set Spanglez to velvet, mesh, mesh foil, novelty mesh or rib ([GK catalogue p.117](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/117?viewerID=448752a2)), offers binding and piping only in certain fabrics, and marks dark mystique colours such as navy, berry and merlot as likely to bleed into white ([GK catalogue p.127](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/127?viewerID=448752a2)). It also warns that heavy embellishment and appliqué reduce stretch enough that a sizing sample will not fit like the finished garment ([GK catalogue p.137](https://secure.viewer.zmags.com/services/resource/pub/167be069/pg2358x2896/3/137?viewerID=448752a2)). The software should treat each panel as a stack (outer fabric plus optional lining) and raise these warnings as the user designs.

Fabric is a minor part of the price. US retail cut prices run about $6–10 a yard for lining, mesh and plain tricot and $15–21 for mystique ([Spandex World](https://www.spandexworld.com/c3/catalog/browse/180)), and a home-sewing gymnastics pattern needs about 1.3–1.6 m of cloth for an adult sleeved leotard ([Jalie](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698)). Crystals, labour and design set the price, which is how makers quote. One gap bears directly on the software: nobody publishes thickness, gloss, sparkle, foil-dot size or mesh openness for any of these fabrics, so realistic materials have to be calibrated against physical swatches.

### Sparkle today means heat-pressed flat-back crystals, mostly 2 to 6.4 mm across

**The software's main placeable object should be the crystal, with the sequin as a second type.** In the trade's vocabulary a crystal (also rhinestone, jewel or diamante) is a small faceted glass stone with a mirrored flat back and a layer of heat-activated glue. The flat disc most people picture as a sequin appears on leotards mainly in a hole-less, heat-applied form the decoration trade calls a spangle, used for team names and logos. GK's Spanglez and Sequinz appear to be its names for this, though GK does not define either term on the pages read, and Sylvia P lists 2–5 mm sequins in iridescent, holographic, glitter and mirror finishes ([ColDesi](https://shop.coldesi.com/blogs/news/what-are-spangle-transfers); [GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gk-stock-collection); [Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/embellishment-options-crystals)). The researchers found no competition-leotard source using sewn sequins, sew-on stones or crystal chain; those belong to dance and skating. Metal nailheads, pearls, laser-cut appliqué and embroidered logos round out the list. Pearls appeared on US Olympic leotards for the first time in 2024, almost 1,000 of them hand-placed on one design ([USA Gymnastics](https://usagym.org/a-fusion-of-fashion-performance-and-patriotism-gk-elite-and-usa-gymnastics-unveil-leotards-and-apparel-for-summer-olympics/)).

At GK Elite the process is industrial. A computer-aided design program fixes the position of every stone, a machine sets the stones onto heat-transfer film, a heat press bonds the whole motif to the fabric, and larger stones and pearls go on by hand with tweezers ([Foley & Lardner](https://foley.com/?p=108824); [NAM](https://nam.org/?p=31672)). A production setting machine of the documented type holds six hoppers, so six size-and-colour combinations per pass; it sets about 170 stones a minute, handles stones of 2–5 mm and covers a 400 × 400 mm field ([ColDesi](https://shop.coldesi.com/products/cams-1v-6p-cams-1v-6p)). Preciosa's application manual gives the recipe for this fabric class as about 160–170 °C for roughly 18–25 seconds with the stretch fabric held slightly stretched, five minutes to cool and 24 hours to cure, and lists stones failing to hold on seams and layered areas as a standard fault ([Preciosa](https://www.preciosa-eshop.com/user/related_files/preciosa-hotfix-application-manual-complete-en-2.pdf)). Those facts become placement rules: keep stones off seams and elastic edges, treat anything above about 5 mm as a hand-set accent, and split big layouts into machine-sized tiles.

Three grades of stone are on sale. Swarovski is the prestige name. It withdrew from the costume and craft components trade in 2021 ([Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/swarovski-corporate-restructuring-and-what-it-means-for-you/)), though GK still used it for the 2024 Olympic leotards, and one US retailer reports that its purchase restrictions were lifted on 1 June 2026; that is a single retailer's account, with no Swarovski statement found ([Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/swarovski-crystals-available-again-2026)). Preciosa's MAXIMA, a Czech lead-free stone with 15 facets in the common sizes, is the premium stone retailers have promoted since ([Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/what-is-the-difference-between-preciosa-viva12-and-maxima-rhinestones)). Below these sit 12-facet stones and cheaper Korean and Chinese glass ([EduTech Wiki](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Hotfix_stone)). Brand menus mirror the tiers. Quatro offers Swarovski, DMC and standard stones in round, boat and waterdrop shapes, sizes SS6 to SS20 and 21 colours ([Quatro](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/tw-stones)). Sylvia P offers standard or Swarovski stones in SS6, SS10, SS16, SS20 and SS34, clear or AB, plus navette, square, kite and pear shapes ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/embellishment-options-crystals)). GK groups its Swarovski range into seven shapes, 18 premium colours and six vibrant colours, with cheaper standard crystals alongside ([GK Elite](https://blog.gkelite.com/leotard-fabrics-embellishments/)).

Stone size is a shared standard with small differences between brands ([Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/rhinestone-size-chart-ss-mm-inches/)). The working range on leotards is ss6 to ss30: a UK crystal retailer recommends ss16, ss20 and ss30 for leotards with smaller stones as fillers ([Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-embellish-stretch-fabric-with-rhinestones)), and the setting machine above ships with hoppers for ss6, ss10, ss16 and ss20.

A stone's colour has three independent parts, and the renderer must treat them separately: the glass itself (clear, tinted or milky), the backing (a mirror foil, or an opaque lacquer), and an optional coating on the faceted top. The best-known coating is Aurora Borealis, or AB, a very thin vapour-deposited metal film introduced by Swarovski in 1956 that gives the stone a shifting rainbow tint ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinestone)). A UK retailer calls AB the most popular choice for sports and dance costumes ([Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-use-hotfix-rhinestones-for-gymnastics-leotards)). Swarovski's 2018 chart for its main apparel stone ran to about 127 colours and effects by the researcher's count ([Swarovski](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0086/3023/9295/files/Swarovski_Non_Hotfix_Collection.pdf?2186)); a practical library needs far fewer.

**Crystal count is the clearest measure of a leotard's tier and its price.** US Olympic leotards went from 184 crystals in 2008 to 1,188 in 2012 and 3,500–5,000 in 2016 ([Atlanta Journal-Constitution](https://www.ajc.com/news/the-news/p6zoageggen8SakMQLoxAJ/)), then to between 6,359 and more than 10,000 each at Paris 2024, where the eight designs carried over 47,000 between them and weighed about a pound apiece ([USA Gymnastics](https://usagym.org/a-fusion-of-fashion-performance-and-patriotism-gk-elite-and-usa-gymnastics-unveil-leotards-and-apparel-for-summer-olympics/); [People](https://sg.news.yahoo.com/team-usa-gymnastics-goes-big-154156319.html)). Club leotards are sold in tiers. One Sylvia P design costs AU$224.80 with about 20 crystals, AU$322.60 with over 640, AU$424.80 with over 1,156 and AU$492.50 with over 1,775, with a Swarovski upcharge on the larger tiers ([Sylvia P](https://www.sylviap.com.au/products/nia-dennis-leotard-3q-sleeve)). Loose premium stones retail at roughly 6 US cents each at ss16 ([Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/swarovski-2078-flatback-rhinestones-crystal-16ss/)). A live stone count by size and colour, with a price band, is one of the most useful read-outs the software can show.

Designers work from a small set of moves, and these map directly onto the brushes you described. They outline a seam, neckline, cut-out or logo; fill a shape or lettering; scatter stones with a density that fades away from an edge, which the sources describe as a cascade, rainfall or meteor shower; build dense clusters; radiate bursts from a centre; repeat stripes and chevrons; draw swirls; alternate two colours or sizes along a line; and place single large stones as accents ([College Gym News](https://collegegymnews.com/2026/02/18/leotard-rankings-week-7-7/); [Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/products/nia-dennis-leotard-3q-sleeve); [Crystal Parade](https://crystalparade.co.uk/blogs/news/how-to-embellish-stretch-fabric-with-rhinestones)). Published rules of thumb are few: mix two or three sizes for depth ([Rhinestones Unlimited](https://rhinestonesu.com/blog/rhinestones-101-what-size-rhinestone-do-i-need/)) and leave about a millimetre between stones when cutting templates ([EduTech Wiki](https://edutechwiki.unige.ch/en/Stitch_Era_-_creating_hot_fix_designs)). The layout software that ships with the setting machines, Sierra's Hotfix Era, offers path fills, several area-fill patterns, mixed-size fills, overlap warnings and single-stone editing, but it is flat 2D software built for T-shirt transfers ([Sierra Software](https://www.sierra-software.com/Sites/Products/HotfixEra/HotfixEra.aspx)). That feature list is the baseline to match. Working on a stretched 3D body is what would be new.

### A design is a short list of named choices, bounded by three rulebooks

**Brands describe every leotard with the same handful of choices, so the software can offer them as swappable pieces and check them against the rules as the user works.** The table gathers the vocabulary found in 2026 catalogues.

| Choice | Options the brands name | Notes for the software |
|---|---|---|
| Garment | Sleeveless tank; long, three-quarter, short or cap sleeve; biketard (short legs); unitard (ankle length); shorts or leggings over or under | One catalogue design is routinely sold in tank, three-quarter and full-sleeve versions |
| Sleeve | Body fabric, unlined mesh or printed mesh; a matched pair, one sleeve in a different fabric, or a single sleeve; raglan or set-in; plain, lettered or striped cuff | Mesh sleeves are now standard at elite level |
| Neckline | Scoop (the default), round, V, soft V, square, boatneck-scoop, high or mock neck with a neckband or collar, halter-style high neck, one-shoulder | A high neck forces a back opening and a closure |
| Illusion layer | Mesh rising from an opaque sweetheart or V line to a higher mesh neckline, lined in a nude tone | The opaque line and the mesh line are separate |
| Back | Closed; scoop (classic, deep, low); V; keyhole (small to large, circular); high back with hook clasp; strappy (parallel, criss-cross, ladder, laced, twisted); open; racerback; mesh-filled or double-layer; asymmetric | The most varied choice, and the one brands name most carefully |
| Closure | Pull-on; hook clasp at the back neck; invisible centre-back zip | Follows from neckline and back |
| Straps | Count, width, path, what lies beneath (skin, nude mesh or a printed imitation), material (fabric binding, rouleau tube, elastic, mystique) | Printed "faux" straps give the look without the openings |
| Leg cut | GK: traditional or modified; Sylvia P: standard or mid | No brand defines these by measurement |
| Fabric zones | Upper and lower body, sleeves, inserts or body panels, belt, binding and straps, underlays and overlays | Simple designs use three zones, elaborate ones six or seven |

The terms come from current product listings and catalogues ([GK special-order collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [GK tank collection](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/shop-all-tank-leotards); [Sylvia P 2026–27 catalogue](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog); [Quatro](https://us.quatrogymnastics.com/collections/all-leotards)).

How common each option is suggests sensible defaults. On Sylvia P's US storefront the researchers tallied 63% of 1,170 women's and girls' listings as three-quarter sleeve, 17% as tanks and 11% as long sleeve ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/all)). These are keyword counts of listings, not sales figures, but they agree with every brand's menu in showing the three-quarter sleeve as the US club default and long sleeves as the elite and college convention. Among the 38 GK special-order competition styles with a described back, keyhole or hook-clasp backs lead with 16 to 18, strappy and criss-cross backs follow with 13, and plain V and scoop backs have three each ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order)). Training tanks default to a scoop back. Mesh goes on sleeves, the upper bodice, waist and side cut-outs and the back, and wherever it sits on the torso it is lined in a skin tone ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/2026-27-womens-competition-catalog)).

What designers say makes a leotard work is consistent enough to build in as guidance. Jeanne Diaz, who leads design of Team USA's leotards at GK, puts function ahead of looks: "First and foremost, it is a performance garment" ([Vogue Scandinavia](https://www.voguescandinavia.com/articles/team-usa-olympic-uniform-is-made-by-swarovski)). Her team avoids a straight stripe along the inner sleeve because on bars it would advertise any bend in the arm, uses lines that lead the eye up toward the face, contrasts matte against shine, keeps velvet to small areas so athletes do not overheat, and wants the team recognisable from colour and design alone ([Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/31/jeanne-diaz-10-designs-leotards-for-womens-olympic-gymnastics-team/); [NBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/paris-2024-summer-olympics/olympic-gymnastics-a-sneak-peek-at-the-leotards-simone-biles-and-team-usa-will-wear-in-paris/3562611/)). Snowflake warns that strap-heavy backs can catch a coach's hand while spotting ([Snowflake Designs](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/gymnastics-leotards.html)). College reviewers dislike a straight horizontal waistline and mixed strap widths, and praise varied stone sizes ([College Gym News](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=155167)). The current decade's markers are nude mesh, printed ombré, velvet's return, matte fabrics, ever more crystals and, in college gymnastics, branding on cuffs, collars and backs ([NCAA.com](https://www.ncaa.com/news/gymnastics-women/article/2024-01-21/ncaa-gymnastics-leotard-trends-through-decades); [College Gym News](https://collegegymnews.com/2026/05/06/4-leotard-trends-that-shaped-2026-and-whats-coming-in-2027/)).

| Rulebook | What it allows and forbids | Penalty |

The rules are taken from the governing bodies' own documents ([USA Gymnastics elite rules, for the FIG neckline rule](https://static.usagym.org/PDFs/Women/Rules/rp/rp_elite.pdf); [FIG clothing and advertising rules](https://www.gymnastics.sport/publicdir/rules/files/en_Advertising%20Rules%20Competition%20Clothing%20-%20valid%20from%20May%202024.pdf); [USA Gymnastics 2026–27 Rules and Policies](https://static.usagym.org/PDFs/Women/Rules/rp/rulespolicies.pdf); [NCAA 2026–27 rules modifications](https://nawgj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026-27_WomensGym-Rules-Mod-FINAL.pdf)).

Two consequences follow. The rulebooks conflict: FIG forbids an athlete's name and slogans while the NCAA explicitly allows names and numbers, so the software needs a ruleset setting for each design. And most limits are geometric and tied to anatomy (sternum, shoulder blade, hip bone), so they can be shown as live pass-or-fail indicators on the 3D body and checked on every size. GK already labels its special-order backs as compliant with FIG and USA Gymnastics rules, which shows makers design to these lines ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/products/9661s)). Two cautions apply. The full FIG attire article could not be opened, so FIG's strap width, leg-cut wording and deductions are unverified. And neither the USA Gymnastics nor the NCAA text names the three-quarter sleeve that dominates club catalogues; how officials treat it is not stated.

Leotards are sold in about 10 to 13 sizes from small child to adult. GK's chart runs from CXXS to A4XL, names the torso loop (shoulder, down the front, through the legs and back to the same shoulder) as the most important measurement, and offers long- and short-torso variants about three inches either side ([GK Elite](https://gkelite.azureedge.net/images/static/sizecharts/size-charts-inches-womens-leos.pdf)). GK specifies different sizing sets for foil fabrics than for plain ones because the stretch differs ([GK Elite](https://gkelite.azureedge.net/images/static/sizecharts/gymnastics-sizing-set-reference-guide.pdf)). Nothing published explains how brands carry panel lines, artwork or crystal patterns from one size to the next.

The market the tool would serve is led by GK Elite (Reading, Pennsylvania; founded 1981; Team USA's outfitter since 2000), with Sylvia P of Australia and Quatro of the UK as the main international custom houses and Snowflake, Plum and Destira serving US clubs at lower prices. The long-standing UK brand Milano Pro Sport went into liquidation in July 2026 ([GK Elite](https://blog.gkelite.com/our-enduring-legacy-the-history-of-gk-elite/); [Milano Pro Sport](https://www.milano-pro-sport.com/cosmic-curve-long-sleeved-leotard-pink.html)). Prices run from about US$20–60 for training tanks, through roughly US$100–380 for made-to-order club competition leotards and up to about US$700 for crystal-heavy team designs, to an estimated US$1,000–5,000 for an Olympic piece ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gym-leotards-competition-special-order); [Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.net/collections/all); [Cornell Daily Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2024/07/31/jeanne-diaz-10-designs-leotards-for-womens-olympic-gymnastics-team/)).

### Factories decorate flat pieces first and close the side seams last

**The software has to think in flat pattern pieces even though the user designs on a body, because that is how the garment is made.** Reporting from inside GK's plant gives the order. Fabric is spread in layers and laser-cut, with pieces bundled by athlete size and alteration. Printed designs are heat-transferred onto the cut pieces. Laser-cut appliqués are placed by hand with glue and then stitched. Logos are embroidered. Crystal motifs are heat-pressed onto the still-flat pieces and extra stones set by hand. Only then are front and back sewn together at the sides, shoulders and crotch, followed by finishing and inspection ([NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/tokyo-olympics/inside-the-creation-of-simone-biles-goat-leotard/186340/); [NAM](https://nam.org/small-manufacturer-shines-at-olympics-31672)). At least 37 people handle each GK leotard ([College Gym News](https://collegegymnews.com/?p=28471)). The consequence for design is direct: anything that crosses a side, shoulder or crotch seam, whether a printed swirl, an appliqué or a line of crystals, must be split cleanly between pieces or finished by hand after sewing, and the software should show the user where that happens.

The pattern is cut smaller than the body and relies on stretch for fit, a reduction pattern-makers call negative ease, and **there is no industry standard for it**. Published figures range from 0–10% of girth in home-sewing guides to about 15–20% of width and 0–5% of length in German trade guidance for four-way-stretch bodywear, and every source says the number must be set for each fabric by stretch-testing and fitting ([Müller & Sohn](https://www.muellerundsohn.com/en/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/204); [Watkins 2011](http://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/13231/1/IJFTR%2036%284%29%20366-379.pdf)). GK stretch-tests every incoming roll by hand and keeps full-time fit specialists ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-the-gk-fit)). No leotard maker publishes its own figures. Two cutting rules are universal: the direction of greatest stretch runs around the body, and napped fabrics such as velvet are cut with every piece facing the same way ([Spandex Simplified](https://spandexsimplified.com/how-to-cut-spandex/)). The software should hold stretch as a property of each fabric and leave the final reduction to the manufacturer.

A design line can be made three ways, and the choice limits its shape. A pieced seam joins two fabrics with 6 mm allowances and suits only straight or gently curved lines with no sharp inside corners. A stitched overlay, or appliqué, allows any shape at the cost of an extra layer and some stretch. A sublimated print needs no seam at all ([Spandex Simplified](https://spandexsimplified.com/colour-blocking-for-spandex/)). Home makers of gymnastics leotards apply the same rule: simple curves are seamed and swirls are appliquéd ([ChalkBucket](https://chalkbucket.com/threads/tips-for-making-a-leotard.44234/)). A specialist author's formulas for backs are concrete enough to encode, such as keeping more than 1.5 inches between a neckline and a keyhole and at least two inches between a side seam and the nearest strap ([Spandex Simplified](https://spandexsimplified.com/neck-back-style-variations/)). Seams are overlocked, openings are finished with elastic about 1 cm wide or with binding, and competition leotards are front-lined or fully lined ([Coats](https://www.coats.com/en/industries/apparel/applications/sports-and-activewear/swimwear/); [Jalie](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0267/4075/2568/files/product_sizes_3901.pdf?v=1605550698)).

Custom orders everywhere follow one path: a brief, a digital design with revisions, sign-off, sizing with a fit kit, then production. GK adds a physical, fully embellished sample for fully custom work ([GK Elite](https://blog.gkelite.com/how-to-personalize-team-leotards-gymnastics-gear/)). GK quotes three weeks for samples, five to six for made-to-order styles and seven to eight for custom designs ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/gk-gym-estimated-production-time)). Sylvia P's order-by dates imply about six weeks off-peak and thirteen in the autumn rush ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/pages/2023-2024-production-timelines)), and small US and UK makers quote two to three weeks ([k-Bee](https://www.k-beeleotards.com/); [Velocity Pro Sport](https://www.gymnasticsleotards.co.uk/pages/bespoke-club-uniforms)). To be useful to a factory, a design has to arrive as things it can act on: pattern pieces in the DXF format that garment design systems exchange, a print image for each piece, a stone map and count for each piece, and a specification sheet ([CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export); [Techpacker](https://techpacker.com/blog/design/what-is-a-tech-pack/)). Which systems the leotard makers themselves use is not public.

The research produced a concrete list of manufacturability checks covering seam shapes, strap and keyhole spacing, stone-free zones at seams and elastic, lining under mesh and heat-sensitive fabrics under stones. Almost none of the thresholds are published as hard numbers by any manufacturer, so they should ship as adjustable settings that each factory tunes.

## Dressmaker shows the build loop; today's brand studios show the gap

### Dressmaker builds a dress in three layers, and its flaws are as instructive as its strengths

**Take from Dressmaker the catalogue of swappable pieces, the fabric-per-panel step and the free rearrangement on a mannequin. Leave behind its manual labour and its missing undo.** The game meant is almost certainly the sewing simulation by Cozy Lives and Free Lives, released on Steam on 21 September 2026, which peaked at 35,650 simultaneous players and stood at 98% positive reviews three weeks later ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4019220/_/); [DLCompare](https://www.dlcompare.com/gaming-news/dressmaker-turns-sewing-into-a-surprisingly-big-steam-hit)). That identification rests on the name, the timing and the fit with your description. It has not been confirmed with you, and the nearest alternative is Roblox's Dress to Impress, where players assemble outfits from whole garments ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dress_to_Impress_%28video_game%29)).

Building in Dressmaker is a fixed pipeline. A customer's brief sets target style scores and a budget. The player measures the client and sets three dials on a mannequin. In a sketchbook the player picks ready-made parts (a bodice with its neckline, a collar, sleeves and a skirt), each of which carries style scores and shows how much fabric it will need. Fabric is bought by the metre and assigned panel by panel, so an expensive cloth can be saved for the parts that show. Pattern pieces are laid on the fabric, where grain direction and the position of a print or stripe carry through to the finished dress, and are cut automatically. The cut panels are pinned to the mannequin for a 3D preview, a stage at which rearranging is free. Each seam is then sewn in a small steering game, after which buttons, bows, lace and appliqué can be placed anywhere in any quantity. A photo studio with adjustable camera and lighting records the result ([AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/?p=207842); [Windows Mode](https://www.windowsmode.com/?p=47112); [iXBT.games](https://ixbt.games/en/reviews/2026/10/05/439975-modnyi-prigovor-sveinomu-simuliatoru-obzor-dressmaker.html)). The game launched with about 150 dress pieces, 450 fabrics and 350 accessories ([Windows Mode](https://www.windowsmode.com/dressmaker-for-windows/amp/)).

Stripped of its story, the game builds a garment in three layers: parts chosen from a catalogue, a fabric assigned to each panel, and small decorations placed freely on top. Your software needs all three, weighted differently. Three things transfer. The piece catalogue maps onto a leotard's front, back style, neckline, sleeve, leg line and inserts. Assigning a fabric to each panel, with the print shown exactly as it will be cut, is the right model for mystique, mesh and sublimated panels. And the pinning stage, where the whole design is visible in 3D and nothing is committed, is how the entire session should feel. Two things do not transfer. Dressmaker has no full undo for decoration, which is tolerable for a few bows and unacceptable for 1,500 crystals. And the cutting and sewing are the game's "work": within three weeks of launch players had published a mod that automates sewing, measuring and pattern layout ([Windows Mode](https://www.windowsmode.com/dressmaker-for-windows/amp/)). Dressmaker also has no documented symmetry or brush tools, so it is a model for assembling pieces, not for placing stones in bulk.

For stone brushes the better models are painting and sculpting tools. Adobe's Substance 3D Painter records every stroke so it can be recomputed later, and its path tool lets an artist click a curve onto a 3D model and keep editing both the curve and the brush afterwards ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/products/substance3d/apps/painter.html); [CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/adobe-releases-substance-3d-painter-9-0/)). Blender's brush settings supply a ready vocabulary: evenly spaced dabs, straight lines, editable curves, random jitter and stroke smoothing ([Blender](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/sculpt_paint/brush/stroke.html)). Nomad Sculpt drops an object on a surface and lets it slide before release, repeats objects along a drawn curve and mirrors them live ([Nomad Sculpt](https://nomadsculpt.com/manual/tools.html)). The classic research on creativity tools reduces "frictionless" to a short list: act directly on the thing itself with immediate results, make undo trustworthy so people dare to experiment, choose a few good high-level building blocks, and keep the entry easy while leaving room for experts ([Resnick et al.](https://www.cs.umd.edu/hcil/CST/Papers/designprinciples.pdf)). Direct manipulation has one documented weakness, which is many actions on many objects ([Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/direct-manipulation/)). That is exactly why your split between single-stone placement and brushes is right.

### No tool examined lets a user draw a panel line or place a single stone

**The gap is specific and, on the evidence found, unoccupied.** Three gymnastics brands' self-serve design studios could be described from their own pages. GK's UDesign describes itself as an online 3D design studio, will not load without the browser's 3D graphics support, and is open to team orders only ([GK Elite](https://www.gkelite.com/pages/udesign)). Sylvia P's StudioSP changes fabric and colour zone by zone, sells crystals as a coverage tier (logo only, light or heavy) in standard or Swarovski stones, and offers a shareable link and a button that sends the design to an account manager ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/products/nylah-leotard-full-sleeve)). Quatro has a kit builder for clubs ordering ten or more ([Quatro](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/teamwear-design-studio)). All three start from an existing style and swap options within fixed zones. None was found to let a user draw a new panel line, move a seam or position individual stones. These descriptions come from the vendors' own pages; none of the studios was operated hands-on. Three further tools exist but could not be examined: Ozone's design studio, Destira's design-your-own pages, and a kit builder at the UK maker Velocity Pro Sport that prices a leotard from the complexity of its design ([Velocity Pro Sport](https://gymnasticsleotards.co.uk/pages/clubs)). The other makers examined, including Snowflake, Foxy's and the cheer brands Varsity and Rebel, route custom design through a human designer or sales representative.

Professional fashion software does not fill the gap. CLO, Marvelous Designer, Browzwear VStitcher, Style3D and Optitex share one model: draw flat pattern pieces, sew them virtually, and simulate the cloth on an avatar. That demands pattern-making skill, and one reviewer estimates four to eight weeks for a newcomer to become productive in CLO ([KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/clo-3d/)). Glitter and gems in CLO are materials that appear only in its offline renderer, not in its live view ([CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115015998728-Material-Type)), and a scan of the 962 article titles in CLO's help centre and the 965 in Browzwear's found none about rhinestones, sequins, crystals or embellishment ([CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us); [Browzwear](https://help.browzwear.com/en/)). For Style3D, Marvelous Designer and Optitex the sources were too thin to say either way. The general teamwear configurators such as Kit Builder are built for printed jerseys and have no concept of physical embellishment ([Kit Builder](https://www.kitbuilder.com/)). A single search pass found no startup, app or research project devoted to leotard, dancewear or skating-dress design, which is a provisional finding given how the search was cut short.

The custom-order process these tools sit inside is slow and indirect: an enquiry, a designer or sales representative, emailed concepts, revisions, sign-off, sizing kits, then four to thirteen weeks of production, usually for teams only, with minimums, and with goods that cannot be returned ([Quatro](https://quatrogymnastics.com/pages/custom-teamwear-design-process); [Snowflake Designs](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/team-information)). Because custom goods cannot be returned, how faithfully a preview matches the delivered garment is a matter of trust as well as beauty. The evidence that customers want to design for themselves is indirect. Three leading brands have paid to build studios, and Sylvia P's own prices show the crystal choice moving one garment from AU$125 to AU$472 ([Sylvia P](https://teamwear.sylviap.com.au/products/nylah-leotard-full-sleeve)). But no first-hand statements from coaches, club owners or parents could be read. A realistic first role for the software is upstream of the manufacturer: it produces a design a factory can quote and make.

### The leotard can be designed directly on the body, with no cloth simulation

**Because a leotard is skin-tight, the software can treat it as a layer on the body's surface, which removes the slowest and hardest part of fashion software from the interactive loop.** Professional tools simulate cloth falling under gravity, which is much of what makes them slow to learn and slow to respond. For skintight clothing the leading research method does the opposite and embeds the garment in the body surface precisely to avoid simulating contact ([Montes et al., SIGGRAPH 2020](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=104289)). The researchers recommend the same approach here. Lines, panels and stones are stored as positions on a standard body, and simulation is kept for the few parts that leave the skin, such as straps spanning the hollow of the back or an attached skirt, and for optional fit read-outs computed in the background. CLO's tool for drawing on the avatar and Marvelous Designer's 2026 3D Pencil both create panels from lines sketched on a body, which confirms the direction ([CLO](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008890627-Flatten); [Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/04/15/marvelous-designer-2026-0-adds-3d-pencil-and-lacing/)). One refinement needs prototyping: real stretch fabric bridges hollows such as the sternum and lower back instead of following them, and none of the published methods' summaries addresses this.

Three building blocks are in good shape. For the body, an openly licensed model named Anny, from NAVER LABS Europe, covers a continuous range from small child to adult with sliders for age, height and build, and is free for commercial use. The better-known SMPL family is restricted to non-commercial use, and its commercial licensing is in flux after Epic Games' reported acquisition, in February 2026, of the company that handled it ([Anny](https://github.com/naver/anny); [SMPL](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html); [GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/epic-games-acquires-meshcapade-and-will-establish-a-presence-in-tubingen/)). Anny is calibrated to general-population statistics and competitive gymnasts are not the general population, so its proportions need checking against real size charts. Because the bodies depict children, the researchers advise a neutral mannequin presentation by default. For drawing, published methods trace smooth pen-tool curves across a 3D surface interactively on models far more detailed than a body ([Mancinelli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.05921)). For stones, the proven approach in visual-effects software is to record each one's address on the surface so that it rides along when the body changes size or pose ([SideFX](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/attribinterpolate.html)).

Every brush then reduces to the same three steps: a curve or region on the body, a rule for spacing points along or within it, and a check that removes collisions. Even spacing along a line, offset rows for thick strokes, honeycomb packing for dense fills, evenly scattered points for sparkle fields and the sunflower spiral all have published, inexpensive methods ([Bridson](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/bridson-siggraph07-poissondisk.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat%27s_spiral)). The researchers recommend storing the stroke and its settings and deriving the stones from them, so that spacing, size or colour can be changed after the fact, with a command to release a stroke into individual stones for hand finishing.

Two questions stay open because they are manufacturing practice, not mathematics. Turning a panel drawn on the body into a flat pattern piece is a solved and fast calculation, but how much smaller to cut it for stretch is the factory's knowledge; the practical first step is to map designs onto a manufacturer's existing graded patterns. And nothing was found on how makers carry a crystal layout across sizes: keep the count and let the spacing shrink on small sizes, or keep the spacing and drop stones. The software should offer both and report counts for each size. A related trap follows from stretch itself. Stones are rigid, so a layout that looks evenly spaced on the body sits closer together when the garment is off the body and relaxed, and the overlap check has to run in both states.

The geometry toolkit for all of this exists as mature open-source libraries, but written for desktop programs, not in ready-made form for the web ([geometry-central](https://geometry-central.net/)). Porting or rewriting the small part needed is real work whichever engine is chosen.

## Physically based rendering is the right foundation, but each fabric needs one or two extra layers

**Yes, PBR is what accurate lighting takes.** Physically based rendering means a material is described by how it physically reflects light, so it responds correctly when the user orbits, zooms or moves a lamp, which is your stated requirement. The catch is that the standard form every engine ships (a base colour, a roughness value and a metal-or-not switch) was designed for hard surfaces such as plastic and painted metal. Cloth breaks it in known ways: fibres glow softly at glancing angles, knit direction stretches highlights, sheer fabric lets the skin show, and foil sits in dots on top of dye. The industry's answer is to add separate, named reflection layers on top of the base. By 2026 these are standardised (an industry specification called OpenPBR, updated in April 2026, defines the set) and most ship in the main web engines ([OpenPBR](https://academysoftwarefoundation.github.io/OpenPBR/); [three.js](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html)).

### Standard layers cover most fabrics; holographic foil, velvet pile and stretch need custom work

| Material | Technique, in plain terms | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Matte lycra | Standard base plus a faint "sheen" layer for the soft glow at glancing angles | Off the shelf |
| Shiny tricot | Standard base with "anisotropy": highlights drawn out along the knit direction | Off the shelf |
| Wet look, cire | The shiny base under a smooth "clear coat" | Off the shelf |
| All-over metallic foil | Metal with a fine crinkle texture | Off the shelf |
| Mystique (foil dots) | A dot pattern that mixes metal with the dyed cloth; base and foil colours set separately | Mostly standard; behaviour at a distance and under stretch is custom |
| Holographic foil | Metal plus a diffraction layer that splits light into rainbow colours according to the direction of microscopic grooves | Custom; the published basis covers rigid surfaces only |
| Iridescent and pearlescent film; AB coating on stones | "Thin-film" interference, the soap-bubble effect | Off the shelf |
| Velvet | A strong "fuzz" layer over a dark base, tilted by pile direction so it reads lighter or darker with the nap | Fuzz layer off the shelf; pile direction and crushed velvet custom |
| Mesh and sheer | Treated as partial coverage and blended with the skin or lining beneath in a single step | Standard parts; the single-step blend is the researchers' proposal |
| Sublimation print | Artwork as the base colour of a matte or shiny cloth, with managed colour | Off the shelf |
| Glitter fabric | A "glint" model that estimates how many flakes in each pixel are catching the light | Custom in web engines; public code exists |
| Spangles and sequins | Individual flat mirrored discs, merging into the glint model at a distance | Custom |
| Crystals | Individual faceted stones drawn in progressively simpler forms with distance | Custom, and unproven at scale |

The off-the-shelf layers and their published foundations are documented in the engines and standards ([three.js](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html); [Belcour and Barla, thin film](https://belcour.github.io/blog/research/publication/2017/05/01/brdf-thin-film.html); [Toisoul et al., holographic surfaces](https://wp.doc.ic.ac.uk/rgi/project/acquiring-spatially-varying-appearance-of-printed-holographic-surfaces/)).

The research concludes that every fabric family can run at interactive frame rates in a browser from one flexible material, and that none requires an academic cloth model, machine learning or ray tracing. The specialist research models are in fact too slow or the wrong kind: the best recent neural model for woven cloth reaches about 60 frames a second only on a top-of-the-range desktop graphics card, and it excludes knits ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17782)). **That conclusion rests on counting the calculations each layer adds, not on measurement**, and the starting values suggested for each fabric are informed guesses to be tuned against real swatches. Three effects central to leotards have essentially no published rendering method behind them and are design risks: how fabric lightens and shows the skin as it stretches, how foil and holographic laminates behave on stretch cloth, and how to show fluorescent "neon" colours, which physically exceed what ordinary reflected colour can represent ([Jung et al.](https://rgl.epfl.ch/publications/Jung2019Wide)).

### Glitter, sequins and crystals each need a different treatment

**Sparkle is three problems, and only the first is solved.** Glitter is millions of flakes too small to draw one by one, so it is handled statistically: for each pixel the software estimates how many flakes happen to be angled to flash. A line of published methods does this in real time. The best known adds roughly 0.2 to 1.1 milliseconds per light at full-HD resolution on a 2018 gaming graphics card, against a budget of 16.7 milliseconds per frame at 60 frames a second ([Deliot and Belcour](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.05051)). Later work extends it to lights of realistic size and to whole environments, and a 2025 method adds individually coloured flakes ([Kneiphof and Klein 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13611v2); [Kneiphof and Klein 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02674); [Kemppinen et al.](https://perso.telecom-paristech.fr/boubek/papers/Glinty/)). Public code exists for each. Among shipping engines only Unreal was confirmed to include a glint option, as an opt-in feature in its most expensive tier ([Epic Games](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine)). No timings on laptop-class graphics were found.

A crystal is optically simpler than a diamond: a shallow glass dome of 8 to 18 facets over a flat mirror, so light enters, bounces once and leaves ([Preciosa](https://img.preciosacomponents.com/files/landingpage/lp-4/downloads/Preciosa_Chaton_Rose_MAXIMA_Benchmark.pdf)). Web jewellery viewers already trace light through gems in real time, but for hundreds of stones, not thousands ([iJewel3D](https://docs.ijewel3d.com/introduction/ijewel.html)). For a leotard carrying 2,000 to 10,000 stones the researchers propose drawing each stone at one of four levels of detail chosen by its size on screen: full facets with internal reflection in close-up, a simplified stone at mid-range, a single dot whose flashes come from a precomputed table at full-body distance, and beyond that a contribution folded into the fabric's own sparkle. The property that would make the transitions invisible is that the same stone flashes at the same moment at every level. **This scheme is the research team's own design. No public demonstration of anything like it was found, and it is the single most important thing to prototype.** Sheer numbers are not the obstacle. By the researchers' estimate even a fully covered garment holds on the order of 100,000 stones, which engines can draw as repeated copies of one object. The difficulty is making each look right and stay stable as the camera moves.

Spangles sit in between. Each is one flat mirror lying in the fabric, so whole areas flash together as the body turns, unlike the pin-point twinkle of crystals.

### Sparkle depends on the lights and the observer as much as on the stone

**Lighting presets and a deliberate definition of "accurate" belong in the product, not just in the engine.** Stones and glitter flash only under small, intense lights; under broad soft light a crystal looks like a flat mirror of the ceiling. Every published glint method began with pin-point lights, and lights of realistic size and whole environments were handled only from 2024 onward. The recommended lighting is therefore a photographed environment for the soft overall light, which can be rotated at no cost, plus a set of explicit small lamps for the sparkle, with lamp size offered as a control because it is the main sparkle dial. No data on real arena or broadcast lighting was collected.

The star-shaped flare people associate with a rhinestone is created mostly by the eye or the camera, not by the stone: aperture blades and lens bloom in a camera, and the eye's own optics in person ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffraction_spike)). Since the references designers compare against are photographs and broadcast footage, the researchers suggest computing the light physically and then applying a named, selectable observer such as the naked eye, a broadcast camera or a phone photo. This is a product decision worth making early, because it defines what "accurate" will be judged against.

Colour needs the same care. The film-style tone curves common in 3D software wash out saturated colours. The Khronos PBR Neutral curve, released in May 2024 for e-commerce and product visualisation, was designed to reproduce a product's base colour faithfully and compress only the highlights, which is what matching a team's dye colour requires ([Khronos](https://www.khronos.org/news/press/khronos-pbr-neutral-tone-mapper-released-for-true-to-life-color-rendering-of-3d-products)). It holds only under neutral white light, so a plain colour-check lighting preset should sit beside the dramatic ones. For flawless stills, the practical technique is to refine the image over a second or so whenever the camera stops moving. A slower, fully ray-traced final render is available in the browser as an optional extra on capable machines ([three-gpu-pathtracer](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-gpu-pathtracer)). The graphics-card ray tracing used by games is not available to browser applications and is not needed for any of the above ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GPUSupportedFeatures)).

Because no measured optical data exists for any leotard fabric or stone, accuracy has to be established by comparison. Photograph physical swatches and stone grids under one small lamp at a few fixed angles, reproduce the set-up in the software, and compare sparkle statistics (how many bright points per square centimetre, how bright, what colours) instead of comparing pixels. The paint and coatings industry already measures sparkle this way with dedicated instruments ([X-Rite](https://www.xrite.com/categories/portable-spectrophotometers/ma-t-family/ma-t12)).

## Build on a pre-built engine and hand-write only the leotard-specific layer

**The answer to "pre-built or hand-rolled" is both, in a specific split.** Use an existing open-source engine for everything common to all 3D applications: loading models and textures, lights and shadows, environment lighting, colour output, working out what is under the cursor, and coping with the quirks of different browsers and graphics chips. Hand-write the layer that makes a leotard look like a leotard and that no engine provides: the sparkle and special-fabric shading, the pipeline that draws thousands of editable stones, and the on-body drawing tools. The engine researchers were asked to report neutrally; the weighing of their evidence below, and the recommendation, are this report's.

### Writing a renderer from scratch buys control the product does not yet need

The professional web tools that built their own renderers did so for reasons that do not apply here. Figma wrote one because nothing existing could draw its 2D vector documents fast enough, and Onshape because its engineering geometry is computed on servers and streamed to the browser as triangles ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/building-a-professional-design-tool-on-the-web/); [Onshape](https://www.onshape.com/en/blog/how-does-onshape-really-work)). Their accounts also show the cost. Before any material work begins, a custom renderer needs its own handling of graphics memory, shader preparation, graphics failures in mid-session and a fallback for machines without the newest graphics interface; Figma ended up maintaining a live fallback and a blocklist of troublesome devices ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/figma-rendering-powered-by-webgpu/)). No source gives a cost in person-months for a renderer of this kind. The one dated data point for a professional 3D design tool built on its own foundations is Shapr3D's statement that those foundations took three years ([Shapr3D](https://www.shapr3d.com/blog/brief-history-of-shapr3d)). The traffic has also run the other way: the architectural visualisation tool Twinmotion ran on its own engine for more than a decade before its makers moved it onto Unreal ([Epic Games](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/twinmotion-breaks-out-of-the-box-with-a-bridge-to-ue4)). Against that, every leotard-specific effect identified in this research is shading code of the kind the engines' documented extension points are built to accept. A custom renderer would give full control over how each frame is assembled and immunity from an engine's changes. Those are real benefits, but they are worth paying for only if a prototype shows an engine getting in the way.

### The big game engines bring the most features and the most strings

Unreal Engine 5.8 ships more of the required picture than anything else, including built-in fuzz, anisotropy, thin-film and glint layers ([Epic Games](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/overview-of-substrate-materials-in-unreal-engine)). The strings are these. Its glint layer is not drawn on the simplified rendering path used on tablets. Its documentation targets games consoles and high-end PCs. Its licence forbids shipping the editor's own tools, such as mesh painting and modelling, inside a product, so those would be rebuilt anyway. It has no in-browser version, only video streaming from a server with a graphics card for each user. And an application sold to outside customers owes 5% of its lifetime revenue above US$1 million ([Epic Games licensing](https://www.unrealengine.com/license); [Unreal Engine EULA](https://www.unrealengine.com/eula/unreal)). Unity's high-fidelity pipeline has fabric and iridescence materials, but Unity states that no new features are planned for it and it does not run on iPad, while the pipeline Unity is investing in lacks those materials ([Unity](https://unity.com/topics/render-pipelines-strategy-for-2026); [Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Manual/render-pipelines-feature-comparison.html)). Unity also steers non-game companies with more than US$1 million in total finances to a plan at US$4,950 per seat per year, with a runtime fee of about 4% of product revenue ([Unity](https://unity.com/products/unity-industry)). NVIDIA's Omniverse requires an RTX graphics card and excludes Macs and tablets ([NVIDIA](https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/dev-guide/latest/common/technical-requirements.html)). The free Godot engine lacks the sheen and thin-film layers, and its browser export is limited to the older graphics interface ([Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/standard_material_3d.html); [Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). In every one of these, the crystal pipeline would still be custom work.

### three.js in the browser is the lead candidate, with Babylon.js as the fallback

The recommendation assumes something only you can confirm: that the people using this include coaches, club owners, parents and small makers on ordinary laptops and tablets, who expect to open a link and share one. The market already works this way. GK's and Sylvia P's studios are web pages, and Sylvia P's has a copy-link button. Nothing in the rendering research requires a capability that browsers lack, and a web application can also be wrapped as an installable desktop program with a known browser engine inside ([Electron](https://releases.electronjs.org/)). On that assumption the product is a browser application that uses the newer browser graphics interface (WebGPU) where available and the older one (WebGL 2) elsewhere. WebGPU is on by default in Chrome and Edge, in Safari 26 on Mac, iPhone and iPad, and in Firefox on Windows and Mac. One survey finds it working in 83% of browsing sessions, so dropping the fallback would exclude about one user in six ([WebGPU implementation status](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status); [Web3D Survey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgpu)). Tablets are the least certain target: WebGPU reaches iPad only from iPadOS 26 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)), and current browser memory limits on iPad could not be established.

Among web engines, three.js is the stronger starting point for four reasons. It has by far the largest community, at about 21 million weekly downloads against roughly 400,000 for Babylon.js, which matters for hiring and for finding answers ([npm](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-week/three); [npm](https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-week/@babylonjs/core)). It already ships the standard cloth layers, the faithful colour curve, built-in handling for overlapping sheer fabric and area lights ([three.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases/tag/r186)). Its mechanism for adding custom lighting behaviour is the most direct of the engines examined, and shading written once runs on both the newer and the older graphics interface ([three.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/r186/src/nodes/core/LightingModel.js)). And the surrounding open-source ecosystem contains reference code for the hardest parts: tracing light through gems, fast picking and painting on 3D models, and an optional ray-traced final render ([three-mesh-bvh](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh)). There is a precedent as well. The commercial 3D design tool Spline rebuilt itself on three.js's new renderer in August 2026 while shipping an older-interface build alongside ([Spline runtime package](https://app.unpkg.com/@splinetool/runtime@2.0.79/files/build); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/8/spline-v2-launches-with-rebuilt-3d-editor-ai-agents-webgpu-and-new-desktop-app/)).

The costs are equally clear. three.js's own manual still calls its new renderer experimental, nearly every release contains breaking changes, and a forum reply as recently as February 2026 advised new projects to stay on the older renderer for stability ([three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/webgpurenderer.html); [three.js migration guide](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/wiki/Migration-Guide); [three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/webgpu-renderer-vanilla-three-js-vs-r3f-maturity-and-pitfalls/89661)). The team would fix on one version, budget time for upgrades and keep the fallback build, which is the pattern the shipped evidence shows. Babylon.js is the credible alternative. Its maintainers describe its WebGPU support as complete, its recent releases flag no breaking changes, it includes more editor conveniences out of the box, and Adobe is contributing a modern material model with fuzz, thin-film and coat layers ([Babylon.js](https://doc.babylonjs.com/setup/support/webGPU/webGPUStatus); [Babylon.js](https://blogs.windows.com/windowsdeveloper/2026/04/02/part-3-babylon-js-9-0-openpbr-and-additional-engine-updates/)). Its weaknesses for this product are that custom shading is patched into the stock material at insertion points its documentation warns can change, and that its mass-copy feature is tuned for objects that stay put, where painting stones means constant additions ([Babylon.js](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/materialPlugins); [Babylon.js](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/mesh/copies/thinInstances)). Of the other web options, PlayCanvas labels its WebGPU support beta ([PlayCanvas](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/graphics/)), and Google's Filament has the most cloth-specific shading built in but the weakest web delivery, its browser package last published in August 2024 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/filament?activeTab=versions)). Every web option is free to license.

| Option | What it gives | What it costs | When it would win |
|---|---|---|---|
| three.js plus a custom layer (recommended) | Standard cloth layers, the largest ecosystem, custom shading written once, browser reach | A new renderer still labelled experimental; frequent breaking changes; editor tools assembled from add-ons | — |
| Babylon.js plus a custom layer | Stable releases, more built-in editor tools, a modern material model in progress | Less direct custom shading; a much smaller community | If the prototype shows three.js's new renderer to be too unstable, or stability matters more than flexibility |
| A hand-rolled renderer | Total control; no dependence on an engine's changes | A long wait before the first good picture; device problems the engines have already solved; reliance on one or two specialists | If the prototype shows an engine blocking the sparkle pipeline or missing the speed target, and a graphics engineer who has built a renderer before is on the team |
| Unreal Engine on the desktop | The richest ready-made picture, including glints and a ray-traced mode | A high hardware floor; no browser; editor tools rebuilt; 5% royalty above US$1 million | If the product is a desktop tool for professional studios with gaming-class graphics cards, and browser and tablet reach do not matter |
| A native app on a small rendering library such as Filament, with a custom interface | The best built-in cloth shading among the small libraries; runs on Windows, Mac and iPad | Everything except the rendering must be built | If the product is tablet-first with a stylus, where browser memory and pen limits bite hardest |

A hand-rolled renderer becomes the right call only when three conditions hold together. The stress test shows the engine's fixed way of assembling a frame preventing the sparkle pipeline or missing the speed target. The product is confirmed as a long-lived, single-purpose tool, since one garment on one body under a few lights is a far narrower job than a general engine's. And an engineer who has built a renderer before is on the team. Even then the sensible route is a thin layer over the browser's graphics interface, not a start from nothing. The tablet-first case deserves its own note: the tablet-first professional tools examined, such as Procreate, run on engines their makers wrote ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2019/9/6/20847944/procreate-5-valkyrie-photoshop-brushes-color-profiles-cmyk)).

### Prototype the hardest picture first

**One stress-test scene would confirm or overturn this recommendation, and it should come before any other building.** It is a single leotard on a body with a mystique torso, a mesh sleeve over skin, a glitter panel, and 10,000 crystals including AB-coated ones laid down by a rough brush, lit by an environment plus a ring of small lamps, and viewed from full body to extreme close-up. It should be run on the three machines that matter (a laptop with the graphics chip built into its processor, an Apple-silicon Mac and a recent iPad) on both graphics interfaces. It passes if it holds 60 frames a second, if stones neither flicker nor visibly change as the camera moves through the levels of detail, if brushing new stones stays responsive, and if nothing stalls when a new material first appears. No such measurements exist anywhere in the sources. Every speed figure in this report was published for gaming-class graphics cards or is an estimate, and the only laptop-graphics timing found for any relevant effect is a contact-shadow pass costing about 2.4 milliseconds at full-HD on an Intel laptop chip ([Intel XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO)). If the three.js version shows instability in its new renderer, the same scene in Babylon.js is the next test.

Two smaller prototypes should run alongside. The first is the on-body drawing loop (draw a seam on the body, get a panel, flatten it to a pattern piece), because that toolkit has to be brought to the web whichever engine wins. The second is not software. Order physical swatches of the main fabric families (GK gives up to five free and Snowflake charges 25 cents each) and a few gross of stones in the common sizes, and photograph them under a single small lamp ([GK swatch store](https://www.gkelite.com/collections/gk-swatches/products.json?limit=250); [Snowflake Designs](https://www.snowflakedesigns.com/fabric-swatches.html)). Without them there is nothing to judge the picture against.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shape of the problem. "Everything about leotard design" turns out to be mostly catalogue data: a finite set of cloths, finishes, stones, option names and rule lines that can be loaded, checked and counted. The difficulty concentrates in two narrow places, truthful sparkle from thousands of editable stones and drawing directly on a body, and those are exactly the two things that no product, engine or research paper found delivers together. That is the case for spending nothing on what can be borrowed and everything on those two.

Two decisions sit with you, not with engineering. The first is who the user is and what device they hold, because that settles browser against desktop against tablet more firmly than any technical argument. The second is what "accurate" means: physically correct light, or a match to how leotards look in photographs and on broadcast, which are a camera's rendition. Both can be served, but the reference photographs of real swatches that define the target should exist before the first material is tuned. And because every design must end as something a factory can quote and make, the stone counts, fabric lists and per-piece layouts the software has to compute anyway are also its most persuasive feature for the people who pay for leotards: a live answer to what this design will cost and whether it is legal to compete in.
