# Takeaway

**Mystique is the fabric to get right first.** It is "A tricot treated on the surface with micro
dots of foil. Creates a 'wet look'."[^lineup-novelty] A GK executive called it "the standard fabric in
the industry" in 2012 (dated source), citing its compression fit and a sheen that looks "dressier and
classier" than a dull finish.[^buzzfeed-2012] It is 80% nylon / 20% elastane, or 80% polyester / 20%
elastane for printable white, at 200-230 g/m², opaque, four-way stretch slightly tighter than the
base, used for bodies, binding and piping.[^notes-fab][^report]

# Construction

- Dyed tricot + adhesive printed in a dot pattern + foil transferred onto the adhesive. The dot (glue)
  pattern and the foil are independent choices (Funki: 37 glue patterns, 5 hologram and 11 metallic
  foil colours, available on all digital prints and five plain bases: matt nylon, shiny nylon,
  Alicante net, Life Recycled, velvet).[^funki-foilme][^notes-fab]
- Adhesion: "The foil since then has been perfected ... The stretch is no longer held back by the
  adhesive, but it might feel slightly tighter until it is worn a few times."[^snowflake-mystique]
- Pine Crest Jewels: "This knit tricot fabric has a four-way stretch and a flat foil."[^pinecrest-jewels]
- Stocked foil tricot is stated to originate in Korea.[^pinecrest-jewels]
- **Gap:** no source describes the factory process (adhesive printing method, lamination temperature
  and pressure, carrier film) or the foil's composition.[^notes-fab]

# Dot scale

Qualitative only: sparkle-hologram micro dots are "similar in size to a plain mystique lycra"; no
measured dot diameter, pitch or coverage exists for any mystique.[^notes-fab] Observed in swatch
photographs (researcher's own visual reading): a dense field of sub-millimetre glints over a clearly
visible base colour; two-tone colourways read as one hue in shadow and another in
highlight.[^notes-fab]

# Durability: the foil is the weak point

| Threat | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Rubbing | "Do not rub this fabric, as it will dull the foil."[^snowflake-mystique] |
| Heat, esp. over crystals | GK: foil can fade with heat, especially over embellished areas;[^gk-swatch] Sylvia P: crystal areas may dull slightly from heat application;[^sp-fabric] GK: foil and hologram dull slightly wherever stones are heat-pressed[^gk-qxli][^report]; Pine Crest: avoid heat for foiled fabrics[^pinecrest-faq] |
| Fabric softener | "Fabric softeners negatively affect the adhesives used in our garments"[^plum-faq]; GK gives the same explanation[^gk-care] |
| Deodorant, sweat, hairspray | Colour bleed, fading or foil loss, especially under the arms where colours meet; alcohol in aerosols damages foil[^gk-care] |
| Washing and wear | GK verbatim: "Careful attention is advised during washing and wear to preserve the fabric's lustrous foil";[^gk-swatch] foil "may fade with wear and washing"[^sw-1916] |

Sources describe dulling, fading and loss, never cracking.[^notes-fab] **[INFERRED]** Wear is
localised (underarms, over heat-set crystals, fabric-on-fabric rub), which a "worn" preset could
model.[^notes-fab]
