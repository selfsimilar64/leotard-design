# Takeaway

**Openings are finished either with ⅜ in (1 cm) elastic sewn to the raw edge, turned in and
topstitched, or with a binding strip that wraps the edge.** Elastic is cut shorter than the opening,
but the rule varies from a fixed per-size chart to 75–100% of the opening; makers override pattern
lengths after a fitting.[^pc-notes] Every unbound opening in a design needs an elastic spec.

# Elastic: material

Standard is ⅜ in (1 cm): Jalie patterns specify 1 cm; Treasurie recommends ⅜ in chlorine-resistant swim
elastic; Porter uses ⅜ in braided white (white so it does not shadow through light fabric) and 1 in for
waistbands and shelf bras; Baby Lock's project used ¼ in.[^jalie-3901][^treasurie][^porter-elastic][^babylock][^pc-notes]

Clear elastic is disputed: Peek-a-Boo recommends it for leg openings (fold-over elastic for neck and
armholes); Treasurie says avoid it; Porter rejects it for legs (does not hold a clean edge) and keeps
it for gathering.[^peekaboo][^treasurie][^porter-elastic][^pc-notes]

# Elastic: cut-length rules

| Source | Rule |
|---|---|
| Jalie[^jalie-2677][^jalie-2792][^jalie-3901] | Fixed cut length per size from a chart, e.g. leg elastic 35.5 cm (girls' F) to 63.3 cm (women's AA) on 2677; total ⅜ in elastic per leotard 1.5–2.5 m (2792) or 2.5–3.3 m (3901) |
| Spruce & Fjell[^spruce] | Front leg 1:1 with the front opening, back leg ¾ of the back opening, plus ½ in overlap |
| Treasurie (reader reply)[^treasurie] | Start at 85% of the wearer's leg and neck measurements when copying a garment |
| Porter (atelier)[^porter-elastic] | Measure straight-line (chord) distances on the flat garment and double them — neck: shoulder seam to CB plus shoulder seam to CF; armhole: shoulder seam to side seam; leg: crotch seam straight up to the side-seam point level with the top of the front leg curve — then stretch elastic slightly more than fabric; no elastic tension on shorts legs |
| Children's pattern (snippet only)[^bonnie] | About 2 cm shorter than the measured opening **[UNVERIFIED]** |

All via the research note.[^pc-notes] A pattern leg elastic proved too tight on a muscular thigh and
was lengthened after checking on the child.[^chalkbucket][^pc-notes]

# Elastic: application (common to all sources)

1. Join the elastic into a loop (ends overlapped ¼–½ in).
2. Quarter both elastic and opening and match the marks.
3. Attach to the wrong side at the raw edge with zigzag or overlock, stretching only the elastic.
4. Fold the elastic to the inside and topstitch through it with zigzag, twin needle or coverstitch
   while stretching.

The attaching pass uses a long stitch: densely packed stitches add bulk and stop the elastic
recovering.[^jalie-2914][^babylock][^porter-elastic][^pc-notes] Industrially, elastic is attached with
overedge stitch 503 (see [seams and stitches](/domain/construction/seams-stitches-and-allowances.md)).

# Binding and bands

- Jalie 3901 binds the neckline and (sleeveless view) the armholes, with elastic only at the legs;
  Jalie 2792 offers an elasticated scoop or banded necklines from a separate stretch-trim strip
  (1.5–2.1 m per garment).[^jalie-3901][^jalie-2792][^pc-notes]
- Home makers finish bindings with a coverstitch machine, or twin needle or three-step zigzag; one
  builds binding by wrapping lycra round clear elastic and twin-needling it.[^chalkbucket][^pc-notes]
- Banded collar strip formulas: see [back, strap and keyhole formulas](/domain/construction/back-strap-and-keyhole-formulas.md).

# Mesh edges

Mesh edges get the same turned elastic, with elastic and thread matched to skin tone or mesh colour;
clear elastic is an alternative on unlined mesh necklines.[^porter-elastic][^porter-straps][^pc-notes]

# Implications **[INFERRED]**

- Model elastic per opening segment as a ratio of elastic length to edge length (defaults 0.75–1.0,
  tighter at the back leg than the front) and export cut lengths per size in the bill of materials,
  as pattern companies do.
- Porter's chord method gives elastic shorter than a curved edge in proportion to its curvature; the
  tool can reproduce it by measuring chord versus arc on the flattened pattern piece.
- Edge allowance on a turned-elastic opening equals the elastic width (about 1 cm); a bound edge has
  zero allowance and a separate binding strip.
- No source documents metered elastic-attaching equipment or binding folders on leotards.[^pc-notes]
