# Takeaway

Designers consistently put **function first**, then use **line placement**, **contrast between
matte and shine** and **crystal density** to flatter the body and read from a distance. Jeanne
Diaz, GK Elite's design director who leads Team USA's leotards: "First and foremost, it is a
performance garment," and the fabric must move with the body so the athlete is not thinking about
what she wears.[^vogue-2024]

# Principles from named sources

| Principle | Source statement |
|---|---|
| Function first | Diaz (above); Snowflake keeps designs functional and warns against strap-heavy styles that catch a spotter's hand[^vogue-2024][^snowflake-training] |
| Lines must not expose faults | GK avoids a straight stripe along the sleeve inseam: on bars it highlights any bend in the arm, a form break judges penalise (Diaz)[^cornell] |
| Lines lead the eye up | The US `American Anthem` leotard used sinuous art-nouveau lines to draw the eye up toward the face on floor[^nbc-2024] |
| Long sleeves show form | GK: long sleeves create clear visual lines that show form and technique[^gk-blog-types] |
| Identity at a distance | Diaz wants Team USA recognisable from colour and design alone, without reading the country mark[^cornell] |
| Light: matte vs shine | Paris 2024 used a tonal approach contrasting matte and shine; athletes now prefer matte fabrics with crystals[^nbc-2024][^vogue-2024] |
| Crystals | Athletes want as many as possible; application is the slowest step, must be precise so none come loose, many placed by hand; adhesion tested per fabric[^nbc-2024][^marieclaire-2024][^cornell] |
| Comfort limits | Velvet only in small areas (overheating); stretch, wash and wear tests on every fabric; crystals kept off leg line and underarm[^cornell] |
| Process | GK starts Olympic designs two years out with an athlete survey (neckline, fabric, colour, crystal use) and theme mood boards[^cornell] |
| Waist and princess lines flatter | Sylvia P copy: panel lines that define the silhouette, belts and V-shaped belt lines, crystal lines following a curved princess line, patterns sized to be seen across the floor; `Transmission` crystals take the colour of the fabric beneath[^sylviap-cat] |
| Story + feedback | Snowflake's Kindra Snow-Walker on the Egypt brief: a work of art that stands out while distinctly Egyptian, reached after a month of trial, error and gymnast feedback[^insidegym-2024] |

# What informed observers dislike and praise

NCAA reviewers (2025) dislike: a straight horizontal waistline (compared to a diaper), mixed strap
widths, small plain hole cut-outs, a cut-out running up to one shoulder, heavy high collars,
recycled designs. They praise varied rhinestone sizes and shapes, texture for depth without
clutter, and strong backs.[^cgn-2025] This is editorial opinion from one outlet, not survey
data.[^styles]

# Design lint ideas [INFERRED]

Warn on straight horizontal waist seams, inner-arm stripes, mixed strap widths and large velvet
areas; encourage curved or V-shaped waist lines and lines converging upward toward the face. Show a
live crystal count and weight estimate (about one pound per 10,000 crystals, from the 2024
`Go for Glory` figure). Render at least two material classes (matte Lycra versus shiny
mystique/foil) plus crystal sparkle, because designers compose with that contrast.[^styles]
