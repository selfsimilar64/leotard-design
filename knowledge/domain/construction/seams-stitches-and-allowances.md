# Takeaway

**Seams are overlocked (ISO 504 or four-thread 514), topstitching is coverstitch (406) or four-needle
flatseam (607), elastic goes on with overedge 503, in fine polyester thread (Tex 18–35) at roughly
12–15 stitches per inch; seam allowance is 6 mm (¼ in) almost universally.** The industrial figures
are Coats' generic swimwear recommendations for the same fabric class; no leotard factory publishes
its own stitch specification.[^coats-swim][^pc-notes]

# Industrial stitch table (Coats, swimwear)

| Operation | ISO 4915 stitch | Density | Needle thread / looper thread | Needle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lockstitch | 301 | 12–14 per 3 cm | Coats Epic (poly corespun) Tex 30 / Epic Tex 30 | Nm 80–90 |
| Seaming (overlock) | 504 | 14–16 per 3 cm | Epic Tex 30 / Gramax Tex 18 | Nm 80–90 |
| Overlock with attached elastic | 503 | 14–16 per 3 cm | Gramax Tex 18 / Gramax Tex 18 | Nm 80–90 |
| Coverseam (hems, topstitch) | 406 | 14–18 per 3 cm | Epic Tex 30 / Gramax Tex 35 | Nm 80–90 |
| Bartack | 304 | — | Epic Tex 30 / Epic Tex 30 | Nm 80–90 |
| Four-thread overlock (knitted beachwear) | 514 | 14–16 per 3 cm | — | Nm 70–80 (snippet only) **[UNVERIFIED]** |

Sources: Coats.[^coats-swim][^coats-beach][^pc-notes] Further Coats notes: load-bearing seams moved
from Tex 40 to Tex 30; 503 is preferred over 504 for elastic because it gives more seam elongation and
a finer seam; four-needle flat seams (607) with a top cover thread are also used; ballpoint needles
for knits; a women's swimsuit consumes about 75 m of thread.[^coats-swim][^pc-notes]

**[INFERRED]** Converted densities for a tech pack: about 12–13.5 stitches per inch for overlock seams
and elastic, 12–15 for coverseams, 10–12 for lockstitch.

# Home and atelier equivalents

- Four-thread overlock for structural seams and elastic; three-thread only for hems; or a stretched
  straight stitch / narrow zigzag (about 1.0–1.5 mm wide, 2.5–3 mm long) on a domestic machine;
  serger differential feed neutral, medium stitch length for seams, long (setting 4) for elastic.[^porter-sew][^porter-elastic][^treasurie][^peekaboo][^pc-notes]
- Jalie's no-serger seam: a wide zigzag on the raw edge without stretching, then a straight stitch
  6 mm in while stretching slightly.[^jalie-2914][^pc-notes]
- Needles 75/11 or 80/12 stretch or ballpoint; polyester thread; regular cone serger thread is fine,
  woolly nylon optional for hems or softer seams.[^peekaboo][^porter-sew][^chalkbucket][^pc-notes]

# Thread colour rule (atelier)

Sew structural seams in one colour start to finish; match the mesh colour wherever mesh is in the
seam or under the elastic; on multicolour suits use the mid or lightest colour.[^porter-sew][^pc-notes]

# Consequences **[INFERRED]**

- Two seams closer than their combined allowances overlap; with 6 mm allowances a panel narrower than
  about 12 mm has no visible face (see [pieced seam geometry](/domain/construction/rule-pieced-seam-geometry.md)).
- Export both stitch line and cut line explicitly (see [DXF exchange](/domain/construction/dxf-pattern-exchange.md)).
