# Takeaway

**Custom orders everywhere follow one path: brief → digital design with revisions → sign-off →
sizing with a fit kit → production.** Only GK documents a physical, fully embellished sample as part
of custom work. Lead time is dominated by queueing, approval and size collection, not touch time,
and is seasonal. Embellishment (stone count and grade) is the main price lever. Figures read on
13 Oct 2026; they will drift.[^pc-notes]

# The order path, by maker

- **GK Elite:** four ordering paths: (1) stock plus logo, name or crystals; (2) special order /
  UDesign, an online 3D studio for team purchases only; (3) sublimation programme (Engineered,
  SoftLux, Custom); (4) full custom design for a one-time flat fee covering design, multiple revisions
  and a fully embellished sample. Recommended team sequence: confirm colours and logo → choose
  garments → review personalisation → collect accurate sizing → plan against the competition
  calendar. Special-order items are not returnable.[^gk-personalize][^gk-udesign][^gk-custom][^pc-notes]
- **Sylvia P:** recolour a catalogue design free in the Studio SP 3D tool, or pay a custom design
  fee to work with in-house designers; an account manager runs the order.[^sp-design][^pc-notes]
- **Quatro:** consultation → free design concepts → digital sign-off document → production →
  delivery; sizing sets and swatches on request.[^quatro-process][^pc-notes]
- **Velocity Pro Sport:** enquiry and brief → digital visuals → approval, sizing confirmation and
  sample sizes → production; no minimum order, but customer-supplied designs may be declined for
  small orders (15 leotards is the example) because of the pattern work.[^velocity][^velocity-faq][^pc-notes]

# Implications **[INFERRED]**

- The universal approval artefact is a digital visual plus a sign-off sheet; a tool producing an
  approvable 3D view and a sign-off sheet fits the existing workflow.
- Recolouring a catalogue style is free and fast (2–6 weeks); a new pattern or artwork costs a fee
  and 7–13 weeks. The tool should distinguish designs that reuse an existing graded block from those
  needing new pattern pieces, because that changes price and lead time.
- A cost estimator should be driven mainly by stone count by size and grade, then fabric count and
  class, panel/seam count, appliqué perimeter and print area. A defensible stage list in factory
  order: design and approval; pattern and grading; marker and cutting; printing and transfer;
  appliqué and embroidery; crystal motifs and hand setting; assembly and elastic; finishing,
  inspection and packing.
- Machine time for stones is small (10,000 stones ≈ one hour at 170 stones/min on one head, a
  theoretical lower bound); the cost is in stones, film handling, pressing and hand corrections.
