# Takeaway

**The leotard block is driven by girth (the torso loop) for length and chest/bust for width, then
waist and hip; necklines, backs and colour-block lines are drawn on a traced copy after size and
length alterations, and every new panel edge gets 6 mm of seam allowance.** These are the manual
procedures the pattern tool automates.[^pc-notes]

# Measurements that drive the block

- **GK:** chest (fullest part, arms down), waist (narrowest), hip (fullest part of seat, feet
  together) and **torso** (centre of one shoulder, down the front, through the legs, up the back to
  the same shoulder), called the most important measurement; partial inches round up; between-size
  athletes take the larger size.[^gk-chart][^pc-notes]
- **Jalie (one-piece bodysuits):** pick the size by bust for width and by girth for length.[^jalie-2677][^pc-notes]
- **Atelier (Porter):** bust, underbust, waist, hips, girth loop, back-waist length, arm, inseam;
  measured over a leotard, taken twice.[^porter-measure][^pc-notes]
- Size charts per brand live in [/domain/sizing/](/domain/sizing/about.md).
- Open precedent for a measurement-driven leotard block: FreeSewing's MIT-licensed "Onyx" one-piece
  covers swimsuits, unisuits, leotards and unitards from about 20 body measurements.[^freesewing][^gg-notes]

# Order of alterations (atelier)

1. Size and length alterations first. Grade between sizes by blending (see [grading](/domain/construction/grading-across-sizes.md)).[^porter-alter][^pc-notes]
2. Figure adjustments with numbers: add about ½ cm of length per cup size above C at the fullest
   chest; remove about 1 in at the back waist for a curved back; redraw the back leg opening outward
   by 1–2+ in to stop it riding up.[^porter-alter][^pc-notes]
3. Neck and back changes on a traced copy.[^porter-neck][^pc-notes]
4. Colour blocking last.[^porter-colour][^pc-notes]

# Necklines and backs on the block (Porter)

| Style | Drafting rule |
|---|---|
| Scoop neck | Mark the low point and redraw; allow about ¾ in of fabric lost to the elastic finish; cut conservatively |
| Boat neck | Needs a shelf-bra lining (1 in elastic under the bust for fuller chests) |
| Keyhole / hole back | Keep > 1.5 in between neckline and top of the opening; neck plus hole must be big enough to step into; extend the opening ½–1 in for closure adjustment; butterfly clasp |
| Strappy back | See [back, strap and keyhole formulas](/domain/construction/back-strap-and-keyhole-formulas.md) |
| Banded (T-shirt) collar | Strip width = 2 × (finished height + ¼ in); strip length = measured neckline − 1 to 2 in; choker length = neck + 2 in |
| Illusion ("cutaway") neckline | Cut bodice to the false neckline, back with sheer mesh overlapping 1 in inside the bodice edge; glue, dry, zigzag as appliqué |

Sources: Porter, neck and back variations.[^porter-neck][^pc-notes] Commercial home patterns use the
same back vocabulary: Jalie 2792 has a plain scoop back, a keyhole back with back-neck clasp and
banded jewel neck, and a banded square neck with clasp.[^jalie-2792][^pc-notes]

# Splitting the block into colour-block panels (Porter)

1. Trace the fitted pattern; draw the design lines.
2. Mark a grain guide parallel to centre front on every piece.
3. Cut apart; add ¼ in (6 mm) seam allowance **only to the new edges** (existing edges already
   include theirs).
4. For asymmetric designs make a full (unfolded) pattern instead of a half on the fold.
5. Keep pieced lines straight or gently curved; plan sewing order so no seam turns a corner or sets a
   square edge into an L-shaped opening; join small pieces into blocks first; stagger pieces at
   points so allowances cross on the seam line; press allowances toward the darker fabric.
6. Intricate or "wild" shapes are appliquéd instead.

Source: Porter, colour blocking.[^porter-colour][^pc-notes] Shape limits are encoded in
[pieced seam geometry](/domain/construction/rule-pieced-seam-geometry.md).

# Sleeves

- A conventional high-crowned set-in sleeve drafted in stretch fabric restricts arm lift and leaves a
  fold at the crown when the arm drops; stretch sleeves need a shallower, shirt-like crown (wider
  underarm). Watkins drafts the analysis bodysuit as if the arms were held out at about 45°.[^watkins][^pc-notes]
- The sleeve must be re-matched every time the body width is reduced.[^dpm-blocks][^pc-notes]
- Mesh is the usual sleeve fabric for competition leotards at GK.[^gk-blog-fabrics][^pc-notes]
- A home maker found pattern long sleeves too tight for a muscular child gymnast.[^chalkbucket][^pc-notes]
- No source gives a numeric rule for drafting mesh sleeves differently from lycra sleeves.[^pc-notes]

# Implications **[INFERRED]**

- The parametric block's driving parameters are girth (length), chest/bust (width), then waist and hip.
- The tool's "draw a panel line" operation is the digital equivalent of steps 1–4 above: new edges
  get allowance, existing edges keep theirs, every piece gets a grain line, asymmetric designs get
  full pieces.
- If the user designs on a 3D body, the arms-at-45° drafting pose and the sleeve-crown rule suggest
  the sleeve should be flattened from an arms-raised pose, not the mannequin stance.
