# Takeaway

**The children's book has no leotard block, but it has what our child blocks lack: real body
proportions by height for girls, a separate chart for 11-14-year-olds whose figures are developing,
and the leg and crotch girths a leotard's openings depend on.**[^aldrich-children] Its own
swimwear is built from vest and briefs blocks that take only about 5-7% off the chest, roughly
dancewear or underwear tension, so the competition reduction must come from elsewhere
(the [adult swimwear block](/domain/construction/aldrich-stretch-body-and-swimwear-blocks.md), the
purchased block, or the yardsticks).[^calc]

# How the charts are built

- **Sized by height (Centilong)** in 6 cm steps; a size covers its label ±3 cm (110 = 107-112.9).
  Age is a secondary label only.[^aldrich-children]
- **75th centile**: the blocks fit about three children in four in each height band, so the charts
  run generous against most retailers.[^aldrich-children]
- **Waist taken with the abdomen relaxed** (BS EN 13402), which reads larger than the "erect" waist
  of the older BS 7231 survey.[^aldrich-children]
- From about 11-14 years height stops predicting girth; label bust and hip as well as height.[^aldrich-children]
- **Size groups carry their own constants.** Drafts state offsets per group (98-116, 122-140,
  146-164 cm), and grades are worked out from the first size of each group, so a quantity such as
  "armhole raised 1 / 1.5 / 2 cm" steps between groups rather than scaling smoothly.[^aldrich-children]

# Measurement definitions (pp. 31-33)

Taken over light underwear, barefoot, with a tape marking the natural waist. Girths: chest under
the arms over the shoulder blades; waist (relaxed); hip/seat at the fullest part; neck at the base,
touching the cervical and the collarbone; upper arm (bent); wrist; ankle. Lengths: across back
(midway between cervical and armhole base); shoulder (neck base to shoulder edge); scye depth
(cervical to the armhole line); back neck to waist; waist to hip; cervical height; waist to knee;
**body rise** (seated, side waist to stool, or waist height minus inside leg); inside leg; arm
length. **Vertical trunk**: from mid-shoulder down the back, under the crotch, up over the front to
the start; this is our [torso loop](/domain/sizing/torso-loop-measurement.md). Scye depth and waist
to hip are hard to take; read them from the chart by height.[^aldrich-children] The chart prints no
values for the vertical trunk.

# Against our body targets

Our [targets](/domain/sizing/body-measurement-targets-by-age.md) sit close for the 8-year-old
(130 cm: chest 66 vs 66-67, back neck-waist 31 vs about 31), with waists 3-5 cm under Aldrich's,
as intended for a slim gymnast build against a 75th-centile relaxed waist. The 13-year-old target
(155 cm) has a back neck-waist of 34 cm against Aldrich's 37.3 (developing chart, interpolated): the
target torso is about 3 cm short **[INFERRED]**; worth checking against the `teen` mannequin's own
measure, since torso length is the most-named cause of leotard fit failures.

# The book's swimwear (ch. 7, p. 158)

Swimwear 1 joins the vest block (quarter chest less 1 cm) to the briefs block (quarter hip less
1 cm) at the waist with 2.5 cm added; Swimwear 2 adds a skirt, Swimwear 3 a leggings bottom. The
close-fitting ribbed tee takes 6-8 cm off the chest by size group.[^aldrich-children] As shares of
the girls' chest that is 4.8-7.3% (vest) and 9.5-11.1% (ribbed tee), against about 16-21% in the
adult swimwear block.[^calc]
