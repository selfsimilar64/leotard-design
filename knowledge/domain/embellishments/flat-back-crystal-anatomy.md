# Takeaway

A flat-back ("rose") crystal is a **faceted glass crown over a flat mirror**: a table plus one or
two rings of crown facets (8–18 depending on cut), no pavilion, a reflective foil underneath and,
for hotfix stones, a heat-activated glue layer on the bottom.[^edutech-hotfix][^wiki-diamondcut][^ecs]
Light enters through the crown, reflects off the foil and leaves through the crown, which makes it
optically much simpler than a diamond.[^srr] Its colour and coating are separate layers; see
[coatings and AB](/domain/embellishments/crystal-coatings-and-ab.md).

# Layers, top to bottom

| Layer | What sources say | Citations |
|---|---|---|
| Crown coating (optional) | AB, Shimmer, metallic effects; on about half or all of the crown | [coatings](/domain/embellishments/crystal-coatings-and-ab.md) |
| Glass crown | Table + crown facets. Ordinary glass n ≈ 1.5; lead glass n ≈ 1.7–1.8 with higher dispersion; lead glass 18–40% PbO, "lead crystal" ≥24%; modern lead-free crystal uses barium, zinc or potassium oxide. Density ≤2.4 g/cm³ (soda-lime) vs ≈3.1 g/cm³ (lead crystal). Swarovski lead-free since 2012. | [^wiki-leadglass][^ecs][^srr] |
| Mirror back | Swarovski: "platinum foiling for extra brilliance and protection". Preciosa MAXIMA: "Silver Reflective Layer" of genuine silver under a bonded "DuraFoiling™" protective layer. Weak foil clouds, dulls or separates under heat, moisture and wear. LacquerPRO-type colours have opaque lacquer instead of foil. | [^swarovski-2018][^ru-underside][^ru-washing][^ecs] |
| Hotfix glue | Turns liquid with heat and solid again after cooling; Wikipedia gives activation at about 180–200 °C (see [press parameters](/domain/embellishments/hotfix-press-parameters.md) for manufacturer ranges). Glue chemistry, thickness and colour are not published. | [^preciosa-manual][^wiki-rhinestone][^ecs] |

Historical note: Strass coated the underside of lead glass with metal powder, later replaced by foil
and vapour-deposited metal.[^wiki-rhinestone]

# Height

Only hobbyist measurements exist (brand unknown): ss10 ≈ 1.4 mm, ss16 = 1.5 mm, ss20 = 2 mm,
ss34 = 3 mm.[^edutech-hotfix] **[INFERRED]** Total height including glue ≈ 0.4 × diameter (ss16
0.38, ss20 0.43, ss34 0.42), rising to ≈0.5 × at ss10.[^ecs] No manufacturer table of table
diameter, crown angle or height per size was found.

# Weight estimate

**[INFERRED]** (assumes a stone fills ~60% of its bounding cylinder, glass 2.5–3.1 g/cm³, hobbyist
heights): ss10 ≈ 13–16 mg, ss16 ≈ 27–33 mg, ss20 ≈ 52–65 mg. Ten thousand ss16-average stones ≈
270–330 g, consistent with the reported "about a pound" Olympic leotard.[^ecs]

# Optical vocabulary

- Brightness/brilliance = total internal and external reflection of white light returned to the
  viewer; fire = dispersion into spectral colours; scintillation = the pattern of light and dark
  and the sparkle when moved.[^gia-cut][^wiki-diamondcut]
- Gemological dispersion (nB − nG): diamond 0.044, flint glass 0.041, quartz 0.013.[^wiki-dispersion]
- Retail claim: sparkle is driven by facet count, cut design and glass quality/refractive index;
  MAXIMA is said to stay brilliant from many angles and reduce dead zones.[^ru-sparkle]
- **[INFERRED]** Each stone throws a handful of discrete direction-dependent glints. Table-in/
  table-out acts as a tilted mirror under glass (bright white highlight tracking the fabric normal);
  paths through differently inclined facets act as prisms (small coloured glints away from the
  mirror direction). With N facets there are ~N² in/out combinations. More facets = more, smaller,
  more frequent flashes. Fire is weak at n ≈ 1.5–1.6 with short paths, so colour fringes should be
  subtle except with AB.[^ecs] Rendering approach: [crystal optics look-alike](/engineering/rendering/sparkle/crystal-optics-look-alike.md).

No refractive index or Abbe number is published for Swarovski "Advanced Crystal" or Preciosa
crystal; Preciosa only claims a "higher refractive index than similar competing products".[^ecs]
