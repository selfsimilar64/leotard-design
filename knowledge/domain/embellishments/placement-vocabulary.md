# Takeaway

Designers work with a **small, recognisable set of moves**: outline a seam, neckline, cut-out or
logo; fill a shape or lettering; scatter with a density gradient ("cascade", "rainfall", "meteor
shower"); build dense clusters; radiate bursts; repeat stripes/chevrons; draw swirls; alternate
colours or sizes; place single large accents — mixing two or three stone sizes and one to six
colours.[^ecs] These map directly onto the app's brushes and fills (tool baseline in
[rhinestone layout software](/domain/embellishments/rhinestone-layout-software.md)).

# Taxonomy for the app's brush and fill tools

| # | Move | What it is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Outline / path | Single or multi-row line along a seam, neckline, keyhole/open back, cuff, colour-block edge or artwork contour | Rhinestone Seattle-skyline outline; racing stripe on a sleeve[^cgn-2026-05]; "rhinestones surrounding the open back"[^cgn-2026-02]; "rhinestoned statement neckline"[^cgn-2025-08] |
| 2 | Lettering and logo fill | Team name on cuff/sleeve, stoned logo on the back; outline + interior fill | Team names on cuffs (UCLA, Missouri)[^cgn-2026-05]; "stoned logo"[^cgn-2025-08]; names/logos "on select locations"[^gk-resilient]; lettering "filled in with the blue sparkles"[^cgn-2026-02] |
| 3 | Region fill | Grid, hex ("brick") or contour-following rows inside a closed shape; hex for density, contour when the edge must read | "dense geometric design", "sparkled-out torso"[^cgn-2025-08] |
| 4 | Gradient scatter | Random non-overlapping placement whose density (often size) falls off from an anchor edge | "Crystals cascade down from the neckline in a heavy crystal necklace shape", sleeves a "scatter"[^sylviap-nia]; "starry/meteor shower look"[^cgn-2026-02]; "rainfall" gap fillers[^cp-stretch]; ombré under stones[^cgn-2026-02] |
| 5 | Clusters | Dense local mixed-size groups, sparse surroundings | "heavy clusters of rhinestones"[^cgn-2026-05]; "clustering … on the cuffs"; sleeves as "big clusters of sparkles"[^cgn-2026-02] |
| 6 | Radial motifs | Bursts, stars, sunbursts; radial fill from a centre | "star shapes … bursts of color"[^marieclaire] |
| 7 | Linear repeats | Stripes, chevrons, lattice; most distortion-sensitive on the body | "stripes"[^marieclaire]; "chevron rhinestone design"; a reviewer preferred "scattered rhinestones instead of linear designs"[^cgn-2026-02] |
| 8 | Flourishes | Swirls and paisley as curved paths with tapering stone size | "paisley patterns … swirls"[^marieclaire] |
| 9 | Accent stones | Individually placed large/shaped stones and pearls at focal points | "bigger orange rhinestones" as front accents[^cgn-2026-02]; "sparse yet intentional stones"[^cgn-2025-08] |
| 10 | Alternation | Two colours or sizes alternating along a path | "alternating orange and clear rhinestones on the back"[^cgn-2026-02] |

Also seen: negative space carved by jewels and sublimation over mesh;[^nbcdfw] a figurative motif
(goat head) built as one crystal transfer;[^nbcphl] flag designs.[^marieclaire]

# Rules of thumb (retail and hobbyist)

- Use "about 2-3 different sizes to help add dimension"; random size mixes make light bounce
  unpredictably; "The bigger the stone, the flashier the shine", fields of small stones give a
  "dazzling effect".[^ru-whatsize]
- Scatter smaller stones between larger ones for "rainfall"; choose heavy coverage vs accents on
  neckline, waist, bust or sleeves; matching stone to fabric colour intensifies the colour,
  contrasting stones make the design stand out; clear and AB are the most used.[^cp-stretch]
- Plan first: sketch or mark dots for even spacing; use placement grids for straight or diagonal lines.[^cp-gym]
- ss16 is the smallest size for comfortable filled lettering by hand.[^edutech-hotfix]
- Spacing numbers: see [placement rules](/domain/embellishments/stone-placement-rules.md) R5.

# Defaults

- **Symmetry** is a per-element toggle: classic designs mirror about centre front/back, but 2026
  NCAA trends show deliberate asymmetry (one-shoulder, single waist cut-out).[^cgn-2026-05] **[INFERRED]**[^ecs]
- **Size gradation**: largest stones nearest the anchor (neckline, focal point), stepping down
  through 2–3 sizes as density thins. **[INFERRED]** — no source states the order.[^ecs]

No leotard designer has published spacing or size-ratio rules; GK's J/T pattern parameterisation is
unknown.[^ecs] The proof of concept implements moves 1, 4, 6, 8 and a phyllotaxis region fill
([stones component](/engineering/poc/stones.md)).
